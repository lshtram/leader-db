# Customer Methodology Writing

Write factual, compact prose for an intelligent reader who may not know the project.

## Writing standard

1. Describe; do not advertise. State the design, action, result, or control directly. Remove self-congratulatory labels such as “designed for transparency,” “robust,” or “best in class” unless evidence and context make the characterization necessary.
2. Prefer affirmative descriptions of roles and actions. Explain what each person or stage does. Avoid lists of actions it does not perform.
3. Use negative wording only for a concrete, material risk, exclusion, or boundary. Name the risk first, then state the control. Example: “A judge who gathered new evidence could apply a different evidentiary standard from the other cases. The judging instructions therefore restrict the judge to the reviewed dossier.”
4. Treat plain language as adult language. Explain unfamiliar concepts and remove unnecessary jargon while preserving precision, nuance, and professional tone.
5. State each substantive point once in the best location. Repeat only when the reader must act on it again, when a later section materially extends it, or when a safety-critical control needs deliberate reinforcement.
6. Keep headings factual. Use headings that name the subject, decision, stage, or question rather than claim a virtue.
7. Prefer a short concrete example over a second abstract explanation.
8. Delete throat-clearing, promotional transitions, summaries that merely restate the preceding section, and lists whose items duplicate nearby prose.

## Revision pass

Before delivery:

1. Mark every sentence that advertises a quality instead of describing observable design or behavior; rewrite or remove it.
2. Mark every “not,” “never,” “without,” and “does not.” Retain it only when it states a material risk, exclusion, or result-changing boundary; otherwise replace it with the affirmative action.
3. Remove repeated claims, including repetitions distributed across overview, callout, conclusion, and section introductions.
4. Check that simplified language remains precise and suitable for an adult customer.
5. Confirm that each heading and callout adds information rather than praise or repetition.
