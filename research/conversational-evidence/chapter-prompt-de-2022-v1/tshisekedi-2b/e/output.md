## Orientation

The exposure gate is clearly met. In 2022, the DRC remained immersed in inherited armed conflicts in the east, while M23’s renewed offensive—independently associated with Rwandan military support—gave the crisis a major cross-border dimension. Other armed groups, Ugandan and Burundian operations, MONUSCO, and weak state control further constrained Tshisekedi’s choices.

Tshisekedi possessed substantial but not exclusive authority. Constitutionally, he was commander-in-chief, chaired the Superior Defence Council, appointed senior commanders jointly with government institutions, could declare a state of siege, and shared defence, security, and foreign policy with the government. Actual civilian control remained limited.

The favorable evidence is substantial on diplomacy: Tshisekedi participated in the Nairobi and Luanda processes, accepted a ceasefire and monitored M23 withdrawal, continued political consultations, DDR review, humanitarian access, prisoner screening, and regional verification. No reviewed evidence shows that he initiated an interstate war, sought annexation, or treated force as the sole first option.

The adverse evidence is strongest on conduct and control. Tshisekedi prolonged an inherited state of siege whose stated civilian-protection objective was not achieved; military authorities suppressed critics and tried civilians. FARDC personnel committed killings, conflict-related sexual violence, attacks on schools, resource predation, and collaborated with armed groups, including documented cooperation with the FDLR during the M23 conflict. Accountability existed at lower levels but remained selective and weak at senior levels.

The net end-state was worse: conflict deaths, displacement, M23 territorial advances, and interstate tension increased during 2022. That deterioration cannot be attributed solely to Tshisekedi because M23 and Rwanda were major independent causal actors and the underlying conflicts predated him. Evidence concerning his personal knowledge of particular atrocities, military procurement patronage, or deliberate public misinformation remains insufficient.

The D13/UCDP local prior was supplied as recording conflict events, fatalities, one-sided violence, and government-actor killings. The D14/SIPRI prior was also identified, but its country-year cells could not be independently opened here; the current SIPRI database confirms its definitions, annual revision, and open-source methodology, not the supplied numeric value.

## Ten-question disposition

1. **2B.1 — Mixed, with meaningful diplomatic evidence.** Records 003–005 show a genuine external threat alongside Tshisekedi’s participation in two negotiated processes. Record 006 shows prolonged reliance on emergency military rule despite serious evidence of ineffectiveness and abuse.

2. **2B.2 — No demonstrated war of choice or annexation; adverse proxy evidence.** The reviewed record does not show Tshisekedi initiating an interstate war. Record 007 nevertheless shows FARDC cooperation with armed groups during the M23 conflict, including FDLR cooperation independently documented by UN experts. Personal presidential authorization is not established.

3. **2B.3 — The central M23/Rwanda threat was genuine, not manufactured.** Record 003 independently corroborates M23 atrocities and Rwandan involvement. Record 006 weakens the justification for indefinitely maintaining the state of siege because deaths increased and the promised security result did not materialize. Prestige, diversion, or revenge motives were not demonstrated.

4. **2B.4 — Adverse overall, with limited corrective measures.** Records 006 and 008 document unlawful killings, sexual violence, military prosecution of civilians, displacement, and abuse. Records 008–009 document action plans, UN-supported training, investigations, and some convictions, but these did not overcome persistent impunity.

5. **2B.5 — Material failure to control forces and armed-group partners.** Record 007 documents FARDC collaboration with armed groups and alleged arms transfers; record 008 documents state-agent abuse. Record 004 shows that Tshisekedi later accepted an explicit regional obligation to end all support to armed groups. Evidence of effective implementation in 2022 is weak.

6. **2B.6 — Official threat claims were substantially corroborated, but the state-of-siege narrative became misleading by omission.** Record 003 supports the government’s core claim about M23/Rwandan involvement. Record 006 shows that authorities continued presenting emergency rule as protective despite worsening deaths and repression. No direct evidence establishes fabricated intelligence ordered by Tshisekedi.

7. **2B.7 — Favorable and comparatively well documented.** Records 004–005 establish participation in ceasefire, withdrawal, verification, consultation, DDR, prisoner-release, child-release, and humanitarian-access arrangements. Implementation was incomplete, and the Luanda framework retained coercive force as a fallback.

8. **2B.8 — Insufficient for a firm patronage finding.** Record 010 establishes the authoritative SIPRI measurement source, while records 007–008 reveal resource predation and weak control within FARDC. The reviewed evidence does not trace 2022 procurement, off-budget expenditure, contracts, beneficiaries, or Tshisekedi-directed enrichment. This lens remains materially underdetermined.

9. **2B.9 — Partial lower-level accountability, little demonstrated presidential accountability.** Record 009 records prosecutions and convictions but widespread impunity, especially at higher ranks. Record 006 contains no acknowledgment or correction by Tshisekedi commensurate with the state of siege’s failed protection rationale. Personal obstruction was not established.

10. **2B.10 — Relations and security deteriorated, with substantial exogenous causation.** M23 resurgence, Rwandan intervention, civilian deaths, displacement, and interstate hostility made the end-of-2022 environment less peaceful than the inherited start. Records 003–005 show Tshisekedi attempting regional containment, so deterioration alone is not proof that he caused it.

- **2B.8:** the exact D14/SIPRI 2022 value, defence-budget execution, emergency or classified accounts, procurement contracts, and beneficiary networks.
- **2B.5/2B.9:** command-level knowledge of FARDC–FDLR cooperation and final dispositions of cases against officers.
- **2B.6:** Tshisekedi’s speeches and internal briefing record concerning Rwanda, M23, and continuation of the state of siege.
- **2B.10:** event-level UCDP comparison between the beginning and end of 2022 and verified implementation of the November ceasefire.

The central conclusions on exposure, genuine external threat, diplomatic engagement, state-of-siege failure, serious FARDC-control problems, and incomplete accountability are already supported by convergent evidence. Additional general searching is now more likely to repeat those findings than change them.
