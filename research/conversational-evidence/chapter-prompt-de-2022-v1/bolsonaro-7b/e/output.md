## 1. Orientation

The period is calendar year 2022, Bolsonaro’s fourth and final year as president. Constitutionally, he headed the federal executive, appointed and dismissed ministers, and directed the federal administration. Congress, the judiciary, the TSE, prosecutors, auditors, federalism, and coalition politics imposed meaningful constraints.

The information environment remained sufficiently open for courts, auditors, journalists, fact-checkers, and civil society to investigate presidential conduct. Polarization and attacks on the press may have chilled some reporting, but silence is not the principal evidentiary problem here.

The strongest admissible adverse evidence concerns:

- Repeated, personally delivered false or unsupported statements about election integrity, including to foreign diplomats, despite contemporaneous official rebuttals.
- Using presidential premises, publicly funded communications services, and Independence Bicentennial events for reelection activity.
- Withholding information relevant to checking Bolsonaro’s public vaccination assertions.
- Resistance to investigative scrutiny, including failure to attend a court-ordered Federal Police interview.
- A later police finding that Bolsonaro personally participated in a December 2022 plan to remain in power; this was allegation/procedural evidence in the opened source, although later official STF pages report a final conviction.
- A significant but temporally inherited gifts controversy whose Bolsonaro-specific ownership and disposition questions emerged principally after he left office.

Important limiting evidence includes investigative findings of no crime in the alleged general interference with the Federal Police, closure of the Covaxin-prevarication matter, dismissal of unsupported Petrobras allegations, and the fact that the lawful transition initially began with Bolsonaro’s authorization. There is little affirmative evidence of costly candor, voluntary correction, rigorous separation of family interests, or integrity-promoting reform.

## 3. Ten-question disposition

- **7B.1 — Does the ruler habitually tell the truth to the public, legislature, courts, allies, and international partners, especially on matters where deception would protect power or reputation?**
  WEB-7B-002 and WEB-7B-003 establish repeated personally attributable false or distorted public claims, especially about elections and the claimed absence of government corruption. WEB-7B-010 documents a later contradiction between his categorical denial of coup discussions and the Federal Police account.

- **7B.2 — Does the ruler admit errors, correct false claims, and allow truthful reporting, or do they knowingly mislead, double down, blame others, and punish truth-tellers?**
  The election claims persisted after repeated rebuttal; no material correction was found. WEB-7B-006 and WEB-7B-007 show opacity or resistance when claims and conduct were examined. Evidence of direct punishment of a particular truth-teller in 2022 is less conclusive.

- **7B.3 — Does the ruler separate personal/family/business interests from state decisions, public contracts, licensing, regulation, law enforcement, and foreign policy?**
  WEB-7B-004 and WEB-7B-005 show failure to separate reelection interests from presidential resources. WEB-7B-011 raises a serious official-gifts issue, but the opened source records a precautionary proceeding, not a final finding of 2022 personal appropriation.

- **7B.4 — Does the ruler or close family profit from office through assets, contracts, monopolies, gifts, bribes, emoluments, insider access, opaque foundations, or hidden conflicts of interest?**
  WEB-7B-011 is the principal admissible lead. It establishes possession and a high-value-gifts investigation, but not a final target-year finding of illicit profit. Earlier material concerning Flávio Bolsonaro does not establish Jair Bolsonaro’s benefit and receives no decisive weight.

- **7B.5 — Does the ruler appoint competent professionals, or fill government with family, friends, cronies, donors, business associates, loyalists, and yes-men to protect personal power or self-dealing?**
  Formal appointment authority is clear from WEB-7B-001. Evidence of extensive military placement exists, but the inspected record did not establish that particular 2022 appointments were made to facilitate Bolsonaro’s self-dealing or concealment. This lens remains underdetermined.

- **7B.6 — Does the ruler tolerate independent investigation of their conduct, assets, campaign finance, conflicts of interest, associates, and concealed official decisions?**
  Courts, PF, TSE, TCU, and CGU continued to function. WEB-7B-007 shows personal nonattendance at a compelled interview; WEB-7B-006 shows excessive withholding. WEB-7B-008 supplies necessary contrary evidence that some investigations operated and cleared or failed to substantiate allegations.

- **7B.7 — Does the ruler use state power to conceal illegal, destructive, or self-serving activity, protect themselves from accountability, punish investigators, or neutralize courts, prosecutors, auditors, media, and whistleblowers?**
  WEB-7B-004–007 provide direct nexus evidence involving public resources, secrecy, and investigative resistance. WEB-7B-010 is potentially the most material power-protection evidence, although the fully opened record describes a police accusation rather than the later final judgment.

- **7B.8 — Does the ruler keep promises and respect formal commitments, or opportunistically reverse positions, manipulate public information, and conceal tradeoffs for personal advantage?**
  WEB-7B-002, WEB-7B-003, and WEB-7B-010 show manipulation of electoral information tied to retention of power. WEB-7B-009 is limited contrary evidence: Bolsonaro authorized transition machinery and urged an end to road blockades, although he did not publicly concede.

- **7B.9 — Does the ruler avoid nepotism, favoritism, clientelism, and transactional politics in appointments, pardons, procurement, enforcement, and access to public information?**
  Campaign use of exclusive public resources is established by WEB-7B-004 and WEB-7B-005. Family-visitor secrecy and gifts are relevant leads, but inspected evidence is insufficient for a broad 2022 nepotism or procurement conclusion.

- **7B.10 — Does the ruler model ethical standards that improve public trust, or normalize deliberate lying, impunity, self-dealing, conflicts of interest, and cynicism?**
  WEB-7B-002–005 show a repeated and judicially checked pattern of false public claims and conversion of official communications or ceremonies to electoral advantage. WEB-7B-008 and WEB-7B-009 prevent treating every allegation or post-election act as adverse.

## 4. Source dispositions

**Independent corroboration**

- TSE’s later Independence Day judgment independently corroborates the contemporaneous campaign-resource concern.
- Reuters independently documented the contents and immediate rebuttal of the ambassadors presentation.
- TSE election-integrity records and OAS observation independently undermine the unsupported fraud narrative.
- CGU, TCU, PF, STF, and TSE proceedings show that several accountability channels remained operational.

**Reused supplied sources**

- E040: TSE campaign-livestream restriction, reopened and verified.
- E045: hunger assertion remained a useful lead but was not retained as a separate record because the election-deception evidence was stronger and more directly adjudicated.
- E049: useful limiting context that Bolsonaro himself had not then been accused of corruption; not used as proof of integrity.
- E050: useful transparency and staffing lead, but attribution and PDF extraction were weaker than the retained records.
- E051: replaced by the correctly opened AP URL and treated as allegation/procedural evidence.

**Uninspected leads**

- Full presidential-family visitor logs and the underlying CGU appellate opinions.
- Complete 2022 asset declarations and beneficial-ownership records.
- Appointment-by-appointment evidence establishing loyalty or family benefit.
- Complete final jewelry-investigation disposition.
- The full AP 2668 criminal judgment.

**Rejected**

- General CPI, V-Dem, WGI, CPI, press-attack, Indigenous-rights, COVID-outcome, environmental, and military-staffing evidence without a demonstrated Bolsonaro integrity nexus.
- Flávio Bolsonaro’s *rachadinha* case as proof against Jair Bolsonaro.
- Unproved vaccine-record falsification allegations.
- Generic national corruption or repression evidence.
- Wikipedia, Reddit summaries, and duplicated news reports.

**Blocked/partial**

- Several official STF news pages concerning AP 2668, Covaxin, Petrobras, and the confidential-data investigation returned 403 or fetch errors when reopened. Their indexed contents were not promoted to fully extracted machine evidence.
- The TCU 2024 complete gifts decision rendered only a landing/search interface.
- The local researcher guide and source-confidence registry could not be accessed because the session exposed no working local-shell reader.

## 6. Remaining questions and saturation

The largest remaining gaps are ruler-specific personal profit, beneficial ownership, family access, procurement favors, and appointment motives. More research in Brazilian case dockets, asset filings, gift inventories, and appointment records could materially improve 7B.3–7B.5 and 7B.9. Additional general searches about election falsehoods or public-resource use are unlikely to add much; those themes are saturated and independently adjudicated.

No score is assigned.
