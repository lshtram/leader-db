## Orientation

Scope is calendar year 2022. AMLO controlled the federal executive, cabinet appointments, budget proposals, infrastructure priorities, regulatory administration, and state enterprises. Congress, courts, states, autonomous regulators, the tripartite wage commission, and especially the constitutionally autonomous Banco de México shared or constrained economic authority. Monetary tightening therefore should not be attributed to AMLO alone.

Mexico entered 2022 recovering slowly from COVID-19, with longstanding low productivity, high informality and inequality, weak competition and rule of law, limited fiscal revenue, infrastructure gaps, and substantial Pemex liabilities. Russia’s invasion of Ukraine, food and energy shocks, U.S. monetary tightening, weaker prospective U.S. growth, and lingering pandemic disruption were major external constraints.

The strongest favorable evidence is the administration-supported 22% minimum-wage increase, improving employment and real labor income, declining labor poverty and labor-income inequality, and continued fiscal restraint. The strongest adverse evidence is untargeted fuel support, opportunity costs from Pemex and flagship projects, weak investment conditions, preferential treatment of state energy companies, deficient procurement transparency, and little measurable improvement against corruption.

No chapter score is assigned, as requested. Local files could not be inspected through the available read interface; the dossier therefore relies on reopened web-accessible underlying sources. This is a material limitation for calibration against local WDI/PIP/WGI/V-Dem priors.

## Ten-question disposition

1. **5B.1 — Broad-based and sustainable prosperity:** Mixed-positive intent and partial implementation. The wage increase, social programs and southern infrastructure strategy show a broad-welfare objective (WEB-5B-003, 006). Labor poverty and real income improved (WEB-5B-004). Sustainability is weakened by poorly targeted subsidies, Pemex burdens, project-selection concerns and low potential growth (WEB-5B-002, 005, 012).

2. **5B.2 — Competent and empowered professionals:** Moderate but incomplete evidence. AMLO retained economist Rogelio Ramírez de la O as finance secretary and appointed experienced public-finance official Victoria Rodríguez Ceja to Banco de México (WEB-5B-010). Institutional autonomy and the bank’s actual tightening counter a pure-loyalist account, but the appointment source does not establish how much independent authority AMLO allowed economic officials outside the central bank.

3. **5B.3 — Macroeconomic stability:** Substantially favorable, with attribution divided. Fiscal policy remained broadly neutral, debt near 56% of GDP, reserves adequate and banks resilient (WEB-5B-002). Banco de México tightened proactively, but its autonomy means AMLO receives limited credit for monetary action (WEB-5B-001). Fuel subsidies were costly and untargeted, while Pemex and weak fiscal buffers posed longer-term risks.

4. **5B.4 — Fair market and investment rules:** Adverse. Longstanding competition problems predated AMLO, but his government’s contract and energy-policy uncertainty further discouraged investment (WEB-5B-005). The June 2022 natural-gas action and other measures advantaged Pemex/CFE and triggered USMCA consultations (WEB-5B-007). Mexico nevertheless retained deep trade integration and a functioning private economy.

5. **5B.5 — Corruption, favoritism and capture:** Weak/adverse overall, with limited favorable enforcement. Procurement transparency was deficient, including one-third of tenders missing documents and almost all sampled INSABI purchases absent from the open-contracting standard (WEB-5B-008). Mexico remained at 31/100 in the CPI (WEB-5B-009), while energy rules favored state incumbents (WEB-5B-007). Supplier sanctions provide contrary evidence of some enforcement (WEB-5B-011), but neither sanctions nor the CPI establish AMLO’s personal integrity or culpability.

6. **5B.6 — Productivity foundations:** Mixed-to-weak. Social protection and public investment increased and southern infrastructure was intended to reduce regional disparities (WEB-5B-006). However, health and education remained strained, infrastructure gaps persisted, non-Pemex public investment was low, and independent reviewers called for stronger cost-benefit analysis and green/human-capital spending (WEB-5B-005, 012).

7. **5B.7 — Evidence and correction:** Mixed/adverse. The consensus wage-setting process and macroeconomic restraint are evidence of pragmatic policy (WEB-5B-002, 003). Conversely, untargeted subsidies, limited targeting and evaluation, procurement opacity, and continued energy preferences despite repeated investment and treaty concerns suggest weak correction in important areas (WEB-5B-007, 008, 012).

8. **5B.8 — Fair distribution:** Favorable movement but large persistent gaps. Labor poverty fell nationally, real labor income rose, and lower-income quintiles gained most late in 2022 (WEB-5B-004). Yet rural labor poverty remained 52.3%, women earned substantially less than men, informal workers earned about half formal workers’ income, and southern-state poverty remained exceptionally high.

9. **5B.9 — Shock management:** Mixed-positive. The government preserved fiscal stability and cushioned fuel prices during the global inflation shock; employment and labor income recovered (WEB-5B-002, 004). The support was broadly untargeted and absorbed the oil-revenue windfall. Inflation reached 8.7%, while the most forceful stabilization came from autonomous Banco de México.

10. **5B.10 — 2022 trajectory relative to inheritance:** Mixed. Output, employment, real labor income and labor poverty improved during pandemic recovery, while debt remained controlled (WEB-5B-002, 004). However, labor poverty remained above its pre-pandemic level, investment uncertainty and weak productivity persisted, and energy favoritism worsened long-term investment conditions (WEB-5B-005, 007). One recovery year cannot resolve the tenure trajectory.

## Source disposition

**Independent corroboration**

- IMF macro-fiscal assessment corroborates fiscal restraint while distinguishing Banco de México’s action.
- CONEVAL household data corroborate that aggregate recovery reached labor incomes, but expose gender, regional and informal-sector gaps.
- OECD analysis independently corroborates both increased social/public investment and weak productivity, competition, health, education and investment conditions.
- USTR’s formal consultation corroborates that energy-policy objections advanced beyond commentary to an interstate treaty procedure.
- IMCO procurement-data analysis adds operational evidence behind broader corruption-risk indicators.
