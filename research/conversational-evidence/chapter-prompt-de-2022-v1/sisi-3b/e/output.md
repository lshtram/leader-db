## Orientation

The target period is calendar year 2022. Abdel Fattah el‑Sisi was president throughout. Egypt’s constitutional and administrative structure gave him substantial authority over national security policy: the president appointed the government, was supreme commander, could ratify or alter nonappealable Emergency State Security Court judgments, and possessed pardon powers. The Interior Ministry controlled the national police, Central Security Forces, and National Security Agency; the armed forces conducted North Sinai operations. The U.S. State Department assessed that civilian authorities maintained effective control over these forces. Responsibility for national policy and presidential acts is therefore relatively strong, though individual incidents cannot automatically be treated as personally ordered by Sisi. [Egyptian Constitution](https://wipolex-res.wipo.int/edocs/lexdocs/laws/en/eg/eg060en.html); [USDOS human-rights report, executive summary](https://www.ecoi.net/de/dokument/2089216.html)

The inherited baseline was already severely repressive: mass political detention, entrenched emergency and terrorism machinery, weak accountability for torture and disappearance, and an insurgency concentrated in North Sinai. Ending the nationwide state of emergency in October 2021 was favorable, but 2021 legislation retained comparable curfew, evacuation, military-assistance, and public-order powers. Emergency courts continued deciding cases initiated before expiration, with judgments not subject to ordinary appeal. [USDOS, lines 38–45 and 182–185](https://www.ecoi.net/de/dokument/2089216.html)

Non-state danger remained real. USDOS recorded at least 95 Sinai terrorist incidents and roughly 260 casualties, while judging terrorist activity lower than in earlier years. These totals were expressly uncertain because access and reporting in Sinai were restricted. The decline is material favorable evidence, but counterterrorism remained intertwined with army-backed militia abuses, extrajudicial executions, displacement, and political misuse of terrorism law. [Country Reports on Terrorism 2022](https://www.ecoi.net/en/document/2101556.html)

The principal favorable evidence is: the lower level of terrorist activity; releases of approximately 850–1,000 detainees after Sisi reactivated the Presidential Pardon Committee; closure of older prisons and transfers to newer facilities; several convictions of police personnel for earlier custodial deaths; concrete FGM prosecutions and declining reported prevalence among younger cohorts; and some minority-protection or inclusion measures.

The adverse balance is substantially broader and more directly connected to physical safety: continuing enforced disappearance and systemic torture; dangerous prison conditions and reported deaths through abuse or medical neglect; indefinite or recycled pretrial detention; presidential confirmation of nonappealable political sentences; reported presidential pardons for 13 police officers convicted in torture-related custodial deaths; retaliation against complainants and torture-video detainees; suppression surrounding COP27 protest calls; army and army-backed militia executions in Sinai; refoulement and abuse of refugees; and targeted abuse of LGBT people and Coptic protesters. The evidence supports patterns and tolerated impunity, but does not establish that Sisi personally ordered every abuse.

No adequately opened source established personal incitement by Sisi in 2022. That is a genuine gap and should not be converted into either favorable or adverse evidence.

Exact 2022 PTS, CIRIGHTS, V-Dem, and UCDP one-sided-violence observations could not be extracted from an underlying dataset in this session. Dataset landing pages and the supplied local-prior summary therefore remain contextual leads, not machine evidence.

## Ten-question disposition

1. **3B.1 — Predominantly adverse evidence.** WEB-3B-003 through 006 establish recurring disappearance, torture, arbitrary political imprisonment, custodial deaths, and weak remedies. WEB-3B-007 records meaningful but limited releases.

2. **3B.2 — Adverse institutional pattern with isolated accountability.** Police, NSA, prison, army, and army-aligned militia abuse appears in WEB-3B-003, 004, 005, 006, and 010. Three finalized police convictions are contrary evidence, but the presidential pardons and general failure to investigate weigh against treating them as a systemic remedy.

3. **3B.3 — Insufficient evidence.** No opened source established that Sisi personally incited hatred, revenge, dehumanization, or violence in 2022. Security rhetoric around terrorism existed, but the inspected material did not meet the requested personal-incitement threshold.

4. **3B.4 — Formal mechanisms, weak independence and effectiveness.** Prosecutors, courts, the NCHR, prison inspections, the pardon committee, and a human-rights strategy existed. WEB-3B-004 to 007 show retaliation, cursory investigations, delayed or unperformed inspections, nonappealable courts, and only limited releases. Some police convictions are genuine contrary evidence.

5. **3B.5 — Adverse use of security powers.** WEB-3B-002 and 008 show that ending the emergency did not end emergency-like powers; terrorism accusations, surveillance, disappearance, and preventive detention were used against peaceful critics and anticipated protesters.

6. **3B.6 — Mixed outcomes, adverse means.** Terrorist activity decreased (WEB-3B-009), but state repression, prison fear, disappearance, and militia killings persisted (WEB-3B-003, 008, 010). Reduced insurgent violence therefore did not translate cleanly into freedom from political fear.

7. **3B.7 — Mixed but substantially deficient protection.** WEB-3B-011 documents abuse of refugees and LGBT people; WEB-3B-012 documents both FGM enforcement and major domestic-violence gaps; WEB-3B-013 shows selective protection and prosecution alongside discriminatory detention and uneven Coptic access to justice.

8. **3B.8 — Strong adverse evidence.** WEB-3B-008 documents nearly 700 detentions associated with protest calls across 18 governorates, approximately 40 people missing from prosecutorial or public view, terrorism charges, surveillance, and lockdown measures. WEB-3B-013 supplies a minority-specific peaceful-protest case.

9. **3B.9 — Adverse crisis response, with counterterrorism qualification.** The COP27 response prioritized preventive repression rather than peaceful assembly (WEB-3B-008). Sinai operations degraded ISIS but included aligned-force executions without reported investigation (WEB-3B-009–010).

10. **3B.10 — No defensible finding of overall safety improvement from this one-year window.** Non-state terrorism declined, and several protective measures were real. Yet widespread state abuse, impunity, political detention, and fear remained. Exact inherited-to-end PTS/CIRIGHTS/V-Dem/UCDP series and independently confirmed casualty denominators remain missing, preventing a stronger aggregate conclusion.
