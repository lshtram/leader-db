**Search and Rejection Summary**
I reviewed roughly 30 distinct documents across official Chinese policy and statistical releases, IMF and World Bank macro surveillance, UNICEF data, and human-rights monitoring.

I retained the smallest non-duplicative set that covered:
- welfare intent and baseline social provision,
- medical and pension/social-security coverage,
- education access and equalization,
- crisis correction under Covid,
- household and labor-market outcomes,
- and contrary evidence on avoidable suffering from zero-Covid.

I rejected or de-prioritized:
- duplicate mirrors and syndications of the same policy story,
- broad China-economy commentary that repeated the IMF or World Bank without new 2022 ruler-period detail,
- 2023-2025 follow-ups that were useful only as retrospectives,
- sources that were too generic, too much like party rhetoric, or too far from the 2022 target period,
- and dataset re-fetches already represented by the local priors.

**Remaining Gaps**
- Direct Xi-personal attribution is still limited for most social policy. Much of the concrete action is State Council, ministry, or party-state system evidence rather than a signed Xi order.
- Education and health access are better evidenced than housing quality, disability services, and early-childhood outcomes.
- I have decent evidence on national averages, but weaker evidence on subnational inequality, quality of service, and the lived experience of migrants, disabled people, and minority regions in 2022.
- The social-protection picture is strong on coverage, weaker on adequacy and benefit value.
- If you want the dossier tightened further, the next useful search would be on disability services, migrant welfare access, and independent evaluation of local delivery in health and education.
