**What Xi’s office looked like in 2022**
- Xi held the core CCP/state posts: General Secretary of the CPC, Chairman of the CPC Central Military Commission, President of the PRC, and Chairman of the PRC Central Military Commission.
- The party constitution gives the General Secretary power to convene the Politburo and its Standing Committee and to preside over the Secretariat.
- The CMC chair has overall responsibility for the armed forces.
- In October 2022, the 20th Party Congress and its first plenary session renewed Xi as General Secretary and CMC chair, with a new Standing Committee packed with loyalists.

**What constrained him in 2022**
- The main constraints were practical and political, not formal checks.
- The zero-COVID system generated rare street and campus protests in late November.
- The government then moved quickly to soften quarantine/testing rules in early December.
- Economic headwinds were already present by mid-2022: mobility restrictions, weak normalization, and slowing growth.
- Rights criticism, especially around Xinjiang, Hong Kong, censorship, and protest suppression, remained intense.

**Best source families**
- Official Chinese party/state texts for office, authority, and policy position.
- Reuters for independent event chronology and external framing.
- SIPRI for security and military context.
- World Bank and IMF for macro conditions.
- Amnesty Chinese for critical local-language human-rights narrative.

**Compact event map**
- October 2022: 20th Party Congress, Xi secures a third term, and the new top leadership is stacked with loyalists.
- Late October 2022: the congress report and party constitution formalize the governing frame and the center’s authority.
- November 2022: rare protests erupt over COVID controls after the Urumqi fire.
- December 7-8, 2022: Beijing softens zero-COVID controls with 10 new measures.
- Mid-2022 through year-end: macro conditions remain weak, with slowed growth and policy strain.
- All year: repression and censorship remain central, with Xinjiang and Hong Kong still major external-pressure points.
