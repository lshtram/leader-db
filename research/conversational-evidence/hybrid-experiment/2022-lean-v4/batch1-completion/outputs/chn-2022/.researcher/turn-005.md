Search and rejection summary
- I considered roughly 30 documents across Reuters syndications, official CPC/State Council texts, Amnesty, HRW, OHCHR, CPJ, and RSF, plus a few duplicated wire copies and multilingual reposts.
- I retained the smallest set that gave me the 2022 power-consolidation record, the protest/crackdown record, the Xinjiang/minority-rights record, the Hong Kong media/surveillance record, and one favorable policy-relaxation counterexample.
- I rejected duplicate syndications of the same Reuters story across Euronews, Investing, Geo, TBS, ThePrint, and other mirrors when the underlying claim was identical.
- I rejected out-of-period or low-yield material, including 2026 follow-ups and older background pieces that did not add a materially different 2022 claim.
- I did not rely on the local structured datasets again for Freedom House, V-Dem, WGI, Polity, or similar baseline indices, because the local prior already supplied the country-year context.
- I rejected broad opinion or analysis pieces that only repeated the same facts without adding a distinct claim or a clearer attribution chain.

Remaining gaps
- There is still no direct source proving Xi personally ordered each protest arrest, the Hu Jintao escort, or the exact mechanics of every censorship action. Attribution is strongest to the Xi-led party-state apparatus, not to a single signed order.
- The record on 4B.4 remains mostly negative rather than affirmative: I found no strong 2022 evidence that Xi strengthened independent courts, legislatures, audit bodies, or other oversight institutions.
- The national-election question is structurally simple in China because meaningful contestability was already absent; the best evidence here is the 2022 congress and party-constitution language, not an election event.
- More searching could still help on formal appointment records, internal party discipline documents, or Hong Kong court/legislative records, but it is unlikely to change the broad 2022 picture: further centralization, sharper censorship, and only a narrow late-year policy retreat on COVID controls.
