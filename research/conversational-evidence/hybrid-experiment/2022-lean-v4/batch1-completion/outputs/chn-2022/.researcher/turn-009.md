**Search and Rejection Summary**
- Considered roughly 30 documents across official CPC/gov.cn, PBC, NBS, Reuters syndications, Bloomberg graphics, World Bank, IMF, and other China-policy coverage.
- Retained a compact, non-duplicative set focused on explicit program articulation, drafting ownership, implementation packages, monetary easing, property rescue, zero-COVID reversal, and year-end outcome data.
- Rejected duplicate syndications of the same Reuters stories, broad explainers that added no new 2022 claim, and later 2023/2024 follow-ups when they only repeated the 2022 pattern without adding period-specific evidence.
- One IMF page opened poorly in the browser, so the search result snippet was used for the claim rather than relying on a partial open.
- I did not use purely contextual authoritarian/corruption material unless it also showed a direct nexus to program execution, coordination, or adaptation.

**Remaining Gaps**
- Direct evidence of Xi personally managing day-to-day implementation is still limited; much of the execution record runs through the State Council, PBC, NBS, or party machinery.
- Provincial and subnational variation is under-evidenced here, especially on which localities executed the program well or poorly.
- The strongest negative evidence is outcome-based: growth and consumption missed the 2022 target, even though the state did mobilize policy responses.
- More independent evidence on private-sector confidence, productivity, and local compliance would improve attribution, but it is unlikely to change the broad picture of clear planning, real mobilization, mixed execution, and partial end-of-year progress.
