**Search and Rejection Summary**
- I considered roughly 30 documents across OHCHR, Amnesty, Human Rights Watch, Reuters, and Chinese official statements.
- I kept the smallest set that covered the main 3B themes: Xinjiang detention and oversight failure, protest policing, zero-COVID coercion, Hong Kong repression, and the government’s own denials and partial rollback.
- I rejected duplicate Reuters syndications where the same story appeared in multiple wrappers.
- I rejected 2023-2026 retrospective pieces when they added no new 2022 factual content beyond earlier sources.
- I rejected sources focused mainly on economics, foreign policy, or nuclear/security doctrine because they belong in other chapters.
- I rejected broad opinion columns and low-specificity summaries when a primary or stronger rights-monitoring source was available.
- I did not re-fetch structured local priors such as Freedom House or V-Dem because the local facts already supplied the relevant baseline and the instructions forbid re-fetching those datasets for this scope.

**Remaining Gaps**
- No public 2022 source shows Xi personally ordering specific arrests, torture, or detention decisions; attribution is therefore mostly system-level and structural.
- The record is strongest for Xinjiang and the protest crackdown. It is weaker on due process, complaint handling, and independent remedy because I found no credible 2022 evidence of effective oversight or sanctions against abusers.
- The protest-crackdown evidence is strong on harassment and surveillance, but the full scale of detentions and case outcomes remains underreported.
- More search would only help if you want direct command-chain evidence or province-by-province implementation data. It is unlikely to change the overall severity class.
