**Search and Rejection Summary**
I reviewed roughly 30 documents across official German government pages, Bundestag records, BMWK/Bundeswehr/Bundesnetzagentur releases, OECD, Destatis, Bundesagentur für Arbeit, and a few Reuters/reporting cross-checks.

Kept evidence centered on four linked 2022 programs:
- defense/Zeitenwende and Bundeswehr procurement,
- energy-security fast tracking and LNG,
- inflation and household/firm relief,
- labor-market and macro resilience under the energy shock.

Rejected or downgraded material included:
- duplicate syndications and translated copies of the same government page,
- generic speeches that repeated the same claims without adding new implementation detail,
- opinion pieces and commentary that did not add independent finding,
- later retrospectives that restated the same 2022 outcomes without new facts,
- country-level indicators and capacity metrics that did not show ruler-specific execution,
- items that were too close to 3B/5B/6B without an actual 8B program-to-implementation link.

**Remaining Gaps**
- Direct personnel/appointment evidence is thin. I have much better evidence for plans, budgets, procurement, and coordination than for who Scholz empowered to execute each piece.
- The Bundeswehr special fund still looks lagged in 2022. More granular procurement schedules and ministry execution reports would help distinguish “correct direction” from actual capability gains inside the target year.
- Some outcome gains are partly inherited or shock-driven, especially the labour market and GDP rebound. Better disaggregation by federal policy versus post-pandemic rebound and weather effects would sharpen attribution.
- More search would most help on 8B.4 and 8B.5, specifically on staffing, delegation, and whether the Chancellery imposed tighter implementation discipline across ministries.
