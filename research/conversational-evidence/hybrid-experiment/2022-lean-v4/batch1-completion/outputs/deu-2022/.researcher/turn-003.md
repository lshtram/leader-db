**Search And Rejection Summary**
I considered roughly 30 documents and kept 8 reused items plus 8 new source-claim units as the core chapter set, staying focused on 2022 conduct rather than broad Ukraine background.

Considered but not retained as primary chapter evidence:
- Duplicate Reuters syndications of the same Scholz quotes across Euronews, Investing.com, Japan Times, and other mirrors.
- Out-of-period retrospectives and 2023-2026 summary pages that were useful for orientation but not direct 2022 evidence.
- General NATO nuclear-sharing background pages that were better suited to Chapter 1B than Chapter 2B.
- The 18 February German-French de-escalation statement on Donbas and Ukraine's borders: useful context, but I excluded it from the retained set because the direct actors were the foreign ministers rather than Scholz himself.
- The 2 March, 12 October, and 14 November UN press releases on Ukraine: relevant context, but I kept the more Scholz-linked and German-government-linked legal/diplomatic items instead.
- Reuters-syndicated reports on heavy-weapons hesitation and later peace messaging that repeated the same underlying episode without adding materially new facts.
- One attempted Reuters open produced an internal error, so I did not rely on it.

Broadly, the retained set covers:
- authority and constraints
- invasion-day condemnation and solidarity
- defensive support for Ukraine
- direct ceasefire diplomacy with Putin
- refusal of dictated peace
- legal framing through ICJ and UN channels
- later-year self-defense/end-use clarification
- one critical counterpoint on caution and delay

**Remaining Gaps**
- I still do not have private cabinet minutes, internal NATO notes, or direct evidence of Scholz's personal role in every alliance decision.
- The record on the heavy-weapons debate is clear enough to show hesitation and later movement, but not enough to quantify how much of the delay was Scholz personally versus coalition, military-stock, or alliance coordination constraints.
- I did not find a clean, ruler-specific source showing a formal 2022 peace initiative led by Scholz beyond calls for ceasefire, sanctions pressure, and legal multilateralism.
- Germany was not a belligerent, so 2B.4 and 2B.5 are necessarily about support policy, end-use framing, and legal restraint rather than battlefield conduct; that limits how far the evidence can go.
- If you want more depth, the best next search would be German-language coverage of the 2022 heavy-weapons cabinet disputes and any archival Bundestag/Chancellery material on Scholz's direct Ukraine calls.
