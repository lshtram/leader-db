**Search and Rejection Summary**
- I screened roughly 30 documents across official presidential releases, budget and finance pages, BSP, PSA, LawPhil, IMF, World Bank, Reuters/MarketScreener, PIA, PNA, BusinessWorld, GMA, and a few later follow-ups.
- I kept the best non-duplicative evidence on program articulation, budget translation, appointments, implementation steps, macro outcomes, and macro constraints.
- I rejected or downgraded several items because they duplicated the same SONA/budget story without adding a new claim, were broader 2023-2024 follow-ups with no extra 2022 execution signal, or were too general and not attributable to Marcos specifically.
- I also excluded several pre-office or inherited baseline items unless they directly helped explain the starting conditions the 2022 administration inherited.
- I did not treat later integrity or rights material as competence evidence unless it directly illuminated program execution, staffing, or administrative follow-through.

**Remaining Gaps**
- Direct evidence of agency-level implementation in 2022 is still thinner than evidence of agenda-setting and budgeting.
- The strongest open question is whether the cabinet and economic team actually coordinated better across departments, beyond being experienced.
- More material would help on whether the FY2023 budget, investment reforms, and food-security measures produced measurable 2022-end or early-2023 changes in investment, prices, and household welfare.
- Regional and sectoral unevenness is only partially visible here; better subnational evidence would sharpen the execution picture.
- More search would likely help, especially on audits, rollout reports, and 2023 implementation documents that explicitly trace 2022 decisions to later outcomes.
