**Search and Rejection Summary**
I considered roughly 30 documents across DOH, DepEd, DSWD, DHSUD, DA, PNA, UNICEF, and World Bank/IMF-linked material. I retained 14 distinct URLs above.

- Duplicate removals: I dropped repeated school-reopening and learning-recovery stories that restated the same DepEd or UNICEF message without adding a new mechanism or target group.
- Out-of-period removals: I excluded 2023-2024 follow-ups unless they explicitly described a 2022 act or 2022 implementation step.
- Attribution removals: I rejected several 2022 aid items that were clearly Duterte-era baseline or pre-Marcos programs and did not cleanly support Marcos-period attribution.
- Weak/too-generic removals: I skipped broad landing pages and general development summaries that did not specify a ruler-period action, implementation change, or vulnerable-group effect.
- Blocked/fragile removals: A few official pages were slow or unstable, so I relied on accessible official mirrors, press releases, and search-result excerpts only when they carried a distinct 2022 claim.

**Remaining Gaps**
- Direct outcome evidence is still thin. I found clear 2022 policy moves on health, education, crisis aid, and food security, but less hard evidence on measurable improvement in school outcomes, malnutrition, poverty, or service quality by end-2022.
- Attribution is often administration-level rather than personal. DepEd, DSWD, and DOH actions were led by agency heads under Marcos, so the dossier should treat Marcos as the period ruler rather than the sole operational actor.
- Distribution evidence is better on vulnerable groups and crisis access than on regional or class-level household effects. More PSA/household-survey breakdowns would help.
- Several items are reform or recovery plans rather than completed delivery. Additional budget-execution, audit, or program-admin data would improve confidence on implementation versus announcement.
