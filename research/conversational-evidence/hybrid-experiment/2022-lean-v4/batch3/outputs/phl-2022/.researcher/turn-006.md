**Search and rejection summary**
I considered roughly 30 documents across Reuters-syndicated reporting, IMF, World Bank, BSP, DOF, DA, NEDA/PIA, PNA, PSA, and BusinessWorld. I retained 16 distinct URLs above and rejected the rest for one of four reasons.

- Duplicate or near-duplicate removals: multiple articles repeated the same 2022 economic team appointments, the same 8-point socioeconomic agenda, the same food-security directive, or the same inflation response. I kept one or two sources per distinct policy move rather than padding the record.
- Weak or promotional removals: several official pages restated the administration's own intentions without adding new facts, so I kept only the strongest official item per policy family and paired it with independent multilateral or Reuters corroboration where possible.
- Blocked or awkwardly accessible removals: some PCO/SONA pages and IMF eLibrary pages were partially blocked or difficult to open cleanly, so I relied on accessible mirrors, search-result excerpts, or alternate official pages carrying the same claim.
- Out-of-period or later follow-ups removed: 2023-2024 budget, PPP, tax, and food-price follow-ups were excluded unless they explicitly described a 2022 act or 2022 policy direction. I did not use later material to expand the 2022 period silently.

**Remaining gaps**
- Direct anti-corruption and capture evidence is still thin. The record shows one early simplification move that cut an anti-corruption body and one criticism about the Marcos wealth-recovery commission, but I did not find strong 2022 evidence of effective anti-corruption enforcement or capture reversal.
- Distributional evidence is better on poverty and inequality than on regions, classes, or gender-specific outcomes. More 2022 FIES/PSA subnational breakdowns or labor-income microdata would improve lens 5B.8.
- The macro record is strong on plans and independent assessments, but only partly on Marcos-specific implementation. More budget-execution, procurement, and program-delivery sources would help separate inherited recovery from ruler-attributed change.
- The evidence on competence of the economic team is strong on resumes and appointments, but weaker on whether Marcos actually empowered those professionals versus merely naming them. More cabinet minutes, delegation records, and implementation follow-through would help.
