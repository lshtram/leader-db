Rejected or downweighted:

- 2020-2021 background reports unless they were needed for inherited context, since the chapter is 2022.
- 2023-2025 pages on corridor evictions and later state-of-emergency crises because they were out of period.
- Advocacy pages that repeated the same claim without adding new chronology, attribution, or evidence type.
- Purely general press-freedom or human-rights landing pages that did not add a specific 2022 claim.

**Remaining gaps**
- Direct proof of Abiy’s personal ordering or awareness for some regional security-force abuses is still limited; much attribution remains governmental or command-level rather than personally signed.
- The balance of responsibility between federal, regional, and allied forces remains contested in some violence outside Tigray, especially Oromia and post-truce incidents.
- I did not find a single definitive domestic accountability mechanism in 2022 that credibly remedied the abuses; the record points more to partial release/rollback than durable oversight.
- More local-language court, commission, or parliamentary material could sharpen attribution, but it is unlikely to change the overall 2022 pattern: partial early restraint, followed by recurring, multi-region repression and serious civilian-protection failures.

Severity-wise, the chapter evidence lands in recurring to widespread repression with systematic features in the Tigray detention/access domain, but not a mass-terror floor.
