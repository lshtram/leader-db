**Search and Rejection Summary**
I reviewed roughly 30 documents across IMF, World Bank, NBE, UNICEF, Reuters/Euronews, Ethiopian government and local-media sources. I retained 12 URLs that were non-duplicative and materially useful; the rest were dropped because they were out of period, too generic, duplicative, blocked, or too weakly attributable to Abiy.

I rejected or deprioritized older baseline poverty studies from 2011/2016 and 2019/2020 because they were useful background but not target-year evidence. I also screened out 2026 World Bank/AfDB pages and other post-2024 explainers unless they directly described reforms that began in 2022 or 2022/23 outcomes; most of those were too retrospective to carry the chapter. Generic procurement/governance methodology notes and global corruption papers were also excluded because they were not Ethiopia-specific enough. Some IMF eLibrary full texts returned 403 in-browser, so I used the accessible abstract/search snippets for the 2024 IMF country-report claims rather than inventing details.

The main retained pattern is mixed: Abiy's government pushed market-opening and digital-sector reform, created or centralized SOE oversight, and kept social-protection programs in play, but 2022 was also marked by severe inflation, FX shortages, debt stress, conflict spillovers, and weak household conditions. A few useful but not retained items were redundant with the kept sources, especially repeated Reuters coverage and broad World Bank country pages that overlapped the same facts.

**Remaining Gaps**
- Direct contemporaneous evidence on Abiy Ahmed's personal role in appointing, empowering, or constraining economic professionals in 2022 is still thin.
- Better 2022 data on household distribution by region, class, and conflict status would sharpen 5B.8.
- Direct audit or procurement evidence on corruption, favoritism, and SOE discipline in 2022 would improve attribution for 5B.5.
