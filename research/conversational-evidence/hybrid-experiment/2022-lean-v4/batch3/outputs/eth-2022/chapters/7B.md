**Search and Rejection Summary**
I screened roughly 30 distinct documents across Reuters/Euronews, CPJ, Freedom House, HRW, VOA, ENA, The Reporter, the New Yorker, Al Jazeera/Reuters, GOV.UK, Africa Intelligence, and a few broader context items. I kept only items with a direct ruler-nexus or a clear, attributable institutional action.

Rejected or not-accepted items included:
- Generic corruption indicators and governance priors such as WGI, V-Dem, CPI, and other country-level measures, because they are context only and not personal-conduct evidence.
- Purely economic or humanitarian materials from IMF, World Bank, UNICEF, WFP, and conflict reports unless they directly tied to Abiy’s own statements, appointments, concealment, or oversight choices.
- Several conflict-and-security pieces that were strong for 3B/4B but too indirect for 7B unless they also showed media suppression, obstruction of scrutiny, or a ruler-level accountability choice.
- 2026 retrospective profile fragments and background election/video materials that were either out of period or too inferential for 2022 integrity.
- Asset-declaration and legal-framework pages that were useful context but did not show Abiy’s own filing, concealment, or personal benefit.
- Opinion pieces and commentary that alleged bad faith without a traceable factual core or independent corroboration.

**Remaining Gaps**
- Direct, audited evidence on Abiy’s personal assets, family wealth, gifts, campaign finance, or hidden business interests is still thin.
- I did not find a strong 2022 source proving direct self-enrichment, kickbacks, or a family conflict of interest.
- Evidence is strongest on media suppression, punishment of journalists, and mixed anti-corruption rhetoric, weaker on personal financial honesty.
- More search would help if you want a tighter 7B judgment on personal enrichment or a stronger read on whether the anti-corruption campaign was genuinely even-handed versus selectively enforced.
