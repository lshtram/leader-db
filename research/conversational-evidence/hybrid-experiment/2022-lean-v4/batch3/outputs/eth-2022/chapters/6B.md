**Search and rejection summary**
I reviewed roughly 30 documents across UNICEF, WHO, WFP, World Bank, FAO, ILO, UNESCO, and related Ethiopia pages. I kept 13 distinct URLs as the strongest non-duplicative evidence and rejected the rest as redundant, too generic, out-of-period, or blocked.

I removed several broad global education and learning-crisis reports because they were useful context but not Ethiopia-specific enough for ruler attribution. I also screened out 2024-2026 retrospective pages unless they directly described a 2022 program or 2022/23 condition; most were too retrospective for the target year. A few potentially useful items were blocked or not fully accessible in-browser, including some IMF and ILO pages, so I did not rely on them where the accessible text was too thin. I also avoided repeating the same drought, nutrition, and education facts through multiple publishers when one strong source already established the claim.

The retained picture is mixed. There is real evidence of welfare-oriented crisis response and program design in 2022: nutrition, drought relief, emergency health teams, school restoration, and safety-net expansion. But the same year also shows very severe humanitarian need, widespread drought and conflict disruption, major education loss, refugee ration cuts, and heavy dependence on donors and partners.

**Remaining gaps**
- Direct evidence that Abiy Ahmed personally drove or supervised these social-welfare actions is still limited; most evidence is government, ministry, or partner implementation rather than his own decision record.
- Better target-year household survey data disaggregated by region, gender, displacement status, and poverty would sharpen distribution and dignity judgments.
- Stronger audit-style evidence on whether welfare and social protection were allocated fairly, or used selectively, would help.
- More direct budget-execution or expenditure-tracking evidence for health and education in 2022 would improve attribution and durability assessment.
