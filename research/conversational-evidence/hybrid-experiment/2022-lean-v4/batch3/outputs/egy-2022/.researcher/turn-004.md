**Search and rejection summary**
I considered roughly 30 documents across the 2022-2023 State Department report, HRW World Report 2023, Amnesty’s Arabic and English COP27 statements, Reuters/Euronews coverage of COP27 protest constraints, presidential statements on the human-rights strategy and pardon committee, Ahram Online’s release reporting, and a smaller set of UN and NGO references.

I rejected or de-emphasized:
- Duplicate COP27 pieces that repeated the same protest-arrest story without adding a materially distinct claim.
- Out-of-period material from 2023-2024 that was useful only as later confirmation.
- Broad economic or climate pages that did not establish a physical-safety nexus for this chapter.
- Purely generic human-rights rhetoric that did not add actual 2022 conduct.
- Sources blocked by technical errors when a mirror or a stronger alternate source existed.
- Older background pages when a 2022 or 2023 source gave the same point more directly.

**Remaining gaps**
- The public record still does not give a clean, independent chain of command for every abuse. The abuse pattern is clear; personal order is less clear.
- Exact counts for disappearances, torture incidents, and protest-related detentions vary by source and likely undercount actual incidence.
- The effectiveness of the pardon commission and human-rights strategy implementation remains uncertain. There were some releases and formal steps, but strong evidence of durable protection or independent remedy is absent.
- More search could refine the scale and timing of specific incidents, but it is unlikely to change the core chapter picture: 2022 Egypt under Sisi shows recurring severe repression, weak accountability, and only narrow, limited remedial action.
