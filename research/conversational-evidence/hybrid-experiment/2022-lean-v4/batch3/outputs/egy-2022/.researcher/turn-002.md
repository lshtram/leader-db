**Search and rejection summary**

I reviewed roughly 30 documents across Egyptian official pages, IAEA/UN treaty records, UN press coverage, Reuters syndication, World Nuclear News, Ahram Online, Egypt Today, Xinhua, CTBTO explainers, and regional disarmament pages.

I rejected or de-emphasized:
- Duplicate Dabaa coverage that repeated the same permit or first-concrete event without adding a materially distinct claim.
- Generic CTBTO FAQ/explainer pages when the UN treaty database already gave the cleaner treaty-status record.
- Broad 2023-2026 retrospectives that were useful for context but not necessary for a 2022 chapter dossier.
- Sources that were too generic, too off-topic, or not Egypt-specific enough to justify inclusion.
- One or two official Egyptian pages that were useful only as snippets because the page fetched with a 502 on open; I relied on the snippet only when it added a unique factual point.
- Old or historical disarmament records when a more direct 2022 source existed, except for the inherited-baseline Cairo Declaration, which is genuinely useful for attribution.

**Remaining gaps**

- Direct evidence of Sisi personally making any nuclear-safety, command-and-control, or crisis-escalation choice in 2022 is thin. Egypt had civilian nuclear governance, not a nuclear arsenal.
- I did not find strong 2022 evidence on whether Egypt had an Additional Protocol in force or on any strengthened safeguards beyond the basic NPT agreement.
- The public record is sparse on internal nuclear-security staffing, emergency planning, or cyber/physical protection decisions, so 1B.3 and 1B.7 remain only moderately evidenced.
- 1B.9 remains weak: I found general Egyptian references to cyber and outer-space risk in UN rhetoric, but not a ruler-specific, programmatic 2022 policy on dual-use catastrophic-risk governance.
- More search could add nuance on treaty diplomacy, but it is unlikely to change the core exposure classification: Egypt in 2022 was a limited-exposure civilian nuclear and nonproliferation case, not a weapon-command or near-use case.
