**Office, Authority, Constraints**
- Kishida was Japan’s prime minister throughout 2022. His official profile page shows him as the 101st PM, and as a House of Representatives member from Hiroshima 1st District.
- Constitutionally, executive power sits with the Cabinet, the PM is designated by the Diet, and the PM appoints and can dismiss ministers. In practice, that means Kishida had real agenda-setting power but remained constrained by Diet confidence, the lower-house majority rule, and coalition management.
- The key 2022 practical constraints were: the LDP-Komeito coalition, the shock from Abe’s assassination, the Unification Church backlash, inflation/yen weakness, COVID spillovers, and the December 2022 security reset after Russia’s invasion of Ukraine and rising regional tension.
