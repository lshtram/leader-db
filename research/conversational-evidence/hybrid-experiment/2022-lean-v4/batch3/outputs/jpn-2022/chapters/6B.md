**Search and Rejection Summary**
- I screened roughly 30 documents across PMO, MHLW, MEXT, Statistics Bureau, OECD, UNICEF, World Bank, WHO, and Reuters/republished Reuters.
- Kept the strongest non-duplicative set that gave both policy intent and at least some outcome/constraint evidence.
- Rejected as duplicates or near-duplicates: repeated Kishida speeches on new capitalism, children, women, and social security across late 2021, 2022, and 2023; the 2022 PMO speeches were enough.
- Rejected as weak or context-only when they did not add material 2022 attribution: generic pension, care, housing, and welfare landing pages; broad WHO donor pages; World Bank country overview pages; older child-benefit history pages; and high-level OECD/UNICEF global reports.
- Rejected as out-of-period or too future-facing: 2023-2025 implementation pages for child-policy and scholarship expansions, 2024-2025 labor and education updates, and later ceremony pages that were only retrospective.
- Rejected some sources because they were better used as background than chapter evidence: the more general social-security and care-system pages, which describe durable institutions but do not pin 2022 choices to Kishida as clearly as the selected sources do.
- No major access block affected the final set; I did not need to rely on blocked or paywalled text.

**Remaining Gaps**
- Direct 2022 poverty-rate and child-poverty-rate data were not easily extractable in a clean English-language source set, so distributional assessment leans on real-income and purchasing-power evidence instead.
- There is still limited direct evidence on whether the 2022 child, family, and inflation measures measurably improved outcomes within 2022 rather than later.
- Subgroup-specific evidence is thin for disabled people, migrants, and regional disparities, so 6B.3 and 6B.8 remain partly inferential.
- More search would help on budget execution, service uptake, childcare availability, and whether Kishida’s new-capitalism measures changed actual household life chances in 2022 rather than just setting the policy direction.
