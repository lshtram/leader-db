**Search and rejection summary**
I considered roughly 30 documents across official Brazilian legal texts and ministries, IBGE releases, UNICEF/UN and NGO reports, CGU audits, and a few secondary summaries. I retained the best non-duplicative evidence above and rejected or downgraded several classes of material: generic WHO country pages that duplicated the local priors without adding ruler-specific 2022 detail; broad OECD/UNICEF education or climate pages that were too indirect; INEP Censo Escolar portal pages that were procedural rather than outcome-bearing in the snippets I found; and later 2023-2026 MDS pages about Lula-era Bolsa Família reforms that were out of period for Bolsonaro 2022. I also avoided repeating the same social-transfer fact through multiple publishers unless the source added a materially different claim, such as the CGU audit’s control-failure finding or the UNICEF/IBGE inequality breakdowns.

**Remaining gaps**
The biggest remaining gap is direct attribution. Brazil’s health, education, water, and sanitation delivery is heavily shared with states and municipalities, so many outcome measures are strong evidence of the 2022 social condition but only partial evidence of Bolsonaro’s personal contribution. More search would help on federal budget execution, program audits, and service-delivery evaluations for health, school meals, and sanitation if a later score were required. Evidence on durable institution-building beyond CadÚnico modernization and the existing SUS/PNI architecture is also thin.
