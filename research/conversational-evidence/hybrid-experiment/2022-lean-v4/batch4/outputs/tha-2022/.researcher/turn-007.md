**Search and rejection summary**
I screened roughly 30 documents across UNICEF/WHO/World Bank/IMF/Thai ministry sources, plus Reuters mirrors and local-government press releases.

Accepted as evidence:
- UNICEF MICS 2022 press release and UNICEF pandemic-impact article.
- WHO migrant vaccination feature.
- World Bank Thailand Public Revenue and Spending Assessment page and its health, education, and social-protection chapters.
- World Bank rural poverty diagnostic.
- IMF June 2022 Article IV mission statement.
- Thai Ministry of Education school-quality policy page.

Rejected or not used:
- Duplicate landing pages for the same UNICEF MICS report and the same World Bank assessment were dropped in favor of the more specific press release or chapter PDFs.
- A few IMF eLibrary and ministry pages failed to fetch or returned 403/500-style errors, so they were not relied on.
- Several 2024-2026 Thai PRD and ministry items surfaced in search but were out of period for 2022 and were excluded.
- Generic global UNICEF/WHO reports and later follow-on stories were rejected when they added no Thailand-2022-specific ruler-period evidence.
- Historical background notes and technical notes from 2017-2021 were treated as background only unless a 2022-targeted claim was available from a stronger source.

**Remaining gaps**
- The biggest gap is direct 2022 ruler-specific attribution: there is still limited evidence that Prayut personally designed, ordered, or corrected the social-welfare and education measures rather than inheriting them through Thailand's existing systems.
- More search would help on 2022 Thai-language budget execution, welfare-card administration, and formal ministry reporting on social protection uptake, especially for poor regions, children, migrants, and older people.
- The full NSO/UNICEF MICS 2022 report PDF would likely sharpen province-level and group-level attribution beyond the press-release summary.
- Independent audits or implementation reviews of the 2022 education reforms and social protection targeting would help distinguish announced policy from actual delivery.
