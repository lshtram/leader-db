Anchoring point: in 2022 Recep Tayyip Erdoğan was Turkey’s president under the post-2017 executive presidency. Formally, that meant broad executive authority, but not unlimited power: presidential decrees were legally bounded, laws prevailed, courts could review decrees, and the legislature retained an early-election lever. **Retained sources**
1. [The Constitution of the Republic of Türkiye](https://www.anayasa.gov.tr/tr/mevzuat/anayasa/?v=1.0.22) - Anayasa Mahkemesi, constitutional text as amended through 21 Jan 2017.  
   - Precise fact: Article 101 sets the president’s term and election rules; Article 104 makes the president head of state and executive authority; Article 116 covers election renewal.  
   - Period fit: stable legal frame for all of 2022.  
   - Ruler attribution: this is the office Erdoğan occupied in 2022.  
   - Limitations: formal law, not de facto practice.  
   - Contrary point: it shows formal checks still existed, even if political power was heavily centralized.

2. [Decision concerning the General Principles for Constitutional Review of Presidential Decrees](https://www.anayasa.gov.tr/en/leading-judgments/decision-concerning-the-general-principles-for-constitutional-review-of-presidential-decrees-e201931-k20205/) - Anayasa Mahkemesi, 23 Jan 2020.  
   - Precise fact: presidential decrees are limited to executive matters and remain subject to constitutional review.  
   - Period fit: directly relevant to Erdoğan’s 2022 governing tools.  
   - Ruler attribution: constrains the office, not just the abstract presidency.  
   - Limitations: one court decision, not the whole constitutional practice.  
   - Contrary point: it undercuts any claim that Erdoğan had unconstrained decree power.

3. [NATO’s nuclear deterrence policy and forces](https://www.nato.int/en/what-we-do/deterrence-and-defence/natos-nuclear-deterrence-policy-and-forces) - NATO, current topic page.

   - Period fit: stable background for 1B, even though the page is current rather than 2022-dated.  
   - Ruler attribution: alliance-level context for Turkey’s host-state role, not a personal conduct source.  
   - Limitations: general NATO policy, not Turkey-specific force data.  
   - Contrary point: it does not prove any particular weapons deployment in Turkey.

5. [Turkey Economic Monitor: Sailing Against the Tide](https://www.worldbank.org/en/country/turkey/publication/economic-monitor) - World Bank, 25 Feb 2022.

   - Ruler attribution: helps explain the economic room Erdoğan had, without pretending to assign sole causality.

6. [Republic of Türkiye: 2022 Article IV Consultation - Press Release; and Staff Report](https://www.imf.org/en/publications/cr/issues/2023/08/17/republic-of-trkiye-2022-article-iv-consultation-press-release-and-staff-report-538023) - IMF, 18 Jan 2023.

   - Ruler attribution: directly relevant to Erdoğan’s economic choices and constraints.  
   - Limitations: IMF framing is macro-stability centric.  
   - Contrary point: it does not by itself prove political causation for every outcome.

7. [2022 Report on Türkiye](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/legal-content/EN/TXT/?uri=CELEX%3A52022SC0333) - European Commission, 12 Oct 2022.
