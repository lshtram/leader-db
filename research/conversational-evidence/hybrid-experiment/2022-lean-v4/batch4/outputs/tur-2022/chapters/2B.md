- I reviewed roughly 25 to 40 documents across UN, NATO, Reuters, Turkish official communications, Ukraine official channels, OHCHR, and HRW.
- Kept sources that added something new on one of four things: Erdoğan’s actual diplomatic choices, his role in peace-brokering, civilian-risk criticism of Turkish operations, or formal authority/context.
- Rejected or de-emphasized generic NATO background pages, duplicate Reuters syndications of the same story, broad Turkey background pages, and older war-crime reports that were useful only as pre-2022 context.
- Existing 2B-relevant evidence from the previous thread was reused rather than recopied, especially the Turkish cross-border operation coverage and the NATO memorandum material.

**Remaining gaps**
- I still do not have a direct 2022 source showing Erdoğan personally weighed and rejected a specific peaceful alternative for the Syria/Iraq operation, beyond the official anti-terror justification and the contrary rights reporting.
- The record is stronger on mediation than on peace settlement. The grain corridor and prisoner exchanges are real achievements, but they are not the same as ending a major conflict.
- More local-language archival reporting could sharpen 2B.3 and 2B.6, especially on the rationale for the November 2022 operation and whether civilian-risk warnings were internalized.
- More independent investigation of the Iraq/Syria strikes would help 2B.4 and 2B.9, especially on command responsibility and civilian casualty verification.
