**Search and rejection summary**
I screened roughly 30 documents across Reuters syndications, OHCHR press releases and special-procedure communications, UN Human Rights Council material, the UN fact-finding mission report, Amnesty International reports in English and Persian, Human Rights Watch English and Persian country coverage, NetBlocks telemetry, Access Now shutdown reporting, and a few secondary explainers on the morality police.

I rejected or deprioritized:
- Duplicate Reuters syndications that repeated the same speech or incident without adding new facts.
- Purely derivative explainers that added no better claim or locator than the primary/near-primary source already retained.
- Out-of-period retrospectives unless they materially summarized the 2022 record or directly described late-2022 consequences.
- Lower-quality advocacy pages that repeated already-retained facts without a distinct material claim.
- Sources about international or nuclear conduct that belonged in Chapters 1B/2B rather than 3B.
- A few blocked or weakly accessible items where the accessible snippet was too thin to support a clean source-claim unit.

**Remaining gaps**
- Direct official Persian transcripts from Khamenei’s office on the protest crackdown would improve ruler attribution, especially for 3B.3 and 3B.8.
- Better chain-of-command evidence linking specific lethal acts, detentions, and internet controls to Khamenei personally, rather than to the state apparatus generally, would sharpen attribution.
- The school-poisoning issue would benefit from a stronger 2022 primary or forensic source on perpetrators and command responsibility.
- The claim that the morality police were “shut down” remains ambiguous and would benefit from a direct authoritative confirmation or clear retraction.
- More granular victim-count data for individual provinces would help if you want a tighter severity map, but it is not necessary to establish the overall pattern of severe, recurring repression.
