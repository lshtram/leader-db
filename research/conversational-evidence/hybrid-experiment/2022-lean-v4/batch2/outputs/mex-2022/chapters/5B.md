**Search and rejection summary**
I reviewed roughly 30 documents across IMF, OECD, CONEVAL, INEGI, Banxico, STPS, SHCP, AMLO/gob.mx, Reuters, Reuters-syndicated coverage, and a few local outlets. I kept 12 distinct URLs and merged closely related claims where they came from the same document and same policy episode.

I rejected or downweighted:
- Out-of-period material from 2023 to 2025 that did not add 2022-specific ruler evidence, except when used as retrospective analysis of the 2018-2022 period.
- Duplicate Reuters syndications of the same underlying story.
- Purely descriptive pages that restated baseline macro context without adding ruler-attributed policy detail.
- Several gob.mx pages that were blocked on direct open, although their search snippets were still usable when they contained concrete 2022 policy details.
- Generic commentary on remittances, inflation, or nearshoring that did not add a distinct claim beyond a stronger document already retained.

**Remaining gaps**
- The weakest area is 5B.2. I have good evidence on AMLO’s policy direction, but less direct evidence about whether he appointed and empowered genuinely independent economic professionals versus loyalists in the full 2022 team.
- I have strong evidence on minimum wage, welfare transfers, inflation response, and some investment policy, but less on firm-level competition, entrepreneurship, property-rights predictability, and regulatory quality beyond the electricity and PEMEX cases.
- The poverty and labor-income improvements are real, but causation is mixed. More search would help separate AMLO’s wage and transfer policy from U.S. labor demand, remittances, oil prices, and pandemic rebound.
- I still do not have a fully independent assessment of whether the 2022 growth trajectory was durable or mostly cyclical. Additional work on investment, productivity, and household consumption would tighten that judgment.
