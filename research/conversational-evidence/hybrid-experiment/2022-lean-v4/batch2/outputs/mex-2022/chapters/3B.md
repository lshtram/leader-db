**Search and rejection summary**
I considered roughly 30 documents across rights monitors, UN bodies, official Mexican sources, and wire/local reporting. I kept only items that added something materially distinct to the 2022 domestic-safety record. I rejected or downweighted:
- duplicate journalist-killing stories that repeated the same deaths without adding new attribution or remedy information,
- older 2020-2021 protest reports except where they were used only as background for a 2022 assessment,
- later 2023/2024 follow-ups when the 2022 summary already captured the relevant facts,
- general crime or macro-governance coverage that lacked a physical-safety nexus,
- state-level incidents that were too decoupled from federal authority to help with AMLO attribution,
- and several blocked gob.mx or UN document pages when the search snippet did not add a distinct material claim.

**Remaining gaps**
- The evidence is strong on recurring repression, violence against journalists, militarization, and weak remedies, but weaker on a single clean federal command chain for each abuse.
- I did not find a source that proves AMLO personally ordered a specific arrest, beating, or killing in 2022; most of the record is institutional, rhetorical, or policy-level.
- The best positive evidence is real but limited: the protection mechanism expanded, and AMLO sometimes endorsed protest rights and compliance with a human-rights recommendation. That does not neutralize the broader pattern.
- I did not find enough to say federal protection reliably improved on the ground for women protesters, journalists, indigenous defenders, or search groups in 2022.
