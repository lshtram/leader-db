**Search and rejection summary**
- I considered roughly 30 documents across HRW, Amnesty, the U.S. State Department, the UK FCDO annual report, UN mechanism references, Prothom Alo, The Daily Star, and Bangladesh Police portals.
- I rejected repeated or near-duplicate protest stories that restated the same 7-December or August-December crackdown without adding new facts.
- I rejected later 2023-2026 retrospectives on protests, disappearances, and the new regime because they do not improve 2022 attribution for this chapter.
- I rejected broad economic, climate, and international-security pieces unless they tied directly to domestic physical safety, repression, or vulnerable-group protection.
- I rejected several weak or blocked direct opens where the source page was unavailable or technical, unless a stable mirrored text or snippet already contained the needed claim.
- I rejected multiple generic Bangladesh Police pages that listed services without a clear complaint or remedy mechanism relevant to abuse accountability.
- I kept the strongest mix as: one official country report, three major rights-monitor summaries, two protest-crackdown reports, two Rohingya-protection reports, one disappearances update, one rights-abuser reward report, one UK annual report, and one official grievance mechanism page.

**Remaining gaps**
- The main gap is direct chain-of-command evidence for specific 2022 abuse orders from Sheikh Hasina herself. The record strongly shows state responsibility, but not always a personal order trail.
- Remedy and oversight remain weakly evidenced. We have formal grievance pathways, but little hard proof that they were independent or effective in 2022.
- The record on minority protection is mixed and more general than I would like; more targeted 2022 evidence on communal violence, Indigenous protection, or women’s safety could sharpen the attribution.
- More searching would help if the goal is to distinguish routine local policing from centrally directed repression, especially around the December opposition crackdown and Rohingya camp enforcement.
