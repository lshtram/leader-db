Using the local prior only for structured 2022 facts, and not the client matrix, the short read is:

- In 2022, Sheikh Hasina was Bangladesh’s prime minister.
- Her formal authority was broad: the constitution places executive power in the PM’s hands and makes the PM the head of the cabinet.
- The real constraint set in 2022 was political rather than constitutional: weak checks from Parliament and the election system, restricted media space, coercive policing, and heavy institutional pressure on opposition and civil society.
- The year was shaped by inherited conditions more than by a clean policy baseline: post-2018 power concentration, entrenched corruption, Rohingya burden, pandemic aftereffects, and climate vulnerability.
- The biggest 2022 events for this ruler-period were the Padma Bridge inauguration, the northeast flood disaster, the fuel-price shock and inflation pressure, IMF stabilization talks, and the December opposition crackdown.

Precise fact: Articles 55-57 place executive power with or under the authority of the Prime Minister, make the Prime Minister head of the Cabinet, and require the President to appoint the MP who commands a parliamentary majority. Precise fact: the report says Bangladesh’s constitution concentrates most power in the PM office, that Hasina remained prime minister, and that security forces committed numerous abuses. Period fit: direct 2022 country report. Ruler attribution: government and security forces under Hasina’s administration. Limitations: U.S. diplomatic framing and broad country-level scope.
