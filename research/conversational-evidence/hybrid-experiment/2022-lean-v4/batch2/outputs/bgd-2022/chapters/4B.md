**Search and rejection summary**
I considered roughly 30 documents across Freedom House, the U.S. State Department, OHCHR/UN, Amnesty International, Human Rights Watch, RSF, Prothom Alo, The Daily Star, and a few Reuters/Reuters-Institute follow-ups.

- Kept because they added distinct 2022 or immediate-retrospective evidence: Freedom House country reports, Freedom on the Net 2022, the U.S. State Department country report, the UN High Commissioner's Bangladesh visit statement, the OHCHR reprisal statement, Amnesty's 2022/23 Bangladesh report, HRW's 2023 Bangladesh chapter, and a few local reports on DSA, Odhikar, and protest crackdowns.
- Rejected as duplicates: later pieces that repeated the same protest-clampdown facts without adding new attribution, and multiple local articles restating the same DSA or rally incidents.
- Rejected as out-of-period: 2023-2026 protest retrospectives, 2024 election postmortems, and 2026 media interviews that did not improve 2022 evidence.
- Rejected as weak or overly generic: broad global explainers, current ranking pages that did not add Bangladesh-specific 2022 detail, and media-freedom summaries that repeated what the Freedom House and Amnesty sources already said more clearly.
- I specifically excluded sources that only discussed economic performance, climate, or generic internet access unless they tied directly to political freedom, censorship, or opposition space.

**Remaining gaps**
- I did not find direct documentary evidence that Hasina personally ordered specific 2022 crackdowns; the record is strong on state responsibility and much weaker on individual command traceability.
- The 2022 election-preparation record is clear on manipulation and capture, but less clear on how much of the remaining local competition was real versus tolerated under constraint.
- Court independence and oversight remain mostly evidenced through negative descriptions rather than detailed case-by-case institutional audits.
- More search could still sharpen attribution on the Election Commission, intelligence-service pressure, and the chain of responsibility for protest policing, but it is unlikely to change the broad pattern already visible.
