**Search and rejection summary**
I considered roughly 30 documents across four main families.

- Kept as strongest: official Bangladesh UN mission statements on Ukraine, the ICJ, the Charter Committee, disarmament and outer space, and Rohingya; UN Digital Library vote records; UN Peacekeeping records; and one Reuters-based regional report.
- Kept as meaningful contrary evidence: the March 2022 abstention and the November 2022 abstention on Ukraine reparations.
- Rejected as duplicates: multiple local and regional articles that repeated the same Ukraine vote outcome, and multiple peacekeeping pages that repeated the same broad contributor fact without adding 2022-specific detail.
- Rejected as too generic: general UN pages on peacekeeping, broad disarmament explainers, and generic charter or development statements that did not add Bangladesh-specific 2022 conduct.
- Rejected as out of period: later 2023-2026 Rohingya and Ukraine retrospectives that did not improve 2022 attribution.
- Rejected as weak for this chapter: sources on humanitarian need or climate vulnerability that did not connect to ruler-attributed international peace choices.
- A few direct opens on mission pages timed out, so I relied on the stable cached text from the search results for those official statements rather than replacing them with weaker secondary summaries.

**Remaining gaps**
- I still do not have direct evidence of Hasina personally making an interstate war decision in 2022, which is expected given Bangladesh’s limited conflict exposure.
- I did not find a 2022 ruler-specific settlement breakthrough on Rohingya repatriation; the record supports diplomatic effort, not success.
- The peacekeeping evidence is strong but institutional, not a direct personal command record.
- More searching would probably only marginally improve attribution on Rohingya diplomacy or on the internal reasoning behind the two Ukraine abstentions; it is unlikely to change the core picture.
