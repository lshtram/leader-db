**Search and rejection summary**
I considered roughly 25-40 documents across UNICEF, WHO, WFP, World Bank, UNDP, the Bangladesh social-protection portal, ministry pages, and several Bangladesh news rewrites. I kept the strongest non-duplicative items that gave either target-year policy intent, implementation detail, or retrospective outcome data.

Retained because they added distinct 2022 or 2022-retrospective evidence:
- UNICEF budget-attitudes survey on education, health, protection, and social workers.
- UNICEF child-protection workforce commitment.
- UNICEF remote-learning strategy for marginalized children.
- WHO country cooperation strategy on health progress and remaining system gaps.
- WFP country strategic plan for 2022-2026 and WFP Bangladesh assessments.
- UNICEF flood-response release.
- World Bank poverty-reduction feature with 2022 outcome data.
- UNICEF Public Finance for Children on MCBP and child-budget implementation.
- WHO/UNICEF breastfeeding report on formula-marketing weakness.
- UNICEF street-children survey release.

Considered but rejected as duplicate, too generic, blocked, or out of period:
- IMF e-library country-report material was blocked by 403, so I relied on accessible UNICEF, WHO, WFP, and World Bank sources instead.
- Broad UNICEF data dashboards and static country profile pages were useful for context but not retained as chapter evidence because they did not add ruler-specific 2022 action.
- Later 2024-2026 SSPS and UNICEF/World Bank pages were not retained unless they clearly reported 2022 conditions retrospectively.
- Government index/listing pages and repository pages without substantive report text were skipped.
- Rewrites of the same flood or budget story across multiple publishers were deduplicated.
- Generic global health or poverty pages that did not add Bangladesh-specific 2022 claims were not kept.

**Remaining gaps**
- Direct, ruler-personal evidence is still thin for social welfare. Most evidence is government- or ministry-level, not a clear personal directive from Sheikh Hasina.
- Better evidence would come from 2022 cabinet, PMO, budget-implementation, or audit material tying specific welfare decisions directly to Hasina rather than to ministries, UNICEF, WHO, or WFP partner structures.
- More precise 2022 data on urban poor, regional disparities, disability coverage, and service quality would help with the vulnerable-groups and dignity lenses.
- Stronger implementation evidence on how much of the social-protection and child-protection expansion actually reached intended beneficiaries would improve confidence.
- More microdata on nutrition, school recovery, and household access by wealth quintile would sharpen the baseline-adjusted life-chances assessment.
