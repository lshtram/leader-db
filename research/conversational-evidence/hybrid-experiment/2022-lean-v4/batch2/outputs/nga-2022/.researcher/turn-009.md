**Search and rejection summary**
I considered roughly 30 distinct documents across the 2022 Buhari program and execution chain, especially State House budget, ministry, retreat, finance, health, and financing releases; CBN fiscal and monetary pages; IMF and World Bank diagnostics; NBS poverty, CPI, and labour releases; UNICEF and WHO health and child-welfare releases; and Reuters coverage of implementation frictions.

What I kept:
- Official program documents that clearly show Buhari’s 2022 governing program, implementation architecture, and personnel choices.
- Independent or multilateral sources that directly assess 2022 macro and delivery performance.
- A small number of rollout-specific health and financing items that show concrete state action, not just rhetoric.

What I rejected or demoted:
- Duplicate State House “achievement” pages that repeated the same claims already covered by the budget speech, retreat, and delivery-tracker material.
- Broad political-freedom, rights, or anti-corruption reports that belonged mainly in 4B or 7B and did not add a distinct 8B competence claim.
- Out-of-period Tinubu-era documents from 2023 to 2026.
- Purely descriptive country-level indicators that lacked a ruler-action chain.
- Some social-sector pages that were useful as 6B evidence but did not add a new competence mechanism beyond the better implementation or outcome sources already kept.

**Remaining gaps**
- I still lack strong independent, audited completion data for the big-ticket infrastructure and service-delivery claims in the NDP and ministerial mandates, especially roads, rail, power, customs, and project completion rates by end-2022.
- The causal link between Buhari’s coordination bodies and actual policy improvement is still only partially evidenced. More direct records on whether the delivery tracker, the economic committee, or the new appointments changed implementation quality would help.
- The evidence on outcome movement is mixed: there are real rollout and measurement actions, but the macro record still shows inflation, subsidy strain, and weak investment. More granular 2022 sector outcomes would clarify how far the administration actually moved toward its own goals.
