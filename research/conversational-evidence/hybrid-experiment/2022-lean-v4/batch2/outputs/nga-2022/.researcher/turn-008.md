Search and rejection summary
- I considered roughly 30 documents across Reuters/Reuters-linked coverage, State House releases, BusinessDay, The Nation, Independent, Access Now, ECOWAS-court reporting, CPJ, Africa Check, and one 2022 academic paper.
- I retained the most material ruler-specific items and pruned near-duplicates on the same stories, especially repeated coverage of the Twitter ban and the Halilu appointment.
- I rejected or demoted several items because they were either too generic, not clearly ruler-specific, or mostly country-level context: media-environment summaries without a clear Buhari nexus, general corruption background, and broad claims about Nigeria’s corruption level.
- I also treated some items as context only, not final evidence, when they were mostly Buhari’s own self-description without external corroboration.
- I did not pull in out-of-period material where it was avoidable; when I used 2023/2024 references in other chapters earlier in the thread, I did not carry them forward here unless they directly illuminated the 2022 record.
- I did not rely on the client matrix as evidence.

Remaining gaps
- Direct evidence on Buhari’s personal assets, campaign finance, hidden conflicts, or private enrichment remains thin. More search in Code of Conduct Bureau records, asset-declaration reporting, and procurement/audit investigations would help.
- I have strong evidence on patronage and censorship, but less on deliberate falsehoods in Buhari’s own 2022 public statements beyond his self-serving end-of-term claims. More fact-checking of his 2022 speeches would help.
- The appointment evidence is strong on favoritism risk, but less decisive on whether those appointees were selected for competence or family access. More independent personnel-analysis reporting would help.
- I found clear evidence of suppression of scrutiny through the Twitter ban and journalist detentions, but less direct evidence tying every enforcement act personally to Buhari rather than his security apparatus. More primary court and administrative records would help tighten attribution.
