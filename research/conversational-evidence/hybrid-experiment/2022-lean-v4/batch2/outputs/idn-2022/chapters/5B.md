**Search and rejection summary**
- I reviewed roughly 30 documents across World Bank/IMF reports, Bank Indonesia monthly reports, BPS macro releases, Kemenkeu and Setkab announcements, Transparency International CPI material, Reuters/Investing reporting, and Jakarta Post analysis.
- I kept the strongest non-duplicative items and removed near-duplicates such as multiple republications of the same Reuters story, overlapping subsidy-press releases that said the same thing in different wording, and general background pages that did not add a new 2022 ruler-specific claim.
- I rejected out-of-period or weak-fit material such as older background pages, 2023 follow-ups that only repeated a 2022 finding, and broad institutional pages without a ruler-period claim.
- I considered the IMF 2022 Article IV page, but the XML endpoint was blocked, so I relied on the accessible abstract plus stronger World Bank, BI, and BPS materials instead.
- I also checked trade-surplus and palm-oil-ban reporting, but those were either outcome-only or redundant with the subsidy-response evidence, so I did not retain them.
- For appointment competence, the evidence is mixed: the cabinet reshuffle coverage suggests political bargaining, while the formal appointment authority is clear from the reused constitutional source.

**Remaining gaps**
- Direct evidence on Jokowi’s personal economic-appointment judgment in 2022 is still thin. The cabinet reshuffle evidence is mostly indirect and mixed.
- More household-level distribution evidence would help, especially regional, gender, and bottom-quintile outcomes beyond the national poverty and Gini releases.
- Case-level corruption or favoritism evidence tied directly to presidential authority would strengthen 5B.5 beyond CPI and analyst commentary.
- More direct evidence on implementation quality for the jobs-law/emergency-regulation route would help separate growth-oriented reform from political expediency.
