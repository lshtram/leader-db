**Search And Rejection Summary**
- I considered roughly 30 documents across Setkab presidential transcripts, ANTARA, Amnesty International Indonesia, Amnesty annual reporting, Human Rights Watch, Reuters republished by Euronews, and a few adjacent background pages.
- I kept source units only when they added something materially new: direct Jokowi remedial action, a distinct abuse episode, a distinct accountability response, or a broad year-end pattern synthesis.
- I removed repeated retellings of the same Kanjuruhan incident, repeated Wadas variants, and repeated Papua protest items when they only paraphrased the same facts.
- I excluded broad civil-liberties or economic pages that lacked a physical-safety nexus for this chapter, and I did not carry over out-of-period background that did not add target-year information.
- I also passed on a few likely duplicate Reuters originals when a directly accessible Reuters republish or a primary Indonesian transcript already captured the same claim more cleanly.

**Remaining Gaps**
- The record is strong on recurring abuse by police and military, especially in Papua, Wadas, Parigi Moutong, and Kanjuruhan. It is weaker on direct presidential command, because most abuse was carried out by security forces rather than by Jokowi personally.
- I found some accountability action after major incidents, especially Kanjuruhan and Mimika, but not enough to show that remedies were complete or that systemic reform followed.
- I did not find solid 2022 evidence of Jokowi personally inciting hatred or ordering domestic violence against a group, so 3B.3 remains thin.
- More search could refine the command chain and the durability of remedies, but it is unlikely to change the basic pattern: recurring serious domestic-safety failures with partial accountability, not systematic mass terror.
