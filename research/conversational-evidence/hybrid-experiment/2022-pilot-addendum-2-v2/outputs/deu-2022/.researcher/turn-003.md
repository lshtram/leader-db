Search and rejection summary
- I reviewed roughly 30 documents across German federal archives, the Foreign Office, Bundestag/OSCE/ICJ material, and Reuters-wired reporting hosted by Euronews or similar outlets.
- I kept the documents that actually moved 2B: war justification and diplomacy on 24 Feb, arms and ceasefire policy in spring and summer 2022, humanitarian and legal-accountability actions, and the late-2022 reconstruction posture.
- I rejected later 2023-2026 archive pages when they mainly restated the same 2022 policy without adding target-period nuance.
- I rejected nuclear-risk pages and procurement pages that were useful for 1B but not materially for 2B.
- I rejected broad Ukraine-support summaries when they were updated long after 2022 and did not add a distinct Scholz-specific 2022 claim.
- I rejected duplicate translations or syndicated copies when they repeated the same Reuters text without adding a new material claim or locator.

Remaining gaps
- Public evidence is thin on internal cabinet deliberations, especially the tradeoff between caution and escalation in spring 2022.
- The record shows Scholz's public position clearly, but less clearly shows what Germany could have done differently at each decision point.
- A few useful items are multilateral or government-level rather than uniquely Scholz-personal, so attribution is strong but not perfectly individualized.
- More search would help only marginally unless you want a deeper file on Bundestag debate records, cabinet minutes, or contemporaneous peace-process reporting.
