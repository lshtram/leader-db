- I reviewed roughly 25-40 documents across federal archives, the Bundestag, the ECtHR, the Bundesverwaltungsgericht, BKA/BMI material, and independent rights reporting.
- Kept the strongest 2022-relevant items that actually move 3B: police-accountability failures, protest-rights protection, one serious police-violence incident, anti-extremism cabinet action, Jewish-life protection rhetoric, and refugee protection.
- Rejected many broad crime, extremism, and statistics pages because they were either too generic, only partially 2022-specific, or mostly descriptive context rather than ruler-relevant evidence.
- Rejected several post-2022 documents when they repeated the same baseline without adding target-year detail.
- Rejected international-war and Ukraine-support material that belonged more properly in 2B unless it had a direct domestic safety consequence; I only retained the refugee-protection item because it directly affected domestic protection for civilians in Germany.
- Rejected local or state-level policing stories when they did not clearly add national-ruler attribution beyond what the national rights and court record already showed.

Remaining gaps
- The record supports a mixed but low-severity domestic-safety picture: open constitutional protections, meaningful protest rights, serious but isolated police/accountability failures, and active anti-extremism measures.
- I did not find a clean 2022 federal mechanism that solved the independent-police-investigation gap; that remains a real weakness in the record.
- There is limited evidence of Scholz personally directing domestic security policy on a day-to-day basis; most of the material is cabinet-level or institutional.
- More search would help if you want deeper coverage on police oversight reforms, regional protest-policing episodes, or 2022 hate-crime implementation, but it is not likely to change the overall evidentiary shape much.
