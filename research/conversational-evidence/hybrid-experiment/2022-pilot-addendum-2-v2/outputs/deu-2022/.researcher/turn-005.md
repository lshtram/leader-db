Search and rejection summary
- I reviewed roughly 25-40 documents across the Federal Government archive, Bundestag, Bundeswahlleiterin, Bundesverfassungsgericht, Bundesverwaltungsgericht, and a smaller set of related press/reporting pages.
- Kept the strongest non-duplicative evidence on election contestability, parliamentary oversight, opposition rights, protest protection, press freedom, surveillance limits, and minority-protection rhetoric/policy.
- Rejected broad democracy-index pages because the local structured priors already cover the regime baseline and re-fetching those datasets was not needed.
- Rejected most media stories about generic politics or coalition disputes because they were either redundant with official constitutional sources or too weakly tied to political-freedom mechanisms.
- Rejected security/Ukraine items unless they had a direct political-freedom effect, since Chapter 4B is about contestability, institutional restraint, and civic/media space rather than international conduct.
- Rejected later 2023-2026 pages when they only restated the same 2022 institutional environment without adding target-period value.
- Rejected incident-heavy or regional items that did not clearly show federal-ruler attribution or did not materially change the chapter picture.

Remaining gaps
- The record is strong on institutional resilience and weak on any direct Scholz-specific entrenchment behavior, which is itself informative.
- I did not find meaningful evidence that Scholz manipulated elections, courts, media, or term rules in 2022.
- Most negative evidence is about contested committee-chair access or general surveillance/legal disputes, which are procedural and legally checked rather than authoritarian in nature.
- More searching would likely only refine the balance between inherited democratic baseline and Scholz-era institutional maintenance; it is unlikely to uncover a materially different 2022 pattern without a very specific allegation.
