**Search and rejection summary**
- I reviewed roughly 30 distinct documents across the Federal Government archive, BMAS, BMF, Destatis, OECD, IMF, Bundesrechnungshof, ifo, Bundestag, and a small amount of supporting archive material.
- I kept the strongest non-duplicative evidence on cabinet formation, economic-team coordination, wages, household relief, the energy shield, inflation, GDP, employment, real earnings, poverty, and external assessments.
- I rejected older pre-2022 items unless they were needed for baseline or appointment context.
- I rejected most generic government pages on growth, sustainability, or social policy when they did not add a distinct 2022 claim.
- I rejected purely descriptive archive pages that repeated the same relief measures without adding a new material point.
- I rejected several pages about later 2023-2026 reforms because they were out of period or only restated the same policy baseline.
- I rejected niche tax or labor-market pages that did not clearly connect to Scholz-era 2022 economic choices.
- I rejected broad macro indicators unless they helped distinguish policy effects from the external shock environment.

**Remaining gaps**
- 5B.2 is still only moderately evidenced: I have cabinet formation and clear coordination around the energy shock, but not a direct external assessment of Scholz’s appointment choices as such.
- 5B.5 remains thin. I did not find strong, ruler-specific evidence of corruption, favoritism, or oligarchic capture in 2022, which is itself informative but still a gap.
- Household distribution data are decent but not perfect. I have poverty risk, real earnings, and the minimum wage, but not a full 2022 household-incidence model for every relief measure.
- The year is dominated by external shocks, especially the Ukraine war and energy prices, so attribution must stay modest. GDP and employment were resilient, but real wages and inflation show the cost to households.
- More searching would help mainly if you want a narrower deep dive on who benefited most from the relief packages, or a more explicit appraisal of finance-ministry design choices and debt-brake compliance.
