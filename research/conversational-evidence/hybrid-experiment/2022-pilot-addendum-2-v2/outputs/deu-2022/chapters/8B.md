**Search and rejection summary**
I reviewed roughly 30 documents across coalition-agreement PDFs, Bundeskanzleramt and Bundesregierung statements, BMAS/BMWSB/BMFSFJ/BMF releases, OECD, IMF, ifo, and secondary reporting. I kept the sources that established a clear Scholz-era program, a concrete 2022 implementation step, or an independent evaluation of that step.

Rejected items fell into these buckets:
- Duplicate coverage of the same 2022 relief or refugee measures without adding a new factual claim.
- Generic German capacity or outcome indicators that did not show Scholz-specific program-to-action linkage.
- Out-of-period follow-ups that only repeated the 2022 record without adding new evidence.
- Commentary pieces that were mostly opinion or lacked direct factual value beyond what official and multilateral sources already showed.
- Sources with weak personal nexus to Scholz when the action was actually ministerial or coalition-level.

**Remaining gaps**
- Direct evidence on 8B.4 is still thinner than on program definition and crisis execution. I have cabinet-level coordination and named-minister execution, but less on Scholz's own personnel selection and how he empowered or corrected specific implementers.
- 8B.5 is only partly evidenced through the refugee, energy, and social-reform files. I still do not have a strong granular record of inter-ministerial bottlenecks, internal discipline, or Scholz personally forcing follow-through.
- 8B.8 is visible in the adaptation to the energy shock, but the record is still stronger on reactive crisis management than on repeated course correction after failed tactics.
- More search would help if it could surface cabinet minutes, staffing changes, budget execution records, or implementation audits that tie directly to Scholz rather than only to ministries or the coalition as a whole.
