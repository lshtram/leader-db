**Search and Rejection Summary**
I considered roughly 30 documents across official Chinese notices, statistics releases, WHO/NHSA/NHC materials, State Council / Xinhua summaries, NBS annual bulletins, SCIO briefings, World Bank and IMF context pieces, Reuters reporting, Human Rights Watch, Amnesty, and some housing/pension items.

I rejected or deprioritized:
- Duplicates of the same underlying claim published by different outlets, especially Reuters republished by Investing.com.
- Out-of-period material that only discussed 2021 or 2023 outcomes without a clear 2022 target-year hook.
- Macro-only or finance-only documents that did not materially improve the 6B judgment beyond the stronger social-policy and crisis-response evidence already collected.
- Sources already covered in the local structured prior where a fresh narrative source was not needed.
- Weak or overly generalized sources that repeated official intent without any 2022 implementation or outcome detail.
- Some later 2023/2024 statistical reports were considered only when they explicitly measured 2022 outcomes; otherwise I left them out.
- Direct evidence on water/sanitation quality, local service variation, and independent program evaluation was thinner than health, insurance, poverty, housing, and crisis-response evidence, so I did not pad with loosely related documents.

**Remaining Gaps**
- Water and sanitation evidence is still thin relative to health, pensions, housing, and child welfare.
- Independent evaluation of service quality, regional inequity, and front-line delivery remains limited; most positive evidence is official.
- More direct evidence on whether welfare and housing benefits were used coercively for political loyalty would help clarify 6B.7.
- The record is strong on crisis harm from zero-COVID, but less complete on whether any compensating welfare measures were effective at scale.
- More subnational data would help separate Xi-era national intent from local implementation and confirm distributional effects across poorer regions and vulnerable groups.
