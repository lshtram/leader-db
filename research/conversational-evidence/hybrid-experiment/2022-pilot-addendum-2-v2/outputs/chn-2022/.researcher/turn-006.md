Search and rejection summary
I reviewed roughly 30 distinct documents across IMF, World Bank, Reuters, the NBS, the PBOC, and CPC/NBS official releases. I kept 14 distinct URLs as the best non-duplicative evidence.

Kept because they added distinct value:
- IMF 2022 Article IV on policy uncertainty, slowdown, and rebalancing.
- Two World Bank 2022 outlook/rebalancing pieces on growth slowdown and structural reform needs.
- Reuters on 2022 property debt maturities, mortgage boycott, property-investment collapse, and elite economic appointments.
- Official CPC Congress text on common prosperity and high-quality development.
- NBS annual GDP, household income/consumption, fixed investment, real estate, and jobs releases.
- PBC quarterly monetary-policy report on reserve cuts, liquidity, rates, and property support.

Rejected or deprioritized:
- Duplicate Reuters syndications and mirrored republishes of the same property and congress stories.
- Later 2023-2026 macro commentary that was outside the 2022 target period.
- Generic source pages without period-specific claims.
- Broad World Bank regulatory-efficiency pages and unrelated older history pieces.
- Repetitive official releases that restated the same GDP or employment statistics without adding a materially distinct claim.
- Search results that only repeated the headline without a usable claim locator.

Remaining gaps
- Direct evidence of Xi's personal micro-level economic judgment remains limited; much of the record is attributable to the Party-state and central bank rather than a personally signed Xi directive.
- Distributional evidence is decent for income and urban-rural gaps, but wealth inequality and household balance-sheet stress are still underdeveloped here.
- I have strong evidence on macro stabilization, property stress, and employment, but less direct evidence on whether appointments were chosen for competence versus loyalty beyond the 20th Congress reshuffle.
- More search would help if you want a finer treatment of private-sector policy, local-government debt, or official correction of policy mistakes, but this dossier is already adequate for chapter-level judging.
