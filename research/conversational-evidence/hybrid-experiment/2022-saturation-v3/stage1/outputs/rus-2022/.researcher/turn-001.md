**Office, Authority, Constraints**
- In 2022, Vladimir Putin was the President of the Russian Federation, i.e. the head of state; the official Kremlin profile and the Constitution’s Chapter 4 set out that role, and the president is also the constitutional guarantor and supreme commander-in-chief.
- The legal ceiling for wartime action is formal and specific: the president can introduce partial or full mobilization and martial law only under aggression or an immediate threat of aggression, using decrees and follow-on legal acts. In 2022 Putin used those powers for partial mobilization on 21 September and martial law in the annexed/occupied regions on 19 October.
- Practical constraints in 2022 were political rather than institutional: the state tightened media control, criminalized war criticism, and suppressed anti-war protest. That mattered for how much dissent, reporting, and implementation friction the Kremlin faced.

**Major Events**
- 24 February 2022: Putin announced the start of the full-scale war / “special military operation” against Ukraine.
- 24 February through March 2022: rapid domestic crackdown on protests and independent reporting.
- 21 September 2022: Putin announced partial mobilization.
- 30 September to 4 October 2022: Kremlin annexation ceremony and formal incorporation laws for occupied Ukrainian territories.
- 19 October 2022: Putin introduced martial law in the four annexed/occupied regions.
- Late 2022: mobilization and wartime administration shifted more burden to regional governors and security organs; dissent remained constrained.
