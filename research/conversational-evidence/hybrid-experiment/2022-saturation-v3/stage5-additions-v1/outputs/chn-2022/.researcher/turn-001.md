**Office and constraints**
- In 2022 Xi held four top posts: CPC general secretary, CPC Central Military Commission chair, PRC president, and PRC Central Military Commission chair.
- His authority was primarily party-centered, not competitive-electoral. The PRC constitution defines the formal state powers of the presidency, while the CPC constitution and 20th Party Congress texts anchor authority in the Party and in Xi’s “core” position.
- The main constraints in 2022 were intra-party discipline, policy performance, and elite-management pressure, especially around zero-COVID, the economy, and succession optics.

- March to May: Shanghai lockdown dynamics exposed the cost of zero-COVID.
- May to July: zero-COVID still dominated policy; property stress and weak growth deepened.
- October 16 to 23: 20th Party Congress, Xi report, congress closure, Party Constitution revision, Xi reaffirmed as core leader.

- Late November: protests spread across China against COVID restrictions.
- December 7 to 8: official optimization of COVID controls began.
- December 15 to 16: Central Economic Work Conference reset the 2023 economic line toward stability and recovery.
