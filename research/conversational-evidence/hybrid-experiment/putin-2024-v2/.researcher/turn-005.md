**Search and rejection summary**
- I considered roughly 30 documents across OSCE/ODIHR, Kremlin pages, CEC/TASS/RG, Reuters wires and syndications, Freedom House, RSF, CPJ, OVD-Info, HRW, Amnesty, Meduza, and a few secondary republications.
- I kept sources that directly affected the 2024 contestability, media space, term/transfer, or digital-control questions.
- I rejected duplicate Reuters republications and image-only pages when they repeated the same facts without adding new evidence.
- I rejected older background items when they only restated Putin’s long-run authoritarianism without adding 2024-specific signal.
- I rejected more general rights reports when they were redundant with the stronger election-specific and media-censorship sources already retained.
- Several Kremlin transcript pages timed out, so I relied on accessible official index pages and Reuters/OSCE republished coverage of the same dated events.

**Remaining gaps**
- Direct evidence of internal elite bargaining and whether any real institutional constraint operated on Putin in 2024 is still thin.
- The exact mechanics of turnout inflation, pressure on voters, and administrative coercion are only partly visible in open sources.
- Attribution is strong at the system level, but more granular evidence would help separate Putin’s own direction from Roskomnadzor, the CEC, courts, and regional officials.
- OSCE could not observe the election, which is itself highly probative, but it also means the outside record is less granular than in more open systems.
