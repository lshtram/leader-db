**Search and rejection summary**
- I reviewed roughly 30 documents across Rosstat, the CBR, the World Bank, the IMF, Reuters wires/syndications, and Kremlin decrees.
- I kept sources that directly bear on 2024 macro performance, household welfare, labor markets, inflation, fiscal priorities, transparency, property rights, and medium-term prospects.
- I rejected older World Bank background reports from 2014/2021 when they only described pre-2024 structural conditions without adding anything specific to the 2024 ruler-period.
- I rejected generic global IMF/WB material and non-Russia country reports.
- I also rejected several Reuters duplicates and republishers when they repeated the same story without adding a materially different fact.
- Some official Russian statistical pages are updated later than 2024 but report 2024 annual data; I kept those only when they gave concrete 2024 figures for GDP, incomes, or poverty.

**Remaining gaps**
- The biggest gap is causal attribution: the record shows 2024 growth and income gains, but much of that came from war spending, labor mobilization, price pressure, and oil revenue rather than broad-based civilian productivity gains.
- I still have limited direct evidence on small-business conditions, competition, and private investment quality outside state-linked sectors.
- Regional inequality remains underdeveloped in the source set; the national averages may conceal weaker household outcomes outside the main growth nodes.
- If you want a tighter finish, more search would help on productivity, SME conditions, and whether any 2024 reforms actually improved fair competition rather than simply financing the war economy.
