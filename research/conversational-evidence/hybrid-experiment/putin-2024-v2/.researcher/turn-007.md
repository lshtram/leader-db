**Search and rejection summary**
- I considered roughly 30 documents across `government.ru`, `kremlin.ru`, `rosstat.gov.ru`, `minzdrav.gov.ru`, `cbr.ru`, `imf.org`, `worldbank.org`, `data.worldbank.org`, `data.unicef.org`, and Reuters wire reproductions.
- I retained 18 URLs above and rejected the rest as follows:
- Duplicates: multiple Rosstat quarterly poverty releases were partly overlapping; I kept the ones that added distinct temporal slices (`Q1`, `Q3`, full-year/Q4, and inequality). Reuters stories syndicated across several platforms were collapsed to the cleanest available wire copy.
- Weak or too generic: global UNICEF annual reports, global HDR pages, broad World Bank poverty pages not Russia-specific, and generic country landing pages without 2024 Russia claims were not useful enough for ruler attribution.
- Blocked/timeout: some `government.ru` pages timed out on open, but the search-result snippets were sufficient where the policy text itself was the evidence. I did not rely on pages whose content could not be recovered.
- Out-of-period: 2025 or 2026 items were kept only when they explicitly reported 2024 outcomes or 2024-adjacent policy implementation. Otherwise they were excluded.
- Relevance pruning: I excluded sources that described general macro conditions without a welfare nexus, or social commentary without a traceable claim about Russian 2024 welfare policy or outcomes.

**Remaining gaps**
- The weakest part of the dossier is direct, independent evidence on service quality, not just coverage or spending. Education quality, rural access, disability inclusion, and mental-health or housing outcomes remain thinly evidenced.
- There is still limited independent evidence on how much of the income and poverty improvement was caused by Putin-era social policy versus war-economy wage pressure, labor shortages, and inflation.
- Regional and group-level distribution is only partly covered. I have one Rosstat inequality release, but not a strong subnational or vulnerable-group breakdown for 2024.
- If you want a tighter future judgment, more search on household surveys, regional health/education access, and disability/child welfare would help more than more macro articles.
