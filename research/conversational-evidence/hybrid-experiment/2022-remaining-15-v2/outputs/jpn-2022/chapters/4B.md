**Search and rejection summary**
I considered roughly 30 documents across IPU election records, Freedom House, RSF, Reuters, Kyodo, Euronews/Reuters, the Prime Minister’s Office, the National Police Agency, and the U.S. State Department. I kept the pieces that added something beyond the local priors and the earlier chapter dossiers.

I rejected:
- Duplicate republications of the same Reuters/Kyodo stories with no new factual content.
- Out-of-period 2023-2026 democracy pages that did not add 2022-specific evidence.
- General Japan background pages that repeated the known democratic baseline without Kishida-period detail.
- Purely descriptive photos or wire image pages with no substantive text.
- Security-policy or foreign-policy articles that did not bear directly on domestic political freedom.
- Press-freedom commentary from before 2022 unless it was needed as inherited baseline context inside the 2022 RSF/Freedom House pages.

**Remaining gaps**
- There is still no evidence of Kishida using direct repression, surveillance, or internet shutdowns against opponents in 2022.
- The strongest negative material is party/church entanglement and media-structure pressure, but much of that looks inherited or systemic rather than uniquely Kishida-designed.
- I did not find a strong direct record of Kishida personally reshaping the media environment in either a clearly liberalizing or clearly repressive direction.
- The election and constitutional-transfer record is solid, but the parliamentary system means 4B.8 is more about compliance with normal democratic procedure than a presidential handoff test.
- More search would help mainly on public broadcasting pressure, any documented use of administrative harassment, and whether Kishida’s responses to the church scandal produced durable institutional safeguards rather than short-term cleanup.
