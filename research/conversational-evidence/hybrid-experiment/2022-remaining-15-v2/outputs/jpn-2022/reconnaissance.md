**Office / Constraints**
- Kishida was Japan’s prime minister in 2022, but the office is parliamentary, not presidential. Under the Constitution, executive power sits in the Cabinet, the PM is the Cabinet’s head, the PM is designated by the Diet, appoints and removes ministers, submits bills, and supervises administration. The Cabinet is collectively responsible to the Diet. That means Kishida had real agenda-setting and personnel power, but within coalition, Diet, and cabinet-responsibility constraints.
- For defense and security, the constraint set matters as much as the office: civilian control, Diet approval, budget process, and the Constitution’s pacifist framework. The 2022 strategic turn has to be read as a cabinet-led shift, not a unilateral ruler decree.
- Kishida’s 2022 authority also depended on political mandate and coalition management, especially after the House of Councillors election and the LDP-Komeito governing arrangement.
