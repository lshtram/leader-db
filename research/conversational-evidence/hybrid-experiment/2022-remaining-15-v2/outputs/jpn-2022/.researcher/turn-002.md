**Search and rejection summary**
- I reviewed about 30-plus documents across Kantei, MOFA, UN, CTBTO, Reuters-syndicated coverage, The Japan Times, AA, and Hiroshima city material.
- I rejected duplicate official republications of the same Kishida speeches, especially English/Japanese variants of the same Kantei text and archive index pages.
- I rejected out-of-period items, especially 2023-2025 follow-up statements, because they do not bear directly on the 2022 chapter.
- I rejected broad security and economic pieces that did not add nuclear-risk choices or attribution.
- I rejected editorial/opinion pieces where stronger primary or official records existed for the same point.
- I rejected later retrospective stories when a 2022 primary source existed for the same event.
- The main blocked/irrelevant cluster was generic Japan security coverage that was useful for context but not needed once the NPT/CTBT/Nagasaki/Hiroshima records were in hand.

**Remaining gaps**
- There is still no direct evidence of any nuclear command-and-control responsibility, because Japan had no nuclear arsenal and Kishida was not a nuclear-weapon-state leader.
- The record is strong on diplomacy and rhetoric but thin on internal cabinet mechanics, especially how Kishida balanced pro-disarmament instincts against alliance reassurance and domestic hawks.
- I do not have a 2022 primary record showing any concrete dual-use, cyber, biological, or AI catastrophe-risk decision attributable to Kishida for this chapter.
- I also do not have a full primary trail on whether the Hiroshima Action Plan changed behavior beyond speeches, convenings, and a UN resolution.
- More search could help on alliance consultation papers and Diet records, but it is unlikely to change the basic chapter shape: limited direct exposure, real diplomatic choices, strong anti-proliferation signaling, and a large inherited regional nuclear-risk environment.
