I reviewed roughly 30 documents across PMO speeches, MHLW and Children and Families Agency pages, UNICEF, and OECD. I kept the strongest Japan-specific, contemporaneous or near-contemporaneous claims and dropped near-duplicates that repeated the same child-policy or Ukraine announcements in different wrappers.

Rejected or de-emphasized items:
- The PMO child-support overview page at `childsupport.html?os=i` was cache-missed on open, so I did not rely on it.
- Several 2021 PMO COVID or support statements were treated as pre-period background only and not used as core 2022 evidence.
- Generic global child-poverty papers and non-Japan-specific UNICEF/OECD material were rejected as too broad for ruler attribution.
- Reuters/secondary writeups repeating PMO announcements were mostly dropped in favor of the primary Japanese transcripts.
- Broader OECD demographic-headwind summaries were not used when the Japan 2022 country note and basic-statistics table already covered the relevant baseline and residual gaps.
- I kept both positive and limiting evidence, but I did not pad the dossier with multiple sources describing the same policy event in slightly different language.

**Remaining Gaps**
- Direct 2022 evidence on whether Kishida's child/fertility package actually changed child poverty, birth outcomes, or service uptake by year-end is still thin.
- More Japan-specific 2022 outcome data on disabled children, single-parent households, housing insecurity, and food insecurity would improve attribution.
- I still lack a strong independent 2022 evaluation of the distributional impact of the universal cash relief and child-support package.
- The evidence is strongest on agenda-setting, legislation, and crisis relief, and weaker on measured end-of-year life-chance gains.
