- I screened roughly 30 documents across Egyptian official pages, UN/IGO material, U.S. and NGO country reports, Reuters mirrors, and local/regional reporting.
- I retained a mixed set that covers the core 2022 domestic-safety story: police torture, death in custody, COP27 repression, prison conditions, arbitrary detention, women’s safety, refugees, LGBTQI+ abuse, and a small amount of remedial/pardon evidence.
- I rejected duplicates where multiple publishers repeated the same incident without adding a materially different claim, especially around the COP27 detentions and the Ayman Hadhoud case.
- I rejected broad background explainers that repeated older Egypt repression history without adding a 2022-specific fact.
- I rejected several advocacy or commentary items that were too generic, not Egypt-specific, or not tied to a ruler-attributable safety decision.
- I also set aside some out-of-period material that only served as background when the 2022 evidence was already sufficient.
- A few direct-site fetches were blocked or noisy, so I used reliable mirrors or open-page searches where the primary page was not accessible.

**Remaining gaps**
- The evidence is strong on recurring and at times widespread repression, but weaker on the exact chain of command from Sisi personally to every police/prison decision.
- The record is better for state security and detention abuse than for granular civilian-protection policy design.
- The positive record is limited to rhetorical reform signals and a handful of pardons/releases; it does not show systemic remedy.
- More search would help only if you want narrower proof on accountability outcomes for specific abusive units, or a more granular measure of whether the pardon/release initiatives materially reduced the detention pool in 2022.
