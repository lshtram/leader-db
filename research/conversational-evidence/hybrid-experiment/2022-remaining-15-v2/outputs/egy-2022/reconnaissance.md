**Core Findings**
- In 2022, Abdel Fattah el-Sisi was Egypt’s president and the formal head of state and executive power.
- The constitutional text gave the presidency significant agenda-setting and appointment power, but those formal checks did not translate into strong practical constraints in the rights/reporting record.
- The biggest inherited conditions in 2022 were the Ukraine-war shock to wheat and FX, long-running macro fragility, demographic pressure, and a weak civil-liberties environment.
- The most useful narrative events for chapter work are: the wheat shock and pound devaluation in spring 2022, El-Dabaa nuclear construction in July, the economic conference in October, COP27 in November, the protest-arrest wave around COP27, and the IMF deal in December.

**Compact Event Map**
- March 3, 2022: Ukraine-war disruption hits wheat imports, a core inherited vulnerability.
- March 21, 2022: The pound is devalued, signaling FX stress and limited policy room.
- April 21 and October 23, 2022: Sisi publicly frames the economic crisis as structural and long-running, not temporary.
- July 1 and July 22, 2022: El-Dabaa gets construction approval and then enters active construction, useful for 1B and state-capacity context.
- November 6 to November 19, 2022: COP27 coincides with a protest-arrest wave, making it the clearest rights-and-control event cluster of the year.
- December 16, 2022: IMF approves the rescue package and formalizes the flexible-exchange-rate / reform constraint set.
