**Search and rejection summary**
I considered roughly 30 documents across official reports, UN/IGO releases, HRW, Amnesty, Reuters-syndicated coverage, Dawn, and other regional reporting.

I rejected or deprioritized:
- older 2021-only reports unless they were clearly useful as inherited baseline,
- duplicate Reuters-syndicated stories on the same protest or shooting episode,
- broad political or economic explainers that did not connect to physical safety, detention, protest, or vulnerable-group protection,
- later 2023-2026 items that were outside the 2022 target period,
- the Freedom House internet report and similar structured-dataset pages because the local prior already covered structured country-year material and the instructions said not to re-fetch those datasets,
- weaker commentary pieces that repeated the same facts without better attribution or a clearer safety nexus.

Main clusters I considered but did not retain as separate evidence items:
- A 2022 workplace-harassment law and the first female Supreme Court judge, because those are positive but mostly inherited or pre-Sharif baseline rather than strong 2022 Sharif-period safety evidence.
- Multiple flood stories from Reuters, UN, UNFPA, and UNDP, collapsed into the three strongest items above.
- Additional PTI-protest stories and internet-disruption reports, which duplicated the same protest-suppression pattern already captured by Reuters, Amnesty, and the State Department.

**Remaining gaps**
- The biggest gap is attribution: much of the abuse appears to be by police, FIA, or intelligence bodies rather than a directly documented Shehbaz Sharif order.
- There is no clean independent adjudication here of the enforced-disappearance and custodial-abuse allegations against specific national decision-makers in 2022.
- I did not find a single decisive source proving a structural improvement in accountability under Sharif beyond the torture bill and public condemnations.
- I also did not find a strong national-level record showing that the government consistently protected journalists, protesters, or minorities after notice rather than reacting case by case.
- More search would help most on two fronts: official inquiry outcomes for the Wazirabad shooting and the disappearances/custodial-abuse cases, plus local-language reporting on provincial violence against minorities and protesters to sharpen attribution.

Overall, the 2022 record points to recurring repression, weak remedy, and selective protective steps, with the flood response and torture bill as real but limited counterevidence.
