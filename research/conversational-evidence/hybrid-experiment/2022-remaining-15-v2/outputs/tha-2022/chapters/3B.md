**Search and rejection summary**
- I considered roughly 30 documents across HRW, Amnesty, TLHR, the U.S. State Department, OHCHR, Thai local media, Thai-language NGO reporting, Fortify Rights, Reuters-syndicated coverage, and a few official Thai pages.
- I retained 10 distinct URLs because they added non-duplicative claims: broad protest repression, detainee/prosecution counts, deep-south emergency powers, torture/disappearance reform, the APEC crackdown, and vulnerable-group treatment.
- I rejected several duplicate reports on the same APEC protest clash when they repeated the same facts without adding a materially new claim.
- I dropped older baseline material that only described 2021 unless it was needed to explain continuity into 2022.
- I also dropped a few follow-up pages from 2023-2026 that merely restated the same 2022 repression pattern without adding better attribution or a different claim.
- I excluded broad democracy summaries and economic pieces that did not materially advance the physical-safety or state-violence questions in this chapter.

**Remaining gaps**
- No source shows Prayut personally ordering a specific torture, beating, disappearance, or lethal force episode in 2022.
- The clearest harms are office-level and system-level: continued prosecutions, protest suppression, detention, and inherited emergency powers, rather than a single mass-violence order.
- The torture/disappearance law is an important positive reform, but the record does not show that enforcement had meaningfully improved by the end of 2022.
- The southern border remains partly an inherited security regime with local insurgency dynamics, so attribution is real but not exclusive.
- More search would help only if you want tighter PM-level attribution for the APEC crackdown, implementation records for the new torture law, or direct cabinet/command evidence on the deep-south emergency regime.
