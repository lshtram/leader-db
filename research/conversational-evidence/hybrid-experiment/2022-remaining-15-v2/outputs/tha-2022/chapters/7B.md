**Search and rejection summary**
I considered roughly thirty documents across Reuters mirrors, Bangkok Post, Nation Thailand, Thai Newsroom, ISEAS, NACC/OIC material, and older Reuters reports on oath and cabinet formation. I kept sources that had a direct personal nexus to Prayut or his close governing network, or that established a concrete 2022 commitment/party-switch/term-limit pattern. I rejected or downgraded several classes of material: broad corruption indices and country-level governance metrics without personal attribution; human-rights or media-freedom reports that showed repression but not integrity nexus; pure economic or social-policy coverage; and repeated coverage of the same censure or term-limit episode when it added no new factual claim. I also treated some opposition allegations as context only, not as findings.

**Remaining gaps**
1. I still do not have a clean 2022 independent finding that Prayut personally lied, profited, or concealed an asset or contract. The evidence is strongest on coalition maneuvering, appointments, and transparency concerns, not on a proven enrichment case.
2. Asset and beneficial-interest evidence remains thin for 2022. A direct NACC record, court finding, or investigative report on Prayut's own declarations would materially improve 7B.4 and 7B.6.
3. Appointment evidence is better, but it is still partly mediated by scholarly analysis and coalition reporting. A primary cabinet-order trail or official appointment rationale would sharpen attribution.
4. If you want a tighter dossier, more search would help on 2022 asset disclosure, procurement, and any official record tied to Prayut's own declarations or corrections.
