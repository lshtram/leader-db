**Search and rejection summary**
I considered a broad set of target-year and near-target-year documents across Thai budget documents, Reuters mirrors, World Bank diagnostics, UNICEF Thailand reports, NSO/UNICEF survey material, and ministry notices. I kept the strongest non-duplicative sources and rejected or demoted several classes: global education-recovery reports that were useful only as background, macro-only investment or EV articles with no clear social-welfare nexus, repeated Reuters mirrors of the same policy announcement, current-only pages that added no target-year detail, and out-of-period material unless it directly measured 2022 conditions. I also collapsed duplicate stories about the same budget or subsidy decision into one claim unit rather than repeating the same event through multiple publishers.

**Remaining gaps**
1. Direct evidence on how much Prayut personally drove social-service implementation, versus cabinet, ministries, and existing welfare institutions, is still thin. More primary Thai cabinet or ministry records would help.
2. Distributional delivery evidence is incomplete. I have budget intent and survey outcomes, but not strong 2022 administrative data on take-up, exclusion errors, or province-level delivery quality for child grants, welfare cards, or health access.
3. The clearest weaknesses remain in vulnerable-group outcomes, especially rural poverty, child food insecurity, learning loss, and child/adolescent mental health. More search could sharpen whether any 2022-specific policy changes materially improved those indicators, but the current record already shows substantial gaps.
