**Search and Rejection Summary**
- I considered roughly 30 documents across State House releases, EU election-observation material, Freedom House, Reuters Institute, Reuters-syndicated reporting, VOA Hausa, and a few secondary pages.
- I kept sources that add distinct mechanics, not just repeated praise or repeated criticism.
- I rejected duplicates of the same claim, especially multiple copies of the same Reuters-syndicated story or multiple State House pieces repeating the same free-fair transition message.
- I rejected most 2023 election final reports because they are outside the 2022 target period unless they only summarized a 2022 reform. I also did not rely on them where 2022 material already existed.
- I rejected broad country-profile pages that duplicated the local structured prior without adding a new ruler-specific narrative claim.
- I skipped blocked or low-value pulls, including some region/language pages that were inaccessible or returned only boilerplate.

**Remaining Gaps**
- I still do not have a clean, independent 2022 audit of how well the Electoral Act 2022 was actually implemented before the 2023 polls.
- I do not have direct evidence of Buhari personally intervening against every instance of media pressure, surveillance, or legal harassment. The evidence is mainly on the administration and regulatory environment.
- I do not have strong evidence on court independence or legislative oversight under Buhari in 2022 beyond reform rhetoric and the election-law package.
- I do not have a robust non-English independent observer report on the 2022 pre-election period beyond VOA Hausa and the EU material.
- More searching could refine the balance between reform and control, but it is unlikely to change the core picture: Buhari in 2022 publicly accepted succession, signed electoral reform, and endorsed contestability, while the system still had meaningful media and digital-pressure problems.
