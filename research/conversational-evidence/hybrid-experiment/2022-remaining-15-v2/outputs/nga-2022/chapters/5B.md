**Search and rejection summary**
I considered roughly 30 documents across World Bank, IMF, NBS, CBN, State House, Reuters-syndicated reporting, and Transparency International.

Retained items were selected because they directly described 2022 policy, 2022 outcomes, or 2022 target-period decisions. I collapsed or rejected:
- Duplicate or mirrored Reuters stories on the same minister-resignation event.
- World Bank landing pages, blogs, and press releases that repeated the same poverty/public-finance findings without adding a new materially distinct claim.
- NBS microdata catalog pages and duplicate report listings when the report page already contained the needed 2022 figures.
- Background pages or later follow-ups that did not add new 2022-specific facts.
- Out-of-period material unless it explicitly reported 2022 data or a 2022 policy act that governed the 2022 fiscal year.

**Remaining gaps**
- Direct evidence on how Buhari chose, evaluated, and empowered economic technocrats is still thin beyond the NBS appointment and his own cabinet speech.
- Budget execution, oil-revenue leakage audits, and social-protection rollout in 2022 would help separate announcement from implementation.
- More direct evidence on regional distributional effects, especially household welfare outside headline poverty and inflation series, would improve attribution.

Net read: the dossier shows some pro-stability and pro-data actions, but 2022 still ended with weak growth, high inflation, deep poverty, and highly regressive subsidy dynamics.
