**Search and rejection summary**
I considered roughly 30 documents across World Bank, IMF, NBS, WHO, UNICEF, WFP, State House, and Reuters-syndicated reporting.

Retained items were chosen because they add distinct 2022-relevant welfare evidence: budget priorities, actual service delivery, social protection, health coverage, WASH, floods, schooling, and distributional poverty data.

I rejected or de-emphasized:
- Duplicate World Bank landing pages that repeated the same poverty/public-spending message without adding new evidence.
- Generic country or programme pages that described ongoing work but did not supply a target-year claim.
- Out-of-period pages unless they clearly reported 2022 data or a 2022 policy action that shaped the year.
- Retrospective reports that did not add materially distinct 2022 evidence beyond the sources already retained.
- Purely promotional pages where the same claim was already captured in a more direct budget, survey, or evaluation source.

**Remaining gaps**
- Independent audit evidence on how much of the announced or reported social spending actually reached beneficiaries in 2022 is still thin.
- Direct national-level evidence on housing quality, sanitation upgrades, and nutrition delivery under Buhari in 2022 is limited.
- More third-party evaluation of BHCPF, NHIA enrollment, and school-feeding execution would improve attribution.
- Better causal evidence on whether federal decisions, as opposed to state or donor programs, drove the localized school-access gains would help.
- The welfare baseline is clear, but the record still mixes real delivery, partner-led programmes, and severe inherited deprivation.

Overall, the 2022 record shows real social-policy intent and some concrete delivery, especially on health coverage, social protection, and schooling, but it sits against very severe child poverty, weak WASH access, flood damage, and major education disruption.
