- I considered roughly 30 distinct documents across HRW, CPJ, Amnesty, the Council of Europe/Venice Commission, Reuters/Euronews mirrors, the U.S. State Department, IRB Canada, and OHCHR material.
- I kept 10 non-duplicative URLs because they each added something materially different: torture/detention, protest treatment, media imprisonment, the disinformation law, legal scrutiny of the bill, speech/criminalization cases, crisis rhetoric, LGBT vulnerability, and one favorable minority-protection point.
- I rejected several Reuters mirrors that repeated the same article with no new facts.
- I rejected later 2023-2024 retrospectives unless they added a distinct 2022 fact relevant to this chapter.
- I rejected multiple OHCHR/CEDAW pages when they were either technical dead ends, too generic, or did not expose a ruler-attributable 2022 domestic-safety claim.
- I rejected broad background pages on elections, religion, or protest rights when they only repeated context already covered by the stronger sources above.
- I also treated the local structured prior as background context, not web evidence, so I did not re-fetch any of the structured datasets already supplied.

**Remaining gaps**
- The main gap is direct internal evidence of Erdoğan’s private decisions on torture, policing, prosecutions, or investigation follow-through.
- There is still limited public evidence on whether complaints against police, prison staff, or prosecutors produced meaningful discipline in 2022.
- The public record is also thin on incident-level proof of direct incitement causation versus broader stigmatizing rhetoric.
- More search could help on parliamentary oversight, judicial outcomes, and any administrative remedy in the women/LGBT/press cases, but it is unlikely to overturn the basic picture: severe and recurring repression with a few real protective exceptions, not mass terror.
