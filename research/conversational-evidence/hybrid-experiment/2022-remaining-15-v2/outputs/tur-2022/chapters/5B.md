**Search and Rejection Summary**
- I reviewed roughly 25 to 30 documents across World Bank, OECD, IMF, CBRT, Reuters wire mirrors, and a few Turkish and regional mirrors.
- I retained the sources that best distinguish target-year policy, implementation, and consequences: low-rate monetary policy, inflation and lira collapse, household impacts, wage relief, and appointment style.
- I rejected a number of duplicates and near-duplicates, especially Reuters syndications repeating the same Nebati or rate-cut story, because they added no new material claim.
- I rejected out-of-period materials from 2021 when they did not materially carry into 2022, unless they were needed as baseline context, and I did not bring in 2023-2024 turnaround pieces because they would blur the 2022 target period.
- I rejected general macro datasets and indicator pages already covered by the supplied local prior, so I did not re-fetch WDI, WGI, CPI, BTI, or similar structured series.
- I also rejected Turkey-Syria and other foreign-policy items from earlier chapters because they do not answer Chapter 5B unless they directly change domestic economic welfare.

**Remaining Gaps**
- The biggest gap is direct evidence on the ruler's exact 2022 appointment choices inside the economic team beyond the finance-minister case and broad central-bank governance structure.
- More target-year evidence on household median income, inflation by region, and inequality would sharpen the distribution lens.
- More direct evidence on corruption, favoritism, and politically connected credit allocation would help the 5B.5 and 5B.4 lenses, but the current dossier already shows a strong macro-instability and credibility problem.
- Additional search would likely add nuance, not reverse the basic picture: short-run growth and some relief measures coexisted with a deeply unstable, low-credibility macro policy regime.
