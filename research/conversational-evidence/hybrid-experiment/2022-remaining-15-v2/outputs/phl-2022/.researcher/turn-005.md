Search and rejection summary
I screened roughly 30 documents across official Philippine election bodies, congressional records, local investigative reporting, Reuters Institute research, press-freedom monitors, and human-rights groups. I kept the items that materially affected a 4B judgment: formal transfer, election-process legitimacy, campaign narrative control, press access, harassment, and early administration posture.

Removed or deprioritized items fell into these buckets:
- Duplicates: several articles repeated the same red-tagging, journalist access, or disinformation claims.
- Out of period: 2023 and later press-freedom or disinformation articles were informative but not 2022 target-period evidence.
- Irrelevant to 4B: foreign policy, nuclear-energy, and domestic-safety sources that do not bear on political freedom were excluded here.
- Blocked or partial access: some official pages and social posts were not cleanly fetchable; where the direct page failed, I used an accessible mirrored or reported copy instead.
- Overly generalized democracy indices: I did not re-fetch Freedom House, V-Dem, RSF, BTI, or Polity because the local prior already supplies those structured facts and the task asked not to re-fetch structured datasets.

Remaining gaps
- I did not find strong 2022 evidence that Marcos independently strengthened courts, legislatures, audit bodies, or local oversight institutions. `4B.4` remains the thinnest lens.
- I did not find direct 2022 evidence of a major institutional reform that improved political equality for excluded groups or opposition regions under Marcos personally. `4B.7` is still weak.
- The record on surveillance and digital controls is mostly indirect: campaign hostility, disinformation, and platform pressure are visible, but I did not find a Marcos-ordered surveillance or shutdown policy in 2022.
- More search would help mainly on appointment choices, formal media-policy decisions, and any concrete changes to institutional independence after the inauguration.
