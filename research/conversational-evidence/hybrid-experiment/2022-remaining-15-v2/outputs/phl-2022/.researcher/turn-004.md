Search and rejection summary
I considered roughly 30 documents across PNA, CHR, HRW, Amnesty, OHCHR, Reuters syndications, Inquirer, PCIJ, VERA Files/UP Dahas, GMA, and a few later retrospective reports. I kept the items that actually moved a 3B assessment: office/authority, early human-rights signaling, red-tagging, protest handling, ICC/accountability, and drug-war continuation.

Removed or deprioritized items fell into these buckets:
- Duplicates: multiple stories repeated the same red-tagging remarks, the same SONA protest sequence, or the same ICC non-rejoin announcement.
- Out of period: 2023-2026 Dahas retrospectives, 2024-2026 CHR inquiries, and later commentary on the same issues were useful for search but not accepted as 2022 evidence.
- Weak or off-topic: election-campaign unity pieces, foreign-policy articles, and economic stories without a direct domestic-safety nexus.
- Blocked or partial access: some PCO and Reuters/republish pages were not practical to open cleanly, so I used accessible official or syndicated copies instead.
- Over-derivation risk: I avoided turning a single protest or a single statement into multiple source units unless the claims were materially distinct.

Remaining gaps
- Direct evidence of Marcos personally ordering police, prison, or intelligence reform in 2022 is still thin. Most of the record is staffing choices, public promises, or institutional reactions.
- I did not find strong 2022 evidence that red-tagging was measurably reduced or that any new accountability mechanism had started to work by year end.
- The public record on torture, enforced disappearance, detention conditions, and remedies under Marcos in 2022 is still sparse compared with the drug-war and protest material.
- More search would help most on vulnerable-group protection, police accountability, and whether protest policing was a local anomaly or part of a broader national pattern.
