**Search and Rejection Summary**
- I screened roughly 30 documents across UN/OHCHR, Amnesty, HRW, CPJ, UNICEF, UNFPA, presidency releases, local-language reporting, and U.S. State Department materials.
- I kept sources that added distinct value on state-agent violence, protest repression, journalist intimidation, emergency-rule abuse, vulnerable-group harm, and concrete remedial steps.
- I rejected repeated coverage of the same journalist-abuse pattern when it added no new factual claim beyond a different date or place.
- I rejected purely international-conflict material that belonged in Chapter 2B unless it had a direct domestic safety consequence, and I skipped sources that only repeated the eastern war narrative without a domestic-protection angle.
- I rejected later 2023-2025 summaries unless they were only used to describe a 2022 policy or baseline; I avoided them when a contemporaneous 2022 source existed.
- I did not use the client matrix, and I did not re-fetch the structured local-prior datasets.

**Remaining Gaps**
- I still do not have direct, ruler-attributed evidence for a presidential order to arrest or abuse specific journalists, protesters, or activists; most incidents are carried out by police, military, or local administrators.
- The record is strong on repression and weak on implementation of remedies. The clearest positive items are the press-law draft, the Kishishe inquiry order, and some prosecutions cited in the State Department report, but I do not have a clean audit showing durable improvement.
- Child protection and GBV evidence is strong on harm and service demand, but weaker on whether Tshisekedi's administration measurably improved protection in 2022.
- The most valuable next search, if you want it, would be one focused pass on implementation evidence: prosecutions, disciplinary actions, or formal investigative reports on the 2022 protest deaths and police/military abuses.
