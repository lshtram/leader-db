**Office, Authority, Constraints**
- Félix Tshisekedi was the DRC’s president in 2022, in office since 24 January 2019 and elected on 30 December 2018. The constitutional frame makes him head of state, guarantor of territorial integrity, able to appoint the prime minister, convene the cabinet, promulgate laws, and name senior officers and ambassadors. See the current presidency bio and constitution: [Présidence RDC bio](https://www.presidence.cd/president/le-president), [Constitution](https://www.leganet.cd/Legislation/Constitution.htm).
- His authority was real but not unilateral. The government, led by the prime minister, defines and conducts national policy; defense/security and foreign affairs are shared domains; state of siege declarations require constitutional procedure and parliamentary involvement. Relevant text is in [Article 78, 79, 81, 83, 85, 91, 119, 144](https://www.leganet.cd/Legislation/Constitution.htm).
- In 2022 he also became chairperson of SADC, which expanded his regional platform but did not change domestic constitutional limits. The handover appears in the SADC summit materials: [SADC 42nd Summit communiqué](https://www.sadc.int/latest-news/communique-42nd-ordinary-summit-sadc-heads-state-and-government).

**Compact Event Map**
- March-April: DRC entered the East African Community, tying Tshisekedi’s agenda to regional integration and eastern-security diplomacy. Source: [Radio Okapi on accession](https://www.radiookapi.net/2022/03/30/actualite/politique/kinshasa-la-rdc-admise-au-sein-de-la-east-african-community).
- May: Tshisekedi reviewed the state of siege in North Kivu and Ituri and put its future under discussion.
- June: he publicly described the eastern war as an external aggression and kept both military and diplomatic tracks open.
- Late July: anti-MONUSCO protests in Goma and Butembo turned lethal and exposed the depth of popular anger and security breakdown.
- August: he took the SADC chair in Kinshasa, adding regional diplomacy to his presidency.
- September-November: UN and local reports show the conflict worsening, with M23 advances and regional mediation intensifying.
- December: IMF still saw growth, but only alongside inflation, humanitarian pressure, and eastern insecurity.
