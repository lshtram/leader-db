**Search and rejection summary**
I reviewed roughly 30 documents across Reuters/Euronews, ENA, UN/UNHCR, HRW, Amnesty, CPJ, AP-linked mirrors, and later country-report summaries.

What I kept:
- Primary or near-primary records for office decisions, prisoner releases, emergency law, refugee protection, and rights abuses.
- Independent monitors for press freedom, detention, accountability, and Western Tigray abuses.
- Year-end summaries only when they added a materially distinct 2022 claim.

What I rejected:
- Multiple Reuters/CPJ articles that repeated the same May 2022 media crackdown without adding a new fact.
- Later 2024-2025 Ethiopia pages on evictions or later state-of-emergency episodes, because they were out of scope for 2022.
- Broad country pages or opinion pieces that added no Ethiopia-specific 2022 evidence.
- Social-media mirrors, headline-only pages, and duplicate reposts of the same wire item.
- Non-2022 refugee or rights material unless it clearly served as inherited-baseline context.

**Remaining gaps**
- Direct ruler-level attribution is still thin for many abuses. The record often shows federal, regional, or allied-actor conduct, but not Abiy Ahmed’s personal order chain.
- The hardest unresolved question is how much of the 2022 detention and violence pattern was centrally directed versus tolerated or only partially controlled.
- The evidence is strong on recurring arrests, abusive detention, media suppression, and conflict-linked civilian harm, but weaker on complete detention totals, all release dates, and internal accountability outcomes.
- More search would help most in Ethiopian-language court records, EHRC releases, and contemporaneous ENA/official statements on the May arrests, Tigray detentions, and the implementation of any promised investigations.
