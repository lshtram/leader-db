**Search and Rejection Summary**
- I screened roughly 30 documents across the White House archive, Commerce, CMS/HHS, Treasury, DOT, BEA, BLS, Census, USDA, CBO, and Reuters.
- Kept the clearest program-to-implementation-to-outcome chain: Biden’s economic framing, CHIPS staffing and steering, IRA rollout, and independent macro/household outcome data.
- Rejected or did not retain out-of-period 2023-2024 retrospectives, duplicate syndications of the same Reuters stories, and broad rhetorical material that repeated the same “middle out / bottom up” theme without new implementation evidence.
- A direct Commerce implementation-strategy page was search-discovered but blocked on open; I relied instead on the accessible Commerce leadership release and the CHIPS steering-council readout.
- I did not reuse general integrity evidence from 7B because it does not bear directly on effectiveness.

**Remaining Gaps**
- Stronger evidence would be agency audits or award-level records showing realized CHIPS and IRA outputs, not just staffing and rollout architecture.
- Course-correction evidence is thin: I found planning, coordination, and ready-to-implement language, but little direct proof of replacing ineffective implementers or materially reallocating resources in response to failure during 2022.
- Macro outcomes are only partly attributable to Biden. Inflation, real earnings, food insecurity, and household income were shaped heavily by the Fed, global energy and supply shocks, and post-pandemic normalization, so causal attribution remains mixed.
