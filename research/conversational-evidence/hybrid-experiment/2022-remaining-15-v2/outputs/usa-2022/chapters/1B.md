**Exposure / Authority**
Joe Biden had material nuclear authority in 2022. He could set declaratory policy, direct treaty diplomacy, authorize force posture decisions through the executive branch, and shape crisis signaling. The main constraints were the inherited U.S. arsenal and doctrine, allied coordination, Congress after the midterms, the Pentagon/bureaucratic process, and Russia’s Ukraine-war nuclear coercion.

None.

**Search and rejection summary**
I screened roughly 30 documents across APP, State, DoD, IAEA, Arms Control Association, AP, Reuters, and Gallup. I rejected or deprioritized:
- Duplicate official statements that repeated the same arms-control language without adding new factual content.
- Out-of-period items from 2021 or 2023+ unless they were only background context, which I did not promote into final evidence.
- Broad domestic-political or inflation articles that did not speak to nuclear authority, arms control, command discipline, or crisis management.
- General commentary on Ukraine, Congress, or approval ratings that was not specific enough to this chapter.
- Later retrospective articles about Russia’s doctrine or U.S. aid that were outside 2022 or mostly summarized the same nuclear-safety crisis already covered by the 2022 IAEA and State Department material.
- Repetitive IAEA updates on Zaporizhzhia where one clear 2022 update was enough to establish the nuclear-safety crisis.

**Remaining gaps**
- I did not find a 2022 presidential-level document showing internal command-and-control or custody changes beyond declaratory policy language.
- I found no strong 2022 Biden-specific evidence on AI, bio, or other non-nuclear catastrophic-risk choices that clearly belonged in 1B.9.
- Public evidence on crisis de-escalation is mostly declaratory and diplomatic; I do not have declassified backchannel records.
- The record is strong on arms control intent and Ukraine crisis management, but weaker on whether those actions materially improved the end-of-2022 existential-risk posture.
