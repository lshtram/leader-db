I reviewed roughly 30 documents across Treasury, BEA, BLS, Census, CBO, IMF, Commerce, DOT, IRS, and White House / APP materials. I kept the most decision-relevant, non-duplicative sources that separately covered fiscal stewardship, growth, inflation, wages, labor markets, industrial policy, and distributional outcomes.

I rejected or merged material in these buckets:
- Duplicates or near-duplicates: multiple White House op-eds, fact sheets, and signing remarks that repeated the same IRA, CHIPS, or infrastructure claims without adding a distinct factual proposition.
- Out-of-period or later retrospectives that were only useful if they reported 2022 facts already captured by official 2022/2023 releases.
- Broad institutional pages such as IRS IRA overview pages, Commerce CHIPS landing pages, and White House category pages that were useful as discovery but not as the best discrete evidence item.
- News noise and unrelated 2025-2026 pages that surfaced in search but had no relevance to Biden’s 2022 economic record.
- Weak attribution items where the link to Biden was too indirect or the claim was just general optimism without a concrete economic mechanism.
- Macro claims from government sources that were too self-described or too aspirational when a stronger statistical or budget source existed.

Remaining gaps:
