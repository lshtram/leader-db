Search and rejection summary:
I screened roughly 30 documents across CMS, HHS, HUD, USDA, FDA, Education, Census, ERS, and White House / APP materials, plus a few later retrospective pages used only when they reported 2022 facts more cleanly than contemporaneous press releases.

I rejected or merged material in these buckets:
- Duplicate or near-duplicate coverage of the same policy, especially repeated CMS or White House summaries of ACA, IRA, or student-debt actions.
- Out-of-period or overly remote pages that did not add a distinct 2022 fact beyond the official releases already used.
- Broad landing pages and program homepages that were useful for discovery but not as the best discrete evidence item.
- Weakly attributable claims that were just generic praise, aspiration, or institutional branding without a concrete welfare mechanism.
- Macro outcome pages that were informative but redundant once I had direct Census, USDA, CMS, HUD, and FDA evidence.
- Non-target topics that surfaced in search but did not materially bear on social well-being or human development in 2022.

Remaining gaps:
- Direct evidence on 6B.4 is still thinner than the policy-and-outcome record: I have strong proof of agency-led implementation, but less on the quality of appointments and internal professional capacity choices.
- Better independent evaluation of how much 2022 policies changed actual household welfare, especially for low-income groups, would sharpen attribution.
- More subnational evidence would help on uneven state uptake of postpartum coverage, vouchers, nutrition programs, and student-debt relief.
- The record is mixed on outcomes: health coverage and targeted supports improved, but poverty, food insecurity, and real purchasing power remained under pressure, so longer-run durability is still not settled.
