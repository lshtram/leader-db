**Search and rejection summary**
I considered roughly 30 documents across the White House, State, Treasury, APP, UN, ICRC/HRW, Reuters, and AP-adjacent reporting. I rejected or deprioritized:
- 2023+ and later retrospective pieces that were outside the target year or too hindsight-driven.
- Repeated State Department briefings that said the same thing about Ukraine support without adding new factual content.
- Several Reuters/AP items that repeated the same no-fly-zone or regime-change clarification without adding distinct evidence.
- Broad commentary on inflation, domestic politics, or polling that did not bear on international peace, lawful security, or proxy restraint.
- Technical-difficulty pages from archived State URLs where the search snippet already captured the needed claim.
- Background material on 2021 diplomacy when it added no 2022-specific discrimination value.
- Some older/secondary discussions of the same Ukraine facts because the UN, G7, Treasury, and Biden transcripts already established the same point more directly.

**Remaining gaps**
- I still do not have a clean 2022 primary source showing internal U.S. controls over Ukrainian operational choices beyond the public territorial-use limits.
- There is little public evidence on Biden’s private bargaining with Russia, so 2B.7 is strong on posture but weaker on negotiated outcome.
- I have good evidence that U.S. support was defensive and law-based, but less direct evidence on civilian-protection review inside the weapons pipeline.
- The record is strong on accountability rhetoric and sanctions, but weaker on whether those measures measurably shortened the war or improved the end-of-2022 peace posture.
- If you want a tighter 2B dossier, the next useful search would be for primary records on U.S.-Ukraine weapons-use restrictions, Black Sea grain diplomacy, and any 2022 declassified or congressional accountability material.
