**Search and rejection summary**
- I considered roughly 30 documents across Indonesian official election bodies, presidential/cabinet statements, Reuters-fed reporting, Freedom House, Amnesty, the Library of Congress legal monitor, and a few 2023-2024 follow-ups used only when they clarified the 2022 baseline.
- I kept the core 2022 sources that added distinct claims on term-limit pressure, election scheduling, protest response, media/civic restrictions, digital controls, and group equality.
- I rejected duplicates that repeated the same April 2022 term-extension episode without adding new factual content.
- I rejected later 2023-2024 election-law stories and court disputes when they were not needed to explain the 2022 period.
- I rejected off-topic economic and general governance pages that did not materially bear on political freedom, term limits, media space, or surveillance.
- I treated the 2023 Freedom House reports as acceptable because they explicitly cover June 2022-May 2023 or key 2022 developments, rather than using them as generic post-window retrospectives.

**Remaining gaps**
- Direct evidence on Jokowi personally strengthening independent courts, legislatures, audit bodies, or local oversight in 2022 is thin. The record is stronger on preserving the election timetable than on active institution-building.
- The clearest 2022 evidence is mixed: Jokowi publicly rejected election delay and term extension talk, but his administration still presided over a tighter speech environment and a new criminal code with broader speech offenses.
- There is still limited direct evidence on 4B.5, especially on whether Jokowi actively resisted politicization of neutral state institutions beyond his public rejection of tenure extension.
- More search could refine attribution around the digital-control measures and internal cabinet dynamics, but it is unlikely to overturn the main picture already visible here.
