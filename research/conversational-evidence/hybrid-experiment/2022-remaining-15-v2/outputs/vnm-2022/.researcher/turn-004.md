**Search and rejection summary**
- I screened roughly 30 documents across HRW, Amnesty, Freedom House, State Department, RSF, OHCHR/UN, Reuters-syndicated reporting, Vietnamese government briefings, and a few general background pages.
- I kept sources that materially changed the 3B picture: the recurring use of arbitrary arrest, prison abuse, house arrest, speech repression, cyber controls, and the handling of high-profile activist cases.
- I rejected a number of duplicate or near-duplicate reports on the same Pham Doan Trang, Le Van Dung, Le Anh Hung, and Nguy Thi Khanh cases when they added no new 2022 facts or attribution.
- I rejected older pre-2022 background items unless they helped establish the inherited baseline for movement restrictions or press control.
- I also excluded generic economy, foreign-policy, and anti-corruption items unless they directly touched physical safety, detention, or protest repression.
- A few direct URLs were awkward to open, so I used the surfaced snippets or an equivalent higher-quality mirror when the claim was the same.

**Remaining gaps**
- I did not find credible evidence of personal Trọng incitement to ethnic, religious, or communal violence in 2022.
- I did not find a clean ruler-specific due-process or oversight reform that would materially offset the repression record.
- The record is severe but not mass-terror: it looks like recurring, state-aligned repression with selective impunity, not national-scale exterminatory violence.
- More search would probably only help if you want tighter evidence on 3B.3 or 3B.4, or if you want to trace specific police-command responsibility behind individual arrests and prison abuse.
