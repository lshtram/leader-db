**Search and rejection summary**
- I considered roughly 30 documents across constitutional texts, Freedom House, State Department, RSF, HRW, Amnesty, Reuters, and Vietnamese state media.
- I retained the sources that directly speak to the chapter: party supremacy, noncompetitive elections, independent media suppression, digital controls, and term-limit/succession context.
- I rejected duplicate mirrors of the same Reuters reports, plus generic anti-corruption or economic items that do not materially bear on political freedom.
- I set aside broader human-rights case reports already used in Chapter 3B when they did not add new 4B-specific evidence beyond repression generally.
- A few pages were useful only as snippets or syndication copies; I kept the best accessible version with the same underlying Reuters claim.
- I did not use the client matrix.

**Remaining gaps**
- Direct public evidence of the internal 2022 decision path is thin: most of the record is structural, not a transcript of Trọng personally directing repression.
- The strongest positive evidence is formal, not substantive: the constitution says the CPV is accountable and the state has legal procedures, but the empirical record shows those constraints were weak.
- I did not find any credible 2022 evidence of opposition victories, real alternation, or an independent legislature checking the CPV.
- More search would mainly help if you want internal party minutes, succession documents, or closed judicial/disciplinary records. Publicly, the 2022 baseline already looks saturated and consistently authoritarian.
