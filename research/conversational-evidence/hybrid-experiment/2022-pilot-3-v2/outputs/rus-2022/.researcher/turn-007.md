- Out-of-period material used only as baseline context, such as 2020 Russia COVID social-protection and learning-gap briefs.
- Global or non-Russia-specific UNICEF/WHO/World Bank reports when they did not materially help Russia 2022 attribution.
- Ukraine-war civilian-harm evidence from other chapters, because it belongs to battlefield or domestic-violence chapters unless it directly translated into Russian domestic welfare effects.
- Direct opens to some Kremlin and Rosstat pages timed out, so I relied on the stable search-result text where necessary.
- The strongest direct Putin-specific 2022 welfare action I found was the April 10 socioeconomic support instructions. The strongest negative 2022 welfare evidence was the war/mobilization-driven hit to incomes, consumer demand, poverty, and the fiscal position.

**Remaining Gaps**
- Direct 2022 evidence on housing affordability, food security, and unmet medical need inside Russia is still thin.
- I do not have strong independent household-level evidence on whether the April 2022 support package was implemented evenly across regions and groups.
- Education quality and learning-loss evidence specific to Russia in 2022 is mostly baseline/contextual, not a clean Putin-period outcome.
- More search would help on distributional effects by region, disability, and poorer households, and on whether the 2022 social support measures were actually received rather than merely announced.
