**Search and rejection summary**
- I considered roughly 30 distinct documents across Kremlin transcripts, Reuters wires, Euronews Reuters syndications, UN/OHCHR updates, Amnesty reports, Meduza reporting, and some auxiliary diplomatic pieces.
- I rejected duplicate rewrites of the same core events, especially multiple versions of the Feb. 24 invasion address, the Sept. 21 nuclear-mobilization speech, and the annexation ceremony.
- I rejected out-of-period or drift-heavy material, including 2023-2024 retrospectives, later nuclear doctrine changes, and follow-on commentary that did not add a distinct 2022 fact.
- I rejected weak or merely derivative pieces that repeated Reuters without adding a cleaner claim or a better locator.
- I kept the set focused on distinct chapter-relevant facts: initiation of the war, the narrow peace-talk window, tactical de-escalation near Kyiv, civilian-harm evidence, grain-export diplomacy, mobilization and annexation escalation, and formal annexation ratification.

**Remaining gaps**
- The weakest area is 2B.5: direct evidence on Putin's personal control over proxies and whether he restrained them is still thinner than the evidence on invasion, annexation, and civilian harm.
- There is also limited direct evidence on private decision-making around ceasefires beyond public talk and tactical pauses.
- Better evidence on who inside the Kremlin shaped the March 2022 negotiation posture would help, but it would likely change confidence more than the overall direction of the chapter record.
- More search would probably not change the core conclusion that 2022 was an aggressive-war and annexation year; it would mostly refine attribution and confidence.
