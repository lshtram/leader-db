- I considered roughly 30 documents across official Russian legal texts, CEC-related materials, Golos election-monitoring reports, EPDE, OVD-Info, Amnesty, Human Rights Watch, CPJ, Reuters wires, and Council of Europe notices.
- I rejected several duplicates of the same themes, especially repeated reports on the same March 2022 censorship package, the same September 2022 Novaya Gazeta closure, and the same foreign-agents law in different outlets.
- I rejected out-of-period material from 2021 on Memorial's shutdown as direct 2022 evidence, because 2022 sources already documented the continuing crackdown more cleanly.
- I rejected broad secondary explainers when they added no materially distinct claim beyond the primary legal or monitoring sources.
- I also rejected some later 2023-2026 stories about Putin challengers and regional elections because they were outside the target period and not needed to establish the 2022 pattern.

**Remaining gaps**
- Direct 2022 evidence on internal court independence and elite contestation remains thin; the record shows erosion and remedy removal more clearly than it shows any positive institution-building.
- I did not find strong 2022 evidence of Putin respecting or strengthening succession rules. The best evidence is actually the inherited 2020 reset that made long-term continuation possible.
- There is limited ruler-specific evidence on whether Putin personally directed individual censorship or candidate exclusions, but the legal and administrative system is clearly attributable to his government.
- More search would probably refine attribution and mechanics, but it is unlikely to change the main chapter picture: 2022 further entrenched an already authoritarian system, especially through war-linked repression, media closure, and electoral narrowing.
