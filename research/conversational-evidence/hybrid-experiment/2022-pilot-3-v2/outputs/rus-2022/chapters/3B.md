**Search and rejection summary**
- I considered roughly 30 distinct documents across Amnesty, HRW, OVD-Info, Meduza, Reuters syndications, RFE/RL, Freedom House, the U.S. State Department, the UN Human Rights Committee/UNHCR materials, DW, and a few lower-value mirrors and explainers.
- I rejected duplicates of the same core episodes, especially multiple rewrites of the March war-censorship law, the September mobilization protests, and the December LGBT law.
- I rejected out-of-period material from 2021 and 2023-2026 unless it was clearly summarizing 2022 and added real comparative value.
- I rejected a number of broad annual reports when they repeated the same facts already covered by sharper, better-placed sources.
- I kept the set focused on distinct domestic-safety mechanisms: censorship, protest repression, civil-society shutdowns, legal-remedy removal, police coercion, torture/prison abuse, and minority targeting.

**Remaining gaps**
- Direct ruler-level evidence on whether Putin personally ordered specific beatings, torture cases, or prison abuses remains thin.
- Evidence on internal accountability is mostly negative: we can see blocked remedies and a few announced investigations, but not credible, consistent punishment of abusers.
- Chechnya-specific abuses are strong evidence of domestic danger inside Russia, but the federal command chain is still only partially documented in the public record.
- More search would help mainly on command attribution and remedy quality; it is unlikely to change the overall conclusion that 2022 was a year of systematic domestic repression rather than isolated misconduct.
