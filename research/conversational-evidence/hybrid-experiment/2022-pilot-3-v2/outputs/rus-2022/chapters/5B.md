**Search and Rejection Summary**
I reviewed roughly 30 documents across IMF, World Bank, Bank of Russia, Rosstat, Reuters/Euronews syndications, and a few legal/analysis pages.

Accepted because they added distinct ruler-linked economic evidence:
- Technocratic staffing and monetary credibility: Reuters on Nabiullina, Bank of Russia rate decisions, IMF press briefing, Bank of Russia annual-report speech.
- Property-rights and capital-control coercion: Reuters on foreign-asset restrictions, external administration, and rouble-for-gas decree.
- Wartime fiscal and welfare effects: Reuters on defence spending, budget reallocation, deficit, inflation, and household-income contraction.

Rejected as duplicates or lower-value overlap:
- Reuters/Euronews versions of the same rouble-payment and sanctions stories when they repeated the same decree without a materially new claim.
- The Reuters 7.5% rate-cut story and the Euronews Reuters item on relaxing non-resident curbs, which overlapped the emergency-rate/capital-control theme already captured by stronger CBR and IMF evidence.
- Later 2023 corporate-exit / asset-sale follow-ups when they did not add materially new 2022-period information beyond the March asset-control and nationalization measures.
- Broad World Bank and IMF global outlook pages when they did not add Russia-specific ruler attribution beyond context already captured by Russia-specific releases.
- Older baseline reports from before 2022 when they were useful only as background and not as target-period evidence.

**Remaining Gaps**
- Direct evidence on whether Putin personally chose the technocratic response versus simply tolerating it. The Nabiullina coverage is strong, but some attribution remains indirect because the central bank operated through its own procedures.
- Better evidence on regional and household distribution inside Russia in 2022, especially who absorbed the losses and who benefited from subsidies, defense contracts, or energy windfalls.
- More granular evidence separating policy skill from external windfalls and sanctions compression, especially for the apparent short-run macro stabilization.
- Stronger direct evidence on productivity investment beyond the negative budget reallocation story, especially education, health, infrastructure, and innovation execution rather than appropriations alone.

More search would help on the first two gaps; the third and fourth are already directionally clear but still worth tightening if you want a more defensible later judge packet.
