Bolsonaro’s office in 2022 was president of Brazil, the constitutionally defined head of the federal executive. He entered the year as an incumbent running for reelection, and his formal term ran through 31 December 2022. His core powers were the standard presidential ones in the 1988 Constitution, especially appointing and dismissing ministers, directing the federal administration, initiating legislation, issuing decrees, and vetoing bills. His main constraints in 2022 were also constitutional and institutional: Brazil’s direct two-round electoral system, the one immediate reelection limit, congressional permission requirements for long absences, and active judicial/electoral oversight from the TSE and STF.

In brief: Brazil in 2022 had low interstate/state-based conflict but nontrivial one-sided violence and repression, middling-to-weak governance and corruption indicators, and a mixed social/economic baseline with recovery from the pandemic but persistent food insecurity and institutional strain.
