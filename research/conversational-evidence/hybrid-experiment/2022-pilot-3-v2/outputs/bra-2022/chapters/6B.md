**Search and rejection summary**
I screened roughly 30 documents across IBGE, UNICEF, World Bank, Planalto/gov.br, MDS/MEC/MS/Anatel, and a small amount of Reuters-syndicated reporting.

Accepted sources were kept only when they added a distinct claim, not a paraphrase:
- Child poverty, school exclusion, food insecurity, and housing/sanitation from UNICEF, World Bank, and IBGE.
- Direct Bolsonaro-era welfare policy actions on cash transfers, conditionalities, education internet access, vaccination, indigenous health, and housing.
- Administrative correction and targeting evidence for Auxílio Brasil.

Rejected material fell into these buckets:
- Purely promotional or duplicative government pages that repeated the same Auxílio Brasil or Internet Brasil claim without adding a new factual unit.
- General 2023-2025 background pages that did not add a 2022-specific claim.
- Macroeconomic or electoral pieces already handled in 5B/4B and not directly tied to social welfare delivery.
- Out-of-period material unless it was clearly retrospective about 2022 and added a new fact.
- Weak or redundant copies of the same source claim across multiple mirrors.

**Remaining gaps**
- I still do not have a strong independent evaluation of the quality and reach of Bolsonaro-era health and education delivery at the subnational level. The dossier has policy and some outcome evidence, but not a full implementation audit.
- Better independent evidence on racial and regional distribution within 2022 social-service delivery would strengthen 6B.3 and 6B.8.
- The evidence on whether social programs were used as political loyalty tools is suggestive but not definitive; more granular beneficiary-flow or audit evidence would help.
- A direct independent evaluation of 2022 school connectivity and health campaign take-up would improve confidence on 6B.2 and 6B.5.
