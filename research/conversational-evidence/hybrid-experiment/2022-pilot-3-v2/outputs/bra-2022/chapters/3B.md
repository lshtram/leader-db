I considered roughly 30 documents across Amnesty, Human Rights Watch, Reuters and Reuters-wrapped coverage, Brazilian court reporting, and election- and Indigenous-rights material. The retained set is intentionally narrow and biased toward direct 2022 ruler conduct.

I rejected or deprioritized:
- 2021-only background pages that did not add 2022 ruler-period information.
- Duplicate rewrites of the same Reuters story when they added no new claim.
- General democracy or corruption pieces that were not tightly linked to physical safety, intimidation, or protection.
- Country overview pages that described Brazil’s broader rights environment without adding Bolsonaro-specific 2022 conduct.
- Retrospective 2023-2025 material where the 2022 facts were already captured by stronger contemporaneous sources.
- Items about purely state- or municipal-level abuse when I could not tie them to Bolsonaro’s feasible command, tolerance, or policy choices.

**Remaining gaps**

- Federal attribution is still imperfect for police violence: many killings and abuses are state-level, so Bolsonaro’s responsibility is indirect unless tied to policy, appointment, or tolerance.
- I did not find a single document proving direct presidential orders for specific domestic abuses in 2022. The stronger case is cumulative: incitement, weakening of protections, and hostile public framing.
- More search could still help on gender- and LGBTQ-specific protection failures, especially direct 2022 Bolsonaro rhetoric, but it is unlikely to change the overall direction of the record.
- The broad shape is already clear: recurring intimidation and protection failures, plus meaningful but incomplete institutional resistance from courts and civil society.
