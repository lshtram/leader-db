**Search and rejection summary**
I screened roughly 30 documents across Reuters/UOL mirrors, Folha, official court and tribunal pages, Petrobras/CVM reporting, and the Receita/Federal Police record.

Retained sources were chosen because they added distinct integrity claims:
- repeated false statements and conditional acceptance of the vote;
- attacks on journalists and investigators;
- close-family use of state machinery to blunt scrutiny;
- cash property transactions and family-finance investigations;
- politically pressured Petrobras appointments.

Rejected material fell into these buckets:
- duplicate mirrors of the same Reuters story;
- generic 2022 election-content moderation and fact-check pages that were useful as context but did not add a Bolsonaro-specific personal-integrity claim beyond the Reuters items already retained;
- broader corruption indices and country-level rule-of-law metrics, which I treated as context only and did not convert into personal-integrity evidence;
- out-of-period 2023-2026 explainers unless they directly described a 2022 act;
- general governance or policy pieces that did not show a personal nexus to Bolsonaro, his family, or his close governing network.

**Remaining gaps**
- I still lack a clean official asset-declaration trail for Bolsonaro himself in 2022. The family-property reporting is strong, but a direct declaration/audit comparison would tighten 7B.3 and 7B.4.
- The dossier is stronger on deception and scrutiny attacks than on self-enrichment findings. More independent audit or court findings would materially help.
- Appointment evidence is concentrated in Petrobras. Broader cabinet and agency appointment records would better test 7B.5 and 7B.9.
- There is still some attribution risk on family-network conduct: it is relevant for this chapter, but more evidence linking Jair Bolsonaro personally to the family-finance responses would improve confidence.
