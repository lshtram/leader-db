**Search and rejection summary**
I screened roughly 30 documents across IBGE, BCB, World Bank, Treasury, Planalto/gov.br, Reuters/UOL, IMF, and PPI pages.

Retained sources were chosen because they added distinct evidence rather than repeating the same macro point:
- Official macro series from IBGE for growth, labor, and inflation.
- Central-bank minutes and annual report for inflation-target breach and policy reaction.
- World Bank material for household welfare, food insecurity, and inflation transmission.
- Treasury and Planalto/gov.br releases for enacted fiscal/investment measures.
- Reuters syndications for election-year spending, fuel-tax framing, and scapegoating rhetoric.

Rejected material fell into these buckets:
- Duplicate Reuters republications of the same story from multiple outlets.
- Generic 2023/2024 Brazil macro summaries that did not add new 2022-specific facts.
- Purely political coverage with no economic-policy content.
- Forward-looking or promotional government pages that stated policy intent but did not add a distinct 2022 implementation or outcome claim.
- Post-2022 coup / election-denial coverage that was outside the target year unless explicitly used only as retrospective context, which I did not rely on here.

**Remaining gaps**
- 5B.2 is still only partially covered. I have one strong direct delegation measure for Paulo Guedes, but not a full appointment/performance audit of the economic team.
- 5B.5 is still thinner than the macro sections. I have evidence of sectoral privileges and election-year spending, but not a deep anti-corruption or capture record for the economic portfolio.
- 5B.8 would benefit from more household-distribution detail by region, class, gender, and race beyond the World Bank’s food-insecurity and poverty material.
- More search on implementation quality for the infrastructure and privatization programs would help separate real productivity gains from announcement-heavy policy.
