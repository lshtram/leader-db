**Search And Rejection Summary**
I reviewed roughly 30 documents across PIB ministry year-end reviews, scheme pages, World Bank, WHO, UNICEF, CAG, World Bank data platforms, ASER, and major reporting. The retained set intentionally favors exact 2022 administrative counts and independent outcome data over generic program descriptions.

Accepted and retained:
- Jal Jeevan Mission year-end review and mission pages.
- PM POSHAN approval note.
- PM eVIDYA / DIKSHA usage note.
- UDISE+ 2021-22 school-enrolment release.
- Women and Child Development year-end review.
- Rural Development year-end review.
- PM-KISAN implementation note.
- World Bank health-sector loan release.
- CAG PM-JAY audit.
- World Bank PIP country profile.
- ASER 2022 reporting.
- UNICEF child-SDG country page.

Considered but rejected as duplicates or too weak:
- Several 2023 and 2024 landing pages that repeated 2022 scheme descriptions without new 2022 evidence.
- Broad country pages and generic world reports that were too context-heavy and not India- or 2022-specific enough.
- Subnational welfare stories that could not be safely generalized to national ruler attribution.
- Repetitive PIB releases on the same program when a stronger, more specific year-end review already captured the key 2022 counts.

**Remaining Gaps**
- Independent 2022 outcome evaluations remain thin for whether PM POSHAN, PM-JAY, PM-KISAN, and JJM actually reduced deprivation among the poorest versus merely expanding nominal coverage.
- Distributional evidence by caste, tribe, district, and gender is still incomplete at the national level.
- Learning-quality evidence is still mostly negative or descriptive; more direct 2022 evaluation of school-learning recovery would help.
- There is still limited independent proof that the national schemes were consistently implemented without state-level exclusion or leakage.
