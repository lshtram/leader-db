**Search and Rejection Summary**
I reviewed roughly 30 documents across UN vote records, PMO/PIB bilateral statements, India-China border releases, evacuation notices, and a smaller set of Reuters-syndicated or later retrospective pages.

Retained because they were directly dated to 2022 and tied to actual ruler conduct:
- UN Security Council and General Assembly Ukraine votes and India’s explanations.
- Modi’s direct calls with Macron, Zelenskyy, and bilateral statements with the UK and the US.
- Operation Ganga evacuation material, including the Hindi PIB release.
- India-China border talks in March and December 2022.

Rejected or not used:
- Later retrospectives from 2023-2025 that described the same Ukraine or border posture but were out of period for this chapter.
- Generic diplomatic pages that repeated “peace” language without a dated decision, call, vote, or operational action.
- Older inherited-context India-China documents from before 2022 that were useful background but not target-period evidence.
- Duplicate evacuation notices and duplicate PMO summaries that restated the same Operation Ganga facts without adding a materially distinct claim.
- Economic or trade reporting on Russia-India ties that was relevant to context but not directly to international peace conduct.

**Remaining Gaps**
- Public evidence is still thin on internal decision-making: we can see the votes, calls, and releases, but not the cabinet-level reasoning behind each abstention or statement.
- There is limited direct public evidence on how much Modi personally controlled the India-China border posture beyond the official releases.
- I did not find a strong 2022 source showing a consequential ceasefire negotiation that Modi personally advanced to completion, so the record is one of restraint and diplomacy rather than settlement success.
