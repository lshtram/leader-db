### Chapter-level continuation results

1B — Nuclear and catastrophic risk

New material improves nuclear-facility exposure, command signaling, treaty restraint, and dual-use procurement. E021 documents operational facility-risk mechanisms; E022 adds both escalation evidence and mitigating pre-war arms-control context; E023 records routine treaty-compliant notification; E034 supplies a formal dual-use procurement case.

Remaining gaps: internal Russian safeguards, expert deliberations, chain-of-command records, and independent evidence about implementation of nuclear-safety measures. Nuclear-facility evidence remains largely external and does not establish a personal order by Putin for each operational risk.

2B — Peace, conflict, and international harm

E039–E041 add remedy, diplomacy, and limited negotiated cooperation. The ICRC source is a direct remedy gap; the UN grain initiative is concrete contrary/mitigating evidence; UN diplomatic records establish alternatives and external calls for restraint. E047 strengthens attribution for the recognition decision.

Remaining gaps: authentic internal deliberations, proxy/subordinate control, evidence testing official counterclaims, and whether any remedy was implemented beyond discrete agreements. The source set distinguishes ICJ orders, ICC warrants, commission findings, indictments, official claims, and diplomatic agreements.

3B — Domestic safety and physical integrity

E024–E027 materially improve national repression, detention violence, protest treatment, vulnerable-group evidence, and the international accountability environment. OVD-Info explicitly warns that reporting volume reflects observer access, while its closed-system limitations remain significant.

Remaining gaps: prison-wide conditions and remedies, national prevalence denominators, direct presidential incitement or authorization beyond formal authority, and independent evidence from inside Russia. Foreign battlefield conduct remains excluded from 3B unless a direct domestic nexus is shown.

4B — Political freedom

E028–E030 provide target-period election administration and contestability evidence, including a Russian-language domestic monitoring source and explicit reporting-volume caveats. The OSCE search also confirmed that its accessible Russia election archive is primarily inherited 2016–2021 material, not a 2022 observation mission.

Remaining gaps: 2022 succession and appointment records, independent institutional accountability outcomes, and a complete target-period official/contrary audit. E019 remains inherited baseline evidence only.

5B — Economic wellbeing

E035–E036 and E048 add independent macro context plus institutional crisis-response evidence from the Central Bank. E045 is a useful official household-poverty lead but remains discovery-only pending precise table extraction. E032 and E034 provide capture/procurement context but do not establish personal economic benefit from office.

Remaining gaps: household distribution, regional effects, implementation audits, procurement enrichment findings tied directly to Putin, and documented correction of policy errors. GDP contraction and financial stabilization are preserved as outcomes/context, not ruler credit or blame by themselves.

6B — Social wellbeing

E037–E043 add WHO data-quality warnings, UNICEF water/sanitation and child-poverty indicators, a health-system archive, and one target-period health-system pilot. These improve service-domain coverage and explicit uncertainty but remain mostly country-level or institutional rather than ruler-attributed.

Remaining gaps: affordability, subgroup and regional reach, delivery quality, politicization, independent mortality reporting, crisis correction, and durability. No absence of evidence is treated as evidence of poor performance.

7B — Personal integrity

This chapter received the most material improvement. E030–E033, E044, E046, and E047 add investigative, sanctions, Russian-language, official-transparency, and decision-chain sources. They establish a credible personal/network nexus for wealth concealment allegations, Putin-linked assets, associate networks, and reduced public disclosure.

Limits remain decisive: E031/E044 are investigative allegations; E032/E033 are sanctions findings rather than criminal judgments; associate enrichment is not automatically Putin’s personal enrichment; E046 shows opacity but not concealment motive. Positive evidence is limited to official declarations and denials, which remain weakly probative in a closed environment.

8B — Effectiveness and competence

E034, E036, E040, E043, and E048 add implementation and adaptation evidence: dual-use procurement execution, Central Bank stabilization actions, negotiated grain operations, a health-program pilot, and new settlement infrastructure. E025 provides a target-period mobilization implementation record, though it is adverse and focuses on coercive execution.

Remaining gaps: independent audits, bureaucratic competence, subnational delivery, correction after failure, and durable progress across the complete program portfolio. Official instructions remain insufficient without independent implementation evidence.
