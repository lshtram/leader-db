## Reconnaissance and authority baseline

Erdoğan was Türkiye’s executive president throughout 2022. The Constitutional Court describes the post-2017 system as vesting executive power solely in the president and authorizing presidential decrees and appointments of senior executives and ministries. Presidential authority was therefore materially relevant to national policy, appointments, security operations, economic policy, and treaty positions, subject to constitutional review, parliament, coalition politics, NATO obligations, courts, and external economic constraints. [Constitutional Court](https://anayasa.gov.tr/en/leading-judgments/decision-concerning-the-general-principles-of-constitutional-review-of-presidential-decrees-e201931-k20205/)

The information environment was highly constrained but not fully closed: independent online media, NGOs, international observers, courts, and external monitors remained active, while government-linked media ownership, prosecutions, content blocking, press-card controls, protest bans, and self-censorship materially limited visibility. The OSCE later documented selective enforcement of the 2022 disinformation law, criminal cases for presidential insult, government-linked advertising advantages, and pressure on journalists. [OSCE/ODIHR](https://odihr.osce.org/sites/default/files/f/documents/1/f/553966.pdf), [Human Rights Watch](https://www.hrw.org/world-report/2023/country-chapters/turkey), [Freedom House](https://freedomhouse.org/country/turkey/freedom-world/2022)
