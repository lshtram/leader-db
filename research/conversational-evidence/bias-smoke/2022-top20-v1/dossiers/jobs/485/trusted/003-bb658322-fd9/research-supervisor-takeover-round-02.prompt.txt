## Chapter yield and gaps

### 1B

Evidence yield: E002–E003, plus local `not_available`.

Supported themes: non-nuclear status, peaceful nuclear-energy program, IAEA safety/security/safeguards infrastructure, CTBT/NPT commitments.

Missing: nuclear rhetoric, crisis de-escalation, command-and-control practice, dual-use cyber/biological/AI governance, proliferation restraint, independent assessment of Rooppur safety, and target-year end-posture comparison.

### 2B

Evidence yield: E011–E014, E019, plus UCDP/SIPRI local context.

Supported themes: domestic political violence is not automatically international conflict; Myanmar spillover and Rohingya diplomacy; India diplomacy; military burden; regional-security constraints.

Missing: ruler-specific initiation or expansion of external force, civilian-protection record in military operations, proxy/arms-recipient conduct, public threat manipulation, military procurement rationale, and accountability for military harm.

### 3B

Evidence yield: E004–E012, E014, plus local PTS/CIRIGHTS/V-Dem/UCDP context.

Supported themes: security-force abuses, disappearances, extrajudicial killings, torture allegations, DSA-related detention, opposition violence, reprisals against monitors, Rohingya restrictions, and limited post-sanctions decline in abuses.

Contrary evidence: HRW’s reported decline in killings/disappearances after sanctions; local CIRIGHTS 2022 zeros for several physical-integrity fields; formal government denials.

Missing: complete target-year incident denominator, independent investigations and remedies, command-chain evidence, vulnerable-group protection beyond Rohingya, and systematic assessment of state versus party versus non-state violence.

### 4B

Evidence yield: E006–E012, E019, plus Freedom House, V-Dem, WGI, and RSF context.

Supported themes: DSA, surveillance, self-censorship, opposition harassment, 2022 BNP protest crackdown, weak electoral environment inherited from 2018, media pressure, and low institutional-constraint indicators.

Missing: 2022 election administration because no national election occurred; detailed 2022 appointment and operation of the Election Commission; judicial and legislative independence in specific cases; term-limit/succession conduct; minority political equality; and verified evidence on internet shutdowns.

### 5B

Evidence yield: E015–E017, E020, plus local GDP, consumption, Gini, investment, and employment context.

Supported themes: recovery and resilience program, financial-sector reforms, procurement digitization, social-protection delivery, climate investment, macroeconomic shock management, and IMF-supported reform.

Contrary evidence: inflation, reserve pressure, external-account deterioration, exchange-rate vulnerability, financial-sector weaknesses, and World Bank/IMF warnings that reform remained necessary.

Missing: appointments and professional independence, corruption/capture in specific 2022 contracts, household distribution by region/gender/class, evidence-based correction, implementation completion, and direct personal Hasina decisions beyond government-level policy.

### 6B

Evidence yield: E013–E018, plus local HDI, health, immunization, schooling, mortality, literacy, and enrollment context.

Supported themes: long-run human-development improvement, pandemic education disruption, social-protection and climate-response systems, Rohingya humanitarian burden, and refugee education/return constraints.

Contrary evidence: prolonged school closures and learning loss; Rohingya movement, livelihood, and education restrictions; underfunding; donor and Myanmar constraints.

Missing: affordability and quality, regional/group distribution, professional management, political conditionality, crisis correction, and durability beyond personal rule.

### 7B

Evidence yield: E004–E007, E010, and E013 where relevant; local corruption indicators used only as context.

Supported personal-nexus themes: Hasina’s award of sanctioned RAB officials; apparent Prime Minister’s Office involvement in monitoring rights-group foreign funding; government response to allegations; DSA policy and treatment of critics.

Strong exclusions: local V-Dem/WGI corruption indicators, general authoritarianism, repression, and weak rule of law were not personalized.

Missing: assets, family/business interests, procurement, beneficial ownership, appointments, sworn statements, independent financial investigations, correction of false claims, and evidence of personal benefit. The integrity record remains materially incomplete.

### 8B

Evidence yield: E002, E013, E015–E019, plus local macro, HDI, governance, and military trajectories.

Potential program components: economic modernization and upper-middle-income transition; infrastructure and energy expansion; digital/public-sector modernization; climate resilience; social development; regime control; regional diplomacy.

Supported execution evidence: World Bank-supported fiscal, financial, procurement, social-protection, and climate programs; IMF reform negotiations; Rooppur construction; India-linked infrastructure and diplomacy.

Missing: a frozen portfolio of explicit 2022 goals, implementation milestones, independent evaluations, personnel quality, adaptation after failures, coordination evidence, and durable outcomes attributable specifically to Hasina rather than the state, donors, or long-running trends.

- Local country indicators were rejected as sole proof of ruler conduct.
- Search-result pages, Wikipedia pages, Reddit discussions, and post-2022 allegations were not retained as final evidence.
- Official government self-assessments were treated as context unless documenting a formal act.
- Claims about the 2024–2026 collapse, prosecutions, or later corruption findings were excluded from the 2022 evidence base except where a later source directly reported a 2022 act.
- The alleged secret detention site was retained only as a reported allegation through HRW, not as established fact.
- No client-matrix material was used.
