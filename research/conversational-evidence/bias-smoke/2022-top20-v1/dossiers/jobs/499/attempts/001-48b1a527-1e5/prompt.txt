## Authority and evidence environment

- Marcos held the presidency from June 30, 2022. The Philippine Constitution makes the president commander-in-chief and grants appointment authority over department secretaries and other senior officials. [Constitution](https://elibrary.judiciary.gov.ph/thebookshelf/showdocs/3/353), [PNA inauguration record](https://www.pna.gov.ph/articles/1177892).
- The period was an open but pressured information environment: active civil society and media coexisted with red-tagging, politically motivated lawsuits, cyberattacks, website blocking, criminal libel, and threats to journalists. [U.S. State Department 2022 report](https://www.ecoi.net/en/document/2089246.html), [Freedom House Freedom on the Net 2022](https://freedomhouse.org/country/philippines/freedom-net/2022).
- National statistics and policy documents are usable for formal plans, budgets, and measured outcomes, but government self-assessments require independent corroboration. The available record is concentrated among government, multilateral, NGO, and major-media sources; local-language and regional reporting remain underrepresented.
- Population-scale context: the local package records approximately 114 million people in 2022. Conflict and violence measures are country-level exposure indicators and do not by themselves establish Marcos’s responsibility.

## Chapter yield and gaps

### 1B

Local facts: no nuclear arsenal or weapons-command evidence; civil nuclear planning only. E018/E019/E023 establish limited civil/dual-use exposure and formal safety language. Missing: nuclear rhetoric, crisis conduct, command safeguards in practice, inspections, proliferation, cyber/biological/AI governance, and end-posture evidence. The material is insufficient for a full ten-lens narrative dossier without further specialized research.

### 2B

Useful evidence: E008, E009, E010, E011. The local conflict series establishes exposure, not initiation or responsibility. Marcos’s documented positions emphasize territorial defense, dialogue, ASEAN, UNCLOS, and rules-based dispute management, while expanded security cooperation creates a need to examine escalation and alliance-risk evidence. Missing: target-period civilian-harm attribution, proxy/arms-recipient conduct, peace talks with insurgents, public threat-messaging accuracy, and accountability for operations.

### 3B

Strongest evidence: E004, E006, E007, E008. The country environment included killings, red-tagging, arbitrary detention, media pressure, protest arrests, and weak inherited accountability. Most serious evidence concerns Duterte-era conduct or national institutions rather than a completed Marcos-directed pattern in the six-month target period. Missing: Marcos-specific orders, security appointments and directives, 2022 investigations/remedies, vulnerable-group protection, and crisis-response evidence. Do not transfer all Duterte-era abuses automatically to Marcos.

### 4B

Strongest evidence: E002–E005. Competitive elections and a peaceful transfer coexist with widespread vote buying, dynastic concentration, election disinformation, media pressure, website blocking, and weak rule-of-law context. Evidence is adequate for a mixed political-freedom narrative, but attribution should distinguish campaign conduct, inherited Duterte-era restrictions, state action after June 30, and independent actors. Missing: legislative/court constraints under Marcos, surveillance and digital-control decisions after inauguration, protest treatment later in 2022, and concrete succession/term-limit conduct.

### 5B

Strongest evidence: E012–E017 and E020. Marcos articulated a broad socioeconomic program and appointed a partly technocratic economic team. Independent World Bank/PIDS material documents inflation, pandemic scarring, fiscal constraints, food/energy burdens, and the distinction between planned and delivered spending. The local outcome series shows 2022 national conditions but not causal credit. Missing: implementation audits, household/distribution outcomes, corruption/capture evidence with personal nexus, and evidence of policy correction within the short period.

### 6B

Strongest evidence: E012, E013, E016, E017, E020. Education, health, social protection, food security, learning recovery, immunization, schooling, HDI, and life expectancy are documented. Most available evidence is agenda/budget/outcome context; attribution, access quality, regional inequality, affordability, and durability remain weak. Missing: 2022 service-delivery evaluations, vulnerable-group outcomes, pandemic/disaster response, political conditionality, and institutional durability.

### 7B

Only limited admissible evidence exists. E015 documents appointments and some personal relationships; E003/E005 document information-control and press controversies with possible integrity relevance; E021 is national corruption context only and cannot be personalized. E022 was rejected because the underlying page could not be precisely inspected. Missing: asset declarations, sworn statements, procurement and beneficial-ownership records, corrections/falsehoods, conflicts of interest, response to investigations, and personal benefit. A personal-nexus search is required before any integrity judgment.

### 8B

Strongest evidence: E012–E017, E018, and E020. The record identifies explicit programs, priorities, appointments, proposed budgets, fiscal targets, infrastructure/social-service plans, and a civil-nuclear roadmap. Independent implementation and outcome evidence is limited by the short period and policy lag. Missing: delivery audits, coordination failures, personnel replacement/adaptation, crisis management, and attributable progress across multiple program components.

### Chapter 2B — Conflict, peace, and escalation

- **E042** — On July 15, 2022, National Security Adviser Clarita Carlos stated that Marcos had the final decision on resuming CPP–NDF peace talks; local engagement continued, and the NTF-ELCAC proposed amnesty and reconciliation measures. Attribution is authority-based and documents an unresolved policy choice, not a completed peace settlement. Contrary evidence: the talks remained stalled and the NTF-ELCAC and AFP retained counterinsurgency roles. [PNA, lines 65–86](https://www.pna.gov.ph/articles/1179031). Lenses: `2B.6`, `2B.7`, `2B.9`, `2B.10`. Disposition: `final_evidence`.

- **E043** — Following an armed clash between government troops and MILF forces on November 8, 2022, government and MILF mechanisms brokered a ceasefire on November 10. The agreement directed AFP commanders to implement the ceasefire, withdraw reinforcements, register local combatants, and establish a joint AFP–PNP–MILF peace-and-order detachment. This is direct target-period evidence of de-escalation and institutional peace mechanisms, while also documenting a contemporaneous armed incident. [PeaceGovPH, lines 55–83](https://peace.gov.ph/2022/11/govt-milf-sign-ceasefire-agreement-in-basilan/). Lenses: `2B.4`, `2B.7`, `2B.9`, `2B.10`. Disposition: `final_evidence`.

### Chapter 3B — Physical integrity and state coercion

- **E044** — The 2022 U.S. human-rights report recorded ongoing police killings, CHR investigations into 111 new alleged extrajudicial or politically motivated killing complaints, 48 drug-related killing investigations, 33 torture cases, and no prosecutions or convictions for extrajudicial killings during the year. It also recorded administrative charges against police personnel but substantial exonerations and continuing impunity. These are target-period national security-force findings; they do not establish Marcos-directed conduct. [U.S. State Department report, lines 43–76](https://www.ecoi.net/en/document/2089246.html). Lenses: `3B.1`, `3B.2`, `3B.4`, `3B.5`, `3B.6`, `3B.9`, `3B.10`. Disposition: `final_evidence`.

- **E045** — The same report found the Marcos administration’s position on red-tagging unclear: National Security Adviser Carlos condemned it in June, while Justice Secretary Remulla defended the practice during the November UN review. The NTF-ELCAC remained the primary actor, and red-tagging continued to target activists, unions, religious groups, academics, and media organizations. This provides contrary evidence against treating a stated restraint policy as implemented correction. [U.S. State Department report, lines 160–169](https://www.ecoi.net/en/document/2089246.html). Lenses: `3B.5`, `3B.8`, `3B.9`, `3B.10`. Disposition: `final_evidence`.

[NAMFREL/ANFREL](https://anfrel.org/namfrels-preliminary-report-2022-elections-went-smoothly-but-not-without-issue/). Lenses: `4B.1`, `4B.2`, `4B.5`, `4B.7`, `4B.8`. Disposition: `final_evidence`.
