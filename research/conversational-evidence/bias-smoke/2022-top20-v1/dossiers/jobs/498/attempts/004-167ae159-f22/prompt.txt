## Reconnaissance and authority baseline

Shehbaz Sharif became prime minister on 11 April 2022 after a parliamentary no-confidence vote removed Imran Khan. The coalition government had a slim majority and consisted of traditionally competing parties; the IMF described the governing environment as politically fragile and protest-prone. [IMF country report](https://www.imf.org/-/media/Files/Publications/CR/2022/English/1PAKEA2022002.ashx#page=7), [Guardian chronology](https://www.theguardian.com/world/2022/apr/11/pakistan-assembly-elects-shehbaz-sharif)

The prime minister had formal responsibility for national policy, cabinet coordination, federal appointments, economic decisions, flood relief, and the civilian government’s external positions. Practical authority was constrained by the coalition’s slim majority and the military’s substantial influence over security and other policy areas. [Freedom House](https://freedomhouse.org/country/pakistan/freedom-net/2022)

The information environment was constrained but not closed: independent media and civil society remained active, while authorities used sedition, cybercrime, blasphemy, internet restrictions, and arrests against critics. Human Rights Watch reported intensified pressure on media, civil society, and opposition activists in 2022. [Human Rights Watch](https://www.hrw.org/news/2023/01/12/pakistan-rights-besieged-amid-political-turmoil), [Freedom House](https://freedomhouse.org/country/pakistan/freedom-net/2022)

The pass searched English-language international, intergovernmental, NGO, official, and local-media sources. No local-language archive search was completed. Source concentration is highest around the April constitutional transition, the floods, economic stabilization, media restrictions, and opposition conflict. These topics should not be treated as representative of all governance activity.

Main gaps: ruler-specific authority over the National Command Authority; appointments and expert governance; target-year crisis decisions; implementation of safeguards; nuclear diplomacy beyond official rhetoric.

### 2B — International peace and lawful security

Local baseline: UCDP facts LF138–LF141 record 266 state-based conflict events and 631 fatalities in 2022, with zero internationalized conflict events/fatalities. SIPRI facts provide military burden. These do not establish initiation, proportionality, proxy responsibility, or personal command.
