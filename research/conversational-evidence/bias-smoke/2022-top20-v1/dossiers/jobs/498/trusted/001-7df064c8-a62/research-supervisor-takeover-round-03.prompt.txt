## Chapter-by-chapter baseline and yield

### 1B — Nuclear and existential risk

Local baseline: FAS facts LF001–LF005 establish a large inherited nuclear stockpile and nondeployed inventory, with 2014 observations; the package explicitly warns that these do not establish Sharif’s conduct. SIPRI military-spending facts LF289–LF292 provide burden context.

Narrative yield: E013 and E014 provide broader doctrine and one target-year Sharif statement. No usable target-year evidence was found for Sharif personally changing nuclear doctrine, command arrangements, safeguards, proliferation policy, arms control, or crisis posture. Dual-use cyber/biological/AI evidence was not found.

Main gaps: ruler-specific authority over the National Command Authority; appointments and expert governance; target-year crisis decisions; implementation of safeguards; nuclear diplomacy beyond official rhetoric.

### 2B — International peace and lawful security

Local baseline: UCDP facts LF138–LF141 record 266 state-based conflict events and 631 fatalities in 2022, with zero internationalized conflict events/fatalities. SIPRI facts provide military burden. These do not establish initiation, proportionality, proxy responsibility, or personal command.

Narrative yield: E009 documents the security environment and counterterrorism powers; E010 documents a temporary TTP ceasefire and failed/limited peace trajectory. The evidence supports exposure and some diplomacy/security action but leaves command attribution, civilian protection, public justification, proxy responsibility, and accountability unresolved.

### 3B — Domestic safety

Local baseline: LF673–LF682 show 2022 CIRIGHTS values of zero for recorded disappearances, killings, physical-integrity violations, political imprisonment, and torture, but the package warns these are aggregate country indicators and not proof of safety. V-Dem and UCDP facts show weak/declining physical-integrity and civil-liberties context plus 12 one-sided violence events and 69 fatalities.

Narrative yield: E007 and E008 document repression, detention, minority vulnerability, security-force abuse, and weak remedies. E005–E006 add internet restrictions and PECA enforcement. These are materially relevant but attribution to Sharif’s short tenure is mixed; much evidence concerns inherited institutions, military influence, or actions by subordinate agencies.

### 4B — Political freedom

Local baseline: LF1303–LF1310 and LF1803–LF1817 provide target-year Freedom House, V-Dem, EIU, RSF, and WGI context. The trajectory shows weak political freedom and media conditions, but also a peaceful parliamentary transfer.

Narrative yield: E002–E006 establish the constitutional transfer, coalition constraints, protest environment, military influence, media restrictions, PECA conflict, and court resistance. This is the strongest chapter-level narrative coverage. The principal unresolved issue is whether Sharif personally directed, tolerated, or attempted to remedy the documented restrictions.

### 5B — Economic well-being

Local baseline: LF2096–LF2101 and LF2554–LF2559 provide 2022 nominal GDP, real GDP, GDP per capita, consumption, government consumption, and capital formation; LS029, LS031, LS035, LS037–LS038, and LS041 show long-run gains but substantial 2022 shock effects. These are outcomes/context, not personal credit.

Narrative yield: E015–E019 establish inherited fiscal slippage, subsidy removal, IMF stabilization, inflation, flood damage, targeted-relief requirements, and recovery planning. The record is mixed and strongly constrained by inherited policy, global energy prices, coalition politics, and floods. Evidence for appointments, fair competition, corruption/capture, regional distribution, and correction of mistakes is thin.

### 6B — Social well-being

Local baseline: LF2689–LF2697 and LS023, LS039, LS047–LS048, LS072, LS074 provide target-year HDI, life expectancy, schooling, under-five mortality, immunization, and secondary enrollment. These establish outcome conditions only.

Narrative yield: E017–E020 document flood-related human suffering and government/UN relief, cash assistance, health, sanitation, shelter, education, women’s health, and recovery programs. Attribution is shared among the federal government, NDMA, BISP, UN agencies, donors, and provincial authorities. Evidence for ordinary non-flood social policy, service quality, political conditionality, and durability is missing.

### 7B — Personal integrity

Local baseline: LF2847–LF2850 and related WGI/V-Dem corruption fields are explicitly country-level and cannot establish Sharif’s personal conduct. The supplied package marks seven 7B lenses as lacking ruler-specific evidence.

Narrative yield: no final-evidence personal-integrity item was established. E023 remains discovery-only because the underlying court judgment was not retrieved. Official claims about nuclear responsibility in E014 are not integrity evidence.

Main gaps: personal assets and beneficial interests, family/business nexus, appointments, procurement, promises and corrections, response to scrutiny, obstruction or acceptance of independent investigations, and reliable adverse/mitigating evidence.

### 8B — Effectiveness and competence

Local baseline: LF2897–LF2898 provide weak government-effectiveness and regulatory-quality context; LF1803 and LF1815 provide accountability/rule-of-law context. These are inherited-capacity priors, not effectiveness evidence.

Narrative yield: E002 and E015 show coalition formation, fiscal stabilization measures, subsidy reversal, and budget implementation. E018–E020 show flood-response planning and cash/recovery implementation. The principal gap is a clearly frozen portfolio of Sharif’s own programs and independent evidence of outcomes, adaptation, personnel quality, coordination, and durability across more than one material program.
