### 3B — Domestic safety and protection

- `E071` — [U.S. State Department 2022 China Human Rights Report](https://www.ecoi.net/en/document/2089071.html), opening summary, ¶1. The report records credible reports of arbitrary detention, torture, forced disappearance, surveillance misuse, restrictions on assembly and association, and punishment of family members. It is an independent external-government assessment, though allegations and access limits remain relevant. Period fit: 2022. Attribution: national and state-system responsibility, not proof of a personal Xi order. Maps: `3B.1`, `3B.2`, `3B.4`, `3B.5`, `3B.9`, `3B.10`.

- `E072` — [Freedom House China Dissent Monitor, October–December 2022](https://freedomhouse.org/report/china-dissent-monitor/2023/issue-2-october-december-2022), ¶¶94–99. The monitor documented 173 anti-lockdown dissent cases, rising student and political dissent, arrests in 92 cases, and some government concessions in 51 cases. This supplies both adverse repression evidence and contrary evidence of limited local correction. Period fit: direct. Attribution: authorities and local/state institutions; Xi-level responsibility remains indirect. Maps: `3B.3`, `3B.5`, `3B.6`, `3B.8`, `3B.9`, `3B.10`.

### 4B — Political freedom

- `E073` — [Freedom House Freedom in the World 2022](https://freedomhouse.org/country/china/freedom-world/2022), ¶¶109–123, 134–150, 209. Direct political elections are limited to lower levels; independent candidates face intimidation, harassment, fraud, or detention; election administration is not independent; and the CCP provides no institutional mechanism for organized opposition. The report also records limited local grievance outlets and some concessions as contrary evidence. Period fit: 2022 assessment. Attribution: institutional political system and Xi-era concentration of authority, not proof of every local act being personally ordered. Maps: `4B.1`, `4B.2`, `4B.4`, `4B.5`, `4B.9`, `4B.10`.

- `E074` — [Freedom House China Dissent Monitor, October–December 2022](https://freedomhouse.org/report/china-dissent-monitor/2023/issue-2-october-december-2022), ¶¶96–99. Protest activity temporarily declined around the 20th Party Congress, possibly because of tightened security or censorship, while authorities made concessions in some cases. This provides contrary evidence against total administrative inflexibility but does not establish competitive selection or meaningful national accountability. Maps: `4B.6`, `4B.9`, `4B.10`.

No credible evidence of genuinely competitive national selection, independent institutional constraint, or Xi-directed favorable political correction was located.

The Bank projected 2.7% growth for 2022, identified recurrent COVID disruption, property stress, youth unemployment, regulatory-predictability problems, social-security gaps, and unequal access to healthcare and education. Attribution remains mixed between national policy, inherited structural conditions, and external shocks. Maps: `5B.1`, `5B.2`, `5B.3`, `5B.4`, `5B.6`, `5B.8`, `5B.9`, `5B.10`.

- `E076` — [World Bank poverty-reduction review](https://www.worldbank.org/en/news/press-release/2022/04/01/lifting-800-million-people-out-of-poverty-new-report-looks-at-lessons-from-china-s-experience), ¶¶144–156. The Bank and Chinese research partners documented major long-run poverty reduction, effective interagency governance, and continuing rural–urban education and social-protection gaps. This is persistent pre-2022 achievement/context, not a 2022 event; it supplies favorable contrary evidence without direct Xi attribution. Maps: `5B.6`, `5B.10`.

No defensible evidence of Xi-linked favoritism, appointment quality, household-level correction, or region-specific 2022 implementation was recovered.

### 7B — Personal integrity and honesty

- `E079` — [Zhuang, “Intergovernmental Conflict and Censorship: Evidence from China’s Anti-Corruption Campaign,” JEEA](https://academic.oup.com/jeea/article/20/6/2540/6615463), ¶¶31–33, 41–59, 717–744. A large empirical study finds that local newspapers underreported high-level corruption cases involving officials from their own province, especially well-connected officials, and that this reduced local accountability. The evidence is based primarily on anti-corruption reporting through 2014, so it is persistent institutional context rather than a target-year event. It must not be personalized to Xi or treated as proof of his obstruction. Maps: `7B.4`, `7B.7`, `7B.9`, `7B.10`.

The State Council dispatched 19 inspection teams to assess implementation of growth, employment, livelihood, rural-revitalization, audit-rectification, and COVID-control tasks across 19 provincial-level regions. The audit identified overstated revenues, irregular taxes and fees, hidden local-government debt, false reporting, and financial-management weaknesses in sampled provincial and local authorities. It also states that central-department budget implementation was generally lawful. This provides adverse implementation evidence plus a favorable central-control/oversight counterpoint. Period fit: audit of 2022. Attribution: administrative system and local implementation; not direct proof of Xi-specific failure. Maps: `8B.4`, `8B.5`, `8B.6`, `8B.7`, `8B.8`, `8B.10`.
