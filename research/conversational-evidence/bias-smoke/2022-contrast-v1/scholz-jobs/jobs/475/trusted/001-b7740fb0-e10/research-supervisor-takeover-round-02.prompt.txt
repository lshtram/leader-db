## Chapter yield

### 1B — Nuclear and existential risk

Local baseline: no directly mapped nuclear-risk facts were supplied. The local package explicitly says arsenal and capability facts, if present elsewhere in the parent, would establish exposure but not Scholz-specific restraint or safeguards.

Narrative yield: E001–E007 and E020. Usable themes include nuclear-sharing continuity, explicit avoidance of NATO co-belligerency, nuclear-site safety diplomacy, arms-control/risk-mitigation initiatives, and dual-use governance. Missing themes include detailed command-and-control authority, cabinet deliberations, nuclear exercises, cyber/biological/AI risk decisions, and measurable end-period change. Evidence is mostly government or multilateral and should not be treated as independent proof of exceptional performance.

### 2B — International peace and lawful security

Local baseline: UCDP facts show zero German state-based and internationalized conflict events and fatalities in 2011, 2020, 2021, and 2022; SIPRI military spending rose from approximately $46.5bn constant USD in 2011 to $62.5bn in 2022. These are country exposure and spending context, not evidence of aggression or ruler motive.

Narrative yield: E001, E002, E004, E005, E008, E009, E020. Positive/mitigating evidence includes condemnation of aggression, continued diplomatic channels, rejection of a no-fly zone, support for Ukraine, and reconstruction planning. Contrary evidence includes criticism of delayed weapons deliveries and uncertainty over implementation. Missing themes include detailed civilian-harm safeguards for German arms, proxy/arms-recipient monitoring, formal accountability for military decisions, and evidence of public threat manipulation.

### 3B — Domestic safety and protection

Local baseline: 2022 CIRIGHTS records physical-integrity score 8/8, disappearances 2/2, killings 2/2, political imprisonment 2/2, torture 2/2, repression 15/17; V-Dem physical-integrity and private-civil-liberties indicators remain high. These are aggregate country-level context and do not establish Scholz’s personal responsibility.

Narrative yield: E010–E012 and E019. Evidence supports a broadly protective constitutional environment, independent courts/media, humanitarian admission measures, and important protest-rights safeguards. Contrary evidence concerns disproportionate assembly restrictions in North Rhine-Westphalia, restrictions on some G7 protests, extremist violence, and absence of an independent police-complaint mechanism in the Amnesty material. Missing themes include federal police command decisions, prison conditions, minority-targeted violence, surveillance practices, and direct Scholz rhetoric or intervention.

### 4B — Political freedom

Local baseline: Freedom House 2022 gives Germany 39/40 political rights and 55/60 civil liberties; V-Dem 2022 values include electoral democracy 0.855, multiparty institutions 0.771, liberal democracy 0.806, expression 0.953, association 0.864, judicial constraints 0.918, legislative constraints 0.981, and rule of law 0.990. These establish a strong inherited institutional baseline, not Scholz-specific credit.

Narrative yield: E010, E011, E019, plus E001 and E008 for formal parliamentary accountability and transfer context. Evidence supports free elections, functioning opposition, independent courts/media, and constitutional constraints. Contrary evidence concerns extremist restrictions, digital hate-speech controls, and protest limitations. Missing themes include Scholz’s treatment of investigative journalism, appointments, surveillance policy, coalition pressure on institutions, and any direct attempt at electoral entrenchment.

### 5B — Economic well-being

Local baseline: 2022 GDP per capita was approximately $50,507 nominal and $63,676 PPP constant 2017 dollars; GNI per capita was approximately $55,340 PPP; Gini was 33.7. Real PPP GDP per capita rose from approximately $43,189 in 2011 to $46,648 in 2022, while 2022 inflation reached 7.9%. These establish outcomes and shocks, not policy competence.

Narrative yield: E014–E017. Evidence covers targeted income relief, child benefits, fuel-tax reductions, energy support, investment priorities, banking resilience, and the severe energy/inflation shock. Contrary evidence includes high inflation, downside risks, structural energy dependence, and uncertainty about long-term investment and productivity effects. Missing themes include appointments of economic professionals, competition/capture, distribution by region and class, labor-market outcomes, implementation audits, and evidence of correction after failed policies.

### 6B — Social well-being

Local baseline: HDI rose from 0.935 in 2011 to 0.950 in 2022; 2022 life expectancy was 80.989 years, expected schooling 17.34 years, mean schooling 14.26 years, under-five mortality 3.65 per 1,000, DTP3 coverage 89%, HepB3 coverage 85%, and secondary enrollment about 100.8%. These are strong country outcomes but cannot establish Scholz’s contribution.

Narrative yield: E012–E015. Evidence covers Afghan and Ukrainian humanitarian admission, healthcare and labor-market integration, refugee housing/schooling support, child benefits, energy support, and social-housing/investment plans. Missing themes include service quality and affordability, regional inequality, disability and gender distribution, political conditionality, professional administration, measurable delivery outcomes, and durability beyond the crisis year.

### 7B — Personal integrity and honesty

Local baseline: WGI control of corruption remained high, V-Dem corruption indicators were low, and rule-of-law/accountability indicators were strong. These are explicitly context only and cannot support a personal-integrity claim.

Narrative yield: E018 only provides a material ruler-specific integrity lead, but it is post-period and evidences a parliamentary dispute rather than a finding of deception or self-dealing. The source records the failed inquiry request and competing coalition/opposition claims. Required unresolved themes include Scholz’s own testimony, contemporaneous records, documentary evidence, corrections, personal benefit, obstruction, appointments, family/business interests, and independent findings. No defensible personal-nexus evidence was established for most 7B lenses.

### 8B — Effectiveness and competence

Local baseline: WGI government effectiveness was 1.29 and regulatory quality 1.52 in 2022, with slight deterioration from 2011. These measure state capacity, not Scholz’s effectiveness.

Narrative yield: E001, E003, E009, E013–E017. The identifiable 2022 program portfolio includes Zeitenwende/defense reform, Ukraine support and reconstruction, energy security, inflation relief, refugee integration, and climate/digital/infrastructure investment. Observable implementation includes the defense special fund, weapons and financial support, refugee benefit arrangements, relief packages, and energy-price measures. Counterevidence includes coalition criticism of delayed weapons, high inflation, energy vulnerability, and unresolved long-term structural problems. Missing themes include personnel quality, inter-ministerial coordination, implementation audits, adaptation after policy failure, and durable outcomes.
