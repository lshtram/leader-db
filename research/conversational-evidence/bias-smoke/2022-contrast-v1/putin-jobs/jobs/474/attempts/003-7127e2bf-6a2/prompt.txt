Attribution: Russian negotiating position and state conduct; Putin’s personal deliberation is not established. Contrary evidence: Russian demands remained expansive, and humanitarian implementation was contested. Methodology IDs: `2B.3`, `2B.4`, `2B.5`, `2B.7`, `2B.8`, `2B.9`, `2B.10`.

- **E034 — proxy-control evidence lead, bounded attribution.** Contemporary reporting records Yevgeny Prigozhin’s September 2022 admission that he founded Wagner and describes the group as serving Kremlin interests. This supports a proxy-control lead and formal network attribution, but not proof that Putin personally directed each Wagner operation. Period fit: 2022. Contrary evidence: Wagner operated through deniable and informal structures, and the available report does not establish Putin’s operational orders. Methodology IDs: `2B.1`, `2B.2`, `2B.4`, `2B.5`, `2B.7`, `2B.8`, `2B.9`, `2B.10`.

#### 3B — physical integrity and human rights

- **E035 — domestic torture and prison conditions.** The U.S. Department of State’s 2022 Russia report records credible allegations of pervasive law-enforcement torture, harsh and life-threatening prison conditions, arbitrary detention, and lack of accountability. It also records violence and discrimination affecting LGBTQI persons, ethnic and religious minorities, and people with disabilities. Period fit: 2022 report covering the target year. Attribution: government-system and local-authority responsibility; not proof that Putin personally ordered individual abuses. Contrary evidence: the report distinguishes allegations and reported conditions from adjudicated findings and does not establish uniformity across all regions. Methodology IDs: `3B.1`, `3B.2`, `3B.3`, `3B.4`, `3B.5`, `3B.6`, `3B.7`, `3B.8`, `3B.9`, `3B.10`.  
  Source: [U.S. Department of State 2022 Country Report, lines 43–50](https://www.ecoi.net/en/document/2089062.html).

- **E036 — political-prisoner treatment and limited remedy.** Amnesty reported in September 2022 that Navalny was repeatedly placed in punishment cells, denied confidential lawyer communication, isolated from other prisoners, and subjected to conditions it characterized as cruel, inhuman, or degrading. Period fit: June–September 2022. Attribution: direct to prison administration; formal presidential responsibility for the national framework is plausible, but personal direction is unproven. Contrary evidence: Amnesty’s account relies partly on lawyers and human-rights sources and is not an adjudicated finding against Putin. Methodology IDs: `3B.1`, `3B.2`, `3B.5`, `3B.7`, `3B.8`, `3B.9`, `3B.10`.  
  Source: [Amnesty International, 16 September 2022, lines 61–76](https://www.amnesty.org/en/latest/news/2022/09/russia-prison-administration-imposes-harsh-conditions-on-aleksei-navalny/).

- **E037 — vulnerable civilians and torture in occupied areas.** Amnesty documented forcible transfers of older people and people with disabilities, abusive filtration procedures, and seven cases of torture or other ill-treatment of civilians detained by Russian forces. Period fit: March–November 2022. Attribution: Russian forces and occupation structures; this does not prove Putin personally ordered each incident. Contrary evidence: the evidence is case-based and does not establish a population-wide rate. Methodology IDs: `3B.1`, `3B.2`, `3B.3`, `3B.4`, `3B.6`, `3B.7`, `3B.8`, `3B.9`, `3B.10`.

#### 4B — political freedom and institutional checks

- **E038 — 2022 regional elections and competitiveness.** OSW reports that September 2022 regional and municipal elections produced victories for Kremlin-backed candidates, that independent monitoring group Golos assessed competitiveness as the lowest since 2012, and that candidate registration, campaigning, and voting were subject to comprehensive official control. Period fit: September 2022. Attribution: institutional and Kremlin-aligned political control; it does not establish Putin’s personal intervention in each election. Contrary evidence: elections were formally held, multiple regions participated, and official results provide a competing account of legitimacy. Methodology IDs: `4B.1`, `4B.2`, `4B.3`, `4B.4`, `4B.5`, `4B.6`, `4B.7`, `4B.8`, `4B.9`, `4B.10`.

- **E039 — digital surveillance and mobilization enforcement.** Human Rights Watch reported that Moscow authorities used facial-recognition surveillance to identify and detain alleged draft evaders and anti-mobilization protesters in October 2022, with weak legal safeguards and documented false positives. Period fit: October 2022. Attribution: Moscow police and state surveillance system; federal presidential responsibility is indirect through the national mobilization framework and security policy. Contrary evidence: the report documents a limited number of cases and does not establish the system’s full national reach. Methodology IDs: `4B.2`, `4B.3`, `4B.5`, `4B.6`, `4B.7`, `4B.8`, `4B.9`, `4B.10`.

It also records that education, health, transfers, and pensions reduced measured inequality, while noting that the figures relied on older data rather than 2022 outcomes. Period fit: persistent baseline relevant to 2022, not a target-year event. Attribution: national institutional context, not personalized ruler evidence. Contrary evidence: fiscal transfers and in-kind services had redistributive effects. Methodology IDs: `5B.2`, `5B.3`, `5B.4`, `5B.5`, `5B.6`, `5B.7`, `5B.8`, `5B.9`, `5B.10`.

This establishes an institutional correction and audit mechanism, but does not show whether a specific 2022 program passed review or whether Putin used the mechanism to correct failures. #### 6B — health, education, and social provision

This is evidence of policy scope and intended delivery, not independent effectiveness. Period fit: 2022 agenda. Attribution: federal executive policy under presidential system; implementation remained partly regional. Contrary evidence: the source is official and aspirational, with no independent outcome audit. Methodology IDs: `6B.1`, `6B.2`, `6B.3`, `6B.4`, `6B.5`, `6B.6`, `6B.7`, `6B.8`, `6B.9`, `6B.10`.
