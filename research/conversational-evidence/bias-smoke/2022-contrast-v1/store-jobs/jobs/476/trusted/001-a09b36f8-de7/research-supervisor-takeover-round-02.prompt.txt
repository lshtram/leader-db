[Storting, Document 15:71 (2022–2023)](https://www.stortinget.no/no/Saker-og-publikasjoner/Sporsmal/Skriftlige-sporsmal-og-svar/Skriftlig-sporsmal/?qnid=76644) (lines 296–324).

- **E026 — 2022 disarmament-verification implementation.** The government’s later report records that the NPT Review Conference occurred in August 2022, that Norway led the second UN intergovernmental expert group on nuclear-disarmament verification beginning in 2022, and that Norway promoted humanitarian-consequence work in UN and NPT forums. It also records Norway’s attendance as an observer at the first TPNW meeting while declining signature because of NATO commitments. This is retrospective official evidence of target-year implementation, with cabinet-level rather than personal attribution. [Norwegian Government, Prop. 1 S (2023–2024), “Report 2022”](https://www.regjeringen.no/no/dokumenter/prop.-1-s-20232024/id2997797/?ch=2) (lines 1801–1806).

- **E027 — Arms-control and security-policy balance.** Foreign Minister Huitfeldt’s 22 March 2022 address stated that Norway’s nuclear policy and restrictions on foreign military activity remained unchanged, while Norway maintained close Allied cooperation and self-imposed limits intended to reduce provocation. This is current-government posture and inherited baseline evidence, not a Støre-specific nuclear decision. [Government.no, “Foreign policy address to the Storting 2022,” 22 March 2022](https://www.regjeringen.no/en/whats-new/utenrikspolitisk-redegjorelse-22.-mars/id2904996/) (lines 155, 420–432).

- **E028 — Independent contrary analysis.** The Norwegian Academy of International Law’s 2020 report records the competing argument that TPNW accession could reduce Norway’s NATO influence and protection, while also describing longstanding criticism of NATO nuclear deterrence and Norway’s limited leverage over nuclear-armed states. This is inherited/constraint context and a contrary interpretation, not target-year evidence. [Norwegian Academy of International Law, “NATO, Norway and nuclear weapons,” posted 23 November 2020](https://intlaw.no/en/issues/nato-norway-and-nuclear-weapons/) (lines 13–19).

- **E029 — Independent policy analysis.** NUPI’s analysis of NATO’s 2022 Strategic Concept describes a balance between recognizing nuclear deterrence and pursuing arms control, disarmament, and non-proliferation. It provides independent alliance-context support for the constraint structure surrounding Norwegian policy, not personal attribution to Støre. [NUPI, “NATO’s 2022 Strategic Concept – Change, Continuity and Implications”](https://www.nupi.no/publikasjoner/cristin-pub/nato-s-2022-strategic-concept-change-continuity-and-implications) (lines 105–107).

- **E030 — Civil-society adverse evidence.** ICAN Norway criticized the 2022 budget as insufficiently ambitious for nuclear disarmament and called for a stronger diplomatic priority, humanitarian initiative, and civil-society support. The item concerns the incoming government’s inherited budget framework and advocacy demands; it does not establish misconduct or personal responsibility by Støre. [ICAN Norway, “Statsbudsjettet for 2022,” 12 October 2021](https://www.icannorway.no/statsbudsjettet_for_2022_arbeiderpartiet_og_senterpartiet_ma_rydde_opp) (lines 34–52).

- **E031 — Civil-society contrary interpretation.** A December 2022 joint submission by the Norwegian Academy of International Law and ICAN argued that relying on allied nuclear weapons as necessary for security reinforced nuclear weapons’ role and weakened the TPNW norm. This is a clearly identified advocacy position, useful as contrary evidence about policy alternatives but not as an independent finding of wrongdoing. [NLA/ICAN, “2022 input to the Defence Commission,” 15 December 2022](https://files.nettsteder.regjeringen.no/wpuploads01/sites/495/ninja-forms/2/2022-NLA-og-ICAN-innspill-forsvarskommisjonen.pdf) (PDF p. 1, lines 35–46).

- **E032 — Safeguards treaty context.** The IAEA country record lists Norway’s NPT safeguards agreement in force from 1972 and its Additional Protocol in force from 2000. This confirms the formal safeguards baseline but is not evidence of a Støre-period decision or performance. [IAEA Office of Legal Affairs, Norway country record](https://ola.iaea.org/Applications/FactSheets/Country/Detail?code=NO) (lines 112–124).

- **E033 — NATO nuclear-governance context.** NATO describes the Nuclear Planning Group as the body for nuclear deterrence policy, doctrine, planning, force posture, safety, security, and political control, with decisions taken by consensus and all Allies except France participating. This establishes the multilateral constraint and shared-authority environment; it does not prove Norway’s individual conduct. [NATO, “Nuclear Planning Group”](https://www.nato.int/en/about-us/organization/nato-structure/nuclear-planning-group-npg) (lines 805–815).

- **E034 — ICAN global-spending/context claim.** ICAN Norway reported that the nine nuclear-weapon states spent an estimated NOK 893.5 billion on nuclear weapons in 2022 and characterized nuclear-complicit states as helping legitimize nuclear weapons. The figures concern nuclear-armed states, not Norwegian expenditure; the complicit-state characterization is advocacy and does not establish Støre attribution. [ICAN Norway, “1 688 000 norske kroner brukt på atomvåpen i minuttet i 2022!”](https://www.icannorway.no/1688000_kroner_brukt_pa_atomvapen_i_minuttet_i_2022) (lines 32–44).
