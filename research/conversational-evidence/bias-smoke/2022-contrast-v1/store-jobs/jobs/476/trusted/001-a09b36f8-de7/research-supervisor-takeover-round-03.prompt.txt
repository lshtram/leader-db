- **E024 — Nuclear-risk, CTBT, arms-control, and NATO posture.** State Secretary Eivind Vad Petersson stated on 8 June 2022 that the CTBT was central to disarmament and non-proliferation; Russia’s nuclear rhetoric was reckless; Norway remained committed to NATO deterrence; and Norway pursued mutual, verifiable, irreversible arms control, disarmament verification, and humanitarian-consequence work. This is target-period government-policy evidence, not a personal Støre statement. [Government.no, “The Modern-Day Nuclear Threat and How to Respond,” 8 June 2022](https://www.regjeringen.no/no/whats-new/nuclear_threat/id2918066/) (lines 176–210).

- **E025 — NPT and TPNW parliamentary scrutiny.** In a written question submitted 11 October 2022, Ingrid Fiskaa cited criticism from the Norwegian Red Cross that Norway had not been clear enough in supporting the humanitarian consequences agenda and asked about new disarmament negotiations. Foreign Minister Huitfeldt replied that the government would increase nuclear-disarmament efforts, emphasize humanitarian consequences, and work inside and outside NATO while retaining the NPT as the foundation. This supplies adverse scrutiny plus the government response, but attribution is to the cabinet/foreign minister, not specifically Støre. [Storting, Document 15:71 (2022–2023)](https://www.stortinget.no/no/Saker-og-publikasjoner/Sporsmal/Skriftlige-sporsmal-og-svar/Skriftlig-sporsmal/?qnid=76644) (lines 296–324).

- **E026 — 2022 disarmament-verification implementation.** The government’s later report records that the NPT Review Conference occurred in August 2022, that Norway led the second UN intergovernmental expert group on nuclear-disarmament verification beginning in 2022, and that Norway promoted humanitarian-consequence work in UN and NPT forums. It also records Norway’s attendance as an observer at the first TPNW meeting while declining signature because of NATO commitments. This is retrospective official evidence of target-year implementation, with cabinet-level rather than personal attribution. [Norwegian Government, Prop. 1 S (2023–2024), “Report 2022”](https://www.regjeringen.no/no/dokumenter/prop.-1-s-20232024/id2997797/?ch=2) (lines 1801–1806).

- **E027 — Arms-control and security-policy balance.** Foreign Minister Huitfeldt’s 22 March 2022 address stated that Norway’s nuclear policy and restrictions on foreign military activity remained unchanged, while Norway maintained close Allied cooperation and self-imposed limits intended to reduce provocation. This is current-government posture and inherited baseline evidence, not a Støre-specific nuclear decision. [Government.no, “Foreign policy address to the Storting 2022,” 22 March 2022](https://www.regjeringen.no/en/whats-new/utenrikspolitisk-redegjorelse-22.-mars/id2904996/) (lines 155, 420–432).

This is inherited/constraint context and a contrary interpretation, not target-year evidence. [Norwegian Academy of International Law, “NATO, Norway and nuclear weapons,” posted 23 November 2020](https://intlaw.no/en/issues/nato-norway-and-nuclear-weapons/) (lines 13–19).

[NUPI, “NATO’s 2022 Strategic Concept – Change, Continuity and Implications”](https://www.nupi.no/publikasjoner/cristin-pub/nato-s-2022-strategic-concept-change-continuity-and-implications) (lines 105–107).

- **E030 — Civil-society adverse evidence.** ICAN Norway criticized the 2022 budget as insufficiently ambitious for nuclear disarmament and called for a stronger diplomatic priority, humanitarian initiative, and civil-society support. The item concerns the incoming government’s inherited budget framework and advocacy demands; it does not establish misconduct or personal responsibility by Støre. - **E031 — Civil-society contrary interpretation.** A December 2022 joint submission by the Norwegian Academy of International Law and ICAN argued that relying on allied nuclear weapons as necessary for security reinforced nuclear weapons’ role and weakened the TPNW norm. This confirms the formal safeguards baseline but is not evidence of a Støre-period decision or performance. [IAEA Office of Legal Affairs, Norway country record](https://ola.iaea.org/Applications/FactSheets/Country/Detail?code=NO) (lines 112–124).

- **E033 — NATO nuclear-governance context.** NATO describes the Nuclear Planning Group as the body for nuclear deterrence policy, doctrine, planning, force posture, safety, security, and political control, with decisions taken by consensus and all Allies except France participating. This establishes the multilateral constraint and shared-authority environment; it does not prove Norway’s individual conduct. [NATO, “Nuclear Planning Group”](https://www.nato.int/en/about-us/organization/nato-structure/nuclear-planning-group-npg) (lines 805–815).

- **E034 — ICAN global-spending/context claim.** ICAN Norway reported that the nine nuclear-weapon states spent an estimated NOK 893.5 billion on nuclear weapons in 2022 and characterized nuclear-complicit states as helping legitimize nuclear weapons. The figures concern nuclear-armed states, not Norwegian expenditure; the complicit-state characterization is advocacy and does not establish Støre attribution.
