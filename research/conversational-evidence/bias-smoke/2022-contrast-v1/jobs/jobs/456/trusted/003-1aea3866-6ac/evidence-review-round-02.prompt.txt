## Chapter continuation results

### 1B

This chapter was the primary continuation target.

The evidence base is now more balanced:

- Official policy: E002–E003.
- Independent expert analysis: E017–E019.
- Local inherited arsenal context: E001.

The search found meaningful contrary evidence: the NPR retained a broad “extreme circumstances” use policy rather than adopting sole-purpose/no-first-use, continued modernization and NC3 programs, and left detailed implementation guidance for later work. It also found risk-reduction evidence: cancellation of SLCM-N and B83-1, reaffirmed arms-control language, and a stated high threshold for use.

The chapter is not fully saturated on:

- actual 2022 command-and-control performance;
- classified crisis decision-making;
- operational safety incidents;
- cyber, biological, and AI catastrophic-risk governance;
- independently measured reduction in nuclear risk.

The absence of public incidents is not evidence of safe implementation because the relevant systems and deliberations are partly classified. The review gap is therefore genuine and not repairable through additional country-level inventory data. Future research would require declassified records, congressional testimony, expert technical assessments, or credible contemporaneous reporting tied to a specific 2022 event.

### 2B

E020 and E021 improve the missing themes of civilian exposure, oversight, proxy/partner control, and accountability. They also add independent oversight beyond CRS. They do not establish Biden’s personal responsibility for Ukrainian or Russian conduct, nor do they prove unlawful assistance. Civilian-harm and downstream accountability remain only partly covered because the period is short and U.S. assistance decisions are institutionally distributed across Congress, DoD, State, USAID, Ukraine, and allies.

### 3B

E022 provides a traceable implementation outcome for the federal misconduct database, but it is retrospective and covers 2018–2023. It cannot resolve state/local attribution or nationwide safety effects. Federal implementation is better documented; protest outcomes, subgroup protection, and independent remedy findings remain open.

### 4B

E023 adds adverse evidence on speech/disinformation policy, dissent-related institutional retreat, and election-worker protection. It is a single investigative source and its interpretation is contested. Treatment of journalists, surveillance, appointments, and practical political-equality effects remain incompletely evidenced.

### 5B

E024 adds independent contemporaneous distributional evidence. It establishes household exposure and an important attribution limitation: inflation and real-income changes reflected both macroeconomic conditions and the expiration of pandemic fiscal policies. It does not independently evaluate the Inflation Reduction Act’s effect during 2022. Implementation quality and policy correction remain open.

### 6B

E025 adds implementation activity for the PACT Act, including claims volume and VA processing volume. It does not establish health outcomes, access quality, subgroup equity, or durable improvement. General-population social-welfare evidence remains separate and unresolved.

### 7B

No new defensible Biden-specific adverse integrity finding was located. Searches found agency-level ethics and conflict material, but not a traceable 2022 finding personally tying Biden to enrichment, deliberate deception, obstruction, nepotism, or retaliation. E015 therefore remains limited evidence of disclosure and review, not proof of broad integrity. This chapter remains materially underdetermined without a personal nexus.

### 8B

E020–E025 improve evidence of adaptation, implementation, oversight, and observable administrative activity. They also add failure and limitation evidence: disrupted Ukraine oversight, delayed deliverables, incomplete disinformation initiatives, inflationary household burden, and the gap between PACT claims processing and health outcomes. Long-term durability remains unobservable within the 2022 window.
