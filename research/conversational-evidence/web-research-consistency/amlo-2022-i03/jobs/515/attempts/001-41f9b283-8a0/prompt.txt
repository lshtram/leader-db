The presidency had formal authority over national policy, federal appointments, executive administration, foreign affairs, and armed-force command, but implementation was shared with Congress, courts, state governments, autonomous institutions, and subnational security authorities. Major 2022 events and environment:

- The April recall referendum produced 17.77% participation, below the 40% legal threshold; 91.86% voted for López Obrador to remain. This demonstrates continued mobilization capacity but is not a normal electoral mandate.
- The government and Congress transferred operational and administrative control of the National Guard toward the Defence Ministry in September. The measure was challenged by the IACHR, Amnesty International, and later invalidated by Mexico’s Supreme Court in 2023; the 2022 decision remains attributable as an executive-legislative policy choice, subject to constitutional constraint.
- The Ayotzinapa commission issued a major August 2022 report calling the disappearance of the students a “crime of state”; this is a material accountability and truth-seeking event, while the report’s conclusions and subsequent judicial process require separate verification.
- Economic conditions included post-pandemic recovery, elevated inflation, weaker external prospects, persistent inequality, and dependence on the United States. The IMF characterized recovery as gradual and highlighted long-standing low growth and inequality.
- The information environment was highly visible but adversarial: López Obrador’s daily *mañaneras* provided direct access and official transparency, while his “Who’s Who in the Lies” segment and repeated criticism of journalists created potential chilling and self-censorship effects. Violence against journalists was driven by both criminal groups and public-authority pressures; repeated reporting should not be counted as independent corroboration.
