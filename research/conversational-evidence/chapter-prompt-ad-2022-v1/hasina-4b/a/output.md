Chapter 4B research is complete for Sheikh Hasina, Bangladesh, 2022 only. No scoring was performed.

## Findings by lens

- **4B.1–4B.2 — Contestability and entrenchment:** Bangladesh entered 2022 with the disputed 2018 election still defining the electoral environment. In January 2022, Parliament finally enacted the constitutionally contemplated Election Commission appointment law. This was a potentially favorable institutional development, but it passed rapidly under the Awami League’s overwhelming majority; opposition requests for public review were defeated, and the president’s appointments remained structurally linked to prime-ministerial advice. Hasina argued that opposition amendments had been accepted. The evidentiary balance therefore supports formal procedural improvement accompanied by substantial independence concerns. [The Daily Star](https://www.thedailystar.net/news/bangladesh/news/ec-bill-passed-snubbing-all-concerns-2948716), [Prothom Alo](https://en.prothomalo.com/bangladesh/government/gazette-of-ec-formation-bill-published-president-to-form-search-committee), [2022 US human-rights report](https://www.ecoi.net/en/document/2089131.html).

- **4B.3 — Tolerance of opposition, protest, journalism and monitoring:** From August through December, police, Awami League supporters and BNP protesters were involved in repeated violent confrontations. Reports documented opposition deaths and injuries, mass arrests, restrictions surrounding rallies, and attacks by ruling-party affiliates. The NGO Affairs Bureau also denied Odhikar’s registration renewal because its reporting allegedly damaged the state’s image. Government and allied-party voices disputed opposition narratives, while some Awami League coalition leaders publicly condemned attacks. [HRW](https://www.hrw.org/news/2022/10/10/bangladesh-crackdown-political-opposition), [Amnesty](https://www.amnesty.org/en/latest/news/2022/12/bangladesh-authorities-must-end-repression-of-demonstrations/), [HRW/Odhikar](https://www.hrw.org/news/2022/06/09/bangladesh-stop-punishing-human-rights-work).

- **4B.4 — Independent institutions:** BTI described prime-ministerial concentration, Article 70 party discipline, missing parliamentary oversight, executive influence over courts, and politicized judicial appointments. The EC law created a formal search committee—including senior judicial and audit officials—but did not clearly insulate appointments from executive influence. These conditions were substantially inherited rather than newly created in 2022. [BTI 2022, pp. 9–10](https://bti-project.org/fileadmin/api/content/en/downloads/reports/country_report_2022_BGD.pdf).

- **4B.5 — Party capture and politicization:** The US report recorded use of law-enforcement resources for charges against opposition leaders and violence or intimidation by Awami League-affiliated organizations with reported impunity. BTI linked control of both government and ruling party in Hasina’s hands to weak legislative checks. Direct 2022 evidence of arbitrary personal loyalty tests or a newly expanded personality cult was not found. [US report, Section 3](https://www.ecoi.net/en/document/2089131.html), [BTI 2022](https://bti-project.org/fileadmin/api/content/en/downloads/reports/country_report_2022_BGD.pdf).

- **4B.6 — Media and information:** Authorities continued DSA arrests and prosecutions. One 2022 case involved a journalist reporting alleged irregularities connected to a local Awami League figure. OHCHR found DSA provisions vague, excessively broad and capable of chilling public-interest debate; the government did not suspend the law. A limiting fact is that the law minister acknowledged misuse and reportedly advised against immediate arrest in less-serious cases, but this did not amount to repeal or demonstrated systemic reform. [CPJ](https://cpj.org/2022/06/bangladesh-journalist-arrested-2-charged-under-digital-security-act/), [OHCHR technical note, pp. 1–7](https://www.ohchr.org/sites/default/files/documents/countries/bangladesh/OHCHR-Technical-Note-on-review-of-the-Digital-Security-Act-June-2022.pdf), [Freedom on the Net 2022](https://freedomhouse.org/country/bangladesh/freedom-net/2022).

- **4B.7 — Political equality:** No law formally barred women or minorities from political participation, and 50 parliamentary seats were reserved for women. One required search-committee member under the new EC law was also female. Nevertheless, parties missed their 33-percent committee-membership target, women in reserved seats were selected indirectly, and religious, ethnic, Indigenous and LGBTQI+ groups remained underrepresented. [US report](https://www.ecoi.net/en/document/2089131.html), [The Daily Star](https://www.thedailystar.net/news/bangladesh/news/ec-bill-passed-snubbing-all-concerns-2948716).

- **4B.8 — Succession and transfer:** No constitutional prime-ministerial term limit applied. No national transfer or succession event occurred in 2022, so actual compliance with transfer rules cannot be tested in-period. Hasina publicly claimed Bangladesh could conduct elections and peaceful transfers, but inherited election deficiencies and late-2022 opposition repression weakened that assurance. This lens remains partly unobservable for 2022.

- **4B.9 — Digital controls and administrative harassment:** Later reporting tied multiple 2022 mobile-internet disruptions to BNP rallies in Khulna, Barishal, Faridpur, Sylhet, Cumilla, Rajshahi and Dhaka. The government/BTRC reportedly denied ordering shutdowns, so attribution remains disputed. Separately, the proposed Data Protection Act would have allowed remote access to encrypted communications, broad government directions and liability exemptions, but it was only a draft in 2022. [Freedom on the Net 2023, lines documenting 2022 events](https://freedomhouse.org/country/bangladesh/freedom-net/2023), [Amnesty](https://www.amnesty.org/en/latest/news/2022/04/bangladesh-new-data-protection-bill-threatens-peoples-right-to-privacy/).

- **4B.10 — Inherited-to-left trajectory:** The strongest defensible inference is that democratic resilience remained weak and experienced additional adverse pressure during late 2022. The EC appointment statute was a limited formal improvement; it was outweighed in practical effect by opposition repression, continued DSA enforcement, Odhikar’s deregistration and rally-associated connectivity restrictions. A precise year-on-year causal estimate remains unavailable because several institutional defects predated 2022 and the shutdown orders were disputed.
