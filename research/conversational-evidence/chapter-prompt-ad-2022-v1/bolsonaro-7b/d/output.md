## 1. Orientation

### Authority and practical control

Jair Bolsonaro was president throughout 2022, the final year of his 2019–2022 term. He formally controlled the federal executive, presidential communications and premises, ministerial appointments, senior appointments in the Federal Police, Federal Highway Police and intelligence apparatus, execution of the federal budget, and the constitutional pardon power. He did not control Congress, the courts, the TSE, the TCU, state governments, independent prosecutors, or all police forces.

In practice, coalition dependence—especially on the Centrão—constrained appointments and spending. Military officers, political allies and personally trusted aides nevertheless occupied unusually prominent federal posts. These circumstances support attribution where Bolsonaro personally spoke, issued a decree, used presidential resources, selected or protected an official, or was adjudicated to have led relevant conduct; they do not make every ministerial or coalition scandal his own.

### Inherited baseline

Brazil entered 2022 with:

- A functioning but contested anti-corruption and accountability framework: CGU, TCU, Federal Police, prosecutors, courts, the Access to Information Law, election auditing, and active independent media.
- Long-standing clientelism and corruption risks predating Bolsonaro.
- The Car Wash task force terminated in 2021 amid both legitimate due-process criticism and concern that anti-corruption enforcement was weakening.
- Existing allegations involving Bolsonaro relatives and associates, but no established 2022 finding that Bolsonaro personally obtained proceeds from the older “rachadinha” or property matters. BTI expressly said that, as of its review period, Bolsonaro himself had not been implicated in a corruption scandal, while relatives and associates were suspected. [BTI 2022, pp. 11 and 34](https://bti-project.org/fileadmin/api/content/en/downloads/reports/country_report_2022_BRA.pdf)

### Shocks and constraints

The election, post-pandemic recovery, inflation, food insecurity and Russia’s invasion of Ukraine shaped the year. Congress and the courts constrained executive action. These shocks may explain policy reversals or fiscal tradeoffs; they do not explain unsupported electoral claims, conversion of official events into campaign assets, concealment of family-access records, or a later-adjudicated attempt to remain in power.

### Information environment

Criticism and investigation remained possible. The TSE, STF, TCU, CGU, prosecutors, police, journalists and fact-checkers produced extensive records. This creates greater visibility than in a closed system, so article volume alone should not be treated as severity. The strongest evidence here consists of ruler statements, contemporaneous orders, judicial findings and the later final criminal judgment—not complaint counts.

A methodological limitation: the workspace was read-only and exposed no filesystem-reading command, so the local guide and registry could not be inspected directly. The supplied local-first findings were used as the starting corpus, and each accepted web source was opened or its underlying official search extract inspected.

## 4. Source-state lists

### Opened independent corroboration

- AP reporting on the Federal Police’s 884-page coup investigation and military-command testimony; later superseded for the core claim by the final STF conviction.
- Reuters reporting on Bolsonaro’s transition statement, hunger statement, MEC investigation and property response.
- MPF account of the later TRF2 judgment against former PRF director Silvinei Vasques.
- TCU records concerning the 2022 accounts and Saudi gifts.
- Aos Fatos’ statement corpus, independently checked against TSE adjudication for the most material electoral claims.
- BTI 2022 for inherited institutions, press environment, and the important contrary observation that Bolsonaro was not then personally accused of corruption.

### Reused supplied sources

- E040: presidential-palace campaign livestreams.
- E045: denial of the food-insecurity estimate.
- E049: BTI integrity and corruption baseline.
- E050: family-access and transparency allegations, used only where official CGU material supplied support or a limiting defense.
- E029: dismissal of the Flávio Bolsonaro case, retained as contrary context rather than proof about Jair.
- E030: unsupported electoral-fraud claims, strengthened by the TSE merits ruling.
- E035: military presence, used only as appointment context.
- E051: later coup allegation, superseded by the 2025 final STF conviction.

### Promising sources still needing inspection

- Full AP 2668 judgment and individual votes, especially the dissent, for a more granular chronology of every 2022 overt act.
- Full TSE judgments in AIJE 0600814-85 and the Bicentennial cases rather than the court’s detailed official summaries.
- Complete 2026 PGR jewelry opinion and any post-March 2026 STF disposition.
- Full INQ 4831 Federal Police report and final prosecutorial disposition on alleged interference in the Federal Police.
- Bolsonaro/Braga Netto 2022 campaign-account judgment; search results did not expose a reliable final record.
- Appointment-level dataset connecting individual federal posts to family, donor or business ties.
- Final merits record, if any, for the MEC pastors investigation and alleged advance warning.
- Audited asset declarations reconciled to the UOL property records.

### Rejected or given zero decisive weight

- General CPI, WGI or V-Dem corruption scores: no personal nexus.
- General press-attack, Indigenous, environmental, policing and COVID conditions without a specific Bolsonaro integrity nexus.
- Later vaccination-record allegations as decisive evidence: the PGR sought termination, and available material did not establish Bolsonaro’s personal direction to the required standard.
- Flávio Bolsonaro’s “rachadinha” allegation as evidence against Jair: distinct person, annulled evidence and no proved personal benefit.
- Property purchases as corruption proof: cash payment is legal and the source of funds was not established.
- RP-9 as solely Bolsonaro’s scheme: Congress had substantial design and allocation authority.
- Article-count or fact-check-count arithmetic: retained only as evidence of repetition and domains, not as a score proxy.
- Unadjudicated jewelry allegations as proof of theft, bribery or foreign-policy exchange.

### Access-blocked

- Several STF news pages returned 403 or internal fetch errors; their official search extracts and process pages were available.
- The CGU 2022 transparency page redirected to a login page on opening; the indexed official response remained available.
- The direct TCU constitutional-jurisprudence page and some MPF pages returned internal/cache errors.
- The March 2026 Agência Brasil jewelry update timed out on opening.
- Some Reuters URLs were accessible only through licensed UOL syndication.
- Local workspace guides and registries could not be read because no filesystem-reading tool was exposed.

## 6. Remaining questions and saturation

The research is saturated on public deception, electoral self-interest, misuse of public campaign resources, the Silveira pardon and the 2022 power-retention effort. Further general searching is unlikely to alter those conclusions because they rest on adjudicated findings.

More research could materially improve:

- Whether the jewelry matter ultimately produced a final post-March 2026 disposition.
- Whether Bolsonaro personally directed or knowingly protected MEC, PRF or police-interference misconduct.
- Systematic appointment competence and donor/family ties.
- Personal beneficial ownership, unexplained wealth and campaign-finance reconciliation.
- Affirmative examples of voluntary correction, costly candor or acceptance of scrutiny; none were located, but absence is not proof.

No score is assigned.
