# Document-Level Evidence Paper: December 2022 Fuel-Tax Stimulus Agreement

**Ruler-period:** Andrés Manuel López Obrador (AMLO), Mexico, 2022  
**Chapter:** 5B — Economic leadership  
**Document:** *Acuerdo 173/2022* concerning fuel-related fiscal-stimulus percentages, amounts, reduced excise-tax rates, and complementary subsidies for 10–16 December 2022  
**Document type / source role:** Legislation; executive-legislative record  
**Scoring:** None

## 1. Source character, authority, and limits

This agreement is a contemporaneous official record issued through Mexico’s Secretariat of Finance and Public Credit (*Secretaría de Hacienda y Crédito Público*, SHCP). It records the fuel-tax treatment legally applicable for one week, **10–16 December 2022**. The instrument was dated **8 December 2022** and was to enter into force the day after publication in the *Diario Oficial de la Federación* (DOF). It was signed by **Karina Ramírez Arras**, head of SHCP’s Non-Tax Revenue Policy Unit, acting in place of the undersecretary of finance. [Unit 1, opening text and legal preamble; Unit 2, “TRANSITORIO ÚNICO” and signature block]

The agreement is strong primary evidence for the **formal policy actually announced and legally implemented for that week**: which fuels received relief from Mexico’s excise tax on production and services (*Impuesto Especial sobre Producción y Servicios*, IEPS), the relief percentages and peso amounts, the resulting tax quotas, and whether an additional per-liter subsidy applied. It also identifies the legal authority and responsible administrative office.

Its evidentiary reach is narrow. The document does **not** report:

- the fiscal cost or revenue forgone;
- the method or market-price data used to calculate the weekly rates;
- actual retail-price pass-through;
- which households, firms, regions, or income groups ultimately benefited;
- fuel-consumption volumes;
- distributional, inflation, environmental, or growth effects;
- audit or independent evaluation results;
- procurement, corruption, or politically connected beneficiaries;
- broader budgetary tradeoffs or financing;
- whether the rates were revised in response to measured outcomes.

Accordingly, it can establish the mechanism’s formal operation during a particular inflation-sensitive period but cannot by itself establish its effectiveness, fairness, sustainability, or full-year fiscal consequences.

## 2. Policy mechanism and institutional sequence

### 2.1 Pre-existing and supplementary legal foundations

The agreement did not create the fuel-relief regime from scratch. It invoked two earlier executive decrees:

1. the decree establishing IEPS fiscal incentives for specified fuels, published in the DOF on **27 December 2016**, together with its later modifications; and
2. the decree establishing **complementary fiscal incentives for automotive fuels**, published on **4 March 2022**.

Under those authorities, SHCP announced the applicable percentages, peso-per-liter incentive amounts, reduced IEPS quotas, and complementary per-liter amounts for the specified week. [Unit 1, legal preamble]

This sequence shows a standing administrative structure: an older statutory or decree-based IEPS-relief mechanism was supplemented in March 2022 by a second instrument capable of providing additional assistance, and SHCP then issued period-specific parameters. The December agreement therefore represents implementation of an established rule-based administrative process rather than an isolated political announcement. The source, however, does not reproduce the underlying formulas, eligibility conditions, fiscal ceilings, or safeguards contained in the two decrees.

### 2.2 Responsible official and formal delegation

The instrument identifies **Karina Ramírez Arras**, head of SHCP’s Non-Tax Revenue Policy Unit, as the issuing official. The signature block says that she acted in substitution for the undersecretary of finance, relying on the final paragraphs of Article One of both enabling decrees. [Unit 1, preamble; Unit 2, signature block]

Observed fact: a named specialist revenue-policy office, rather than AMLO personally, executed the weekly rate-setting instrument under cited legal authority. This is relevant to administrative regularity and professional implementation. It is not enough to determine how Ramírez Arras was appointed, whether she had operational independence, whether political officials dictated the rates, or whether economic personnel were retained or replaced according to performance.

### 2.3 Timing and legal effect

The agreement was dated **8 December 2022**, specified rates for **10–16 December**, and entered into force the day after its DOF publication. [Unit 1, Article One introduction; Unit 2, Articles Two–Four and “TRANSITORIO ÚNICO”]

The short gap between issuance and the covered period, combined with a one-week applicability window, indicates a rapid and frequently adjustable policy mechanism. That feature could support responsiveness to volatile fuel prices. It could also create short-horizon tax variation and fiscal uncertainty. The document supplies no surrounding weekly series, formula, explanatory memorandum, or impact assessment with which to decide between those interpretations.

## 3. Exact measures applied from 10 to 16 December 2022

### 3.1 Regular gasoline: less than 91 octane

For gasoline below 91 octane, the agreement set:

- an IEPS stimulus equal to **26.96%** of the otherwise applicable tax;
- a fiscal-stimulus amount of **MXN 1.4803 per liter**;
- a reduced IEPS quota of **MXN 4.0114 per liter**; and
- **no complementary stimulus**, with the additional amount fixed at **MXN 0.0000 per liter**.

[Unit 2, Articles One–Four, rows for “Gasolina menor a 91 octanos”]

The figures are internally consistent with a pre-relief IEPS burden of MXN 5.4917 per liter: MXN 1.4803 in relief plus the remaining MXN 4.0114 quota. Thus, ordinary gasoline received partial rather than complete excise-tax relief. Consumers or firms purchasing it remained subject to most of the fuel-specific tax.

### 3.2 Premium gasoline and non-fossil fuels: 91 octane or more

The agreement grouped gasoline of at least 91 octane together with “non-fossil fuels.” For that category it set:

- an IEPS stimulus of **0.00%**;
- a fiscal-stimulus amount of **MXN 0.0000 per liter**;
- an applicable quota of **MXN 4.6375 per liter**; and
- a complementary stimulus of **MXN 0.0000 per liter**.

[Unit 2, Articles One–Four, rows for “Gasolina mayor o igual a 91 octanos y combustibles no fósiles”]

This category therefore received no tax reduction under either component during the week. The document does not explain why premium gasoline and non-fossil fuels were combined, why neither received relief, or whether “non-fossil fuels” in this legal classification covered products with materially different economic or environmental characteristics.

### 3.3 Diesel

For diesel, the agreement set:

- an IEPS stimulus of **59.77%**;
- a fiscal-stimulus amount of **MXN 3.6071 per liter**;
- a reduced quota of **MXN 2.4283 per liter**; and
- **no complementary stimulus**, with the additional amount fixed at **MXN 0.0000 per liter**.

[Unit 2, Articles One–Four, rows for “Diésel”]

The figures imply a pre-relief IEPS burden of MXN 6.0354 per liter: MXN 3.6071 in relief plus the remaining MXN 2.4283 quota. Diesel consequently received the largest relief in both percentage and peso-per-liter terms. Its 59.77% relief exceeded the 26.96% rate for regular gasoline by **32.81 percentage points**. Its MXN 3.6071 per-liter benefit was approximately **2.44 times** regular gasoline’s MXN 1.4803 benefit.

### 3.4 Differentiated incidence built into the instrument

The policy was differentiated by fuel product, not uniformly applied:

| Fuel category | Stimulus | Relief (MXN/liter) | Remaining quota (MXN/liter) | Complementary relief |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Gasoline below 91 octane | 26.96% | 1.4803 | 4.0114 | 0.0000 |
| Gasoline ≥91 octane and non-fossil fuels | 0.00% | 0.0000 | 4.6375 | 0.0000 |
| Diesel | 59.77% | 3.6071 | 2.4283 | 0.0000 |

[Unit 2, Articles One–Four]

Observed fact: diesel received the strongest treatment; regular gasoline received partial relief; premium/non-fossil fuel received none; and the separate complementary mechanism was inactive for all three categories.

Possible policy interpretation: greater diesel relief could cushion freight, agricultural, public-transport, or other diesel-intensive costs and thereby limit indirect price propagation. That causal rationale is **not stated in the agreement**, and the document supplies no sectoral pass-through evidence. Diesel relief could also accrue substantially to commercial operators or higher-volume users. Likewise, regular-gasoline relief could benefit many motorists, but per-liter assistance naturally gives larger absolute benefits to those consuming more fuel. The source does not provide incidence evidence needed to determine who captured the relief.

## 4. Evidence relevant to the Chapter 5B questions

### 4.1 Broad-based sustainable prosperity versus rents or short-term popularity — 5B.1

The instrument documents a concrete fiscal intervention that lowered the IEPS burden on regular gasoline and diesel for one week. The policy operated through published, product-specific rates rather than a discretionary payment to named firms, regions, or political constituencies. [Unit 2, Articles One–Four] That formal structure is consistent with a broadly available tax-price intervention for qualifying fuel transactions.

However, the agreement’s very short duration—**10–16 December 2022**—and per-liter consumption basis make it principally evidence of immediate price or tax cushioning, not of a long-term productivity or sustainable-prosperity program. [Unit 1, Article One introduction; Unit 2, Articles Two–Four] It contains no investment component, productivity objective, transition plan, fiscal-cost statement, or analysis of effects on long-run growth. It also does not reveal whether the relief was financed by oil-related revenues, spending reductions, borrowing, or foregone public investment.

There is no document-level evidence that the measure directed rents toward a named politically connected actor or explicitly purchased loyalty. Conversely, the absence of named beneficiaries does not establish neutral actual incidence. Consumption-based fuel relief can distribute larger absolute gains to higher-volume consumers, while diesel relief can favor particular commercial sectors. The source does not identify recipients or quantify distribution.

Decision-useful conclusion: this record supports the existence of an executed, broad product-based short-run tax intervention, but it is insufficient to characterize the policy as either sustainable prosperity policy or popularity-oriented fiscal relief.

### 4.2 Appointment and independence of economic professionals — 5B.2

The agreement records administration by a named SHCP revenue-policy official, **Karina Ramírez Arras**, using formal delegation while substituting for the undersecretary. [Unit 1, preamble; Unit 2, signature block] This is evidence that implementation passed through a specialized finance-ministry unit and a legally identified chain of authority.

The document contains no evidence about:

- the official’s qualifications or selection process;
- central-bank appointments or monetary independence;
- the degree of discretion enjoyed by the issuing unit;
- whether AMLO empowered or overrode technical advice;
- personnel retention or dismissal based on competence, performance, or loyalty.

It therefore bears only narrowly on administrative form, not on the substantive personnel standard in 5B.2.

### 4.3 Fiscal, tax, debt, monetary, and financial rules — 5B.3

The agreement is direct evidence of tax administration under cited legal authority. It specified exact percentages, peso amounts, resulting quotas, covered products, application dates, and commencement of legal effect. [Unit 1, legal preamble and Article One; Unit 2, Articles Two–Four and transitory provision] These features make the week’s formal tax liability ascertainable and show compliance with an established publication mechanism.

At the same time, the agreement reduced fuel-tax revenue by up to MXN 1.4803 per liter of regular gasoline and MXN 3.6071 per liter of diesel sold under the measure, relative to the stated pre-relief quotas inferable from the table. [Unit 2, Articles Two and Three] It does not report aggregate liters sold, total revenue forgone, budget authorization, financing, compatibility with fiscal rules, debt consequences, or opportunity costs. No evidence is provided regarding monetary policy, banking regulation, or financial stability.

The weekly reset could represent disciplined formula-based tax adjustment, but the formula is not reproduced. It could also introduce recurring near-term changes in tax treatment. The primary record proves publication and legal specificity; it does not prove long-term fiscal credibility or sustainability.

### 4.4 Fair regulation for competition, entrepreneurship, property, trade, investment, and jobs — 5B.4

The policy applied by fuel category, with no named company-specific exception in the supplied text. [Unit 2, Articles One–Four] This reduces the appearance of ad hoc firm-level favoritism within the four corners of the agreement.

Nevertheless, the document does not address market-entry rules, energy-sector competition, fuel importation, pricing conduct, property rights, trade barriers, investment approvals, employment regulation, or enforcement. Nor does it identify which sellers claimed or transmitted the benefit. It cannot establish whether the broader fuel market was competitive or whether consumers received the tax reduction through lower prices.

### 4.5 Connected actors, procurement, disclosure, audit, and anti-corruption — 5B.5

No procurement award, firm, intermediary, politically connected beneficiary, audit finding, court proceeding, or corruption allegation appears in the agreement. The published rates and legal bases provide some formal transparency concerning the tax terms. [Unit 1, preamble; Unit 2, Articles One–Four]

That publication is not equivalent to evidence of anti-corruption enforcement. The document does not report:

- claims or reimbursements by fuel suppliers;
- controls against false claims or retention of benefits;
- audits of implementation;
- disclosure of firm-level fiscal benefits;
- sanctions, recoveries, or remedial action;
- cooperation with courts or oversight institutions.

The source is therefore largely non-probative for 5B.5 beyond showing public promulgation of the applicable rates.

### 4.6 Budget quality and public capacity — 5B.6

The agreement shows administrative capacity to issue a legally operative, product-specific weekly tax schedule through SHCP. The categories, rates, peso amounts, effective dates, and authorizing decrees were all specified. [Unit 1, preamble and Article One; Unit 2, Articles Two–Four and transitory provision]

It does not show that enacted or executed budgets produced infrastructure, education, health, technology, or durable administrative improvements. Nor does it state the measure’s budgetary cost or whether foregone IEPS revenue displaced public services or investment. It thus offers a small piece of evidence about revenue administration, but no outcome evidence for the central public-investment and service-delivery components of 5B.6.

### 4.7 Economic information, evaluation, audit, and correction — 5B.7

The instrument publicly disclosed unusually precise operative information:

- the exact covered week;
- a stimulus percentage for each fuel category;
- the corresponding relief in pesos per liter;
- the remaining IEPS quota;
- the separate complementary amount;
- the responsible office and official;
- the prior decrees supplying authority.

[Unit 1, preamble and Article One; Unit 2, Articles Two–Four and signature block]

This permitted market participants and observers to know the formal tax treatment. It is concrete administrative disclosure rather than a slogan.

The disclosure was nonetheless limited to policy inputs. The agreement did not publish the underlying fuel-price series, calculation method, fiscal cost, expected effect, realized retail prices, distributional incidence, evaluation findings, or reasons for the differences among fuels. It did not identify a monitoring indicator or corrective threshold. The fact that the rates applied for only one week implies the possibility of frequent revision, but this single document does not demonstrate that revisions were evidence-based or that officials corrected a failed policy.

### 4.8 Fair distribution of gains and burdens — 5B.8

The formal incidence was unequal across products:

- diesel users received MXN 3.6071 per liter and a 59.77% tax reduction;
- regular-gasoline users received MXN 1.4803 per liter and a 26.96% reduction;
- premium-gasoline and non-fossil-fuel users received no relief;
- all users continued to pay a positive IEPS quota.

[Unit 2, Articles One–Four]

This differentiation is central to distributional analysis. Diesel received more than twice regular gasoline’s relief per liter, while the premium/non-fossil category received none. Yet the agreement contains no income, class, gender, regional, rural, occupational, firm-size, or household information. It does not distinguish freight operators from private diesel motorists, or low-income commuters from affluent high-volume drivers.

A per-liter instrument is not means-tested. Absolute gains rise with fuel consumption, all else equal. That is an implication of the mechanism, not an observed distributional outcome in the source. Possible indirect benefits through lower transport and goods prices are also not measured. The record therefore establishes the statutory distribution by fuel but not the ultimate fairness of incidence across people or regions.

### 4.9 Response to inflation, commodity, or other shocks — 5B.9

The March 2022 complementary-fuel decree and this December weekly agreement together document that the government had a mechanism capable of changing fuel support rapidly during 2022. [Unit 1, legal preamble] For 10–16 December, the government maintained substantial ordinary IEPS relief for diesel and smaller relief for regular gasoline, while setting all complementary amounts to zero. [Unit 2, Articles One–Four]

This is evidence of a timely and operational shock-response instrument in the limited sense that exact relief was promulgated immediately before the covered week. The differentiated rates also show that support could be calibrated rather than universally maximized.

But the agreement itself never states that inflation, an oil-price shock, or a cost-of-living emergency motivated the measure. It supplies no funding total, targeting rationale, pass-through monitor, price benchmark, outcome data, or correction narrative. The zero complementary amounts could mean that additional support was judged unnecessary that week, but that is an interpretation; the source gives no reason. Thus, it demonstrates an active instrument but not whether the intervention was well targeted, cost-effective, transparently funded, or corrected according to measured effects.

### 4.10 Durable economic trajectory — 5B.10

This one-week tax schedule cannot establish whether AMLO left Mexico with a stronger or more durable economic trajectory. It records a temporary reduction in fuel taxation, not long-run outcomes in investment, productivity, employment, debt, inflation, resilience, or distribution.

Potentially favorable mechanisms include cushioning fuel costs and possibly limiting transmission into transport and goods prices. Potentially unfavorable mechanisms include foregone revenue, benefits correlated with fuel consumption, continued fossil-fuel incentives, and short-horizon policy variation. None of those downstream effects is measured in the agreement. The document should therefore be treated as one policy-action observation within a larger 2022 record, not as evidence sufficient to resolve trajectory.

## 5. Material qualifications and competing interpretations

### 5.1 Targeting versus regressivity

The stronger diesel relief may have been designed to limit economy-wide cost propagation through freight and productive activity. The document does not state this rationale or show pass-through. An alternative interpretation is that high-volume commercial diesel users captured large direct benefits. Similarly, regular-gasoline relief may have softened household transport costs, but larger consumers mechanically received larger absolute tax relief. Both interpretations remain plausible from the mechanism; neither is established by outcome data.

### 5.2 Responsiveness versus short-termism

Weekly specification allowed rapid adjustment to changing prices and may indicate administrative responsiveness. Yet a one-week instrument, viewed alone, gives businesses and households little information about medium-term tax treatment. Whether the wider system was predictable depends on the underlying formula and publication history, neither of which appears here.

### 5.3 Rules-based administration versus executive discretion

The agreement cites prior decrees, names the authorized office, gives exact figures, and uses a formal effective-date provision. Those are observable features of rule-governed administration. However, because the source omits the calculation formula and decision record, it cannot show how much discretion executive officials exercised or whether rates followed a stable, independently verifiable rule.

### 5.4 Transparency of rates versus transparency of policy performance

The government disclosed the operative tax parameters in detail. It did not disclose aggregate cost, beneficiary incidence, policy objectives, calculations, or results in this instrument. The source therefore supports a conclusion of **transactional/legal transparency** for the week but not comprehensive fiscal or performance transparency.

### 5.5 Environmental tension

The agreement subsidized regular gasoline and, especially, diesel through reduced excise-tax liability while giving no relief to the combined premium/non-fossil category. [Unit 2, Articles One–Four] It does not discuss emissions, energy transition, climate objectives, or why non-fossil fuels shared a category with premium gasoline. Any conclusion about environmental consistency requires external evidence. Still, the formal product pattern is relevant when assessing whether short-term price cushioning conflicted with longer-term sustainability incentives.

## 6. Bottom-line evidentiary contribution

*Acuerdo 173/2022* establishes that AMLO’s administration, through SHCP, implemented a legally specified fuel-tax intervention for **10–16 December 2022**. It reduced IEPS by **26.96% or MXN 1.4803 per liter** for regular gasoline and by **59.77% or MXN 3.6071 per liter** for diesel, left premium gasoline/non-fossil fuels without relief, and activated no complementary per-liter subsidy for any category. The remaining quotas were **MXN 4.0114**, **MXN 2.4283**, and **MXN 4.6375** per liter respectively. [Unit 2, Articles One–Four]

The document is most useful for questions **5B.3, 5B.7, 5B.8, and 5B.9** because it shows the exact tax mechanism, public legal disclosure, differentiated statutory incidence, and rapid weekly implementation. It offers narrower administrative evidence for **5B.2 and 5B.6** and little direct evidence for **5B.4 or 5B.5**.

The record does not resolve whether the intervention was fiscally sustainable, progressive, competitively neutral in practice, transparently funded, or effective at containing inflation. It also cannot show whether it strengthened Mexico’s long-run economic trajectory. Those conclusions require evidence on total fiscal cost, fuel volumes, retail-price pass-through, household and sector incidence, financing, comparison with alternative spending, formula-based revisions, audit findings, and medium-term macroeconomic outcomes.
