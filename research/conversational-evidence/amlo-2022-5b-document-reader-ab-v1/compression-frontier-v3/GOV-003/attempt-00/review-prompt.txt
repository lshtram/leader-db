# Evidence Paper — AMLO, Mexico, 2022, Chapter 5B

**Source:** Comunicado No. 9 — Informes sobre la Situación Económica, las Finanzas Públicas y la Deuda Pública al cuarto trimestre de 2022 (Secretaría de Hacienda y Crédito Público, 30 de enero de 2023). Official executive-branch publication (gob.mx).

## 1. Macroeconomic outcomes reported for 2022

The Mexican economy grew 2.9% through November 2022, "un mejor desempeño que el esperado por los analistas," supported by the labor market, consumption, and private investment (UNIT 2). Supply-chain disruptions and transport costs decreased, easing inflationary pressures, and upward revisions to global growth estimates were noted (UNIT 2). The administration's framing: "las disrupciones en las cadenas de suministro y los costos de transporte disminuyeron, lo cual aminoró las presiones inflacionarias" (UNIT 2). For 2023, global growth was projected at 2.1% annually, supported by China's reopening and lower commodity prices (UNIT 2).

Private consumption grew 7.1% year-to-date through October 2022, more than double its historical average of 2.7%; services consumption had already recovered to pre-pandemic levels (UNIT 2). Gross fixed investment grew 5.5% annually through October, above the historical average of 1.6% for the same period; within this, machinery and equipment rose 12.2% annually, above the historical average of 5.0% (UNIT 3).

## 2. Labor-market and wage outcomes

The administration reported record-low unemployment rates: 2.8% overall and 2.5% for women at the close of 2022, the lowest in 17 years, attributed to 2.4 million jobs created in 2022, "en su mayoría formales" (UNIT 2). The full-year 2022 average unemployment rate was 3.3%, "de las más bajas desde que se tiene registro" (UNIT 3). 753,000 new IMSS-registered jobs were added in 2022, "principalmente de carácter permanente," described as the third-highest figure since 1997 (UNIT 3). The employed population grew by 2.4 million relative to 2021 (UNIT 3). The administration stated real average wages grew 2.7% annually (UNIT 3) and that rising real wages had a positive effect on tax collection (UNIT 4).

Across the sexenio (December 2018 to December 2022), 1.3 million IMSS jobs were created, predominantly permanent, a figure the report says is higher than those recorded in the Calderón and Fox sexenios for the equivalent period (UNIT 3).

## 3. External sector and investment

Mexico's foreign trade in 2022 reached a historic high of USD 1.2 trillion, with record manufacturing exports and imports of intermediate and capital goods (UNIT 3). Foreign Direct Investment (IED) reached USD 32 billion through Q3 2022, the highest level since 2013 (UNIT 2). New investments within IED totaled USD 14 billion, the second-highest annual figure after 2013 (UNIT 3). At Q3 2022, the report notes greater appetite to invest in Mexico and USD 19 billion less capital outflow compared to 2021 (UNIT 3).

## 4. Public revenues and fiscal performance

In 2022, public revenues totaled MXN 6.595 trillion, MXN 422 billion above the programmed budget; up 2.5% versus 2021, marking two consecutive years of growth (UNIT 4). The Federal Government obtained additional revenues over the program of MXN 632 billion, of which MXN 397 billion corresponded to lower collection from the IEPS fuel stimulus (UNIT 4).

Oil revenues reached MXN 1.482 trillion, equivalent to 5.2% of GDP, the highest level since 2014, with a real annual increase of 18.7% over 2021, attributed to high oil prices (UNIT 4). The report highlights anti-evasion efforts continuing to feed tax revenues without raising or creating new taxes (UNIT 4). Non-IEPS-fuel tax revenues reached 13.6% of GDP, the highest since records began, with 7.8% real annual growth, the highest rate since 2016 (UNIT 4). The Income Tax (ISR) reached a historic maximum of MXN 2.270 trillion (8.0% of GDP), with 11% annual growth, the highest since 2016 (UNIT 4). VAT collection reached MXN 1.222 trillion, 4.3% of GDP, above the historical average of 3.9% (UNIT 4).

## 5. Public spending and social/infrastructure priorities

Public spending grew 4.1% in real annual terms in 2022, reaching MXN 7.569 trillion (26.5% of GDP), extending three consecutive years of growth (UNIT 5). Social protection expenditure grew 8.7% in real terms, reaching 5.3% of GDP, described as "un nuevo máximo histórico" (UNIT 5). Physical investment (inversión física) grew 25.2%, the highest rate since 2009, reaching 3.3% of GDP, the highest level since 2016 (UNIT 5). Federal transfers to states and municipalities (participaciones) reached the highest amount on record, with 7.3% real annual growth (UNIT 5).

Fiscal aggregates: primary balance at -0.5% of GDP; public deficit at 3.4%; Public Sector Financial Requirements (RFSP) at 4.4% (UNIT 5). The report describes a "blindaje de amortiguadores fiscales" totaling USD 262 billion, comprising international reserves, the IMF Flexible Credit Line, the US Federal Reserve swap line, and stabilization funds, intended to address internal and external shocks (UNIT 5).

## 6. Debt management and market operations

The Shcp's opening statement asserts that public finances continued to be managed responsibly, the debt level remained stable, and spending was oriented efficiently toward the well-being of Mexican families (UNIT 1). Public debt stood at 49.4% of GDP, 1.6 pp below the 2022 budget and 1.4 pp below 2021, attributed to strong budget revenues even after the IEPS subsidy (UNIT 1). The report states that in 2022 public debt was at a sustainable level lower than 2021, due to efforts to reduce the cost of financing and lower exposure to exchange-rate and interest-rate distortions (UNIT 5).

Specific market operations disclosed:

- **7 October 2022**: buyback auction of MXN 68,017 million in debt maturing 2023–2046, paired with a long-term debt sale auction of MXN 48,596 million in Bonos and Udibonos (UNIT 6).
- **30 November 2022**: third placement of Bondes G — MXN 7,090 million at 1-year, MXN 2,660 million at 2-year, MXN 250 million at 3-year (UNIT 6).
- **7 December 2022**: auction of a new Bono M maturing May 2033, MXN 12,500 million, 7.5% coupon rate (UNIT 6).
- **8 December 2022**: placement of Bonos M maturing 2033 and 2053, MXN 50,863 million, plus a buyback of Bonos M maturing 2029–2047 (UNIT 6).

The report states that debt pressures for 2023 and the following two years were reduced on average by 73% through buyback operations, freeing USD 3 billion for the next administration (UNIT 6). Over the four years of the administration, Mexico received 21 international recognitions for its debt management strategy and maintained investment grade with a stable outlook from all 8 rating agencies that evaluate the debt (UNIT 6).
