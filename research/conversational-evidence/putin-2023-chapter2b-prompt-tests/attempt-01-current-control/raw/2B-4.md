## 2B.4 Research Note: Vladimir Putin, Russia, 2023

**Working judgment:** **Mostly no.** The 2023 record contains strong evidence of unlawful and indiscriminate wartime conduct, while the best pro-restraint evidence is limited to ICRC access and humanitarian dialogue around prisoners and civilians. I found no solid 2023 evidence that Putin’s military operations consistently respected civilian protection, necessity, proportionality, or prisoner-treatment norms.

### Accepted claims

- **Title:** `ICC arrest warrants over deportation of Ukrainian children`  
  **Publisher:** International Criminal Court  
  **Date:** 17 Mar 2023  
  **URL:** [https://www.icc-cpi.int/situations/ukraine](https://www.icc-cpi.int/situations/ukraine)  
  **Claim-level summary:** The ICC announced arrest warrants for Putin and Maria Lvova-Belova over the unlawful deportation and transfer of Ukrainian children from occupied Ukraine. This is the clearest 2023 legal finding pointing to serious violations of civilian protection and the laws of war.  
  **Question IDs:** `2B.4`, `2B.2`

- **Title:** `Russia - Ukraine international armed conflict: ICRC continues to help people in need`  
  **Publisher:** ICRC  
  **Date:** 14 Jun 2023  
  **URL:** [https://www.icrc.org/en/document/russia-ukraine-international-armed-conflict-icrc-continues-to-help-people-in-need](https://www.icrc.org/en/document/russia-ukraine-international-armed-conflict-icrc-continues-to-help-people-in-need)  
  **Claim-level summary:** The ICRC said the increase in military operations and the destruction of the Nova Kakhovka dam created additional threats for civilians, especially around infrastructure indispensable to civilian survival. That is strong evidence against respect for necessity and proportionality.  
  **Question IDs:** `2B.4`

- **Title:** `Russia - Ukraine international armed conflict: ICRC continues to help people in need`  
  **Publisher:** ICRC  
  **Date:** 14 Jun 2023  
  **URL:** [https://www.icrc.org/en/document/russia-ukraine-international-armed-conflict-icrc-continues-to-help-people-in-need](https://www.icrc.org/en/document/russia-ukraine-international-armed-conflict-icrc-continues-to-help-people-in-need)  
  **Claim-level summary:** The same report says the ICRC had visited over 1,500 prisoners of war in Ukraine and Russia and delivered thousands of family messages, showing that some humanitarian access and communication channels remained open. This is limited positive evidence, but it reflects ICRC mediation rather than clear voluntary restraint by Putin.  
  **Question IDs:** `2B.4`

- **Title:** `Urgent humanitarian needs, prisoners of war top agenda for President Spoljaric’s first visit to Moscow`  
  **Publisher:** ICRC  
  **Date:** 18 Jan 2023  
  **URL:** [https://www.icrc.org/en/document/urgent-humanitarian-needs-prisoners-war-top-agenda-president-spoljarics-first-visit-moscow](https://www.icrc.org/en/document/urgent-humanitarian-needs-prisoners-war-top-agenda-president-spoljarics-first-visit-moscow)  
  **Claim-level summary:** The ICRC said its president visited Moscow to press for humane treatment of prisoners of war and regular access to detainees on both sides. This shows humanitarian pressure channels existed, but it is only weak counterevidence because it does not show Putin’s own military operations were compliant.  
  **Question IDs:** `2B.4`

- **Title:** `ICRC President Spoljaric concludes visit to Moscow: Progress on prisoners of war is urgent`  
  **Publisher:** ICRC  
  **Date:** 20 Jan 2023  
  **URL:** [https://www.icrc.org/en/document/icrc-president-spoljaric-concludes-visit-moscow-progress-prisoners-war-urgent](https://www.icrc.org/en/document/icrc-president-spoljaric-concludes-visit-moscow-progress-prisoners-war-urgent)  
  **Claim-level summary:** The ICRC said it raised international humanitarian law and POW concerns directly with Russian officials, including the foreign minister and defense ministry, during its Moscow visit. This is limited evidence of engagement with humanitarian law, but not evidence of broad operational respect.  
  **Question IDs:** `2B.4`

### Search gap

I did **not** find reliable 2023 evidence that Putin’s forces generally respected civilian immunity, proportionality, or prisoner-treatment norms in a way that would outweigh the contrary record. The positive evidence is narrow and procedural, mostly about ICRC access and dialogue, while the strongest substantive evidence points to serious violations.

### Bottom line

**Mostly no.** The best-supported 2023 reading is that Putin did **not** consistently respect civilian protection or humanitarian-law constraints in military operations, even though limited humanitarian channels and POW access continued to exist.
