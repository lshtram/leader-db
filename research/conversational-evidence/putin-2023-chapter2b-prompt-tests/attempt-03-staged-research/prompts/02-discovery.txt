Begin a staged Chapter 2B investigation of Vladimir Putin in 2023: international peace versus aggression and war. This turn is the discovery stage, not the final evidence synthesis.

The chapter asks whether Putin chose diplomacy or force; initiated, expanded, or prolonged wars and coercion; separated genuine security needs from prestige or regime motives; respected civilians and humanitarian law; restrained forces, proxies, and clients; truthfully explained threats; pursued ceasefires and settlements; used military resources responsibly; accepted accountability; and left international relations more peaceful and lawful.

Search broadly across all ten issues. Use varied event-based, institution-based, archive, source-specific, adverse, contrary, and Russian-language query formulations. Seek primary Russian statements and legal records, UN and other international-organization records, court or investigative material, conflict-monitoring organizations, human-rights and humanitarian reporting, serious scholarship, and reputable reporting. Search actively for evidence that mitigates responsibility or demonstrates diplomacy, restraint, defensive necessity, or attempted de-escalation, as well as evidence of aggression and abuse.

Do not impose a fixed search-call limit and do not select only the first plausible results. Build a broad candidate pool. For each candidate record the title, publisher, date, URL, source type, likely 2B lenses, what claim it may establish, whether the underlying document was opened, and any access or credibility concern. Search-result snippets may identify candidates but are not evidence.

Continue until additional query families mostly return duplicates or sources already represented in the pool. End this turn with:

1. The query families attempted.
2. A structured candidate-source inventory.
3. A preliminary source-family and lens-coverage matrix.
4. A prioritized extraction queue identifying the documents that should be opened and read next.
5. Explicit discovery gaps, including missing primary, contrary, local-language, or attribution evidence.

Do not assign a score and do not pretend uninspected candidates have been verified.
