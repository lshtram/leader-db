Identity and authority: Félix-Antoine Tshisekedi Tshilombo was the serving president throughout 2022, within the five-year term beginning after the December 2018 election. By 2022, his practical central-government authority was materially stronger than at inauguration because the Sacred Union coalition had displaced Joseph Kabila’s parliamentary dominance. That authority remained constrained by a heterogeneous coalition, weak institutions, imperfect civilian control of security forces, armed groups controlling or contesting eastern territory, foreign intervention, and dependence on MONUSCO and regional forces.

The evidence environment is uneven. Kinshasa-level political, fiscal, and legal developments are relatively well documented. Eastern-conflict evidence is abundant but attribution is difficult: state forces, foreign forces, M23, ADF, local militias, and unidentified actors overlap. Government and advocacy reporting frequently disagree over responsibility. Rwanda denied supporting M23, contrary to UN-expert and independent reporting. National outcomes should not automatically be attributed personally to Tshisekedi, and none of the material located establishes a Chapter 7B personal-integrity finding.

Sources opened and retained include the DRC Presidency, Human Rights Watch’s 2022 retrospective, its state-of-siege investigation, the IMF’s July 2022 Article IV/ECF report, the World Bank’s 2022 governance-reform operation, UN Security Council material, RSF, CPJ, and Congo Research Group/Ebuteli.
