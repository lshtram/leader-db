Evidence environment

The strongest evidence combines UN records, international financial institutions, and detailed rights investigations. Important attribution cautions remain:

- National conditions cannot automatically be attributed personally to Tshisekedi.
- The state of siege has a direct presidential-policy nexus, but individual abuses still require chain-of-command evidence.
- Economic improvements were government-wide outcomes influenced by mineral prices and external conditions.
- Government and presidential sources establish decisions and stated policy, not independent effectiveness.
- Conflict reporting is unusually difficult in eastern DRC; armed groups, security services, political actors, and regional governments all shape or restrict information.
- Credible contrary evidence exists on several themes: macroeconomic discipline alongside severe poverty; anti-corruption institutional activity alongside impunity; indigenous-rights legislation alongside delayed enactment; and expanded presidential authority alongside weak territorial control.
