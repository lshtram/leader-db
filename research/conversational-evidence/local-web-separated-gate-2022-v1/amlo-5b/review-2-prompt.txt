## Scope and orientation

**Target period:** 1 January–31 December 2022. Earlier conditions establish the inherited baseline; later publications are used only when they extract or retrospectively evaluate 2022 data. No chapter score is assigned.

**Authority:** Andrés Manuel López Obrador (AMLO) controlled executive economic strategy, the federal budget proposal and administration, cabinet appointments, social-program design, energy policy, federal procurement, and flagship infrastructure priorities. Congress approved budgets and legislation; states governed important local services. Banco de México (Banxico) was constitutionally autonomous: AMLO nominated Governor Victoria Rodríguez Ceja, subject to Senate confirmation, but neither he nor the cabinet set interest rates.

**Inherited baseline:** Mexico entered 2022 recovering from the 2020 pandemic contraction, with output still recovering more slowly than in many peer economies. Structural constraints included low productivity growth, high inequality and informality, large regional disparities, low tax revenue, deficient health and education capacity, infrastructure gaps, weak competition and rule of law, and heavily indebted Pemex. Mexico nevertheless inherited strong macro-financial institutions, a flexible exchange rate, adequate reserves, a well-capitalized banking system, USMCA access, and an IMF precautionary credit line.

**External conditions:** The U.S. recovery supported exports and remittances, while Russia’s invasion of Ukraine, supply disruptions, food and energy prices, global inflation, and rapid monetary tightening created adverse price and financing shocks. Higher crude prices generated a major fiscal windfall because Mexico exported crude, although its imports of refined fuel also became more expensive. Pandemic reopening generated substantial catch-up growth.

**Information environment:** INEGI, CONEVAL, Banxico, SHCP, ASF, IMSS, Compranet, international organizations and independent researchers supplied usable data. Government releases reliably document formal acts and administrative totals but are not treated as proof of causal success. Procurement records were materially incomplete or late. CPI is a perception composite, not a direct count of corruption. Household-survey revisions and population-weight updates affect some historical comparisons.

The local workspace could not be enumerated through the available read interface, so the requested local-first registries and time-series files could not be directly extracted. Their principal indicators were checked against accessible WDI, INEGI, CONEVAL, IMF, OECD, Banxico and CPI publications.

## Overall working account

The strongest favorable evidence is not headline GDP alone. It is the combination of a large minimum-wage rise, additional employment, higher real labor income late in 2022, declining labor poverty, expanded transfers, and a broadly neutral fiscal stance that avoided a debt or balance-of-payments crisis. Formal employment reached a record and foreign investment remained substantial.

The strongest adverse evidence concerns durability and institutional quality: untargeted fuel subsidies consumed essentially the entire oil windfall; fiscal buffers were not rebuilt; Pemex and fossil-fuel projects remained privileged; energy rules generated treaty consultations and discouraged clean-energy investment; procurement became less competitive and often incomplete or late; health-service access deteriorated sharply; and flagship investment was not consistently founded on transparent cost-benefit appraisal.

Attribution is therefore asymmetric:

- AMLO can be directly associated with wage, transfer, budget, fuel-price, Pemex, energy-rule and flagship-project choices.
- Banxico deserves primary credit for interest-rate tightening and monetary credibility. AMLO’s attributable contribution is limited to nominating a governor and not overriding the institution.
- 2022 growth, jobs, FDI and household recovery also reflect reopening, U.S. demand, remittances, autonomous monetary policy, private decisions and prior institutions.
- Inflation arose substantially from global supply and commodity shocks, but targeting and financing of the government response were discretionary.
- CPI and generic governance indicators provide context, not proof of AMLO’s personal corruption.

## Ten-lens disposition

**5B.1 — Broad and sustainable prosperity:** Supported, but disputed. The 22% minimum-wage increase, transfers and labor-income recovery demonstrate a genuine redistributive program (WEB-5B-005, 006, 015). Sustainability is weakened by untargeted fuel relief, Pemex exposure, thin buffers and low tax capacity (WEB-5B-003, 004, 012, 016). Evidence supports both welfare intent and substantial political-popularity incentives; it does not establish that loyalty-buying was the dominant purpose.

**5B.2 — Economic professionals:** Moderately supported. Finance Minister Rogelio Ramírez de la O was a highly trained economist, and the 2022 fiscal record was broadly prudent. Rodríguez Ceja had extensive public-finance experience but little prior monetary-policy experience; Banxico nevertheless tightened forcefully and retained credibility (WEB-5B-002, 014). Evidence about genuine delegation inside the presidency remains limited.

**5B.3 — Macroeconomic stability:** Strongly supported for the immediate year, with attribution caveats. Debt remained near 56% of GDP, reserves and banks were adequate, and the fiscal stance complemented monetary tightening (WEB-5B-001, 002). Banxico rather than AMLO controlled rates. Depleted stabilization buffers, Pemex risks and low revenue limit the durability finding (WEB-5B-004, 012, 016).

**5B.4 — Fair market and investment rules:** Mixed to adverse. USMCA consultations identified specific rules and permit practices favoring CFE and Pemex; OECD found elevated policy and contract uncertainty (WEB-5B-010, 012). Conversely, USMCA remained intact, FDI reached $35.3 billion, and the government launched nearshoring finance for the south-southeast (WEB-5B-013).

**5B.5 — Corruption, favoritism and capture:** Adverse institutional evidence, but no direct proof of AMLO’s personal enrichment. Thirty-eight percent of procurement value was directly awarded; many records were incomplete or published late, and risk increased in most federal institutions (WEB-5B-011). Energy measures explicitly advantaged state firms (WEB-5B-010). CPI remained 31/100 (WEB-5B-017), but this perception measure cannot identify individual responsibility.

**5B.6 — Productivity foundations:** Mixed. Public investment and social protection increased, and the south-southeast received infrastructure and nearshoring attention. Yet OECD found persistent health, education and infrastructure gaps, weak project appraisal and regulatory uncertainty; health-access deprivation rose dramatically (WEB-5B-008, 012, 013).

**5B.7 — Evidence and correction:** Weak-to-mixed. Fiscal authorities maintained a policy mix broadly endorsed by the IMF and accepted external financial surveillance (WEB-5B-001). The government nevertheless retained untargeted fuel subsidies because of AMLO’s real-price pledge, continued Pemex-centered strategy despite repeated sustainability warnings, and did not correct procurement weaknesses or health-access deterioration during 2022 (WEB-5B-003, 008, 011, 012).

**5B.8 — Fair distribution:** Meaningful gains with major gaps. Labor poverty and real labor income improved nationally and in rural and urban areas (WEB-5B-006). Yet rural labor poverty remained 52.3%, Chiapas reached 68.8%, women earned substantially less than men, and the poorest quintile had not regained its pre-pandemic real labor income (WEB-5B-007). Universal pensions improved coverage but were less targeted than alternatives.

**5B.9 — Shock management:** Competent in avoiding instability, less efficient in distribution and preparedness. Fiscal restraint, fuel-price smoothing and autonomous monetary tightening limited immediate damage (WEB-5B-001–003). The subsidy favored higher-income fuel consumers and used the oil windfall rather than rebuilding depleted buffers (WEB-5B-003, 004). Inflation still averaged about 8%.

**5B.10 — Inherited-to-left trajectory:** The 2022-only trajectory was better than the pandemic trough but not clearly stronger than the pre-pandemic inherited path. Employment, real labor income and poverty improved, while health access, regulatory certainty, fiscal buffers and productive-investment quality remained serious weaknesses (WEB-5B-006–012, 018). A tenure-wide conclusion would require data beyond this calendar-year assessment.
