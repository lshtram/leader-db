# Three-case evidence saturation test — 2022

## Question

Can substantially more research increase *quality, nonduplicative, decision-relevant*
evidence by 10x, 100x, or 1000x for one ruler–chapter case?

## Cases and method

The test used three deliberately different cases from the completed 2022 cohort:

1. Vladimir Putin / Russia / 2B international peace — exceptionally high-volume war case.
2. Joe Biden / United States / 5B economic well-being — high-volume open-system policy case.
3. Félix Tshisekedi / DR Congo / 4B political freedom — lower-visibility, French/English case.

For each case, the existing reviewed chapter evidence was the baseline. Ten local-prior
lenses were queried first. External research then used twelve purposeful queries in
three waves: broad discovery, lens/source-family gaps, and contrary/saturation checks.
Search results were candidates only. A URL was counted as additional quality evidence
only after its underlying page or PDF was opened successfully and it added a materially
distinct claim, stronger provenance, meaningful contrary evidence, or a missing lens.
Equivalent pages, current-period noise, snippets, blocked pages, Wikipedia/Reddit leads,
and additional articles repeating an established fact were not accepted.

This is a bounded saturation probe, not a replacement dossier or a new score-bearing
judgment. It estimates the useful ceiling from marginal yield.

## Results

Across all 160 ruler–chapter packets in the completed 2022 run, the current mean is
11.9 distinct mapped URLs and the median is 11 (range 0–35). A total of 148/160 chapter
packets contain fewer than 20 mapped URLs. The ruler-level mean of 84.3 URLs therefore
should not be mistaken for chapter depth: URLs are spread and reused across eight
chapters.

| Case | Existing URLs | Additional opened quality URLs | Combined observed quality URLs | Increase | Estimated useful ceiling |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Putin 2B | 16 | 11 | 27 | 1.69x | 35–50 |
| Biden 5B | 12 | 14 | 26 | 2.17x | 30–45 |
| Tshisekedi 4B | 13 | 8 | 21 | 1.62x | 25–35 |

The additional URLs do not all represent independent source families. Several improve
the locator or authority for an already-known theme. This is valuable, but less valuable
than a new governing decision or a genuinely contrary finding.

### Putin 2B

The baseline already established invasion, failed negotiations, annexation, civilian
harm, the grain deal, and some diplomacy. Additional high-quality material strengthened:

- direct documentation of executions and grave abuses (Amnesty and HRW);
- a UN commission finding of war crimes and a detailed OHCHR pattern report;
- torture, disappearances, POW treatment, forcible transfer, and filtration;
- false-genocide justification and the legal/institutional response;
- OSCE findings on the war and illegal annexation;
- contrary/restraint material on the grain agreement and a Putin-approved prisoner swap.

Examples: [UN Commission](https://ukraine.un.org/en/200623-un-commission-concludes-war-crimes-have-been-committed-ukraine-expresses-concern-about),
[OHCHR report](https://ukraine.un.org/sites/default/files/2022-09/ReportUkraine-1Feb-31Jul2022-en.pdf),
[OSCE Moscow Mechanism](https://odihr.osce.org/odihr/515868),
[OSCE annexation finding](https://odihr.osce.org/odihr/528075),
[Amnesty filtration report](https://www.amnesty.org/ar/wp-content/uploads/2022/11/EUR5061362022ENGLISH.pdf),
[Carnegie grain analysis](https://carnegieendowment.org/russia-eurasia/politika/2022/09/why-is-russia-jeopardizing-the-ukraine-grain-deal), and
[Washington Post prisoner-swap report](https://www.washingtonpost.com/national-security/2022/10/01/prisoner-exchange-putin-fsb-ukraine/).

There are hundreds or thousands of credible articles and incident reports about this
war. They could increase document count by 10x or 100x, but most would repeat the same
chapter mechanisms or subdivide civilian-harm incidents. For a holistic 2B judgment,
roughly 35–50 well-chosen URLs should capture the major distinct choices, conduct,
contrary evidence, attribution, and legal findings. More could be justified for a war-
crimes incident database, but that is a different research unit.

### Biden 5B

The baseline already covered GDP, inflation, real earnings, employment, the IMF Article
IV, the IRA, CHIPS, infrastructure implementation, and poverty. Additional material added:

- independent criticism that nominal-income claims ignored inflation;
- evidence that earlier stimulus contributed partly to demand pressure, alongside
  supply, labor-market, energy, and monetary causes;
- CBO budget estimates for CHIPS and the IRA;
- distributional evidence on lower-wage recovery and the expiration of the expanded
  child tax credit;
- independent limitations on CHIPS funding and strategy;
- Fed independence/appointments and the 2022 monetary-policy pivot;
- GAO findings on fraud and weak controls in emergency relief.

Examples: [IMF inflation study](https://www.imf.org/en/publications/wp/issues/2022/10/28/understanding-u-s-525200),
[CBO CHIPS estimate](https://www.cbo.gov/publication/58319),
[CBO IRA estimate](https://www.cbo.gov/publication/58366),
[Census 2022 poverty report](https://www.census.gov/library/publications/2023/demo/p60-280.html),
[Federal Reserve June 2022 report](https://www.federalreserve.gov/monetarypolicy/2022-06-mpr-part1.htm),
[GAO emergency-relief oversight](https://www.gao.gov/products/gao-22-105715), and
[Brookings semiconductor strategy](https://www.brookings.edu/wp-content/uploads/2022/11/FP_20221103_semiconductor_strategy.pdf).

The first expansion wave added many useful records because the original twelve URLs
were thin for appointments, distribution, policy criticism, and implementation risk.
After about 25–30 URLs, new results increasingly repeated inflation/jobs/IRA narratives
or assessed implementation years later. A useful ceiling is approximately 30–45 URLs.

### Tshisekedi 4B

The baseline already covered the state of siege, arrests of journalists and opposition,
press-freedom incidents, electoral-law debate, and institutional weakness. Additional
research added or improved:

- the primary JED 72-page report and its incident totals;
- a UK synthesis tracing the Kabund arrest and restrictions to 2022 sources;
- official electoral implementation records and the CENI legal framework;
- a concrete women-representation provision debated in the 2022 electoral reform;
- RSF evidence of both reform commitments and nonimplementation;
- executive use of delegated ordinance authority during parliamentary recess.

Examples: [JED 2022 report](https://jed-afrique.org/wp-content/uploads/2022/11/RAPPORT-2022-VF_compressed_1pdf.io1_compressed_1pdf.io_.pdf),
[HRW state-of-siege report](https://www.hrw.org/news/2022/03/22/dr-congo-martial-law-brings-crackdown-east),
[Amnesty state-of-siege briefing](https://www.amnesty.org/en/latest/news/2022/05/drc-one-year-on-state-of-siege/),
[UK opposition synthesis](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/democratic-republic-of-the-congo-country-policy-and-information-notes/country-policy-and-information-note-opposition-to-the-government-democratic-republic-of-the-congo-november-2023-accessible),
[CENI implementation decision](https://www.ceni.cd/manuel-electoral/decision-ndeg36ceniap2022-du-25-aout-2022-portant-mesures-dapplication-de-la-loi), and
[RSF reform assessment](https://rsf.org/ar/node/33597).

French-language searching improved the record, but access failures and source-family
concentration appeared quickly. After roughly 20–25 URLs, most new hits were individual
journalist incidents already synthesized by JED/CPJ, repeats of the state-of-siege
finding, or later election reporting. A useful ceiling is approximately 25–35 URLs.

## Conclusion and proposed targets

The test does not support a 10x, 100x, or 1000x increase in *quality final evidence*.
It supports roughly a 1.5x–3x increase over the current chapter packets. A
10x–100x increase is possible only in candidate/article counts for globally covered
events; it mostly purchases repetition, incident subdivision, later commentary, and
same-family corroboration.

Recommended per ruler–chapter targets:

- candidate URLs discovered: 80–150 normally; up to 250 for exceptional war/crisis cases;
- successfully opened and inspected: 30–70;
- accepted distinct URLs: 20–35 normally;
- accepted ceiling for information-rich cases: 40–60;
- accepted source-claim units: 25–50, allowing multiple claims from a major report but
  reporting that concentration explicitly;
- independent source families/domains: 10–20;
- contrary/mitigating sources: at least 3–6 where the record permits;
- stop rule: stop after a purposeful wave of 15–20 newly inspected candidates yields
  fewer than two new material claims and no unresolved decisive lens or attribution gap.

Counts should be targets, not gates. The stop rule, lens coverage, attribution quality,
source independence, and contrary evidence are stronger saturation signals than a raw
document total.

On this evidence, the 2022 process is efficient and well reviewed but still too thin at
the chapter level. Raising the normal accepted-URL target from roughly 12 to 20–35 is
likely to improve robustness materially. Requiring 80–100 accepted URLs per chapter is
not supported by the observed marginal yield.
