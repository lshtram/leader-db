**Inspection Priorities**
1. Kremlin program docs and annual speeches that reveal explicit goals.
2. Government national-project and budget notices that translate those goals into mechanisms.
3. Rosstat GDP, industry, poverty, housing, health, education, and demography outputs.
4. Reuters / Interfax / TASS reporting on labor shortages, defense production, inflation, grain, oil, and diplomacy.
5. Regional Russian-language statistics for subnational implementation and service delivery.
6. World Bank / IMF outlooks for structural constraints and whether outcomes moved as claimed.

**Gaps**
- More direct evidence is still needed on whether tactical adjustments were effective, especially when plans missed.
- Some of the strongest official pages are programmatic, not proof of execution.
- Subnational implementation in health, education, and housing is still thinner than macroeconomic and defense coverage.
- I still want more 2023 direct evidence on appointment quality and whether Putin replaced ineffective implementers quickly enough.
- For 8B, moral concerns are intentionally not part of the score logic, but I still need better outcome-attribution separation where sanctions, war, and inherited capacity confound the picture.

**Saturation Assessment**
- Saturated: war economy, technology sovereignty, budget pressure, labor shortages, and grain/energy adjustment.
- Moderately covered: national-project architecture, regional development, and transport/infrastructure.
- Weaker: direct implementation quality at the ministry/region level, and adaptation when specific tactics failed.
- The main remaining value is in tighter attribution and a few more subnational implementation records, not more broad macro summaries.
