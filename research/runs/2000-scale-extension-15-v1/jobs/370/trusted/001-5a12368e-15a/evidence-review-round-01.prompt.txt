## 0. Period framing and ruler identity

- Boris Yeltsin unexpectedly resigned on 31 December 1999 and named Prime Minister Putin acting president. Putin won the 26 March 2000 presidential election in the first round with 39,740,434 votes (52.94%; Central Election Commission tally). On the same day Putin signed Decree №1 granting Yeltsin and his family immunity from prosecution. Putin took the oath of office 7 May 2000 in the Andreyevsky Hall of the Grand Kremlin Palace — Russia's first peaceful democratic transfer of power between an elected president and his chosen successor.
- Putin inherited the Second Chechen War (started 1999, ongoing in 2000) as acting president/PM, an economy that had rebounded sharply after the 1998 ruble crisis, a federal structure dominated by governors, an oligarch-controlled media landscape (Gusinsky's Media-Most/NTV; Berezovsky's ORT), and a demographic crisis in which male life expectancy had fallen to ~59 years.
- Through 2000 he: created seven federal districts (May 13, 2000 Decree #849), signed a new military doctrine (21 April 2000), ratified START II (4 May 2000), pushed through a 13% flat income tax (signed August 2000, effective 1 January 2001), arrested Gusinsky (13 June 2000), and signed federal laws restoring the Soviet anthem and tsarist symbols (25 December 2000). The year is dominated by three crises: the Second Chechen War in its final large-scale phase, the August 12 Kursk nuclear submarine disaster, and the take-over of independent television (NTV/Gusinsky).
- Authority at start of period: Acting President from 31 Dec 1999 (executive powers) → elected President from 26 March 2000 → inaugurated 7 May 2000. Constitutional powers, control of security services, Kremlin-aligned Duma majority, no coalition constraint, no formal coalition partner.
