## Substantive Synthesis Notes (for downstream judge, NOT scoring)

For Rawlings / Ghana / 2000, the dossier establishes:

1. A non-nuclear ruler with `no_discriminating_opportunity` for chapter 1B (low exposure, no specific 2000 risk-bearing nuclear/existential choice surfaced).
2. A continuing peace-broker role (ECOWAS chairman since 1994, ECOMOG contributor, refugee host) with no new aggression initiated in 2000; thin documentation of intra-year mediation specifics.
3. A domestic safety record marked by ongoing criminal libel/sedition prosecutions against journalists critical of the ruler and his wife, prison-condition deterioration (106 deaths in 2000), election-period Bawku violence (~10–13 deaths), but also by continued operation of an independent CHRAJ and a free press that could criticize the government.
4. A defining political-freedom achievement: constitutional term-limit compliance, peaceful handover of power to opposition (the first in Ghana's history), credible OAU observer endorsement, code of domestic election observers (CODEO) accredited from 2000.
5. An economic programme (Vision 2020 + 1998-2000 IMF PFP) delivered with real but slowing 2000 outcomes (3.7% GDP growth, deteriorating terms of trade), with HIPC acceptance deferred to March 2001 under the successor administration; a corruption scandal implicating Rawlings' cousin's circle (Mabey & Johnson, 1994-99).
6. A social-wellbeing programme (Vision 2020 human-development themes) with national-level gains but severe regional disparity (northern poverty 70-90% vs. southern urban <10%); major cash-transfer and insurance reforms were launched after 2000 under Kufuor.
7. A personal-integrity record with concrete negative items (sedition prosecutions, family-associate corruption links, GNPC chief later tried for financial loss) but no judicial finding against Rawlings personally for 2000 conduct; CHRAJ investigations at his invitation in 1995-96 show some tolerance for independent scrutiny.
8. An effectiveness record showing macro slowing in 2000 alongside the most consequential execution achievement — a peaceful power handover — and an SPR energy programme that helped mitigate fuel shortages.

The dossier is reasonably saturated across all eight chapters; gaps reflect genuine absence of public record rather than research shortfall.
