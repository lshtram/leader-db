# Shehbaz Sharif, Pakistan, 2023

## 1. Overview

Shehbaz Sharif was Pakistan’s constitutional prime minister from 11 April 2022 until the National Assembly was dissolved on 9 August 2023. In 2023, his Pakistan Democratic Movement coalition governed through Parliament until August; a caretaker government then took office for the remainder of the year. His formal powers included cabinet leadership, budget and economic policy, federal administration, security coordination, and advice to the president on dissolution.

His practical influence was constrained by a fragile coalition, the courts, provincial governments, the Election Commission, severe fiscal dependence on the IMF and foreign creditors, and—most importantly—the military establishment. The June 2023 Special Investment Facilitation Council formally gave the army chief a role in economic decision-making. The federal government also had limited control over provincial policing and security, particularly in Khyber Pakhtunkhwa and Balochistan.

The year combined an IMF-supported stabilization effort with severe inflation, renewed militant violence, political repression after the 9 May unrest, delayed elections, restrictions on media and internet access, continued flood recovery needs, and the forced-return policy toward undocumented Afghans.

## 2. Evidence records

### Record 1 — Shehbaz Sharif’s authority was shared with powerful unelected institutions

**Finding.** Sharif chaired the federal government, but the 2023 creation of the Special Investment Facilitation Council institutionalized military participation in economic policy. The army chief and military officials received seats alongside civilian officials. This is important because it shows that the prime minister’s formal authority did not translate into exclusive control over major strategic decisions.

**Strongest source.** “Pakistan Establishes the Special Investment Facilitation Council,” UNCTAD Investment Policy Monitor, 20 June 2023, [direct link](https://investmentpolicy.unctad.org/investment-policy-monitor/measures/4366/pakistan-establishes-the-special-investment-facilitation-council-sifc/), section describing the council’s establishment and purpose.

**Period/status.** Directly concerns 2023.

**Connection to ruler.** Strong institutional connection: the council was established by Sharif’s government and its meetings were chaired by the prime minister.

**Complication.** The government presented the council as a coordination mechanism to reduce red tape and attract investment, not as a transfer of political authority.

**Cautions.** UNCTAD summarizes the measure but does not independently evaluate the informal balance of power. Military influence is partly an inference from institutional design and Pakistan’s broader civil-military structure.

## Workplan

1. **Orient.** Establish the ruler's formal and practical authority, the inherited situation, major external shocks or constraints, and the information environment.
2. **Plan coverage.** For every selected lens, identify the important favorable, adverse, disputed, and exculpatory possibilities that research should test.
3. **Find the main record.** Begin with strong overviews and syntheses. Identify the consequential decisions, laws, budgets, appointments, statements, implementation, corrections, and outcomes.

5. **Test the account.** Look for contrary evidence, independent corroboration, relevant local-language material, source dependencies, and alternative explanations. Distinguish inherited conditions and country context from conduct attributable to the ruler.
6. **Deepen where useful.** Repeat steps 3–5 while new searches add a material fact, a stronger source, a missing perspective, or a necessary correction. Finish when further searching mainly repeats the established record or reaches a concrete access barrier.
7. **Prepare the handoff.** Organize the evidence by underlying fact and map each developed claim to every selected lens it supports.
