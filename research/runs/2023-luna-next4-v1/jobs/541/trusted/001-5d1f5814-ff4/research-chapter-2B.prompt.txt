# Bongbong Marcos, Philippines, 2023

## 1. Overview

Ferdinand “Bongbong” Marcos Jr. held power throughout 2023 as president of the Philippines, commander-in-chief, head of the executive branch, and concurrent secretary of agriculture for much of the period. His authority was substantial but constrained by Congress, courts, constitutional commissions, local political dynasties, the military and police bureaucracy, coalition partners—including Vice President Sara Duterte—and dependence on foreign trade, investment, and security relationships.

The period combined an outwardly active foreign-policy reset toward the United States and Japan with continuing domestic rights problems inherited from Rodrigo Duterte. The administration pursued infrastructure, investment, agricultural, and food-price objectives. Economic growth remained positive, but inflation and rice prices generated political pressure. Reliable information was available from courts, oversight agencies, international organizations, and independent media, but human-rights reporting was impaired by intimidation, red-tagging, disappearances, official denial, and weak accountability.

## 2. Evidence records

### Record BM-2023-01 — Marcos held executive power while operating within a competitive constitutional system

**Claim and significance.** Marcos was the sitting Philippine president throughout 2023. He exercised national executive authority and command over the security apparatus, but policy implementation depended on Congress, constitutional bodies, courts, local governments, and coalition politics. This matters because national indicators cannot automatically be attributed to him personally; attribution is strongest where he issued orders, signed laws, or publicly directed policy.

**Strongest source.** The official Philippine executive orders and government releases identify Ferdinand R. Marcos Jr. as president and show him approving national policy measures, including the rice price ceiling and Maharlika Investment Fund.

**Period status.** Directly concerns 2023.

**Ruler connection.** High for presidential decisions and executive orders; moderate for broad outcomes involving agencies or Congress; low for inherited structural conditions.

**Cautions and complications.** Government sources describe policy intent and official accomplishments, not independent effectiveness. Presidential control over local government, police conduct, and military operations is real but mediated through bureaucratic and political networks.

**Independent support.** Human Rights Watch, Freedom House, the Commission on Human Rights, and BTI all treat Marcos as the responsible national executive while distinguishing his administration from inherited Duterte-era abuses. ## Workplan

1. **Orient.** Establish the ruler's formal and practical authority, the inherited situation, major external shocks or constraints, and the information environment.
2. **Plan coverage.** For every selected lens, identify the important favorable, adverse, disputed, and exculpatory possibilities that research should test.
3. **Find the main record.** Begin with strong overviews and syntheses. Identify the consequential decisions, laws, budgets, appointments, statements, implementation, corrections, and outcomes.
4. **Verify material claims.** Open the underlying sources. Use precise laws, official records, audits, judgments, datasets, scholarship, monitoring, and credible reporting where they establish or challenge an important claim. Assess each source’s authority, independence, method, proximity, and date.
5. **Test the account.** Look for contrary evidence, independent corroboration, relevant local-language material, source dependencies, and alternative explanations. Distinguish inherited conditions and country context from conduct attributable to the ruler.
6. **Deepen where useful.** Repeat steps 3–5 while new searches add a material fact, a stronger source, a missing perspective, or a necessary correction. Finish when further searching mainly repeats the established record or reaches a concrete access barrier.
7. **Prepare the handoff.** Organize the evidence by underlying fact and map each developed claim to every selected lens it supports.
