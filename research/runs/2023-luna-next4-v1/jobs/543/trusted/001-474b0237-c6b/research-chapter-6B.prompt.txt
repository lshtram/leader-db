# Prayut Chan-o-cha — Thailand, 2023

## 1. Overview

General Prayut Chan-o-cha remained Thailand’s prime minister at the start of 2023 and governed through a military-backed coalition under the 2017 constitution. He was also leader of the United Thai Nation Party, while rival General Prawit Wongsuwan led Palang Pracharath. Prayut’s influence was substantial but constrained by the monarchy, the military establishment, a junta-appointed Senate, coalition bargaining, the Constitutional Court, and powerful bureaucratic and security institutions.

The May 2023 election was competitive and produced a decisive rejection of the military-backed parties: Move Forward won the most House seats, 151, while Prayut’s party won 36. The military-appointed Senate then helped prevent Move Forward leader Pita Limjaroenrat from forming a government. Pheu Thai subsequently formed a coalition that included former military-aligned parties, and Srettha Thavisin became prime minister in August. Prayut therefore governed most of the requested period but left office after the election and transition.

The strongest overall interpretation is continuity rather than policy transformation: economic recovery continued, especially through tourism, but political liberalization remained sharply limited and the government did not resolve the southern insurgency, corruption concerns, or the institutional dominance of the military and monarchy.

## 2. Evidence records

### Record PRAYUT-2023-01 — Prayut’s authority was institutionally constrained by the military-monarchical order

**Fact.** Prayut formally headed the cabinet and controlled the executive branch, but his effective authority operated within a political system in which the monarchy, military, appointed Senate, Constitutional Court, and senior bureaucracy retained decisive influence. The 2017 constitution allowed the junta-appointed Senate to participate in choosing the prime minister. Prayut was influential within the military but depended on coalition partners and institutional allies.

**Why it matters.** Government outcomes cannot be attributed to Prayut alone. His authority was real, but embedded in a broader military-monarchical system.

**Strongest source.** *BTI 2024 Country Report: Thailand*, Bertelsmann Stiftung, 2024, pp. 3–10. The report describes the junta-created constitution, appointed Senate, military influence, coalition structure, and Prayut’s continuing political role. It covers February 2021–January 2023, so it is primarily inherited-period evidence rather than a full 2023 assessment: [BTI 2024 Thailand report](https://bti-project.org/fileadmin/api/content/en/downloads/reports/country_report_2024_THA.pdf).

**Connection to ruler.** Strong for the institutional conditions under which Prayut governed; weaker for decisions made after January 2023.

**Complicating evidence.** Prayut still exercised ordinary executive powers, led the cabinet, represented Thailand internationally, and remained the candidate

## Workplan

1. **Orient.** Establish the ruler's formal and practical authority, the inherited situation, major external shocks or constraints, and the information environment.
2. **Plan coverage.** For every selected lens, identify the important favorable, adverse, disputed, and exculpatory possibilities that research should test.
3. **Find the main record.** Begin with strong overviews and syntheses. Identify the consequential decisions, laws, budgets, appointments, statements, implementation, corrections, and outcomes.
4. **Verify material claims.** Open the underlying sources. Use precise laws, official records, audits, judgments, datasets, scholarship, monitoring, and credible reporting where they establish or challenge an important claim. Assess each source’s authority, independence, method, proximity, and date.
5. **Test the account.** Look for contrary evidence, independent corroboration, relevant local-language material, source dependencies, and alternative explanations. Distinguish inherited conditions and country context from conduct attributable to the ruler.
6. **Deepen where useful.** Repeat steps 3–5 while new searches add a material fact, a stronger source, a missing perspective, or a necessary correction. Finish when further searching mainly repeats the established record or reaches a concrete access barrier.
7. **Prepare the handoff.** Organize the evidence by underlying fact and map each developed claim to every selected lens it supports.
