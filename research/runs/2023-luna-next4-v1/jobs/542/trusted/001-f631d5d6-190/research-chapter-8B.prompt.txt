# Vladimir Putin, Russian Federation, 2023

## 1. Overview

Vladimir Putin was President of the Russian Federation throughout 2023 and remained the decisive actor in foreign policy, military strategy, security policy, senior appointments, and the legislative agenda. His formal authority was extensive, but implementation depended on the security services, military command, regional administrations, courts, state-owned enterprises, and politically connected economic actors. The June 2023 Wagner mutiny demonstrated that coercive authority was not perfectly unified: Yevgeny Prigozhin’s forces advanced toward Moscow before a negotiated withdrawal.

The period was dominated by the continuing full-scale war against Ukraine, intensified domestic repression, military mobilization and war spending, efforts to preserve economic stability under sanctions, and preparation for the 2024 presidential election. Information was difficult to assess because Russian authorities criminalized independent war reporting, restricted civil society, controlled major media, and reduced transparency around military casualties and wartime spending.

## 2. Evidence records

### Record VP-2023-01 — Putin’s formal office and concentration of executive authority

Putin held the office of President of the Russian Federation in 2023. The presidency controlled the armed forces, foreign policy, senior security appointments, and the issuance of binding decrees. The legislature and courts remained formally separate institutions, but available comparative assessments describe Russia as an authoritarian system with severely restricted political competition and judicial independence.

This matters because attribution of national security, repression, and strategic policy decisions to Putin is strong, although many operational acts were carried out by subordinate ministries, security agencies, regional officials, and military commanders.

- Strongest source: Freedom House, “Russia: Freedom in the World 2023,” Freedom House, 2023, country assessment sections on political pluralism, executive dominance, speech, and rule of law. [Direct link](https://freedomhouse.org/country/russia/freedom-world/2023)
- Period: 2023, with some inherited institutional conditions.
- Attribution: Strong for presidential direction and appointments; weaker for individual abuses not directly documented as presidential orders.
- Complicating evidence: Formal constitutional institutions, elections, courts, and parliament continued to operate and sometimes affected implementation.
- Cautions: Freedom House is an advocacy-oriented comparative evaluator; its ratings should be combined with official records, court materials, and independent reporting.
- Independent support: V-Dem, BTI, Freedom House Nations in Transit, and the 2023 OVD-Info review.

## Workplan

1. **Orient.** Establish the ruler's formal and practical authority, the inherited situation, major external shocks or constraints, and the information environment.
2. **Plan coverage.** For every selected lens, identify the important favorable, adverse, disputed, and exculpatory possibilities that research should test.
3. **Find the main record.** Begin with strong overviews and syntheses. Identify the consequential decisions, laws, budgets, appointments, statements, implementation, corrections, and outcomes.
4. **Verify material claims.** Open the underlying sources. Use precise laws, official records, audits, judgments, datasets, scholarship, monitoring, and credible reporting where they establish or challenge an important claim. Assess each source’s authority, independence, method, proximity, and date.
5. **Test the account.** Look for contrary evidence, independent corroboration, relevant local-language material, source dependencies, and alternative explanations. Distinguish inherited conditions and country context from conduct attributable to the ruler.
6. **Deepen where useful.** Repeat steps 3–5 while new searches add a material fact, a stronger source, a missing perspective, or a necessary correction. Finish when further searching mainly repeats the established record or reaches a concrete access barrier.
7. **Prepare the handoff.** Organize the evidence by underlying fact and map each developed claim to every selected lens it supports.
