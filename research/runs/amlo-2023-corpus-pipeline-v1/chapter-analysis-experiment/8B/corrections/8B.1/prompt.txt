# 8B. Ruler Effectiveness and Competence

Status: draft; v4 objective-evidence questions require controlled rejudgment

## Chapter Identity

- Chapter: `8B`
- Category: `ruler_effectiveness_and_competence`
- Evidence strategy: one ruler-period dossier, researched across ten overlapping lenses
- Judge strategy: one chapter judge applies one comparative meter across all eligible rulers
- Rubric version: `chapter_8b_v4`

## Chapter Purpose

Assess how effectively the ruler defines a governing program, converts it into
state or ruling-network action, coordinates implementation, adapts, and advances
their own stated or reliably revealed objectives. The ten questions are evidence
lenses feeding one final 1-10 chapter score, not equal sub-scores.

This chapter is ideology-neutral. Moral worth and execution competence are
separate. A ruler may execute a cruel, aggressive, corrupt, or authoritarian
program competently and score highly in 8B while scoring very poorly in other
chapters. Never smuggle moral approval or condemnation into the competence score.

## Ten Evidence Lenses

|---|---|---|---|

| **8B.3 — Mobilizing the ruling system** | Did the ruler effectively mobilize the state and ruling network toward those goals? | Did executed resources and administrative records show effective mobilization of the state, party, military, coalition, or ruling network toward the ruler's chosen program? | **Resources**; **Personnel**; **Implementation and operational conduct** |

## Scope and Non-Goals

- Establish an explicit or reliably revealed program before judging execution.
  Repeated discriminating choices may reveal goals; hindsight speculation may not.
- Evaluate strategic clarity, operationalization, mobilization, personnel fit,
  coordination, implementation, outcomes, adaptation, crisis management, and
  durable progress as mutually informing evidence.
- Do not judge whether goals are democratic, peaceful, lawful, prosperous, humane,
  or personally honest. Those judgments belong in 1B-7B.
- Do not reward repression, propaganda, longevity, survival, economic growth,
  administrative size, or institutional quality by itself. Ask whether it
  effectively served the ruler's identified program.
- Do not assume failure from an outcome the ruler did not seek, except where
  maintaining basic government function was necessarily part of governing.
- Do not use the client matrix as evidence.

Before reading outcomes or assigning a score, freeze a portfolio of the ruler's major
stated or reliably revealed programs plus necessary core-government functions. Apply
the same breadth rule across regime types. Do not cherry-pick the easiest achieved
goal, reduce a democratic government to its broadest welfare promises, or reduce an
authoritarian government to survival, weapons, or power consolidation. Survival and
consolidation may be one material program component, never the entire portfolio by
default.

## Researcher Workflow and Evidence Themes

Follow the local-first researcher guide and source-confidence registry. Query the
exact case locally. WGI, V-Dem, BTI, budgets, and outcome indicators are useful
context or corroboration, but generic state capacity is not ruler effectiveness.

Research chronologically across themes rather than issuing one search per lens:

1. Identify stated programs from platforms, manifestos, coalition agreements,
   speeches, strategies, directives, budgets, and plans. Identify revealed goals
   only from repeated choices that distinguish them from rhetoric or speculation.
2. Trace conversion into priorities, resources, laws, institutions, appointments,
   timelines, enforcement, and measurable milestones.
3. Examine whether the state, party, military, coalition, bureaucracy, territorial
   network, or informal machinery actually mobilized, coordinated, and followed
   through; include bottlenecks and implementation gaps.
4. Assess whether personnel were capable for the assigned program. Ideological or
   coercive operators may be effective implementers in this narrow chapter even
   when their conduct is reprehensible elsewhere.
5. Compare promised/revealed objectives with implementation and outcomes after
   realistic lags, inherited trends, peer conditions, shocks, and resources.
6. Trace learning, replacement, reallocation, crisis response, resistance
   management, short-term gains, reversals, durability, and end-of-period position.

Prefer primary program and budget documents, legislation, administrative and
audit records, implementation data, credible outcome series, contemporaneous
records, serious academic histories, independent evaluations, and reputable
reporting. Official sources establish declared goals and formal actions but not
successful execution by themselves. Preserve failures, contradictions, abandoned
priorities, propaganda-only claims, alternative causal explanations, and adverse
side effects; record the latter for cross-chapter use without morally deducting
them in 8B.

Reuse each stable evidence item across relevant lenses. Natural-language coverage
and gaps are acceptable; publication must not depend on exact status terms,
sequential IDs, or exhaustive mapping syntax.

## Common Evidence-Item Semantics

Each material evidence item should carry a stable evidence ID; a concise factual
claim; source URL, title, publisher, and publication/access date where available;
source type and confidence with a short reason; a quotation or precise locator
when available; target-year/ruler-period fit; ruler attribution and
inherited-baseline relevance; its positive, negative, mixed, contextual, or
contrary role; and the supported lens IDs. Fields may be normalized later and
natural-language equivalents are acceptable. The substantive minimum is a
traceable source, intelligible claim, temporal fit, attribution, and evidence
role. One item may support multiple lenses without duplication.

## Attribution, Sparse Evidence, and Historical Cases

Separate ruler capability from inherited state capacity, predecessor programs,
subordinate initiative, favorable trends, external patrons, and luck. Assess
formal/informal control, tenure, coalition and institutional constraints,
resources, shocks, lag, ownership of appointments and priorities, response to
implementation information, and plausible counterfactual trajectory.

For obscure or historical rulers, later scholarship, archives, diplomatic
records, budgets, institutional histories, and observable implementation may be
more informative than missing modern indicators. Oral or informal programs can
be evidenced by repeated choices. Sparse evidence should reduce confidence and
widen the defensible range, not force a neutral score or invalidate the case.
Short tenure may permit judgment of direction and mobilization while leaving
outcomes unresolved. Closed systems may document plans abundantly while hiding
failures; official volume is not proof of execution.

## Holistic 1-10 Chapter Rubric

Synthesize the causal chain from program through action to durable progress.
Lenses are redundant and qualitatively weighted: clear plans with no execution
cannot score highly; favorable outcomes without ruler-attributable direction or
action warrant limited credit; a major strategic collapse can outweigh several
administrative successes. Never calculate a simple mean or score by citation
count.

Strategic clarity, centralized control, personnel discipline, and planning without
independently evidenced implementation and goal progress cannot exceed 5. Scores above
5 require attributable implementation plus progress across material parts of the
frozen portfolio. Scores above 7 additionally require independent evidence of outcomes,
durability, and adaptation rather than official architecture, coercion, longevity, or
survival alone. Harmful programs remain morally neutral here, but they must still show
actual advancement rather than mere capacity to command.

| Score | Chapter anchor |
|---:|---|
| 1 | No coherent program or effective governing control, or catastrophic inability to mobilize and execute: recurring chaos, paralysis, collapse, or decisive movement away from the ruler's own objectives. |
| 2-3 | Weak or unstable direction and serious operational failure; plans remain largely symbolic, coordination repeatedly breaks down, adaptation is poor, and major objectives substantially recede. |
| 4-5 | Mixed competence: some clear priorities and real delivery coexist with major implementation gaps, inconsistent control, failed initiatives, weak adaptation, or fragile/temporary results. |
| 6-7 | Generally effective direction and execution across important priorities, with meaningful mobilization and progress but notable delays, failures, constraints, reversals, or durability concerns. |
| 8-9 | Strong strategic-operational alignment, capable personnel and coordination, adaptive implementation, and substantial durable advancement of the ruler's program across major priorities. |
| 10 | Exceptional, sustained mastery of direction, mobilization, execution, adaptation, crisis/resistance management, and durable goal realization under demanding conditions. |

These anchors apply equally to benevolent and harmful programs. The rationale
must separately name the program and state that moral effects are evaluated in
other chapters.

## Confidence

Report confidence separately on a 0-100 scale. Consider program identification,
ruler attribution, causal trace from inputs to implementation/outcomes, source
independence, time fit and lag, external-shock controls, source/theme diversity,
closed-system opacity, and contrary explanations. Missing lenses lower confidence
rather than automatically lowering the chapter score. Give a defensible score
range where evidence or attribution is materially uncertain.

## One-Judge Comparative Workflow

One judge owns 8B across all eligible rulers for the target year/period:

1. Read every 8B dossier, identify each ruler's explicit or reliably revealed
   program, and quarantine identity failures—not sparse evidence.
2. Separate moral evaluation from execution before scoring. Record cross-chapter
   harms without deducting them here.
3. Establish batch anchors spanning democratic and authoritarian, beneficial and
   harmful, modern and historical, high- and low-capacity contexts.
4. Assign tentative holistic scores along the program-to-results chain, then
   compare neighbors for consistent treatment of inherited capacity, resources,
   shocks, tenure, lag, luck, resistance, and durability.
5. Revisit cases dominated by generic governance indicators, official claims,
   survival/tenure, favorable outcomes without attribution, or moral halo/horns.
6. Emit one chapter score, confidence/range, identified program, rationale,
   strongest execution and failure evidence, unresolved causal alternatives,
   missing themes, and review flag.

## Acceptance Checklist

- Missing implementation evidence never establishes ineffectiveness; it produces a
  null when no ruler-attributed execution fact remains. Scores above `5` require
  observable implementation or coordination, and scores above `7` additionally
  require independent evidence of attributable outcomes, adaptation, or durable
  progress across more than one material program component.

- All ten authoritative lenses are present verbatim and serve one holistic score.
- Ideology-neutrality is explicit and moral worth is separated from execution in
  research, scoring, calibration, and rationale.
- An explicit or reliably revealed program is established before execution is
  judged; hindsight speculation is labeled and constrained.
- Local-first order, citations, source profiling, and run profile are retained.
- Evidence items satisfy the common traceability, temporal-fit, attribution,
  evidence-role, and lens-link semantics.
- Ruler action is separated from inherited capacity, subordinates, luck, trends,
  shocks, tenure, and realistic outcome lags.
- Missing lenses reduce confidence rather than invalidate or mechanically depress
  the score.
- One judge compares all rulers using one meter; lenses are neither equally
  weighted nor mechanically averaged.
- Final output uses the common chapter-judge envelope, including adjacent-anchor
  comparisons and an explicit manual-review flag.
- Smoke testing and revision occur before production activation.

But Facts Show it is a Failed Strategy\nMexico\nPeace & Security\n,\nRule of Law\nTeam WOLA\nContrary to his campaign promises to “return the army to its barracks,” Mexican President Andrés Manuel López Obrador has announced his intention to reform the National Guard so that it is “completely under” the authority of the Ministry of Defense (Sedena). Thus, the current government threatens to continue deepening the militarization of public security despite the high human rights costs of this strategy and its proven failure to address violence and drug trafficking, two major challenges in the country.\nWe spoke with WOLA’s Director for Mexico Stephanie Brewer about the present, past, and potential future of militarization as a security strategy in the country and the state of bilateral relations with the United States.\n\n1. What is the López Obrador administration’s current proposal on security and militarization and what are its possible implications?\nLet’s start with the context. Mexico faces high levels of insecurity and violence, with more than 35,000 homicides registered annually, a huge increase in the last 15 years, and more than 100,000 people recognized as missing or disappeared. Violence, including homicides, disappearances, and other forms of violence, has become a sadly common tool used by different actors and groups to impose their will, send messages, and seek to control illegal markets and territories.\nIn this context, President López Obrador says that the deployment of military forces in police tasks throughout the country must be expanded and made practically permanent. He says that the armed forces are the only ones who can take care of this serious problem, and that local and civilian institutions can’t handle it.\nThis is the same argument that Mexico’s federal government has pushed for the last decade and a half, at least, with disastrous security and human rights consequences. When Felipe Calderón became president in 2006, he announced militarization as a temporary measure that would supposedly allow the government to strengthen police and other institutions and regain territorial control in certain areas of the country. What actually happened was that military deployment was prolonged throughout the Calderón administration, and then continued with the administration of President Enrique Peña Nieto.\n2. But before López Obrador was elected in 2018 he had promised to return the armed forces to their barracks…\nYes, that’s what he had promised prior to being elected, but, on the contrary, one of his first actions was the creation of a National Guard to replace the Federal Police. This National Guard was constitutionally established as a civilian security force, but it has been managed from the start as a military force. The vast majority of its more than 100,000 members are military and, in practice, it is already under the operational control of Sedena. And that’s not all: in addition to the National Guard, the armed forces (the army and the navy) are still deployed. So today, military deployment is at its highest in recent history.\nWith López Obrador, we can see certain changes in the way the armed forces are used. Armed confrontations between security forces and civilians, for example, have dropped in absolute terms. The strategy, especially with the National Guard, has been to increase the territorial presence of state forces, which may be relevant, especially in certain areas, but it is far from enough to have a significant impact on the situation of insecurity and crime.\nThere’s another change with López Obrador: he doesn’t speak of militarization as a temporary measure, but rather has insisted that the National Guard become part of Sedena, that it become an armed force, so that there is no longer any federal civilian police force in Mexico. He says that what he wants is to make it very difficult or impossible for future governments to reverse this model. And he has also said in recent weeks that the deployment of the army and the navy must also continue long term, beyond 2024, which is currently the deadline established by a transitory article of the constitutional reform that created the National Guard in 2019.\n3. This could have strong implications for the future in Mexico.\nOf course. López Obrador has already sent a bill to the Mexican Congress to reform a series of laws to give Sedena operational and administrative control of the National Guard, seeking to make it a body made up entirely of military personnel, which could even assist the armed forces in purely military missions.\nThis goes against the Constitution of Mexico, which, in article 21, establishes that the National Guard is a civilian police institution.\n4. What implications do these proposals have for the power and role of the armed forces\nvis-à-vis\nthe civilian government?\nWhat these proposals seem to seek is to deepen the government’s clear tendency to grant more and more civilian functions and budget, and thus more power, to military institutions, especially Sedena.\nUnder this model, the armed forces are not only deployed in policing tasks, but also have a very important role in migration control tasks at the borders, are in charge of ports and customs, and build and manage large infrastructure projects, among others.\nIt would seem that, instead of investing in the consolidation of the institutions that any democratic government should have, the answer is to skip that necessary step and deploy the armed forces instead. Certainly, militarization seems to offer an easy or quick way to present solutions to the population and say, “look, I’m taking firm actions, this is how we’re going to end corruption, this is how we’re going to provide security”, but all the evidence so far tells us that the desired results will not be achieved this way. On the contrary, this path of militarization is intensifying a dynamic in which the Mexican armed forces, which have always enjoyed a worrying degree of autonomy and lack of transparency and accountability, will now have more and more power\nvis-à-vis\ncivilian authorities.\n5. What consequences could this proposal have in terms of human rights, specifically for migrants, women, and indigenous people, who have been some of the most affected historically by militarization?\nPresident López Obrador has said that he has given instructions for the armed forces not to repress and not to violate human rights. Of course, even with instructions, there continue to be cases of excessive use of force and arbitrary deprivation of life by members of the armed forces and the National Guard during his government.\nAt the same time, available data show a reduction, in absolute terms, in the levels of serious human rights violations such as torture, enforced disappearances, and extrajudicial executions as compared to ten years ago, for example, during the Calderón administration.\nWhat we have to remember, however, is that these military institutions are the same ones responsible for the levels of abuses seen just a few years ago and they have not undergone any effective accountability process. Nor have they been reformed to guarantee transparency and build effective control mechanisms. In the case of the National Guard, for example, the government has even refused to comply with a ruling of the Inter-American Court of Human Rights that mandated the creation of an external oversight mechanism.\nAdditionally, neither the National Human Rights Commission nor the National Prosecutor’s Office have been effective control mechanisms in terms of investigations of military abuses.\n6. What public policies should Mexico implement to effectively address the current situation of violence and insecurity?\n\nViolence and the operational capacity of criminal groups persist in Mexico essentially because the country has not consolidated a reliable, capable, and well-resourced criminal investigation system that solves and punishes crimes and can investigate complex criminal phenomena.\nSecurity and justice institutions operate, especially in the states, in often precarious conditions, with deep-rooted problems of lack of capacity, but also frequently of corruption. This contributes to the fact that the overwhelming majority of crimes committed against the population go unpunished.\nThe key pending task, and I’m not saying anything new here, is to strengthen and reform civil institutions so that the country has a system capable of responding to and preventing crimes. I’m not saying there are no efforts and projects in that direction, but so far militarization has captured political and financial resources while the strengthening of civilian institutions does not receive sufficient attention.\n7. Allí, bajo un gran rótulo que decía \"Gracias, México, no les voy a fallar\", López Obrador agradeció a sus oponentes el rápido reconocimiento de su triunfo en este \"día histórico\", llamó \"a todos los mexicanos a la reconciliación\", prometió \"erradicar\" la corrupción, \"que no es un fenómeno cultural, sino el resultado de un régimen en decadencia\", y tendió la mano a Trump, ofreciéndole \"una relación de amistad y cooperación para el desarrollo, siempre afincada en el respeto mutuo y en la defensa de nuestros paisanos que viven y trabajan honradamente en Estados Unidos\". Precisamente, Trump publicó en Twitter un cordial mensaje de felicitación y buena disposición para su flamante par mexicano.\n\nA continuación, López Obrador y su comitiva de colaboradores y familiares se trasladaron al Zócalo, donde les esperaban una muchedumbre enfervorizada, lista para escuchar el discurso de la victoria hasta bien entrada la madrugada del lunes 2 de julio. A sus seguidores,\nAMLO\nles garantizó la aplicación de \"los tres principios básicos: no mentir, no robar y no traicionar al pueblo\", anunció una \"transición con armonía\", desarrollada \"de manera ordenada, pacífica\", y prometió que los cambios iban a ser \"profundos, pero con apego al orden legal establecido\". \"Escucharemos a todos, atenderemos a todos, respetaremos a todos, pero daremos preferencia a los más humildes y olvidados; en especial, a los pueblos indígenas de México. Por el bien de todos, primero los pobres, dijo el orador, que recalcó también: \"Bajo ninguna circunstancia, el próximo presidente permitirá la corrupción ni la impunidad. Sobre aviso no hay engaño: sea quien sea, será castigado. Incluyo a compañeros de lucha, funcionarios, amigos y familiares\".\nEl 8 de julio, una vez completados los cómputos distritales, el INE publicó los resultados finales del escrutinio, que concedían la Presidencia a López Obrador con el 53,19% de los votos, habiendo alcanzado la participación el 64,4% del censo. Anaya había quedado segundo con el 22,27%, Meade tercero con el 16,40% y el independiente Jaime Rodríguez Calderón cuarto con el 5,23%. El morenista había sido el más votado en todos los estados salvo Guanajuato, decantado por Anaya. El 8 de agosto, por ultimo, el TEPJF dictaminó la validez de todo el proceso electoral y entregó la constancia de mayoría al ganador, haciéndose oficial su condición de presidente electo.\nLos resultados de las elecciones al Congreso de la Unión y de las celebradas en la mayoría de los estados terminaron de dibujar el alcance de la victoria de MORENA, comparable a un tsunami, y la derrota del PRI, calificable de colapso. El próximo Gobierno de López Obrador iba a gozar de mayoría absoluta en los dos hemiciclos federales: en la Cámara de Diputados, los tres partidos de Juntos Haremos Historia sumaban 308 escaños de 500 y en el Senado 69 escaños de 128. De las nueve gubernaturas sometidas a renovación el 1 de julio, MORENA se llevó las de la Ciudad de México (para Claudia Sheinbaum), Chiapas (Rutilio Escandón), Tabasco (Adán Augusto López) y Veracruz (Cuitláhuac García), mientras que su aliado el PES (Cuauhtémoc Blanco) se hizo con la de Morelos. El PAN, por su parte, retuvo los ejecutivos de Guanajuato y Puebla, y arrebató al PRI el de Yucatán, lo mismo que el Movimiento Ciudadano el de Jalisco.
