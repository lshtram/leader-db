## 1. Ruler-period orientation (one-pass reconnaissance)

Erdoğan entered 2023 as incumbent president (since 28 Aug 2014), trailing the AKP-MHP "People's Alliance" into simultaneous presidential and parliamentary elections on 14 May 2023. The first round pushed the race into a 28 May runoff — the first in Türkiye's presidential history. Three weeks before voting, two Mw 7.8 / 7.7 doublet earthquakes devastated 11 southeastern provinces on 6 February 2023 (≥50,000 dead, 1.5 million homeless, $34bn World Bank direct-damage estimate). The handling of the disaster, documented by BBC and Time, materially eroded but did not erase his political standing. He won 52.18% (27,834,589 votes) against CHP leader Kemal Kılıçdaroğlu's 47.82%; AKP and the People's Alliance retained parliamentary dominance (AKP 268 seats; AKP+MHP+New Welfare 323 seats; CHP 169; YSGP 61). The OSCE/ODIHR/Council of Europe observation mission concluded the ruling parties "enjoyed an unjustified advantage, including through biased media coverage" and that public broadcasters "clearly favoured the ruling parties."

In the second cabinet term, Erdoğan appointed Mehmet Şimşek (former finance minister 2009–2015 and former Merrill Lynch chief economist) as Treasury and Finance Minister on 4 June 2023, and Hafize Gaye Erkan (former First Republic Bank CEO) as Central Bank governor. Şimşek explicitly framed his mandate as a return to "rational" monetary policy; the CBRT raised its policy rate from 8.5% (pre-election) to 25% by August 2023 and 42.5% by year-end. Annual CPI inflation nonetheless stood at ~65% by December 2023. This is the late-2023 "Erdoğanomics" partial reversal of his decade-long interest-rate-cuts-against-inflation doctrine.

On the international security front: (a) the 23 October 2023 PKK suicide attack on the Interior Ministry in Ankara triggered (b) 5–10 October 2023 Turkish drone and air strikes across northeast Syria targeting power and water infrastructure affecting ~4.3 million people, and (c) the broader PKK-Türkiye conflict in northern Iraq. Sweden NATO accession was advanced only on 23 October 2023, when Erdoğan signed the accession protocol and sent it to the TBMM (final ratification came in January 2024). Türkiye was a non-nuclear NPT state with no operative nuclear capability in 2023; the FAS D17 facts (unavailable in the local package) confirmed this absence, not a posture choice. Erdoğan's October 2023 response to the Gaza war marked a rupture from the 2022 rapprochement with Israel: he called the Israeli blockade and bombing "a massacre," offered Turkish mediation, and publicly defended Hamas.

Domestic repression continued to compound: Gezi trial convictions upheld by the Court of Cassation 28 September 2023 (Kavala life sentence; 18 years for four co-defendants); Osman Kavala remained in prison despite two binding ECtHR judgments and Council of Europe infringement proceedings; 18 Kurdish journalists on trial in Diyarbakır (15 released after 13 months pretrial), 11 in Ankara (9 released after 7 months); at least ~40 journalists in pretrial detention or serving sentences per Expression Interrupted; RTÜK fines against Halk TV, Tele 1, and others; the September 2023 closure case against the HDP before the Constitutional Court (the HDP ran instead under the Green Left Party banner and won 61 seats); İmamoğlu corruption trial opened in June 2023; ~15,050 FETÖ convicts in custody per the Justice Minister's August 2023 statement.
