- **8B.3 mobilization:** Strong ability to mobilize military/security and ruling-party networks; governance reform and education delivery show administrative action.
- **8B.4 personnel:** Competitive appointments in selected utilities/funds; broader personnel quality unresolved.
- **8B.5 coordination:** Serious failures in election logistics, health staffing, education conditions, proxy relationships, and security-force control.

Rights abuses, repression, and proxy use are relevant here only insofar as they demonstrate control, coordination, implementation, or failure against the identified program. Their moral wrongness belongs primarily in Chapters 2B–4B and did not determine this research mapping.

- Budget execution by major program.
- Independent outcome data across more than education.
- Appointment and coordination chains.
- Evidence that tactical security changes reflected learning rather than reactive substitution.
- Durability beyond the election year.

### Saturation rationale

The program-to-action chain is well represented across multiple sectors and source types, but independent outcome evidence is uneven. The chapter has substantial breadth; remaining gaps primarily concern causal attribution, durability, and exact execution rates.

## Chapter results

### 2B

The AU communiqué materially improves diplomatic attribution: Tshisekedi personally participated in the Luanda process, which commended his dialogue commitment and established coordinated political, military, humanitarian, and security timelines. The IMF review also clarifies that security and elections materially redirected fiscal spending.

Proxy-control and operational-command evidence remains unresolved. The opened sources do not establish that Tshisekedi knew of specific allied-group abuses or personally ordered unlawful operations.

### 3B

The State Department report adds precise national evidence on killings, torture, arbitrary detention, prison conditions, assembly restrictions, and limited prosecutions. It also preserves contrary evidence: some officials were investigated, prosecuted, and punished.

The evidence still does not establish prior presidential knowledge or an order for the Goma massacre. National prevalence and follow-up after the narrow command investigation remain gaps.

### 4B

The AP election report adds direct election-day logistics and access failures, including delayed openings, faulty machines, displaced-voter exclusion, and extended voting. These are strong administration-level execution evidence but do not prove presidential manipulation.

The CENI appointment chain, observer findings, and technical ambiguity around EU equipment remain the appropriate attribution limits.

### 5B

The IMF fifth review closes the previously blocked macroeconomic lead at claim level: growth remained resilient, but fiscal pressure increased as spending shifted toward security and elections, with revenue shortfalls and continuing reform risks.

The PDL-145T audit-resistance evidence strengthens the governance and execution record. Household poverty, wages, employment, inequality, and regional distribution remain only partly recoverable; official VNR inequality and water figures are useful but self-reported or not exact-year outcomes.

### 6B

UNICEF adds measurable 2023 education disruption, cholera, displacement, water, sanitation, and child-protection evidence. These materially broaden the chapter beyond maternity care and education design.

The evidence remains shared among government, conflict actors, donors, and humanitarian agencies. Mortality, learning outcomes, housing, disability outcomes, and durable social-protection delivery remain weak.

### 8B

Effectiveness evidence now covers:

- coordinated peace diplomacy;
- macroeconomic reprioritization under security and election pressure;
- PDL-145T audit and execution failures;
- election logistics and voter-access problems;
- education disruption;
- cholera and humanitarian response;
- water, sanitation, and inequality conditions.

The central conclusion remains mixed at the evidence level: formal programs and corrective instructions existed, but execution, coordination, and outcomes were uneven.
