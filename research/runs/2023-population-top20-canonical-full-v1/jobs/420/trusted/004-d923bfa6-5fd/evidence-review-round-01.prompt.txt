### Saturation rationale

Searches were broad and adverse, but reliable target-period personal-integrity evidence remained sparse. The chapter reached the numeric unit goal only by including institutional context; personal nexus remains the major unresolved weakness.

## Chapter 8B — Effectiveness and competence

### Local baseline

LP-003 provides weak accountability, rule-of-law, and BTI governance context. These measure inherited/state capacity and outcomes, not ruler effectiveness. They cannot be used without an identified program and execution evidence.

### Candidate-pool searches

- `Tshisekedi 2023 government programme achievements PDL-145T implementation audit`
- `RDC 2023 Tshisekedi PDL 145 territoires bilan exécution audit`
- `Félix Tshisekedi 2023 cabinet appointments economic reform IMF program implementation`
- `DRC 2023 election observers Tshisekedi irregularities`
- `DRC 2023 Tshisekedi Luanda Nairobi process M23 diplomacy`
- Adverse searches for implementation gaps in education, health, security, election administration, and development programs.

### Frozen program portfolio supported by evidence

1. Restore security and state control in eastern DRC.
2. Continue free primary education and expand human-capital access.
3. Launch/expand free maternity and health coverage.
4. Implement economic-governance and transparency reforms.
5. Organize the 2023 electoral cycle and secure a second constitutional term.
6. Develop underserved territories and strengthen basic state reach—partially evidenced, but PDL-145T exact 2023 execution remained unresolved.

### Retained units

LP-003, WEB-001–WEB-003, WEB-010–WEB-012, WEB-014, WEB-017–WEB-026.

- **Mapped source-claim units:** 18
- **Organizations:** Carter Center, HRW, Amnesty, ISS, World Bank, Guardian, APRI
- **Source types:** election observer, NGO, policy institute, intergovernmental, media, structured datasets

### Lens coverage

- **8B.1 strategic direction:** Clear programs in security, education, health, governance reform, and reelection.
- **8B.2 operationalization:** State of siege, regional force deployments, World Bank-backed reform milestones, education projects, maternity rollout, and election administration.
- **8B.3 mobilization:** Strong ability to mobilize military/security and ruling-party networks; governance reform and education delivery show administrative action.
- **8B.4 personnel:** Competitive appointments in selected utilities/funds; broader personnel quality unresolved.
- **8B.5 coordination:** Serious failures in election logistics, health staffing, education conditions, proxy relationships, and security-force control.
- **8B.6 declarations to implementation:** Free education and governance reforms show implementation; transitional justice, security restoration, and some welfare programs showed major gaps.
- **8B.7 outcomes:** Enrollment expanded, but security deteriorated; election administration fell short; broader economic and welfare outcomes remain incompletely sourced.
- **8B.8 adaptation:** He shifted among UN, Ugandan, EAC, SADC, state, militia, and private-security approaches, showing tactical change but not clearly effective learning. Health and education correction evidence is weak.
- **8B.9 crisis/resistance management:** He secured reelection and maintained coalition power, but conflict, civic repression, and disputed electoral administration reveal high costs and incomplete control.
- **8B.10 progress:** Mixed across the frozen portfolio: political survival and several reforms advanced; security and implementation quality lagged severely.

### Morality separation

Rights abuses, repression, and proxy use are relevant here only insofar as they demonstrate control, coordination, implementation, or failure against the identified program. Their moral wrongness belongs primarily in Chapters 2B–4B and did not determine this research mapping.

### Strongest contrary/mitigating evidence

- Inherited state weakness, enormous geography, conflict, external M23 support, and low income constrained implementation.
- Some flagship programs generated tangible outputs, especially school enrollment and formal governance reforms.
- Repeated security-strategy shifts may indicate adaptation, but they also may indicate ineffective substitution without results.
- Electoral reelection is an achieved power objective, but survival alone cannot establish broad competence.

### Unresolved gaps

- Exact 2023 PDL-145T completion and audit records.
- Budget execution by major program.
- Independent outcome data across more than education.
- Appointment and coordination chains.
- Evidence that tactical security changes reflected learning rather than reactive substitution.
- Durability beyond the election year.

### Saturation rationale

The program-to-action chain is well represented across multiple sectors and source types, but independent outcome evidence is uneven. The chapter has substantial breadth; remaining gaps primarily concern causal attribution, durability, and exact execution rates.
