- **G29 — Human-rights accountability gap.** Amnesty’s November 2023 six-month assessment said authorities had failed to address the cost-of-living shock and called for investigation and accountability for security-force abuses, including southeastern operations involving arbitrary arrest, detention, torture, and excessive force. Locator: paragraphs 65–73. [Amnesty](https://www.amnesty.org/en/latest/press-release/2023/11/after-six-months-in-office-nigerian-president-tinubu-has-failed-to-uphold-human-rights/)

- **G30 — Social-cushioning announcement.** Tinubu’s 17 October speech launched a conditional cash-transfer programme and listed grains, fertilizer, CNG buses, SME support, shelter, school feeding, and out-of-school education interventions. Locator: paragraphs 29–62. These establish announced policy inputs, not delivery or outcomes. [State House](https://statehouse.gov.ng/president-tinubus-speech-at-the-occasion-of-the-international-day-for-the-eradication-of-poverty-and-the-flag-off-of-the-renewed-hope-conditional-cash-transfer/)

- **G31 — Security-crisis persistence.** HRW’s 2024 Nigeria chapter records continuing banditry, Boko Haram/ISWAP violence, displacement, and indiscriminate airstrike concerns during the 2023 reporting period. These are country-level and institutional facts, not proof of a Tinubu-directed operation. [HRW](https://www.hrw.org/world-report/2024/country-chapters/nigeria)

Shortfall: no precise evidence was recovered tying Tinubu personally to a 2023 protest-suppression order or specific security-force operation.

## 4B — Political freedom versus authoritarian rule

- **G32 — Election-quality contrary evidence.** The EU Election Observation Mission’s final-report package covers the campaign environment, media, participation, disputes, and election administration. Its preliminary statement described obstruction and organized violence limiting free voter expression. [EU EOM](https://www.eeas.europa.eu/eom-nigeria-2023_en?s=410279) [preliminary statement](https://www.eeas.europa.eu/sites/default/files/documents/2023/EU_EOM_Nigeria_2023_PRELIMINARY%20STATEMENT_FINAL.pdf)

- **G33 — Judicial-independence concern.** Freedom House records that the Supreme Court issued an unusual March 2023 statement defending itself after election-related rulings raised concerns about independence and impartiality; CSOs petitioned the UN Special Rapporteur in December 2023 over a Kano election judgment. Locator: Freedom on the Net 2024, line-equivalent paragraph under judicial independence. [Freedom House](https://freedomhouse.org/country/nigeria/freedom-net/2024)

- **G34 — Tinubu’s post-ruling institutional posture.** In his 26 October 2023 statement, Tinubu accepted the Supreme Court outcome, affirmed faith in the judiciary, and promised to serve Nigerians of all political persuasions. This is evidence of public commitment and acceptance of formal adjudication, not independent proof that the election or courts were substantively fair. [State House](https://statehouse.gov.ng/statement-by-president-tinubu-it-is-time-to-build-our-great-nation-together/)

- **G35 — Media and civic-space deterioration.** Freedom House’s 2024 report documents journalist arrests and corruption-reporting pressure during the Tinubu period; the source supports institutional attribution but not personal direction. [Freedom House](https://freedomhouse.org/country/nigeria/freedom-net/2024)

Shortfall: no direct 2023 evidence was recovered on personality cult, term-limit posture, or a specific Tinubu order for digital surveillance.

## 5B — Economic well-being and prosperity

- **G36 — Official 2023 growth outcome.** NBS reports real GDP growth of 2.74% in 2023 versus 3.10% in 2022; Q4 growth was 3.46%. Locator: executive summary. [NBS](https://www.nigerianstat.gov.ng/elibrary/read/1241460)

- **G37 — Independent macroeconomic assessment.** The IMF records rapid implementation of subsidy removal and FX-window unification, while also reporting lackluster growth, accelerating inflation, naira pressure, widespread poverty, and rising food insecurity. Locator: December 2023 report, p. 5, lines 165–188. [IMF](https://www.imf.org/-/media/files/publications/cr/2024/english/1ngaea2024001.pdf)

- **G38 — Social-cost forecast.** The World Bank estimated that, without compensation, subsidy-related price increases could push approximately 7.1 million additional Nigerians into poverty and recommended a social compact and targeted transfers. [World Bank report](https://documents1.worldbank.org/curated/en/099092725100040741/pdf/P175280-5411185c-2351-4cc1-9a38-812f769da5fa.pdf)

- **G39 — Fiscal rationale and conditions.** The World Bank estimated approximately ₦2 trillion in 2023 fiscal savings from subsidy removal but emphasized that compensatory transfers were essential. [World Bank](https://www.worldbank.org/en/news/press-release/2023/06/27/nigeria-can-seize-the-opportunity-to-realize-its-growth-potential)

The prior reform-arc evidence remains usable, but 2024 retrospective outcomes must remain temporally separated from 2023.

## 6B — Social well-being and human development

- **G40 — Social-protection programme architecture.** Tinubu’s October 2023 speech established the intended architecture: social-register expansion, conditional transfers, grants, shelter, food, school feeding, and alternative education. These are announced inputs only. [State House](https://statehouse.gov.ng/president-tinubus-speech-at-the-occasion-of-the-international-day-for-the-eradication-of-poverty-and-the-flag-off-of-the-renewed-hope-conditional-cash-transfer/)

- **G41 — Health-sector policy direction.** WHO reported in December 2023 that Coordinating Minister Mohammed Ali Pate described the Tinubu-led administration as pursuing health transformation and universal health coverage by 2030. Locator: 16 December 2023 report. This establishes policy direction, not health outcomes. [WHO](https://www.afro.who.int/countries/nigeria/news/nigeria-stakeholders-are-charting-sustainable-pathway-achieving-universal-health-coverage-all)

- **G42 — Education and child-development implementation context.** UNICEF’s 2023 annual report records the Nigeria Learning Passport reaching 500,000 users, a national learning-crisis framework, maternal/child-health campaigns, social-policy dialogue, and the e-CRVS launch registering 11 million children. Attribution is mixed between government and UNICEF-supported programmes; implementation should not be attributed wholly to Tinubu. [UNICEF](https://www.unicef.org/nigeria/media/9261/file/annual%20Report%202023)

Shortfall: housing, WASH, and target-period outcome measures remain weak. The evidence now supports policy inputs and sector coordination, but not broad welfare effectiveness.

## 7B — Personal integrity and honesty

- **G43 — Formal asset-declaration requirement.** Nigeria’s Code of Conduct Bureau states that public officers must declare assets and liabilities upon assuming office, including foreign assets, business interests, and shares. This establishes the applicable accountability mechanism, not Tinubu’s compliance. [CCB](https://ccb.gov.ng/?page_id=325)

- **G44 — Betta Edu appointment and corrective response.** The State House records Tinubu’s January 2024 suspension of his humanitarian-affairs minister, direction of an EFCC investigation, and a diagnostic review of social-investment finances. The alleged conduct occurred during the target-period appointment, but the suspension is post-period and does not establish Tinubu’s prior knowledge or benefit. [State House](https://statehouse.gov.ng/president-tinubu-suspends-minister-of-humanitarian-affairs-and-poverty-alleviation-from-office/)

- **G45 — Anti-corruption commitment in official programme.** The October 2023 poverty speech expressly linked social-investment restructuring to transparent and accountable delivery. This is a stated commitment; later implementation and the Edu episode provide contrary evidence against assuming fulfillment. [State House](https://statehouse.gov.ng/president-tinubus-speech-at-the-occasion-of-the-international-day-for-the-eradication-of-poverty-and-the-flag-off-of-the-renewed-hope-conditional-cash-transfer/)

Shortfall: no publicly accessible 2023 Tinubu asset declaration, family-business separation record, or direct personal-benefit evidence was recovered. The credential dispute remains procedurally unresolved, not a finding of falsification.

## 8B — Effectiveness and competence

- **G46 — Rapid reform execution.** IMF independently confirms that the Tinubu administration quickly implemented fuel-subsidy removal and FX-window unification. Locator: December 2023 report, p. 5. [IMF](https://www.imf.org/-/media/files/publications/cr/2024/english/1ngaea2024001.pdf)

- **G47 — Adaptation attempt.** The October 2023 official programme translated the subsidy response into planned transfers, grain releases, fertilizer, transport, shelter, education, and SME measures. These establish adaptation and implementation intent, but not reach or effect. [State House](https://statehouse.gov.ng/president-tinubus-speech-at-the-occasion-of-the-international-day-for-the-eradication-of-poverty-and-the-flag-off-of-the-renewed-hope-conditional-cash-transfer/)

- **G48 — Outcome constraint.** NBS reports 2023 growth below 2022; IMF reports high and accelerating inflation, naira pressure, poverty, and food insecurity. This is strong contrary evidence against treating rapid policy action as demonstrated effectiveness. [NBS](https://www.nigerianstat.gov.ng/elibrary/read/1241460) [IMF](https://www.imf.org/-/media/files/publications/cr/2024/english/1ngaea2024001.pdf)

- **G49 — Crisis-management sequence.** The ECOWAS communiqué documents both escalation—sanctions and possible force—and diplomatic/constitutional processes. The UNGA address documents Tinubu’s later diplomatic framing of restoration through regional leadership and partnership. [ECOWAS](https://ecowas.int/wp-content/uploads/2023/07/Final-Communique_Extra-Summit-on-Niger.pdf) [UN record](https://digitallibrary.un.org/nanna/record/4025887/files/A_78_PV.5-EN.pdf?registerDownload=1&version=1&withMetadata=0&withWatermark=0)

Shortfall: full-cabinet competence, internal coordination, and durable trajectory remain insufficiently evidenced for a single-year dossier.
