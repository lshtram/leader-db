## Starting material

Earlier reconnaissance:

**Overview**
Xi Jinping held power in China throughout 2023 as Communist Party general secretary, PRC president, and chairman of the Central Military Commission. Formally, the state presidency has limited constitutional powers; in practice, Xi’s authority was broader because the party controls the legislature, government appointments, courts, security organs, and the military. The main institutional limits were not independent checks in the liberal-democratic sense, but the need to operate through party-state structures, the State Council’s administrative machinery, and external constraints from the economy, foreign governments, and international markets.

**Evidence Records**

1. **Ruler identity and power concentration**
- **Fact:** Xi Jinping was the top leader in 2023, combining party, state, and military leadership. He was re-elected president and CMC chairman in March 2023, while the legislature also endorsed the cabinet lineup and other top officials.
- **Why it matters:** This establishes who actually governed China during the period and shows that 2023 decisions were made from the apex of a highly centralized system.
- **Strongest source:** *Li Qiang endorsed as Chinese premier* / Xinhua via English SCIO, March 11, 2023. Direct link: https://english.scio.gov.cn/topnews/2023-03/11/content_85161156.htm
- **Support passage:** Xi nominated Li Qiang as premier; deputies also endorsed CMC vice chairmen and members, and Xi signed the presidential order appointing Li. See also *Xi Jinping unanimously elected Chinese president, PRC CMC chairman* (NPC/Xinhua, March 11, 2023): https://en.npc.gov.cn.cdurl.cn/2023-03/11/c_868263.htm
- **Period:** In-period.
- **Connection to ruler:** Direct and strong.
- **Complicating evidence:** The state presidency is constitutionally bounded and formally acts “pursuant to decisions” of the NPC and its Standing Committee.
- **Cautions:** State media presents elite voting as unanimous and may compress real political bargaining.
- **Other support:** PRC Constitution; March 2023 NPC proceedings; party communiques that reaffirm Xi’s core position.

2. **Formal office vs. actual authority**
- **Fact:** The PRC Constitution gives the president mainly promulgation, appointment, diplomatic, and emergency/mobilization powers, all tied to NPC or NPC Standing Committee decisions. Xi’s actual influence exceeded the presidency because he also led the Party and CMC.
- **Why it matters:** This separates formal constitutional authority from actual governing power.
- **Strongest source:** *Constitution of the People’s Republic of China* / English govt text, Article 79-81. Direct link: https://english.www.gov.cn/archive/lawsregulations/201911/20/content_WS5ed8856ec6d0b3f0e9499913.html
- **Support passage:** Article 80 ties presidential action to NPC/NPCSC decisions; Article 81 says the president engages in state affairs and receives foreign envoys on behalf of the PRC.
- **Period:** Inherited constitutional framework.

## Workplan

1. **Orient.** Establish the ruler's formal and practical authority, the inherited situation, major external shocks or constraints, and the information environment.
2. **Plan coverage.** For every selected lens, identify the important favorable, adverse, disputed, and exculpatory possibilities that research should test.
3. **Find the main record.** Begin with strong overviews and syntheses. Identify the consequential decisions, laws, budgets, appointments, statements, implementation, corrections, and outcomes.
4. **Verify material claims.** Open the underlying sources. Use precise laws, official records, audits, judgments, datasets, scholarship, monitoring, and credible reporting where they establish or challenge an important claim. Assess each source’s authority, independence, method, proximity, and date.
5. **Test the account.** Look for contrary evidence, independent corroboration, relevant local-language material, source dependencies, and alternative explanations. Distinguish inherited conditions and country context from conduct attributable to the ruler.
6. **Deepen where useful.** Repeat steps 3–5 while new searches add a material fact, a stronger source, a missing perspective, or a necessary correction. Finish when further searching mainly repeats the established record or reaches a concrete access barrier.
7. **Prepare the handoff.** Organize the evidence by underlying fact and map each developed claim to every selected lens it supports.
