# Andrés Manuel López Obrador, Mexico, 2023

## 1. Overview

Andrés Manuel López Obrador (AMLO) was Mexico’s constitutional president and commander-in-chief throughout 2023. He exercised unusually strong agenda-setting influence through the Morena-led federal government, daily press conferences, control of the executive bureaucracy, and close relationships with the armed forces. His government also relied on the military for public security, infrastructure, customs, welfare administration, and major projects.

His influence was limited by Mexico’s federal structure, autonomous institutions, opposition parties, courts, the Supreme Court, the electoral authority (INE), state governors, organized crime, and practical dependence on military cooperation. In 2023, the Supreme Court invalidated López Obrador’s “Plan B” electoral reforms for serious legislative-procedure violations. Security policy remained a major failure area: homicide, disappearance, impunity, military accountability, and journalist safety remained severe problems. The government reported social and economic gains, but many 2023 social indicators were actually measurements of 2022 conditions.

## 2. Evidence records

### Record 1 — Military expansion into civilian security and state functions

- **Fact:** During 2023, López Obrador’s administration continued expanding the armed forces’ role in public security and civilian administration. The National Guard’s operational and administrative control had been transferred to the Defense Ministry, while the Army and Navy performed extensive civilian tasks. The Inter-American Commission on Human Rights concluded that this represented backsliding from the planned withdrawal of the armed forces from public security.
- **Why it matters:** This was a central institutional choice of the president’s security model and increased military influence over domestic governance.
- **Strongest source:** *Annual Report 2023, Chapter V: Mexico*, Inter-American Commission on Human Rights, 2024, pp. 3–5 and 104–105. [Direct source](https://www.oas.org/en/iachr/docs/annual/2023/chapters/IA2023_Cap_5_Mexico_ENG.PDF)
- **Locator:** PDF pp. 3–5, paras. 7–13; PDF pp. 104–105, para. 438.
- **Period:** 2023, with inherited institutional developments from 2019–2022.
- **Ruler connection:** Strong. The president promoted and defended the military-centered model.
- **Complicating evidence:** The government reported that the National Guard and military deployment improved state capacity and public security coverage; the IACHR also recorded state claims of a gradual withdrawal plan.
- **Cautions:** The IACHR report combines state submissions, civil-society information, and the Commission’s own assessment; it does not independently measure every operational outcome.
- **Independent support:** Human Rights Watch, *Ayotzinapa, Mexico’s Army, and López Obrador’s Silence* (2023); Human Rights Watch, *World Report 2024: Mexico*.
