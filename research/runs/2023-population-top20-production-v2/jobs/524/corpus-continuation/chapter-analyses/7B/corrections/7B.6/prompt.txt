# 7B. Ruler Personal Integrity and Honesty

Status: draft; v5 objective-evidence questions require controlled rejudgment; v1 invalid

## Chapter Identity

- Chapter: `7B`
- Category: `ruler_personal_integrity_and_honesty`
- Evidence strategy: one ruler-period dossier, researched across ten overlapping lenses
- Judge strategy: one chapter judge applies one comparative meter across all eligible rulers
- Rubric version: `chapter_7b_v5`

## Chapter Purpose

Assess whether the ruler uses public office honestly and for public rather than
personal, family, business, factional, or patronage advantage. The chapter joins
truthfulness, correction, conflicts of interest, self-enrichment, appointments,
independent scrutiny, concealment, commitments, favoritism, and ethical example
into one final 1-10 integrity score.

The ten questions are evidence lenses, not ten equally weighted sub-scores.
Evidence may illuminate several lenses, and missing evidence for one lens lowers
confidence rather than invalidating the dossier or preventing a chapter score.

## Ten Evidence Lenses

| Lens | Simple question | Detailed research question | Priority evidence |
|---|---|---|---|
| **7B.1 — Truthfulness** | Did the ruler tell the truth when lying could protect their power or reputation? | Does the ruler tell the truth in verifiable public statements, formal records, legislative testimony, courts, and international commitments, especially when deception would protect power, benefit, or reputation? | **Rhetoric and representations**; **Formal acts and law** |
| **7B.2 — Correcting falsehoods and errors** | Did the ruler admit and correct false claims and mistakes? | When reliable records, courts, audits, or investigations expose error or falsehood, does the ruler correct the record, comply, and remedy harm rather than retaliate, conceal, or knowingly repeat the claim? | **Rhetoric and representations**; **Implementation and operational conduct**; **Outcomes** |
| **7B.3 — Conflicts of interest** | Did the ruler keep personal and family interests separate from public decisions? | Does the ruler support and personally comply with conflict-of-interest, disclosure, recusal, divestment, and ethics rules separating personal, family, and business interests from state decisions? | **Formal acts and law**; **Implementation and operational conduct**; **Outcomes** |
| **7B.4 — Personal profit from office** | Did the ruler or close family improperly profit from public office? | Do asset, tax, gift, ownership, contract, foundation, emolument, bribe, insider-access, and legal records show that the ruler or close family profited from office, and did the ruler permit final findings, recovery, and accountability? | **Resources**; **Implementation and operational conduct**; **Outcomes** |

## Scope and Non-Goals

- Judge conduct attributable to the ruler, including conduct knowingly directed,
  enabled, protected, or tolerated by the ruler's close governing network.
- Do not infer personal dishonesty from a country's inherited corruption level.
- Do not equate policy disagreement, honest error, changed policy after new
  evidence, rhetoric, secrecy justified by genuine security/privacy/diplomatic
  needs, or an unproved allegation with deception or corruption.
- Do not reward polished communications, formal ethics rules, prosecutions of
  rivals, or selective transparency without evidence of genuine practice.
- Do not score general competence, prosperity, freedom, or popularity except
  where the facts directly demonstrate integrity or abuse of office.
- Never use the client matrix as evidence.

Country corruption indicators, authoritarian institutions, censorship, detention,
human-rights abuse, and political-freedom conditions are context only unless the
evidence establishes a direct nexus to ruler-specific deception, personal or network
benefit, favoritism, concealment, obstruction of scrutiny, retaliation against an
integrity investigation, or deliberate abuse of office. Lenses 7B.7 and 7B.10 do not
convert every repressive act into personal dishonesty. Score those acts primarily in
3B/4B unless that integrity nexus is evidenced.

Apply this as an item-level admissibility gate, not merely a caveat after scoring. For
each potentially decisive item, identify the ruler's personal statement, interest,
benefit, appointment, protected network, response to scrutiny, concealment, correction,
obstruction, or knowing tolerance after notice. If none is shown, the item receives zero
scoring weight even when it proves authoritarianism, repression, country corruption, or
weak institutions. Do not cite a zero-weight item as decisive or use it to reject an
adjacent anchor.

## Researcher Workflow and Evidence Themes

Follow the local-first researcher guide and source-confidence registry. Query the
exact ruler/country/year or bounded period locally before web discovery. Treat
WGI, V-Dem, and CPI as country-level context and triangulation, not proof of the
ruler's personal conduct.

Research by themes that cut across lenses, not by ten isolated searches:

1. Material public statements, corrections, sworn evidence, and documented
   concealment, including examples of costly candor and adverse findings.
2. Assets, beneficial ownership, gifts, business/family interests, procurement,
   licensing, regulation, pardons, campaign finance, and foreign-policy benefits.
3. Appointments and access patterns: competence, kinship, donor/client ties,
   loyalty demands, and whether appointees facilitate self-dealing or impunity.
4. Courts, prosecutors, auditors, legislatures, ethics bodies, inquiries,
   journalists, and whistleblowers: independence, obstruction, retaliation,
   remedies, and ruler response.
5. Promise and commitment records, ethical rules, institutional reforms, and
   whether practice strengthened or normalized trust and accountability.

Prefer primary legal and financial records, judgments, audit/inquiry findings,
legislative records, asset declarations, procurement records, official
transcripts, sanctions findings with stated evidence, reputable investigative
reporting, and serious academic histories. Official sources are strong for the
fact that a statement, appointment, filing, or rule exists, but conditional for
self-serving claims of honesty. Allegations must remain allegations; seek an
independent finding, underlying record, response, and contrary evidence.

Each accepted item should retain its citation, date, claim, period fit, source
type/confidence, ruler-attribution assessment, and uncertainty. Reuse a stable
evidence item across every relevant lens. Coverage descriptions may use natural
language; no exact status vocabulary is required.

## Common Evidence-Item Semantics

Each material evidence item should carry a stable evidence ID; a concise factual
claim; source URL, title, publisher, and publication/access date where available;
source type and confidence with a short reason; a quotation or precise locator
when available; target-year/ruler-period fit; ruler attribution and
inherited-baseline relevance; its positive, negative, mixed, contextual, or
contrary role; and the supported lens IDs. Fields may be normalized later and
natural-language equivalents are acceptable. The substantive minimum is a
traceable source, intelligible claim, temporal fit, attribution, and evidence
role. One item may support multiple lenses without duplication.

## Attribution, Sparse Evidence, and Historical Cases

Separate personal direction or tolerance from inherited institutions and acts of
family, ministers, parties, businesses, or security services. Consider formal and
informal authority, time in office, credible knowledge, ability to intervene,
action after notice, coalition constraints, and whether the ruler benefited.
Close-associate misconduct is not automatically the ruler's, but repeated benefit,
protection, appointment, obstruction, or failure to act after credible notice can
support attribution.

For older, obscure, closed, short-tenure, or poorly archived cases, use the best
available contemporary records and reputable later scholarship. Do not interpret
silence as integrity where reporting and accountability were suppressed. A dossier
with only a few strong themes can still be judged; narrow coverage, one-sided
archives, weak attribution, or missing adverse-case searches should reduce
confidence and may widen the defensible score range. Do not manufacture lens
coverage or impose a modern disclosure regime anachronistically.

Silence, favorable institutions, or the absence of reported allegations cannot prove
integrity. Scores above 5 require affirmative ruler-specific evidence of candor,
separation of interests, correction, professional appointments, or acceptance of
genuine scrutiny. Scores below 4 require ruler-specific recurring deception,
self-dealing, favoritism, patronage, concealment, or obstruction. If neither side is
supported by traceable ruler-specific conduct, return null rather than rewarding or
punishing the information environment.

### Absolute-scale and visibility guardrails

The annual cohort does not define the endpoints. Use fixed cross-period reference
patterns rather than assigning the most opaque current ruler to the floor:

- **1:** Personal or family extraction, deliberate deception, cronyism, and
  state-backed concealment or impunity are central instruments of rule, supported by
  direct findings or strong convergent ruler-specific evidence. Mobutu-era kleptocracy
  or Marcos-family extraction are reference patterns; generic authoritarian control is
  insufficient.
- **2–3:** Severe or recurring ruler-specific deception, conflicts, favoritism,
  self-dealing, or obstruction spans important domains, without reaching that floor.
- **4–5:** Genuinely mixed personal conduct: attributable safeguards or honest conduct
  coexist with material lapses, patronage, conflicts, broken commitments, or weak
  accountability.
- **6–7:** Generally honest public service with real scrutiny and correction, but
  notable attributable lapses or opacity remain.
- **8–9:** Sustained candor, separation of interests, professional appointments, and
  acceptance of independent scrutiny, including evidence under adverse incentives.
- **10:** Exceptional costly candor and durable strengthening against self-dealing and
  impunity under strong contrary incentives.

Normalize visibility before scoring. Numerous fact checks, press controversies, or
allegations in an open system do not establish a worse pattern than silence in a closed
system. Conversely, closed-system silence does not establish integrity or personal
corruption. Compare materiality, repetition, findings, personal benefit, response, and
remedy—not article counts. When admissible personal-nexus evidence is thin on both
sides, return null rather than converting repression into a low score or silence into a
midpoint.

## Holistic 1-10 Chapter Rubric

Synthesize the ruler's pattern, materiality, intent, personal benefit, use of state
power, duration, corrective behavior, and institutional effect. Severe direct
self-dealing or obstruction may properly dominate several weaker positive lenses;
conversely, an isolated error should not outweigh a broad record of candor and
accountability. Do not average lens labels or count citations.

| Score | Chapter anchor |
|---:|---|
| 1 | Deliberate deception, personal or family extraction, cronyism, and state-backed concealment/impunity are central instruments of rule and materially corrupt public office. |
| 2-3 | Severe or recurring dishonesty, conflicts, self-dealing, favoritism, or obstruction span important domains; independent accountability is attacked or largely ineffective. |
| 4-5 | A material mixed record: meaningful integrity safeguards or honest conduct coexist with recurring deception, patronage, conflicts, broken commitments, or weak accountability. |
| 6-7 | Generally honest and public-serving conduct with real scrutiny and correction, but notable lapses, opacity, favoritism, conflicts, or accountability weaknesses remain. |
| 8-9 | Strong, sustained truthfulness, separation of interests, professional public service, openness to scrutiny, correction, and ethical example; credible lapses are rare and remedied. |
| 10 | Exceptional and durable integrity under adverse incentives: costly candor, exemplary separation of interests, robust independent accountability, and institutions strengthened against self-dealing and impunity. |

## Confidence

Report confidence separately on a 0-100 scale. Base it on source authority and
independence, ruler attribution, temporal fit, information environment, diversity
of themes and source types, contrary-evidence search, and agreement. Evidence
volume alone is not confidence. Missing lenses normally lower confidence; they do
not mechanically lower the chapter score. State the most plausible score range
when uncertainty is material.

## One-Judge Comparative Workflow

One judge owns 7B for the target year/period across all eligible rulers:

1. Read every dossier's 7B evidence and explicit gaps without demanding uniform
   coverage terminology or perfect mapping tables.
2. Separate identity-quarantined cases; retain sparse cases with reduced
   confidence rather than discarding them.
3. Establish provisional low, middle, and high batch anchors using diverse
   regimes, periods, visibility levels, and evidence environments.
4. Make tentative holistic scores, then compare neighboring cases for consistent
   thresholds of intent, materiality, personal benefit, obstruction, correction,
   and attribution.
5. Revisit outliers and cases whose score rests mainly on country indicators,
   official self-report, accusation volume, or media visibility.
6. Emit one final chapter score, confidence/range, rationale, strongest positive
   and negative evidence, missing themes, contrary evidence, and review flag.

## Acceptance Checklist

- Every numeric score requires direct ruler-specific integrity evidence: a personal
  act, disclosure, conflict response, truthfulness episode, accountability choice, or
  comparably attributable pattern. Generic repression, institutional strength, and
  absence of reported scandal are context only. Without the personal nexus, return
  null rather than `5`; evidence explicitly described as absent cannot support above
  `5`.

- All ten authoritative lenses are present verbatim and treated as overlapping,
  non-equal evidence lenses.
- Local-first order, source-confidence fields, citations, and run profile are
  retained.
- Evidence items satisfy the common traceability, temporal-fit, attribution,
  evidence-role, and lens-link semantics.
- Ruler conduct is separated from inherited national corruption and associate
  conduct is attributed explicitly.
- Allegations, findings, honest error, secrecy, deception, correction, benefit,
  and obstruction are distinguished.
- Missing lenses reduce confidence rather than invalidate or mechanically depress
  the score.
- One judge compares all rulers with one holistic rubric and records adjacent-case
  calibration.
- Final output uses the common chapter-judge envelope, including adjacent-anchor
  comparisons and an explicit manual-review flag.
- Smoke testing and revision occur before production activation.

Die Aufarbeitung dreht sich seit längerem vor allem um die Frage der Rückzahlung: Eigentlich hätte die Warburg-Bank allein für Cum-Ex-Geschäfte aus dem Jahr 2009 insgesamt 47 Millionen Euro zurückzahlen müssen. Nach einem Gerichtsurteil hatte die Bank 2020 eigenen Angaben zufolge schließlich alle ausstehenden Steuerrückforderungen beglichen, versucht aber auf juristischem Weg weiter, das Geld zurückzubekommen.\nWas sind\nCum-Ex-Geschäfte?\nCum-Ex-Geschäfte heißen so, weil große Pakete von Aktien mit (\"cum\") und ohne (\"ex\") Dividendenanspruch rund um den Stichtag für die Ausschüttung in rascher Folge hin- und hergeschoben wurden. Die bewusst undurchsichtigen Transaktionen hatten nur ein Ziel: Sie sollten bei den Finanzbehörden möglichst große Verwirrung stiften. Mit diesem Trick ließen sich die Beteiligten im großen Stil Kapitalertragsteuer erstatten, die nie gezahlt wurde. Die Gewinne wurden aufgeteilt.
