## Your research lenses

Evidence categories:

- **Formal acts and law:** Laws, bills, votes, decrees, regulations, treaties, directives, strategies and formal commitments. Typical sources: legislative summaries and histories, legal databases and gazettes, treaty and court records, official archives, legal scholarship, NGO and journalistic analysis.
- **Resources:** Authorized and actual spending, staffing, procurement, contracts, equipment and infrastructure. Typical sources: budgets and execution reports, procurement records, audits, legislative analysis, academic and NGO studies, investigative reporting.
- **Personnel:** Appointments, removals, qualifications, conflicts, tenure and practical autonomy. Typical sources: appointment records, official biographies, confirmation proceedings, institutional reports, biographies, academic work and reporting.
- **Implementation and operational conduct:** Delivery, enforcement, administration, inspection, compliance, correction and remedy. - **Rhetoric and representations:** Speeches, testimony, interviews, promises, explanations, threats, denials, propaganda and corrections. Typical sources: transcripts and recordings, official and campaign archives, diplomatic records, memoirs and biographies, histories, contemporary reporting.
- **Outcomes:** Observed changes in conduct, institutions, rights, security, welfare, delivery and compliance. Typical sources: statistics and datasets, evaluations and surveys, audits, academic research, NGO and international-organization reports, histories and reporting.

restraint or safety need independent corroboration; official texts remain strong for
formal doctrine, appointments, treaty acts, and dated statements. Preserve contrary
evidence and uncertainty. Do not infer responsible rhetoric from silence or safety from
the absence of reported accidents.

target-year evidence, broader ruler-period evidence, inherited baseline, and later

**Overview**

Olaf Scholz was Germany’s Federal Chancellor in 2023, leading the SPD-Greens-FDP “traffic light” coalition. His formal powers were strong on paper, but practical authority was constrained by coalition bargaining, ministerial autonomy, Bundestag majorities, federalism, and judicial review. The biggest 2023 shocks were the war in Ukraine, the Israel-Hamas war, and the Federal Constitutional Court’s 15 November 2023 budget ruling, which forced a major fiscal reset.

**Evidence Records**

1. **Scholz’s office and real authority**
- **Fact:** Scholz held the office of Federal Chancellor in 2023 and could set general policy guidelines, appoint ministers, and direct cabinet business, but he could not govern unilaterally.
- **Why it matters:** This defines how much of 2023 can be attributed to him personally versus coalition and institutional constraints.
- **Strongest source:** Federal Government, “Structure and tasks,” plus Scholz biography.  
  - Link: [Structure and tasks](https://www.bundesregierung.de/breg-en/federal-government/structure-and-tasks-470508)  
  - Link: [Olaf Scholz biography](https://www.bundeskanzler.de/bk-en/chancellery/federal-chancellors-since-1949/olaf-scholz)
- **Support:** The Chancellery says the Chancellor determines the general guidelines of government policy and chooses ministers; it also says the Chancellor cannot automatically intervene in ministers’ portfolios and that coalition agreements bind practice. Scholz’s biography confirms he was Chancellor from December 2021 through 2025.
- **Period:** Inherited office structure, operating throughout 2023.
- **Connection to ruler:** Direct and strong.
- **Complicating evidence:** Germany is federal; Länder control major areas like policing, education, and many administrative functions, and the Bundestag/Bundesrat structure limits executive freedom.
- **Cautions:** This is official self-description, so it describes formal power more than actual political leverage.
- **Other support:** Bundestag on federal competencies and Chancellor election; Basic Law Art. 65.
- **Stable locator:** Official government role description and constitutional provisions.

2. **Coalition government and internal veto points**
- **Fact:** Scholz led a coalition with SPD, Greens, and FDP, so policy depended on compromise across three parties.
- **Why it matters:** Many 2023 disputes were coalition disputes, especially over budgets, climate spending, and fiscal rules.
- **Strongest source:** Federal Government profile of Scholz’s chancellorship and coalition description.  
  - Link: [Olaf Scholz (2021–2025)](https://www.bundeskanzler.de/bk-en/chancellorship-olaf-scholz-2344802)
- **Support:** The Chancellery identifies the first traffic-light coalition and states its program focused on modernization, social justice, and climate protection. The government structure page says coalition agreements constrain the Chancellor in practice.
- **Period:** Inherited condition from 2021, heavily active

## Workplan

1. **Orient.** Establish the ruler's formal and practical authority, the inherited situation, major external shocks or constraints, and the information environment.
2. **Plan coverage.** For every selected lens, identify the important favorable, adverse, disputed, and exculpatory possibilities that research should test.
3. **Find the main record.** Begin with strong overviews and syntheses. Identify the consequential decisions, laws, budgets, appointments, statements, implementation, corrections, and outcomes.
4. **Verify material claims.** Open the underlying sources. Use precise laws, official records, audits, judgments, datasets, scholarship, monitoring, and credible reporting where they establish or challenge an important claim. Assess each source’s authority, independence, method, proximity, and date.
5. **Test the account.** Look for contrary evidence, independent corroboration, relevant local-language material, source dependencies, and alternative explanations. Distinguish inherited conditions and country context from conduct attributable to the ruler.
6. **Deepen where useful.** Repeat steps 3–5 while new searches add a material fact, a stronger source, a missing perspective, or a necessary correction. Finish when further searching mainly repeats the established record or reaches a concrete access barrier.
7. **Prepare the handoff.** Organize the evidence by underlying fact and map each developed claim to every selected lens it supports.
