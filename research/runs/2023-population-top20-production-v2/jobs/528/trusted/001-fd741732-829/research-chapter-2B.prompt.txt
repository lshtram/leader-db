- **Formal acts and law:** Laws, bills, votes, decrees, regulations, treaties, directives, strategies and formal commitments. ## Researcher Guidance

Begin with resolved identity and local D13 UCDP and D14 SIPRI facts. Research coherent
themes: inherited conflicts and threat environment; initiation and alternatives;
objectives/justifications; conduct and civilian harm; proxy/arms-recipient control;
public claims; negotiations and settlements; military patronage; accountability; and
end-state change. Reuse evidence across lenses when substantively justified.

Prefer UCDP/PA-X/UN records, treaties and court findings, ICRC and UN humanitarian
reporting, official operational/legal records, legislative or independent inquiries,
credible NGOs, peer-reviewed histories, and reputable reporting. Distinguish allegation,
documented event, legal finding, command policy, foreseeable consequence, and ruler
direction. Belligerent official claims require independent corroboration. Researchers
collect cited claims and counterevidence, not a final score.

Establish a conflict/exposure gate before judging: active or inherited interstate
war, cross-border operations, proxy support, coercive diplomacy, peace-process
authority, or a documented absence of such exposure. If conflict exposure is
plausible but initiation, alternatives, civilian conduct, proxy control, and ruler
authority are absent, continue research or return null rather than assigning a
midpoint. Purely domestic repression and ordinary policing belong in 3B. Nuclear
threats enter 2B only when they materially serve an international conflict or
coercive-security choice; nuclear-risk responsibility itself belongs in 1B.

**Overview**

Narendra Modi held power in India in 2023 as prime minister, heading the central executive with a strong parliamentary base and wide control over the Union government. His authority was substantial, but not unlimited: the Supreme Court, India’s federal structure and state governments, and political opposition still constrained him, and the Manipur crisis in 2023 showed that security, policing, and public-order failures could still force judicial intervention.

The period was politically dominant but not calm. The biggest 2023 themes were the Manipur ethnic violence, pressure on journalists and online speech, the BBC documentary dispute, Rahul Gandhi’s temporary disqualification and later reinstatement, and a strong G20 year that the government treated as a diplomatic success. Economically, India remained a fast-growing large economy, but inflation and weak social indicators stayed relevant.

## Workplan

1. **Orient.** Establish the ruler's formal and practical authority, the inherited situation, major external shocks or constraints, and the information environment.
2. **Plan coverage.** For every selected lens, identify the important favorable, adverse, disputed, and exculpatory possibilities that research should test.
3. **Find the main record.** Begin with strong overviews and syntheses. Identify the consequential decisions, laws, budgets, appointments, statements, implementation, corrections, and outcomes.
4. **Verify material claims.** Open the underlying sources. Use precise laws, official records, audits, judgments, datasets, scholarship, monitoring, and credible reporting where they establish or challenge an important claim. Assess each source’s authority, independence, method, proximity, and date.
5. **Test the account.** Look for contrary evidence, independent corroboration, relevant local-language material, source dependencies, and alternative explanations. Distinguish inherited conditions and country context from conduct attributable to the ruler.
6. **Deepen where useful.** Repeat steps 3–5 while new searches add a material fact, a stronger source, a missing perspective, or a necessary correction. Finish when further searching mainly repeats the established record or reaches a concrete access barrier.
7. **Prepare the handoff.** Organize the evidence by underlying fact and map each developed claim to every selected lens it supports.

## Evidence records

Create one record for one source supporting one material claim. If one source supports several materially different claims used downstream, create a separate record and locator for each claim. Give records about the same underlying fact or event the same `underlying_fact_key`, and identify reports that depend on the same investigation, dataset, wire story, or official claim.

Each developed record includes:

- one precise factual claim and why it matters;

- target-period, inherited, or later-retrospective status;
- the ruler-attribution basis;
- source limitations, dependencies, and credible contrary evidence;
- independent corroboration; and
- every selected question ID the claim supports.

Keep accepted evidence, corroboration, reused sources, uninspected leads, rejected sources, and blocked sources in clearly labelled groups. Explain the source landscape when a lens remains unanswered.
