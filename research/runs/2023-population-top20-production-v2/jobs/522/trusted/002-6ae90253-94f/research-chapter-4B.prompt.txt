Earlier reconnaissance:

**Overview**
Xi Jinping was the dominant ruler of China in 2023. He held the formal posts of general secretary of the Chinese Communist Party, president of the PRC, and chairman of both the party and state Central Military Commissions, giving him direct control over party, state, and military channels. In practice, his authority was constrained less by independent institutions than by internal party incentives, bureaucratic implementation problems, elite turnover, local-government debt and fiscal stress, and external pressure from the U.S. and regional rivals. The 2023 period mixed high centralization, continued repression, a fragile post-COVID recovery, and escalating external friction, especially around Taiwan and the South China Sea.

**Evidence Records**
1. **Formal authority and real power**
- **Fact:** Xi held the core state and party offices in 2023, including CPC general secretary, PRC president, and chair of both CMCs; this made him the top decision-maker across party, state, and military systems.
- **Why it matters:** This is the baseline for attributing policy outcomes, repression, diplomacy, and military decisions to Xi.
- **Strongest source:** *Xi Jinping unanimously elected Chinese president, PRC CMC chairman*, Xinhua via National People’s Congress, March 10, 2023. https://en.npc.gov.cn.cdurl.cn/2023-03/11/c_868263.htm
- **Support locator:** Search result snippet states he was unanimously elected president and CMC chairman; official bio also lists his concurrent offices.
- **Period:** Current to 2023.
- **Connection to ruler:** Direct and maximal.
- **Complicating evidence:** Formal titles do not prove unilateral control over every outcome; implementation still depended on party organs and the state bureaucracy.
- **Caution:** Official PRC reporting is polished and omits institutional friction.
- **Independent support:** Xinhua bio page; CPC constitution; foreign-policy analyses noting “one-man politics.”
- **Later retrospective evidence:** None needed.

2. **Institutional limits inside the PRC system**
- **Fact:** China’s constitution says the NPC is the “highest state organ of power,” but it is controlled by a party-dominated political system in which the NPC, state council, courts, and military are structurally subordinate rather than independent.
- **Why it matters:** It clarifies the main limit on Xi’s power: not competitive institutions, but internal party hierarchy, bureaucratic execution, and policy tradeoffs.
- **Strongest source:** *Constitution of the People’s Republic of China*, NPC. https://www.npc.gov.cn/englishnpc/constitution2019/201911/t20191120_384295.html
- **Support locator:** Articles 57, 58, 62, 67.
- **Period:** Inherited structure, still operative in 2023.
- **Connection to ruler:** Indirect but important: Xi operates inside this system and dominates it through party office.
- **Complicating evidence:** The constitution formally gives the NPC and its Standing Committee powers that are broader than their real

## Workplan

1. **Orient.** Establish the ruler's formal and practical authority, the inherited situation, major external shocks or constraints, and the information environment.
2. **Plan coverage.** For every selected lens, identify the important favorable, adverse, disputed, and exculpatory possibilities that research should test.
3. **Find the main record.** Begin with strong overviews and syntheses. Identify the consequential decisions, laws, budgets, appointments, statements, implementation, corrections, and outcomes.
4. **Verify material claims.** Open the underlying sources. Use precise laws, official records, audits, judgments, datasets, scholarship, monitoring, and credible reporting where they establish or challenge an important claim. Assess each source’s authority, independence, method, proximity, and date.
5. **Test the account.** Look for contrary evidence, independent corroboration, relevant local-language material, source dependencies, and alternative explanations. Distinguish inherited conditions and country context from conduct attributable to the ruler.
6. **Deepen where useful.** Repeat steps 3–5 while new searches add a material fact, a stronger source, a missing perspective, or a necessary correction. Finish when further searching mainly repeats the established record or reaches a concrete access barrier.
7. **Prepare the handoff.** Organize the evidence by underlying fact and map each developed claim to every selected lens it supports.
