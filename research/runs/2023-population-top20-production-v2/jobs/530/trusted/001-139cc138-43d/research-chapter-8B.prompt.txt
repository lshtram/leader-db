## Researcher Workflow and Evidence Themes

Follow the local-first researcher guide and source-confidence registry. Query the
exact case locally. WGI, V-Dem, BTI, budgets, and outcome indicators are useful
context or corroboration, but generic state capacity is not ruler effectiveness.

Research chronologically across themes rather than issuing one search per lens:

1. Identify stated programs from platforms, manifestos, coalition agreements,
   speeches, strategies, directives, budgets, and plans. Identify revealed goals
   only from repeated choices that distinguish them from rhetoric or speculation.
2. Trace conversion into priorities, resources, laws, institutions, appointments,
   timelines, enforcement, and measurable milestones.
3. Examine whether the state, party, military, coalition, bureaucracy, territorial
   network, or informal machinery actually mobilized, coordinated, and followed
   through; include bottlenecks and implementation gaps.
4. Assess whether personnel were capable for the assigned program. Ideological or
   coercive operators may be effective implementers in this narrow chapter even
   when their conduct is reprehensible elsewhere.
5. Compare promised/revealed objectives with implementation and outcomes after
   realistic lags, inherited trends, peer conditions, shocks, and resources.
6. Trace learning, replacement, reallocation, crisis response, resistance
   management, short-term gains, reversals, durability, and end-of-period position.

Prefer primary program and budget documents, legislation, administrative and
audit records, implementation data, credible outcome series, contemporaneous
records, serious academic histories, independent evaluations, and reputable
reporting. Official sources establish declared goals and formal actions but not
successful execution by themselves. Preserve failures, contradictions, abandoned
priorities, propaganda-only claims, alternative causal explanations, and adverse
side effects; record the latter for cross-chapter use without morally deducting
them in 8B.

Reuse each stable evidence item across relevant lenses. Natural-language coverage
and gaps are acceptable; publication must not depend on exact status terms,
sequential IDs, or exhaustive mapping syntax.

## Starting material

Earlier reconnaissance:

**Overview**
Fumio Kishida was Japan’s prime minister in 2023 and also led the ruling Liberal Democratic Party. He governed through the normal Diet/parliamentary system and the LDP-Komeito coalition, which gave him substantial agenda-setting power but not unconstrained control. The main limits were the coalition partner, the Diet, senior civil servants, public opinion, and, in practice, the need to manage intra-LDP factions and cabinet discipline.

In 2023, his government’s main themes were foreign/security policy, inflation relief, wage growth, defense buildup, and an effort to stabilize Japan’s place in the U.S.-led order after Russia’s war on Ukraine. The year was also marked by domestic security shocks, a major political-funds scandal, continuing fallout from the Unification Church controversy, and persistent questions about media access, criminal-justice practices, and immigration/refugee policy.

**Evidence Records**

1. **Formal power, coalition rule, and real constraints**
- Factual claim: Kishida was chosen as prime minister by the elected parliament, selected his cabinet, and governed with only limited room to act alone because Japan’s system left policy in the hands of elected officials, senior civil servants, and the LDP-Komeito governing bloc.
- Why it matters: This sets the ceiling on how directly to attribute 2023 outcomes to Kishida personally.
- Strongest source: Freedom House, *Japan: Freedom in the World 2024 Country Report* (publisher: Freedom House; page reflects 2023 conditions), direct link: https://freedomhouse.org/country/japan/freedom-world/2024
- Locator: lines 41-44 and 89-91 in the opened report.
- Period status: inherited condition, with 2023 continuation.
- Connection to Kishida: strong for formal office; moderate for policy outcomes because of coalition and bureaucratic mediation.
- Complicating/contrary evidence: Japan’s PM can be replaced by the Diet and by party leadership dynamics; that is formal constraint, not a contradiction.
- Cautions: Freedom House is an external assessment, not a constitutional text; it summarizes rather than exhaustively proves institutional mechanics.
- Independent support: PMO policy speeches from January and October 2023; Reuters coverage of coalition dynamics.

2. **G7 Hiroshima leadership, Ukraine alignment, and nuclear diplomacy**

## Workplan

1. **Orient.** Establish the ruler's formal and practical authority, the inherited situation, major external shocks or constraints, and the information environment.
2. **Plan coverage.** For every selected lens, identify the important favorable, adverse, disputed, and exculpatory possibilities that research should test.
3. **Find the main record.** Begin with strong overviews and syntheses. Identify the consequential decisions, laws, budgets, appointments, statements, implementation, corrections, and outcomes.
4. **Verify material claims.** Open the underlying sources. Use precise laws, official records, audits, judgments, datasets, scholarship, monitoring, and credible reporting where they establish or challenge an important claim. Assess each source’s authority, independence, method, proximity, and date.
5. **Test the account.** Look for contrary evidence, independent corroboration, relevant local-language material, source dependencies, and alternative explanations. Distinguish inherited conditions and country context from conduct attributable to the ruler.
6. **Deepen where useful.** Repeat steps 3–5 while new searches add a material fact, a stronger source, a missing perspective, or a necessary correction. Finish when further searching mainly repeats the established record or reaches a concrete access barrier.
7. **Prepare the handoff.** Organize the evidence by underlying fact and map each developed claim to every selected lens it supports.
