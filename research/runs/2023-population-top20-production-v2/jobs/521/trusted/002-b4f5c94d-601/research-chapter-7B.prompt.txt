- **Outcomes:** Observed changes in conduct, institutions, rights, security, welfare, delivery and compliance. Typical sources: statistics and datasets, evaluations and surveys, audits, academic research, NGO and international-organization reports, histories and reporting.

2. Assets, beneficial ownership, gifts, business/family interests, procurement,
   licensing, regulation, pardons, campaign finance, and foreign-policy benefits.

   loyalty demands, and whether appointees facilitate self-dealing or impunity.

   remedies, and ruler response.

   whether practice strengthened or normalized trust and accountability.

legislative records, asset declarations, procurement records, official

reporting, and serious academic histories. Official sources are strong for the
fact that a statement, appointment, filing, or rule exists, but conditional for
self-serving claims of honesty. Allegations must remain allegations; seek an
independent finding, underlying record, response, and contrary evidence.

**Overview**
Luiz Inácio Lula da Silva was the elected president of Brazil in 2023, heading the federal executive after narrowly defeating Jair Bolsonaro in 2022. His authority was real but constrained: he governed with a fragmented Congress, needed centrist and right-leaning votes for major legislation, and faced an assertive judiciary, a politically volatile security environment after the January 8 attacks, and an inherited military establishment still being re-normalized after the Bolsonaro years.

**Evidence Records**

1. **Presidency with constrained governing power**
- **Fact:** Lula held the presidency and could direct the federal executive, but he did not have free hands over the legislature or coercive institutions. Reuters reported him saying the government “needs Congress” and that the left had at most 136 votes, far short of the 257 needed for ordinary approval; a Reuters account also described a large congressional bloc led by Arthur Lira as a force the government had to bargain with.  
- **Why it matters:** This defines the actual scope of Lula’s influence in 2023: executive agenda-setting, but dependence on coalition management and bargaining.
- **Strongest source:** Reuters via UOL, 2 June 2023, “Lula defende diálogo com oposição diante de minoria da esquerda no Congresso.” [Link](https://economia.uol.com.br/noticias/reuters/2023/06/02/lula-diz-que-e-preciso-conversar-com-que-nao-gosta-do-governo-porque-esquerda-e-minoria-no-congresso.htm)
- **Locator:** Reuters quote on congressional math and need to dialogue with opposition.
- **Period:** Requested period.
- **Connection to ruler:** Direct, within Lula’s own authority.
- **Complicating evidence:** The same year Lula’s allies also won key congressional leadership votes, which improved his capacity to govern but did not eliminate dependence on bargaining.
- **Cautions:** This is a political statement to journalists, not a formal institutional measurement.
- **Independent support:** Reuters on the Senate/Camber leadership vote backing Lula, and the Freedom House report on a polarized political arena.
- **Retrospective note:** None.

2. **January 8, 2023 attack on the core institutions**

## Workplan

1. **Orient.** Establish the ruler's formal and practical authority, the inherited situation, major external shocks or constraints, and the information environment.
2. **Plan coverage.** For every selected lens, identify the important favorable, adverse, disputed, and exculpatory possibilities that research should test.
3. **Find the main record.** Begin with strong overviews and syntheses. Identify the consequential decisions, laws, budgets, appointments, statements, implementation, corrections, and outcomes.
4. **Verify material claims.** Open the underlying sources. Use precise laws, official records, audits, judgments, datasets, scholarship, monitoring, and credible reporting where they establish or challenge an important claim. Assess each source’s authority, independence, method, proximity, and date.
5. **Test the account.** Look for contrary evidence, independent corroboration, relevant local-language material, source dependencies, and alternative explanations. Distinguish inherited conditions and country context from conduct attributable to the ruler.
6. **Deepen where useful.** Repeat steps 3–5 while new searches add a material fact, a stronger source, a missing perspective, or a necessary correction. Finish when further searching mainly repeats the established record or reaches a concrete access barrier.
7. **Prepare the handoff.** Organize the evidence by underlying fact and map each developed claim to every selected lens it supports.

Keep accepted evidence, corroboration, reused sources, uninspected leads, rejected sources, and blocked sources in clearly labelled groups. Explain the source landscape when a lens remains unanswered.
