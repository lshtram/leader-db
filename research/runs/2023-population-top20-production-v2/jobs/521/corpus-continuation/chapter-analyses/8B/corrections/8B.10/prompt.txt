# 8B. Ruler Effectiveness and Competence

Status: draft; v4 objective-evidence questions require controlled rejudgment

## Chapter Identity

- Chapter: `8B`
- Category: `ruler_effectiveness_and_competence`
- Evidence strategy: one ruler-period dossier, researched across ten overlapping lenses
- Judge strategy: one chapter judge applies one comparative meter across all eligible rulers
- Rubric version: `chapter_8b_v4`

## Chapter Purpose

Assess how effectively the ruler defines a governing program, converts it into
state or ruling-network action, coordinates implementation, adapts, and advances
their own stated or reliably revealed objectives. The ten questions are evidence
lenses feeding one final 1-10 chapter score, not equal sub-scores.

This chapter is ideology-neutral. Moral worth and execution competence are
separate. A ruler may execute a cruel, aggressive, corrupt, or authoritarian
program competently and score highly in 8B while scoring very poorly in other
chapters. Never smuggle moral approval or condemnation into the competence score.

## Ten Evidence Lenses

| Lens | Simple question | Detailed research question | Priority evidence |
|---|---|---|---|
| **8B.1 — Clear governing program** | Were the ruler's real goals clear enough to evaluate? | Did the ruler state or reliably reveal a sufficiently clear program in dated speeches, manifestos, strategies, directives, or formal acts to freeze and test its policy, ideological, power, and international goals? | **Rhetoric and representations**; **Formal acts and law** |
| **8B.2 — Turning goals into machinery** | Did the ruler turn goals into concrete plans, rules, resources, people, and institutions? | Did the ruler translate that program into enacted laws, budgets, appointments, timelines, institutions, regulations, and enforcement mechanisms within actual authority? | **Formal acts and law**; **Personnel**; **Implementation and operational conduct** |
| **8B.3 — Mobilizing the ruling system** | Did the ruler effectively mobilize the state and ruling network toward those goals? | Did executed resources and administrative records show effective mobilization of the state, party, military, coalition, or ruling network toward the ruler's chosen program? | **Resources**; **Personnel**; **Implementation and operational conduct** |
| **8B.4 — Capable implementers** | Did the ruler choose and manage people capable of carrying out the program? | Did the ruler appoint, empower, retain, and when necessary replace people capable of executing the program, whether professionals, technocrats, organizers, loyal operators, or coercive administrators? | **Personnel**; **Implementation and operational conduct**; **Outcomes** |
| **8B.5 — Coordination and control** | Did the ruler maintain coordination and follow-through across the system? | Did the ruler maintain documented coordination, territorial reach, milestone completion, and compliance across ministries, regions, institutions, security forces, and implementing agencies? | **Implementation and operational conduct**; **Outcomes** |
| **8B.6 — From plans to practice** | Did the ruler turn plans and announcements into real government action? | Did legislation, budgets, and directives become observable enforcement, services, projects, and institutional practice rather than remain slogans, plans, or symbolic acts? | **Formal acts and law**; **Implementation and operational conduct**; **Outcomes** |

## Scope and Non-Goals

- Establish an explicit or reliably revealed program before judging execution.
  Repeated discriminating choices may reveal goals; hindsight speculation may not.
- Evaluate strategic clarity, operationalization, mobilization, personnel fit,
  coordination, implementation, outcomes, adaptation, crisis management, and
  durable progress as mutually informing evidence.
- Do not judge whether goals are democratic, peaceful, lawful, prosperous, humane,
  or personally honest. Those judgments belong in 1B-7B.
- Do not reward repression, propaganda, longevity, survival, economic growth,
  administrative size, or institutional quality by itself. Ask whether it
  effectively served the ruler's identified program.
- Do not assume failure from an outcome the ruler did not seek, except where
  maintaining basic government function was necessarily part of governing.
- Do not use the client matrix as evidence.

Before reading outcomes or assigning a score, freeze a portfolio of the ruler's major
stated or reliably revealed programs plus necessary core-government functions. Apply
the same breadth rule across regime types. Do not cherry-pick the easiest achieved
goal, reduce a democratic government to its broadest welfare promises, or reduce an
authoritarian government to survival, weapons, or power consolidation. Survival and
consolidation may be one material program component, never the entire portfolio by
default.

## Researcher Workflow and Evidence Themes

Follow the local-first researcher guide and source-confidence registry. Query the
exact case locally. WGI, V-Dem, BTI, budgets, and outcome indicators are useful
context or corroboration, but generic state capacity is not ruler effectiveness.

Research chronologically across themes rather than issuing one search per lens:

1. Identify stated programs from platforms, manifestos, coalition agreements,
   speeches, strategies, directives, budgets, and plans. Identify revealed goals
   only from repeated choices that distinguish them from rhetoric or speculation.
2. Trace conversion into priorities, resources, laws, institutions, appointments,
   timelines, enforcement, and measurable milestones.
3. Examine whether the state, party, military, coalition, bureaucracy, territorial
   network, or informal machinery actually mobilized, coordinated, and followed
   through; include bottlenecks and implementation gaps.
4. Assess whether personnel were capable for the assigned program. Ideological or
   coercive operators may be effective implementers in this narrow chapter even
   when their conduct is reprehensible elsewhere.
5. Compare promised/revealed objectives with implementation and outcomes after
   realistic lags, inherited trends, peer conditions, shocks, and resources.
6. Trace learning, replacement, reallocation, crisis response, resistance
   management, short-term gains, reversals, durability, and end-of-period position.

Prefer primary program and budget documents, legislation, administrative and
audit records, implementation data, credible outcome series, contemporaneous
records, serious academic histories, independent evaluations, and reputable
reporting. Official sources establish declared goals and formal actions but not
successful execution by themselves. Preserve failures, contradictions, abandoned
priorities, propaganda-only claims, alternative causal explanations, and adverse
side effects; record the latter for cross-chapter use without morally deducting
them in 8B.

Reuse each stable evidence item across relevant lenses. Natural-language coverage
and gaps are acceptable; publication must not depend on exact status terms,
sequential IDs, or exhaustive mapping syntax.

## Common Evidence-Item Semantics

Each material evidence item should carry a stable evidence ID; a concise factual
claim; source URL, title, publisher, and publication/access date where available;
source type and confidence with a short reason; a quotation or precise locator
when available; target-year/ruler-period fit; ruler attribution and
inherited-baseline relevance; its positive, negative, mixed, contextual, or
contrary role; and the supported lens IDs. Fields may be normalized later and
natural-language equivalents are acceptable. The substantive minimum is a
traceable source, intelligible claim, temporal fit, attribution, and evidence
role. One item may support multiple lenses without duplication.

## Attribution, Sparse Evidence, and Historical Cases

Separate ruler capability from inherited state capacity, predecessor programs,
subordinate initiative, favorable trends, external patrons, and luck. Assess
formal/informal control, tenure, coalition and institutional constraints,
resources, shocks, lag, ownership of appointments and priorities, response to
implementation information, and plausible counterfactual trajectory.

For obscure or historical rulers, later scholarship, archives, diplomatic
records, budgets, institutional histories, and observable implementation may be
more informative than missing modern indicators. Oral or informal programs can
be evidenced by repeated choices. Sparse evidence should reduce confidence and
widen the defensible range, not force a neutral score or invalidate the case.
Short tenure may permit judgment of direction and mobilization while leaving
outcomes unresolved. Closed systems may document plans abundantly while hiding
failures; official volume is not proof of execution.

## Holistic 1-10 Chapter Rubric

Synthesize the causal chain from program through action to durable progress.
Lenses are redundant and qualitatively weighted: clear plans with no execution
cannot score highly; favorable outcomes without ruler-attributable direction or
action warrant limited credit; a major strategic collapse can outweigh several
administrative successes. Never calculate a simple mean or score by citation
count.

Strategic clarity, centralized control, personnel discipline, and planning without
independently evidenced implementation and goal progress cannot exceed 5. Scores above
5 require attributable implementation plus progress across material parts of the
frozen portfolio. Scores above 7 additionally require independent evidence of outcomes,
durability, and adaptation rather than official architecture, coercion, longevity, or
survival alone. Harmful programs remain morally neutral here, but they must still show
actual advancement rather than mere capacity to command.

| Score | Chapter anchor |
|---:|---|
| 1 | No coherent program or effective governing control, or catastrophic inability to mobilize and execute: recurring chaos, paralysis, collapse, or decisive movement away from the ruler's own objectives. |
| 2-3 | Weak or unstable direction and serious operational failure; plans remain largely symbolic, coordination repeatedly breaks down, adaptation is poor, and major objectives substantially recede. |
| 4-5 | Mixed competence: some clear priorities and real delivery coexist with major implementation gaps, inconsistent control, failed initiatives, weak adaptation, or fragile/temporary results. |
| 6-7 | Generally effective direction and execution across important priorities, with meaningful mobilization and progress but notable delays, failures, constraints, reversals, or durability concerns. |
| 8-9 | Strong strategic-operational alignment, capable personnel and coordination, adaptive implementation, and substantial durable advancement of the ruler's program across major priorities. |
| 10 | Exceptional, sustained mastery of direction, mobilization, execution, adaptation, crisis/resistance management, and durable goal realization under demanding conditions. |

These anchors apply equally to benevolent and harmful programs. The rationale
must separately name the program and state that moral effects are evaluated in
other chapters.

## Confidence

Report confidence separately on a 0-100 scale. Consider program identification,
ruler attribution, causal trace from inputs to implementation/outcomes, source
independence, time fit and lag, external-shock controls, source/theme diversity,
closed-system opacity, and contrary explanations. Missing lenses lower confidence
rather than automatically lowering the chapter score. Give a defensible score
range where evidence or attribution is materially uncertain.

## One-Judge Comparative Workflow

One judge owns 8B across all eligible rulers for the target year/period:

1. Read every 8B dossier, identify each ruler's explicit or reliably revealed
   program, and quarantine identity failures—not sparse evidence.
2. Separate moral evaluation from execution before scoring. Record cross-chapter
   harms without deducting them here.
3. Establish batch anchors spanning democratic and authoritarian, beneficial and
   harmful, modern and historical, high- and low-capacity contexts.
4. Assign tentative holistic scores along the program-to-results chain, then
   compare neighbors for consistent treatment of inherited capacity, resources,
   shocks, tenure, lag, luck, resistance, and durability.
5. Revisit cases dominated by generic governance indicators, official claims,
   survival/tenure, favorable outcomes without attribution, or moral halo/horns.
6. Emit one chapter score, confidence/range, identified program, rationale,
   strongest execution and failure evidence, unresolved causal alternatives,
   missing themes, and review flag.

## Acceptance Checklist

- Missing implementation evidence never establishes ineffectiveness; it produces a
  null when no ruler-attributed execution fact remains. Scores above `5` require
  observable implementation or coordination, and scores above `7` additionally
  require independent evidence of attributable outcomes, adaptation, or durable
  progress across more than one material program component.

- All ten authoritative lenses are present verbatim and serve one holistic score.
- Ideology-neutrality is explicit and moral worth is separated from execution in
  research, scoring, calibration, and rationale.
- An explicit or reliably revealed program is established before execution is
  judged; hindsight speculation is labeled and constrained.
- Local-first order, citations, source profiling, and run profile are retained.
- Evidence items satisfy the common traceability, temporal-fit, attribution,
  evidence-role, and lens-link semantics.
- Ruler action is separated from inherited capacity, subordinates, luck, trends,
  shocks, tenure, and realistic outcome lags.
- Missing lenses reduce confidence rather than invalidate or mechanically depress
  the score.
- One judge compares all rulers using one meter; lenses are neither equally
  weighted nor mechanically averaged.
- Final output uses the common chapter-judge envelope, including adjacent-anchor
  comparisons and an explicit manual-review flag.
- Smoke testing and revision occur before production activation.
