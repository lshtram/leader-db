Evidence categories:

- **Formal acts and law:** Laws, bills, votes, decrees, regulations, treaties, directives, strategies and formal commitments. - **Outcomes:** Observed changes in conduct, institutions, rights, security, welfare, delivery and compliance. Typical sources: statistics and datasets, evaluations and surveys, audits, academic research, NGO and international-organization reports, histories and reporting.

## Researcher Workflow and Evidence Themes

Follow the local-first researcher guide and source-confidence registry. Query the
exact ruler/country/year or bounded period locally before web discovery. Treat
WGI, V-Dem, and CPI as country-level context and triangulation, not proof of the
ruler's personal conduct.

Research by themes that cut across lenses, not by ten isolated searches:

1. Material public statements, corrections, sworn evidence, and documented
   concealment, including examples of costly candor and adverse findings.
2. Assets, beneficial ownership, gifts, business/family interests, procurement,
   licensing, regulation, pardons, campaign finance, and foreign-policy benefits.
3. Appointments and access patterns: competence, kinship, donor/client ties,
   loyalty demands, and whether appointees facilitate self-dealing or impunity.
4. Courts, prosecutors, auditors, legislatures, ethics bodies, inquiries,
   journalists, and whistleblowers: independence, obstruction, retaliation,
   remedies, and ruler response.
5. Promise and commitment records, ethical rules, institutional reforms, and
   whether practice strengthened or normalized trust and accountability.

Prefer primary legal and financial records, judgments, audit/inquiry findings,
legislative records, asset declarations, procurement records, official
transcripts, sanctions findings with stated evidence, reputable investigative
reporting, and serious academic histories. Official sources are strong for the
fact that a statement, appointment, filing, or rule exists, but conditional for
self-serving claims of honesty. Allegations must remain allegations; seek an
independent finding, underlying record, response, and contrary evidence.

Each accepted item should retain its citation, date, claim, period fit, source
type/confidence, ruler-attribution assessment, and uncertainty. Reuse a stable
evidence item across every relevant lens. Coverage descriptions may use natural
language; no exact status vocabulary is required.

**Overview**
Félix Tshisekedi was the elected president of the Democratic Republic of the Congo in 2023 and sought a second term in the December 2023 election. In practice, his authority was constrained by a fragmented political system, the inherited influence of Joseph Kabila’s camp and parliamentary bargaining, the state of siege in North Kivu and Ituri, continuing armed conflict in the east, and the leverage of security institutions, the electoral commission, the courts, and foreign-backed regional security dynamics.

## Workplan

1. **Orient.** Establish the ruler's formal and practical authority, the inherited situation, major external shocks or constraints, and the information environment.
2. **Plan coverage.** For every selected lens, identify the important favorable, adverse, disputed, and exculpatory possibilities that research should test.
3. **Find the main record.** Begin with strong overviews and syntheses. Identify the consequential decisions, laws, budgets, appointments, statements, implementation, corrections, and outcomes.
4. **Verify material claims.** Open the underlying sources. Use precise laws, official records, audits, judgments, datasets, scholarship, monitoring, and credible reporting where they establish or challenge an important claim. Assess each source’s authority, independence, method, proximity, and date.
5. **Test the account.** Look for contrary evidence, independent corroboration, relevant local-language material, source dependencies, and alternative explanations. Distinguish inherited conditions and country context from conduct attributable to the ruler.
6. **Deepen where useful.** Repeat steps 3–5 while new searches add a material fact, a stronger source, a missing perspective, or a necessary correction. Finish when further searching mainly repeats the established record or reaches a concrete access barrier.
7. **Prepare the handoff.** Organize the evidence by underlying fact and map each developed claim to every selected lens it supports.

## Evidence records

Create one record for one source supporting one material claim. If one source supports several materially different claims used downstream, create a separate record and locator for each claim. Give records about the same underlying fact or event the same `underlying_fact_key`, and identify reports that depend on the same investigation, dataset, wire story, or official claim.

Each developed record includes:

- one precise factual claim and why it matters;
- source title, publisher, date, direct URL, and stable locator;
- target-period, inherited, or later-retrospective status;
- the ruler-attribution basis;
- source limitations, dependencies, and credible contrary evidence;
- independent corroboration; and
- every selected question ID the claim supports.

Keep accepted evidence, corroboration, reused sources, uninspected leads, rejected sources, and blocked sources in clearly labelled groups. Explain the source landscape when a lens remains unanswered.

## Deliverables

Return:

1. a concise orientation covering authority, baseline, shocks, and information environment;
2. a compact evidence index;
3. a disposition for every selected lens, with evidence IDs or a clear gap explanation;
4. the labelled source groups;
5. source counts and remaining research gaps; and
6. one machine line for every developed record.

Each machine line begins `SOURCE_CLAIM_JSON:` outside a code fence, followed by a valid JSON object with `title`, `publisher`, `publication_date`, `url`, `claim`, `locator`, `provisional_id`, `canonical_fact_key`, `disposition`, `chapter_ids`, `methodology_ids`, `source_type`, `source_confidence`, `source_confidence_reason`, `final_evidence_use`, `period_fit`, `ruler_attribution`, `contrary_evidence`, `underlying_fact_key`, and `lenses`.

Machine records represent accepted, context, or discovery evidence. Keep rejected sources and uninspected leads in their labelled groups. Use `final_evidence`, `context`, or `discovery_only` for `disposition` and `final_evidence_use`. Use unique IDs beginning `WEB-7B-`, the chapter label `7B`, and exact selected question IDs from ["7B.1", "7B.10", "7B.2", "7B.3", "7B.4", "7B.5", "7B.6", "7B.7", "7B.8", "7B.9"]. The parent workflow will merge these records into the cumulative ledger.

## Discovery/extraction boundary

A separate discovery stage has already built the candidate source catalogue shown in Resources already identified. This turn is evidence extraction and gap-directed inspection, not the primary broad-discovery pass. Open and inspect at least 12 of the most promising candidates across independent source families when access permits, then extract located facts. Perform additional searches only for a concrete factual, attribution, contrary-evidence, or source-family gap exposed during inspection. Evidence-unit targets are depth diagnostics and must not be used as proof that the source universe is saturated. For every candidate actually inspected, emit one SOURCE_CANDIDATE_JSON line using the discovery contract and change access_status to accepted, rejected, opened, or access_blocked. accepted means at least one SOURCE_CLAIM_JSON evidence record was retained from that same URL; rejected means it was inspected but provided no usable evidence. If access prevents the inspection target, emit INSPECTION_SATURATION_BLOCKER_JSON with chapter_id, a nonempty documents_attempted array, and a concrete limitation.
