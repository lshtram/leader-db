- Fact: In 2023, security forces violently dispersed protests, arrested opposition-linked actors, and journalists faced repeated intimidation and assault.
- Why it matters: This bears directly on political freedom, public safety, and the integrity of the electoral environment.
- Strongest source: Human Rights Watch, “DR Congo: Peaceful Protests Violently Repressed,” 2023-05-29. Direct link: https://www.hrw.org/news/2023/05/29/dr-congo-peaceful-protests-violently-repressed
- Locator: lines 596-597.

- Connection to ruler: Strong. The policing and intimidation occurred under the Tshisekedi administration.
- Complicating evidence: Police said they opened an investigation and arrested three officers; that mitigates but does not erase the underlying abuse.
- Cautions: HRW relies on witness reporting and advocacy standards; still, the event is concrete and well documented.
- Independent support: CPJ’s 2023 alerts document detentions and assaults on journalists; Freedom House says journalists face threats, arrests, and attacks and that only limited outlets reach the whole country.

5. **Election administration problems and disputed result**
- Fact: The December 2023 election was chaotic, with delayed or missing polling, extended voting, and widespread irregularities alleged by opposition and observer missions.
- Why it matters: This is the key evidence base for judging political competition, legitimacy, and institutional quality.
- Strongest source: Reuters, “Congo rules out election re-run as observers flag irregularities,” 2023-12-28. Direct link: https://www.investing.com/news/world-news/congo-rules-out-election-rerun-as-observers-flag-irregularities-3264529

- Period relevance: In-period.
- Connection to ruler: Strong but mediated through the CENI and election-security apparatus.
- Complicating evidence: The CENI said delays occurred but that the vote remained on track; the constitutional court later upheld Tshisekedi’s victory.
- Cautions: Reuters is reporting on observer claims and government responses, not independently auditing the count.
- Independent support: CENCO-ECC election monitoring, CENI result portal, and the presidency’s January 2024 confirmation of the court ruling.

6. **2023 re-election confirmed, but only after contested tabulation**
- Fact: The constitutional court confirmed Tshisekedi’s re-election in January 2024 after rejecting legal challenges to the December 2023 results.
- Why it matters: This is the formal endpoint of the 2023 electoral cycle and anchors the regime’s continuity.

7. **Economic policy: resilience, but inflation and fiscal strain**
- Fact: The economy stayed resilient in 2023, driven by mining, but inflation accelerated sharply and the fiscal position worsened.
- Why it matters: This is the main economic performance story for the period.

- Connection to ruler: Strong. Fiscal and monetary policy were under the administration’s control, though shaped by global commodity and exchange-rate pressures.

8. **Social welfare: poverty, weak services, and human capital constraints**
- Fact: DRC entered 2023 with very low living standards, weak health and education outcomes, and persistent service-delivery gaps; free primary education remained one of Tshisekedi’s signature policies, but the broader system was still strained.

- Strongest source: World Bank, DRC country page. Direct link: https://www.worldbank.org/ext/en/country/drc

- Connection to ruler: Moderate. Tshisekedi inherited the constraints, but his administration was responsible for the free-primary-education rollout and service reforms.
- Complicating evidence: World Bank notes major public investments and enrollment gains, but also persistent poverty, stunting, and low learning outcomes.
- Cautions: The page mixes current and retrospective metrics; it is best used for structural context rather than a single-year verdict.
- Independent support: World Bank 2024 education project materials note nearly 4 million more children in public primary school in 2022-2023 because of free primary education.

9. **Corruption and weak accountability**
- Fact: Corruption remained systemic in 2023, with weak enforcement and public financial mismanagement continuing despite formal anti-corruption institutions.
- Why it matters: This is central to judging integrity and state capacity.
- Strongest source: U.S. Department of State, “2023 Country Report on Human Rights Practices: Democratic Republic of the Congo,” 2024. Direct link: https://www.ecoi.net/en/document/2107668.html

- Complicating evidence: The government did have an anti-corruption agency under the president’s office, which shows an institutional response even if enforcement remained weak.
- Cautions: A country report is broad and can aggregate many issues without granular case depth.
- Independent support: Reuters’ 2023 election factbox said anti-graft efforts fell short and scandals dogged the administration; IMF repeatedly emphasized governance and transparency reforms.

**Information Environment**
Reliable information on Congo in 2023 was available, but uneven and often partial.

- In eastern conflict zones, access was limited by insecurity, armed groups, and military operations, so civilian casualty and displacement figures are often estimates rather than complete counts.
- During the election period, political polarization was high, the CENI was contested, and observers reported logistical failures and irregularities.
- Press freedom was weak. Journalists faced detention, assault, and criminal pressure, which likely reduced independent reporting.
- Courts and security services were themselves part of the story, which makes official claims about order, legality, and accountability less reliable on their own.
- Macroeconomic data were stronger than political and security data, but even there the best sources mainly describe national aggregates, not local living conditions.

**Overview**
Félix Tshisekedi’s 2023 responsibility on this chapter was limited but real. Congo, the Democratic Republic of the, had no nuclear arsenal or active nuclear-weapons program in the sources inspected; its existential-risk posture in 2023 was mostly about nonproliferation, treaty compliance, radiological safeguards, cyber governance, and avoiding escalation in a violent eastern conflict.

The strongest 2023-positive items are institutional: Congo remained an NPT state under IAEA safeguards, had already ratified the CTBT and the TPNW, and Tshisekedi’s government validated a national cybersecurity strategy in 2023 and promulgated a digital code aimed at data protection, cyber governance, and crisis management. The strongest negatives are that the country’s main security energy was absorbed by the M23 war and state-of-siege rule in the east, while there is no visible 2023 evidence of a mature, dedicated AI or broader catastrophic-risk governance program beyond the digital sector.

**Evidence Index**
1. Congo remained under nuclear nonproliferation and safeguards commitments throughout 2023.
- Fact: The DRC was an IAEA member, had safeguards under the NPT, and had an additional protocol in force or signed with the IAEA.
- Why it matters: This is the core baseline for 1B. It shows a non-nuclear state with formal verification ties, not a latent weapons program.
- Strongest source: IAEA Office of Legal Affairs country page, “Democratic Republic of the Congo,” 2026 update, but recording long-standing treaty status. https://ola.iaea.org/Applications/FactSheets/Country/Detail?code=CD
- Locator: lines 41-45.
- Period fit: Inherited baseline, still operative in 2023.
- Ruler attribution: Indirect but valid at the state level; these obligations bound the Tshisekedi ప్రభుత్వం in 2023.
- Contrary evidence: None in the inspected record suggesting a nuclear weapon or weapons-development program.
- Cautions: This is a formal status page, not an implementation audit.
- Independent support: UN treaty status records for CTBT and TPNW.

- Fact: Congo is listed as a CTBT participant with signature on 4 Oct 1996 and ratification on 28 Sep 2004.
- Why it matters: It is a concrete anti-test commitment and a useful marker of responsible nuclear posture.

- Independent support: IAEA safeguards record and UN treaty status page.

--- CHAPTER RESEARCH 2B ---

**Overview**
Tshisekedi’s 2023 international-peace record was dominated by eastern Congo’s armed conflict, especially M23, Rwanda-related escalation, and the government’s reliance on emergency security measures and UN withdrawal planning. The period contains some genuine peace-seeking behavior, including MONUSCO disengagement planning and regional diplomacy, but the evidence also shows repeated force-first framing, an unresolved conflict, and serious civilian harm from both rebel and state/security forces.

**Evidence Index**
1. **MONUSCO withdrawal as a core policy choice**
- Fact: In September 2023 Tshisekedi asked for accelerated MONUSCO withdrawal to begin before year-end.
- Why it matters: This is the main formal peace-and-security decision of the year and directly affected international peacekeeping and the state’s own security burden.
- Strongest source: Reuters, 2023-09-21. https://theprint.in/world/congo-president-asks-un-peacekeepers-to-start-packing-up-this-year/1771039/
- Locator: Reuters text quoted in the search result, including Tshisekedi’s instruction to start accelerated withdrawal from December 2023.
- Period relevance: In-period.
- Ruler attribution: Direct. He publicly instructed the government and explained the policy at the UN.
- Contrary evidence: The move risked weakening civilian protection while conflict remained active; the same reporting notes deadly anti-UN unrest and insecurity.
- Cautions: Wire reporting on a speech and policy direction, not proof of successful withdrawal management.
- Corroboration: UN Security Council resolution 2717 later adopted a phased withdrawal plan.

2. **Formal disengagement plan with MONUSCO**
- Fact: The Congolese government and MONUSCO signed a disengagement plan in November 2023 for accelerated, gradual, orderly withdrawal.
- Why it matters: This is the clearest formal act on lawful security and international peace.
- Strongest source: MONUSCO press release, 2023-11-22. https://monusco.unmissions.org/en/press-releases/pr-the-government-of-the-drc-and-monusco-sign-a-disengagement-plan-for-the
- Locator: search-result text summarizing the signed note and timetable.
- Period relevance: In-period.
- Ruler attribution: Indirect but strong; negotiated by the government under Tshisekedi’s authority.
- Contrary evidence: Withdrawal planning did not end the conflict; it may have reduced external stabilization capacity before security improved.
- Cautions: Official mission source; strong for the formal plan, not for judging its wisdom.

3. **UN Security Council endorsed a staged MONUSCO withdrawal**
- Fact: In December 2023 the Security Council adopted Resolution 2717, extending MONUSCO one year and setting a phased drawdown with transfer of responsibilities to the government.
- Why it matters: Shows the international framework Tshisekedi was operating within.
- Strongest source: UN Press, 2023-12-19. https://press.un.org/en/2023/sc15538.doc.htm
- Locator: search-result summary of Resolution 2717.
- Period relevance: In-period.
- Ruler attribution: Indirect. This reflects state-to-UN negotiations and international constraints.
- Contrary evidence: The drawdown started while conflict and elections were ongoing.
- Cautions: UN summary is authoritative for the resolution, not for DRC domestic effectiveness.
- Corroboration: Reuters September 2023 and MONUSCO November 2023.

4. **Council demanded M23 withdrawal and dialogue**
- Fact: The Security Council’s 2023 statement called for calm, dialogue with Rwanda, and full implementation of M23 withdrawal/cantonment commitments from the Luanda process.
- Why it matters: This is the international-law and negotiation baseline for the conflict.
- Strongest source: UN Press, 2023-09-? / 2023 presidential-statement coverage. https://press.un.org/en/2023/sc15443.doc.htm
- Locator: search-result summary describing calls for dialogue and M23 withdrawal.
- Period relevance: In-period.
- Ruler attribution: Indirect, but directly relevant to Tshisekedi’s external conflict policy.
- Contrary evidence: M23 did not withdraw; fighting continued.
- Cautions: It records Council terms, not Congolese compliance.

5. **Tshisekedi blamed Rwanda and framed the conflict as external aggression**
- Fact: In early and late 2023 Tshisekedi publicly accused Rwanda of backing M23 and described the crisis as aggression requiring sanctions and resistance.
- Why it matters: This is the public justification for his conflict policy.
- Strongest source: Africanews, 2023-03-05; 2023-09-15; 2023-12-11.  
  https://www.africanews.com/2023/03/05/tshisekedi-calls-upon-france-to-sanction-rwanda-over-m23-violence  
  https://www.africanews.com/2023/09/15/un-before-the-general-assembly-the-drc-warns-of-aggression-by-rwanda  
  https://www.africanews.com/2023/12/11/drc-incumbent-president-tshisekedi-vows-to-rid-country-of-m23-rebels-at-goma-campaign-rall/
- Locator: search-result summaries and quoted wording.
- Period relevance: In-period.
- Ruler attribution: Direct rhetoric.
- Contrary evidence: Rwanda denied the allegations; independent sources documented eastern complexity and abuses by multiple actors.
- Cautions: Political reporting; useful for rhetoric and chronology, not proof of the allegations’ full truth.
- Corroboration: UN and HRW reporting on cross-border tension and M23/RDF evidence.

6. **M23 and RDF support created a real cross-border war risk**
- Fact: HRW and UN-related reporting describe Rwanda-backed M23 offensives, RDF support, and a real risk of direct confrontation between DRC and Rwanda.
- Why it matters: This is the main interstate-security exposure in 2023.
- Strongest source: HRW, 2023-06-13. https://www.hrw.org/news/2023/06/13/dr-congo-killings-rapes-rwanda-backed-m23-rebels
- Locator: lines 600-605 from the opened page.
- Period relevance: In-period.
- Ruler attribution: Strong for the external-threat environment, indirect for Congo’s response.
- Contrary evidence: Rwanda denied support; M23/ Rwanda narratives remain disputed.
- Cautions: HRW is strong but advocacy-based; the claim about RDF support should be read alongside UN reporting and state responses.
- Corroboration: US State Department report, UN briefing, Security Council report.

7. **Tshisekedi did pursue some diplomacy, but only after military failure was apparent**
- Fact: Reuters reporting indicates Congo became open to direct talks with M23 in 2024 after earlier resistance; in 2023 the government was still using a military approach while diplomacy was developing in parallel.
- Why it matters: For 2B.1 and 2B.7, the main question is whether peaceful alternatives were seriously pursued early enough.
- Strongest source: Reuters-derived reporting on 2025/2024 negotiations, plus 2023 Reuters/Reuters-feed material showing debate around dialogue. Since the 2023 record is thin, this point is best treated as context.
- Locator: Reuters-feed search snippets describing Tshisekedi’s earlier refusal to talk to M23 and later movement toward talks.
- Period relevance: Mixed; mostly retrospective context.
- Ruler attribution: Direct to the policy line, but the 2023 evidence is incomplete.
- Contrary evidence: Tshisekedi had repeatedly branded M23 a terrorist proxy and resisted direct dialogue.
- Cautions: Because the 2023 record is incomplete, this remains a weakly supported lens area.

8. **Lawful-security record is compromised by state-force abuses**
- Fact: The DRC State Department report says government security forces used deadly and disproportionate force against a protest, killing 56 and injuring 75, and that the state of siege restricted assembly and enabled abuses.
- Why it matters: This directly bears on civilian protection, lawful force, and accountability in conflict-adjacent security operations.
- Strongest source: US State Department report via ecoi.net, 2023 Country Report, lines 18, 81-85, 110-111, 138-139. https://www.ecoi.net/en/document/2107668.html
- Locator: lines 18-19 and 81-85 and 138-139.
- Period relevance: In-period.
- Ruler attribution: Strong; these were government forces under Tshisekedi’s administration.
- Contrary evidence: The report also says some members were arrested/suspended and investigations continued.
- Cautions: A human-rights country report aggregates many events, but the protest massacre and state-of-siege restrictions are concrete.
- Corroboration: HRW protest repression report and Amnesty state-of-siege critique.

9. **M23 abuses were documented, but they were not evidence of Congolese restraint**
- Fact: HRW documented killings, rape, and other abuses by M23, with Rwandan military support and joint armed-group collaboration worsening the conflict.
- Why it matters: This is the core hostile environment Tshisekedi claimed to be facing.
- Strongest source: HRW, 2023-06-13. https://www.hrw.org/news/2023/06/13/dr-congo-killings-rapes-rwanda-backed-m23-rebels
- Locator: lines 600-605 and 639-645.
- Period relevance: In-period.
- Ruler attribution: Indirect; this is about the threat environment and the rationale for Tshisekedi’s policy choices.
- Contrary evidence: None that negates the documented abuses.
- Cautions: HRW does not exonerate Congolese security forces; it documents abuses by multiple sides.
- Corroboration: US State Department report, UN human-rights reporting.

10. **The government accepted limited accountability steps, but impunity remained**
- Fact: The 2023 State Department report says the government took some steps to investigate and punish officials, but impunity remained a serious problem.
- Why it matters: This is the best evidence for 2B.9.
- Strongest source: US State Department report via ecoi.net, lines 9, 16, 33, 43-45, 53, 82-85. https://www.ecoi.net/en/document/2107668.html
- Locator: lines 9, 16, 33, 43-45, 53, 82-85.
- Period relevance: In-period.
- Ruler attribution: Strong, because these were state institutions under Tshisekedi.
- Contrary evidence: The same source says official access was denied to some detention facilities and impunity persisted.
- Cautions: Broad country report, but it is a useful accountability baseline.

**Lens Disposition**
- 2B.1 Peaceful alternatives: partially supported, but weak. There is evidence of diplomacy and eventual engagement, but not enough to show that peaceful options were pursued seriously and early before force.
- 2B.10 Lasting international peace: mixed to negative. MONUSCO withdrawal planning and diplomacy are positives, but the year ended with war, regional tension, and unresolved legal/security fragility.
- 2B.2 Starting or prolonging war: negative evidence present. Tshisekedi did not start M23 conflict, but he did continue a force-first posture while conflict expanded and regional war risk rose.
- 2B.3 Honest justification for force: weakly supported only for the existence of a stated defensive narrative. He repeatedly justified his stance as resisting external aggression; independent evidence shows real threats, but also contested attribution and persistent escalation.
- 2B.4 Civilian and prisoner protection: negative. State forces used deadly force and the state of siege restricted rights.

**Overview**
Tshisekedi’s 2023 domestic-safety record is defined by emergency-rule governance in the east, recurring security-force abuse, weak accountability, and repeated restrictions on protest, dissent, and press activity. The strongest positive evidence is limited: some investigations, suspensions, and arrests followed major abuses, but the record does not show durable protection from state violence or a strong correction of impunity.

**Evidence Index**
1. **State of siege remained active and restrictive**
- Fact: North Kivu and Ituri remained under the state of siege in 2023, with military authorities replacing civilian administration and rights curtailed.
- Why it matters: This is the central domestic-safety policy choice affecting policing, detention, and accountability.
- Strongest source: Freedom House, *Freedom in the World 2023: Democratic Republic of the Congo*, 2023. https://freedomhouse.org/country/democratic-republic-congo/freedom-world/2023
- Locator: lines 89-90, 105, 131, 139-145, 163-173.
- Period relevance: In-period, though rooted in 2021.
- Ruler attribution: Direct, because Tshisekedi’s administration imposed and renewed the measure.
- Contrary evidence: The government framed the state of siege as necessary to restore order; the east remained violent.
- Cautions: Country-profile synthesis; broad and partly retrospective.
- Corroboration: US State Department report; Amnesty; UNJHRO.

2. **Security forces used deadly force in Goma in August 2023**
- Fact: On 30 August 2023 in Goma, security forces used deadly and disproportionate force against a banned protest, killing 56 people and injuring 75.
- Why it matters: This is the clearest single domestic physical-safety abuse in the period.
- Strongest source: US Department of State, 2023 Country Report on Human Rights Practices via ecoi.net. https://www.ecoi.net/en/document/2107668.html
- Locator: lines 17-18.
- Period relevance: In-period.
- Ruler attribution: Strong. These were government security forces under Tshisekedi’s administration.
- Contrary evidence: The report says some officers were arrested or suspended and the North Kivu military governor was removed.
- Cautions: Country-report aggregation, but the incident is concrete and well dated.
- Corroboration: Freedom House and UNJHRO reporting on state-of-siege abuses.

3. **Journalists and dissenters were targeted with arrests and criminal charges**
- Fact: In 2023, journalists and human-rights defenders were regularly targeted by arbitrary arrests; officials in the state-of-siege provinces used contempt, defamation, false-rumor, and insult charges to silence critics.
- Why it matters: This directly bears on safe dissent, oversight, and fear climate.
- Strongest source: US Department of State report via ecoi.net. https://www.ecoi.net/en/document/2107668.html
- Locator: lines 68-69 and 110.
- Period relevance: In-period.
- Ruler attribution: Strong enough for the national administration, especially where the state of siege was involved.
- Contrary evidence: The report also notes some prosecutions and some limited speech remained possible.
- Cautions: Broad report, but methodical and specific.
- Corroboration: Freedom House and CPJ alerts.

4. **Peaceful assembly was restricted, especially in the east**
- Fact: Authorities restricted peaceful assembly and prevented critics from exercising that right, especially in the eastern provinces under the state of siege.
- Why it matters: This is part of the domestic safety envelope because protest policing and assembly bans often turn into violence.
- Strongest source: US Department of State report via ecoi.net. https://www.ecoi.net/en/document/2107668.html
- Locator: lines 138-139 and 163.
- Period relevance: In-period.
- Ruler attribution: Strong. The restrictions were implemented by state authorities under Tshisekedi’s government.
- Contrary evidence: The constitution formally protected assembly, and demonstrations did occur in urban areas.
- Cautions: The report focuses on rights restrictions more than incident-level protest history.
- Corroboration: Freedom House.

5. **Security forces and armed actors both contributed to widespread insecurity**
- Fact: In 2023, armed groups and government security forces both committed serious abuses; conflict-affected provinces accounted for more than 80% of documented abuses in the first half of the year.
- Why it matters: This is the main outcome measure for domestic safety.
- Strongest source: US Department of State report via ecoi.net. https://www.ecoi.net/en/document/2107668.html
- Locator: lines 81-84.
- Period relevance: In-period.
- Ruler attribution: Partial. The violence is not fully attributable to Tshisekedi, but his state’s response and discipline failures matter.
- Contrary evidence: The administration did conduct some security operations and removed at least one official after the Goma killings.
- Cautions: The measure aggregates all abuses and does not isolate causal responsibility.
- Corroboration: Freedom House and UNJHRO.

6. **Some accountability steps followed major abuses, but impunity remained**
- Fact: After the Goma killings, the government opened an investigation, arrested or suspended security personnel, and relieved the North Kivu military governor.
- Why it matters: This is the clearest evidence for remedy and discipline.
- Strongest source: US Department of State report via ecoi.net. https://www.ecoi.net/en/document/2107668.html
- Locator: lines 17-18.
- Period relevance: In-period.
- Ruler attribution: Direct.
- Contrary evidence: The same report and Freedom House say impunity remained common and security-force abuse persisted.
- Cautions: A response happened, but the broader pattern did not shift decisively.
- Corroboration: Freedom House, UNJHRO.

--- CHAPTER RESEARCH 4B ---

**Overview**
Tshisekedi’s 2023 political-freedom record is mixed but mostly negative. The year featured a competitive election that was formally held and eventually certified, but the process was heavily contested, delayed, and accompanied by serious irregularities, pressure on the media, and security-heavy governance in the east. The strongest positive evidence is that elections were in fact held and the constitutional court finalized the result; the strongest negative evidence is weak institutional neutrality, pressure on critics and journalists, disputed tabulation, and a political environment that did not offer reliably equal contestation.

**Evidence Index**
1. **The election existed and resulted in a formal transfer-through-contest outcome, not cancellation**
- Fact: Congo held the December 2023 elections, and the constitutional court later confirmed Tshisekedi’s re-election.
- Why it matters: This is the baseline for contestability and succession.
- Strongest source: Presidency of the DRC, 2024-01-09. https://www.presidence.cd/actualite-detail/actualite/election_presidentielle_2023_felix_tshisekedi_proclame_vainqueur_par_la_cour_constitutionnelle
- Locator: lines 55-58.
- Period relevance: Retrospective confirmation of the 2023 election.
- Ruler attribution: Direct.
- Contrary evidence: Observer missions reported major irregularities and opposition candidates contested the result.
- Cautions: Official confirmation proves legal finality, not broad legitimacy.
- Corroboration: CENI results portal; observer reporting; Reuters coverage of irregularities.

2. **The election process had documented irregularities**
- Fact: Observer missions reported divergences in electoral data, missing or inconsistent polling information, and other anomalies.
- Why it matters: This is the main evidence against genuinely free contestation.
- Strongest source: Actualite.cd reporting on the CENCO-ECC mission findings, 2023-11-13 and 2024-06-19.  
  https://actualite.cd/index.php/2023/11/13/fichier-electoral-et-bureaux-de-vote-divergences-de-chiffres-et-anomalies-rapportes-par  
  https://actualite.cd/index.php/2024/06/19/rdc-elections-ce-quil-faut-retenir-du-rapport-de-la-moe-cenco-ecc
- Locator: article bodies summarizing the mission’s divergence and anomaly claims.
- Period relevance: In-period and immediate retrospective.
- Ruler attribution: Strong, because election administration sat under the national political order Tshisekedi led.
- Contrary evidence: CENI disputed parts of the observer findings.
- Cautions: Local reporting on observer findings; best used with the observer report itself if opened later.
- Corroboration: Reuters on irregularities and CENI response; CENI portal.

3. **The electoral commission and vote administration were contested**
- Fact: The CENI defended the process and rejected some observer allegations, including claims about vote operations in schools and military academies.
- Why it matters: Shows active institutional dispute over the integrity of the process.
- Strongest source: Actualite.cd, 2023-12-22. https://actualite.cd/index.php/2023/12/22/elections-en-rdc-la-ceni-refute-le-rapport-de-la-moe-cenco-ecc-confirmant-la-tenue-des
- Locator: article body on CENI’s rebuttal.
- Period relevance: In-period.
- Ruler attribution: Indirect but relevant to the administration’s electoral posture.
- Contrary evidence: Observer missions and opposition disputes remained unresolved.
- Cautions: Official defense of the process; self-interested.
- Corroboration: Reuters and observer mission materials.

4. **Opposition and civil society faced pressure during the campaign**
- Fact: During the run-up to the election, media monitors warned of an incendiary campaign environment; journalists and reporters faced attacks and intimidation.
- Why it matters: A political system is not fully free if opposition information and campaign scrutiny are chilled.
- Strongest source: RSF, “Pre-election tension in DRC fuels attacks on reporters covering politics.” https://rsf.org/en/pre-election-tension-drc-fuels-attacks-reporters-covering-politics
- Locator: article body and title.
- Period relevance: In-period.
- Ruler attribution: Strong enough to matter at the national level, though the direct perpetrators may be regional or security actors.
- Contrary evidence: Some opposition activity and independent reporting did continue.
- Cautions: Advocacy source; strongest for documenting the media climate, not for electoral mechanics.
- Corroboration: CPJ alerts; Freedom House country report.

5. **The president publicly attacked the opposition as an obstacle to elections**
- Fact: Tshisekedi publicly blamed the opposition for trying to prevent elections.
- Why it matters: This is the clearest ruler rhetoric on political contestation in 2023.
- Strongest source: Actualite.cd, 2023-05-25. https://actualite.cd/index.php/2023/05/25/rdc-comme-lavait-fait-peter-kazadi-felix-tshisekedi-charge-lopposition-et-laccuse-de
- Locator: article body summarizing his accusation.
- Period relevance: In-period.
- Ruler attribution: Direct.
- Contrary evidence: Opposition and observers said the administration itself created or tolerated barriers and irregularities.
- Cautions: This is political rhetoric, not proof of fact.
- Corroboration: observer reporting and Reuters coverage.

6. **The campaign and tabulation environment were seen as disorderly**
- Fact: A research note from Congo Research Group predicted turnout could reach a record low, indicating deep public distrust and a weak contest environment.
- Why it matters: This helps explain the legitimacy environment of 4B.
- Strongest source: NYU Congo Research Group, 2023. https://cic.nyu.edu/resources/drc-2023-presidential-election-turnout-could-reach-record-low
- Locator: report title and summary.
- Period relevance: In-period.
- Ruler attribution: Indirect.
- Contrary evidence: A vote still occurred and turnout was not zero; legal institutions still functioned formally.
- Cautions: Forecasting note, not outcome audit.
- Corroboration: observer reports and Reuters.

7. **The political environment included restrictions on journalists and campaign reporting**
- Fact: RSF warned that journalists needed to cover the election freely and safely, implying the legal and practical environment was not secure enough.
- Why it matters: Information freedom is central to political freedom.
- Strongest source: RSF, “Reporters must be able to cover DRC presidential election freely and safely.” https://rsf.org/en/reporters-must-be-able-cover-drc-presidential-election-freely-and-safely
- Locator: article body.
- Period relevance: In-period.
- Ruler attribution: National environment under Tshisekedi.
- Contrary evidence: Some independent outlets continued to operate.
- Cautions: Advocacy framing; still useful as contemporaneous warning.
- Corroboration: CPJ and Freedom House.

8. **Courts ultimately validated the result, but only after a disputed process**
- Fact: The constitutional court’s final validation ended the formal contest but did not erase the allegations of irregularity.
- Why it matters: This is the best evidence for the formal institutional endpoint.
- Strongest source: Presidency, 2024-01-09; Actualite.cd coverage of the same ruling.
- Locator: lines 55-58 on the presidency page.
- Period relevance: Retrospective on 2023.
- Ruler attribution: Direct.
- Contrary evidence: Opposition and observer missions still contested the credibility of the process.
- Cautions: Legal finality is not the same as democratic quality.

**Disposition by Lens**
- 4B.1 Genuine electoral choice: partial/negative. Elections occurred, but the process was heavily contested and not clearly fully contestable.
- 4B.10 Lasting democratic resilience: negative. Legal continuity existed, but institutional quality and trust did not clearly improve.
- 4B.2 No entrenchment of power: negative/mixed. No hard proof of a formal power grab in 2023, but manipulation allegations and pressure on electoral space are serious.
- 4B.3 Opposition and civic freedom: negative. Opposition and journalists faced pressure, and the campaign climate was coercive.
- 4B.4 Independent checks on power: negative/mixed. Courts and observers mattered, but the checks did not eliminate controversy or capture concerns.
- 4B.5 Politically neutral institutions: negative. CENI and security-linked institutions were strongly contested.
- 4B.6 Independent information and media: negative. The media environment was pressured and unsafe.
- 4B.7 Equal political rights: mixed/negative. Political rights existed formally but were uneven in practice, especially for critics and reporters.
- 4B.8 Term limits and transfer: partial positive. Tshisekedi did not evade the 2023 election cycle, but the deeper issue is whether the competition was clean.
- 4B.9 Digital freedom and surveillance: weak evidence. I found no strong 2023 record of major digital-control policy in this chapter, so this remains underdocumented.

**Overview**
Tshisekedi’s 2023 economic record sits on a weak structural base: very low income levels, narrow reliance on mining, heavy poverty, weak state capacity, and recurring governance problems. The year’s main story is macroeconomic resilience under pressure, not broad prosperity. Growth was supported by mining and external demand, but inflation, exchange-rate pressure, fiscal strain, and corruption risk limited how much benefit reached households.

**Evidence Index**
1. **Macro resilience with worsening inflation and fiscal pressure**
- Fact: The IMF said DRC’s economy remained resilient in 2023, but inflation accelerated and the fiscal position worsened, requiring tighter policy.
- Why it matters: This is the core macro story for the year.
- Strongest source: IMF, 2023-10-31. https://www.imf.org/en/news/articles/2023/10/31/pr23369-democratic-republic-of-the-congo-imf-team-reached-sla-fifth-review-ecf
- Locator: lines 240-246.
- Period relevance: In-period.
- Ruler attribution: Strong at the policy level, though commodity prices and external conditions also mattered.
- Contrary evidence: Resilience does not mean household prosperity; the same IMF review flags deterioration in macro balances.
- Cautions: Macro-level summary, not a distributional audit.
- Corroboration: BCC monetary-policy reporting; World Bank macro outlook.

- Fact: In March 2023 the central bank’s monetary committee raised the policy rate from 8.25% to 9%.

- Ruler attribution: Indirect but relevant, because Tshisekedi’s administration oversaw the macro framework.

- Cautions: Local news reporting on a central-bank decision; good chronology, not a full policy evaluation.
