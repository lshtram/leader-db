legislative records, asset declarations, procurement records, official

reporting, and serious academic histories. Official sources are strong for the

independent finding, underlying record, response, and contrary evidence.

**Overview**

Sheikh Hasina was Bangladesh’s prime minister in 2023 and remained the dominant political actor through the 7 January 2024 election campaign. Under the constitution, executive power sits with the prime minister and cabinet, while the president is formally head of state and generally acts on the prime minister’s advice. In practice, Hasina’s authority was broad, but not unlimited: Parliament’s formal oversight existed, yet opposition weakness, judicial dependence, and external pressure from the United States and other partners constrained the government unevenly.

What follows is an orientation memo, not a final judgment. I have kept one underlying fact per record and separated retrospective material where needed.

**Evidence Records**

1. **Constitutional executive power sat with the PM, not the president**
- Fact: Bangladesh’s constitutional design gave the prime minister and cabinet the operative executive authority in 2023. The president was head of state and generally acted on the prime minister’s advice, so Hasina’s office carried real governing power rather than a merely ceremonial role.
- Why it matters: This is the baseline for attributing decisions on security, elections, economic policy, and repression.
- Strongest source: The Constitution of Bangladesh, official text, [bdlaws.minlaw.gov.bd](https://bdlaws.minlaw.gov.bd/act-367/act-chapter-print-680.html) and [bdlaws.minlaw.gov.bd](https://bdlaws.minlaw.gov.bd/act-print-367.html).
- Locator: Article 55 and 56 for the cabinet and prime minister; Article 48 for the president’s role.
- Period fit: Inherited constitutional structure, active in 2023.
- Connection to ruler: Direct and strong.
- Complicating evidence: None on the formal allocation of authority; the complication is practical, not textual.
- Cautions: Constitutional text does not show how power actually functioned in practice.
- Supporting sources: Banglapedia’s constitutional summary, Constitution Project country profile.

2. **Hasina governed through a dominant-party system with weak parliamentary checks**
- Fact: By 2023 the Awami League had consolidated political power, and the National Parliament had reduced capacity to check the executive because opposition representation was thin and the system was heavily tilted toward the ruling party.
- Why it matters: This explains why formal checks existed but were not effective constraints.
- Strongest source: Freedom House, *Freedom in the World 2024: Bangladesh*, [freedomhouse.org](https://freedomhouse.org/country/bangladesh/freedom-world/2024).
- Locator: Overview and C2 discussion on policy set by the ruling AL and weak checks.
- Period fit: Retrospective assessment of 2023 conditions.
- Connection to ruler: Strong, though the source is evaluative rather than documentary.
- Complicating evidence: The constitution still required cabinet responsibility to Parliament, and local elections sometimes produced opposition or independent wins.

## Workplan

1. **Orient.** Establish the ruler's formal and practical authority, the inherited situation, major external shocks or constraints, and the information environment.
2. **Plan coverage.** For every selected lens, identify the important favorable, adverse, disputed, and exculpatory possibilities that research should test.
3. **Find the main record.** Begin with strong overviews and syntheses. Identify the consequential decisions, laws, budgets, appointments, statements, implementation, corrections, and outcomes.
4. **Verify material claims.** Open the underlying sources. Use precise laws, official records, audits, judgments, datasets, scholarship, monitoring, and credible reporting where they establish or challenge an important claim. Assess each source’s authority, independence, method, proximity, and date.
5. **Test the account.** Look for contrary evidence, independent corroboration, relevant local-language material, source dependencies, and alternative explanations. Distinguish inherited conditions and country context from conduct attributable to the ruler.
6. **Deepen where useful.** Repeat steps 3–5 while new searches add a material fact, a stronger source, a missing perspective, or a necessary correction. Finish when further searching mainly repeats the established record or reaches a concrete access barrier.
7. **Prepare the handoff.** Organize the evidence by underlying fact and map each developed claim to every selected lens it supports.
