# Andrés Manuel López Obrador — Mexico, 2023

## 1. Overview

Andrés Manuel López Obrador (AMLO) was Mexico’s constitutional president from 1 December 2018 to 30 September 2024 and the dominant figure in the governing MORENA coalition. In 2023 he controlled the federal executive, set the political agenda through daily press conferences, and retained high approval. MORENA and allied parties held a congressional majority but lacked the supermajority required for constitutional amendments, while courts, autonomous electoral institutions, state governments, criminal organizations, economic constraints, and the United States limited his practical power.

His “Fourth Transformation” emphasized anti-corruption, social transfers, state-led infrastructure, energy sovereignty, and the declared reduction of violence. In 2023, the government expanded military responsibilities, defended major infrastructure projects, pursued an electoral reform later invalidated by the Supreme Court, and claimed declining homicide rates. Evidence also indicates persistent criminal territorial control, high impunity, risks to journalists and activists, institutional weakening, contested management of disappearances, and major infrastructure delays and cost increases.

## 2. Evidence records

### Record AMLO-2023-01 — Executive dominance constrained by Congress, courts, and criminal power

**Fact and significance.** AMLO exercised unusually strong agenda-setting power over the federal government and his party, but the 2021 legislative elections removed MORENA’s constitutional supermajority. Criminal groups also controlled or influenced some municipalities and regions, limiting the state’s effective authority. The Bertelsmann Stiftung reported that MORENA and allies retained 278 of 500 Chamber seats, enough for ordinary legislation but not constitutional reform; it also identified criminal organizations as a major constraint on governing power.

**Ruler connection.** Direct. The balance of congressional power and AMLO’s relationship with MORENA directly shaped his ability to enact his program.

**Complications.** AMLO retained high approval and expanded MORENA’s subnational reach; congressional majorities allowed substantial ordinary legislation.

## Workplan

1. **Orient.** Establish the ruler's formal and practical authority, the inherited situation, major external shocks or constraints, and the information environment.
2. **Plan coverage.** For every selected lens, identify the important favorable, adverse, disputed, and exculpatory possibilities that research should test.
3. **Find the main record.** Begin with strong overviews and syntheses. Identify the consequential decisions, laws, budgets, appointments, statements, implementation, corrections, and outcomes.
4. **Verify material claims.** Open the underlying sources. Use precise laws, official records, audits, judgments, datasets, scholarship, monitoring, and credible reporting where they establish or challenge an important claim. Assess each source’s authority, independence, method, proximity, and date.
5. **Test the account.** Look for contrary evidence, independent corroboration, relevant local-language material, source dependencies, and alternative explanations. Distinguish inherited conditions and country context from conduct attributable to the ruler.
6. **Deepen where useful.** Repeat steps 3–5 while new searches add a material fact, a stronger source, a missing perspective, or a necessary correction. Finish when further searching mainly repeats the established record or reaches a concrete access barrier.
7. **Prepare the handoff.** Organize the evidence by underlying fact and map each developed claim to every selected lens it supports.
