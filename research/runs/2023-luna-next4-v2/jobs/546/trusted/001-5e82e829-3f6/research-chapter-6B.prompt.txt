# Joe Biden — United States, 2023

## 1. Overview

Joe Biden was President of the United States throughout 2023, exercising executive authority over foreign policy, federal administration, the armed forces, regulation, and enforcement. His influence was substantial but constrained by Congress, federal courts, state governments, independent agencies, treaty partners, and a highly polarized electorate. Republicans controlled the House of Representatives; Democrats held the Senate, making major legislation dependent on cross-party negotiation.

The period combined strong employment and investment outcomes with persistent inflation, intense immigration and border disputes, continued support for Ukraine, the Gaza war after October 7, and congressional investigations into Biden-family business dealings and classified documents. Information quality was relatively high compared with many countries, but partisan polarization, litigation, secrecy around national-security matters, fragmented crime data, and competing political narratives complicated attribution.

### E001 — Executive authority was constrained by divided government

Congress enacted the Fiscal Responsibility Act of 2023 on June 3. The law suspended the debt limit through January 1, 2025, imposed discretionary-spending caps, rescinded some unobligated funds, changed work requirements, and ended the pause on student-loan payments. This demonstrates both Biden’s ability to secure a consequential compromise and the limits imposed by a Republican House and closely divided Senate.

Strongest source: Congressional Research Service, “Debt Limit Suspensions,” updated 2024, and Congress.gov bill summary. The relevant sections describe the June 3 enactment, suspension period, spending caps, and program changes. Period: 2023.

Connection to Biden: strong. The president signed the legislation, but the outcome was negotiated with congressional leaders and reflected Congress’s constitutional control over borrowing and appropriations.

Complications: The agreement avoided an immediate default but accepted spending constraints and policy concessions. The administration did not control the underlying debt-limit mechanism.

Cautions: CRS is authoritative but summarizes legislation rather than evaluating political responsibility. Congress.gov provides legal status and bill content, not an impartial account of bargaining.

Independent support: CRS analyses of the Fiscal Responsibility Act and debt-limit episode.

## Workplan

1. **Orient.** Establish the ruler's formal and practical authority, the inherited situation, major external shocks or constraints, and the information environment.
2. **Plan coverage.** For every selected lens, identify the important favorable, adverse, disputed, and exculpatory possibilities that research should test.

5. **Test the account.** Look for contrary evidence, independent corroboration, relevant local-language material, source dependencies, and alternative explanations. Distinguish inherited conditions and country context from conduct attributable to the ruler.
6. **Deepen where useful.** Repeat steps 3–5 while new searches add a material fact, a stronger source, a missing perspective, or a necessary correction. Finish when further searching mainly repeats the established record or reaches a concrete access barrier.
7. **Prepare the handoff.** Organize the evidence by underlying fact and map each developed claim to every selected lens it supports.
