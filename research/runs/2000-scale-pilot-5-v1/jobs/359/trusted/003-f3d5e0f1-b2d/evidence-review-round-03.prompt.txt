## 2. Source-Confidence Profile Standards

Every ledger item in this dossier carries the four required fields:

```yaml
source_confidence: one of high | medium_high | medium | medium_low | low | very_low
source_confidence_reason: brief justification tied to source type, claim type, and corroboration
source_type: one of primary_government | intergovernmental | ngo | think_tank | media | encyclopedia
final_evidence_use: one of final_evidence | context | discovery_only
```

Per `source-confidence-registry.json` plus the supplied I001/I002 instructions:

- **primary_government** (presidential records, DOJ, Census/BLS/CDC, FAS, UCS, official archives, GAO, CRS, USSS, ACT archive of Clinton WH) → default `medium_high` to `high` for `formal_fact_or_primary_legal_record` claims (e.g., Clinton's speech text, executive orders, Census table data, ratified-foreign-policy statements) → `final_evidence`.
- **intergovernmental / NGO** (ACA, Amnesty, HRW, OSCE/ODIHR, MIT, IIASA) → `medium_high` for documented, dated, signed claims → `final_evidence` for chronology and rollout; not sole basis for personal-conduct claims about Clinton himself.
- **think_tank** (Brookings, CATO, Heritage, AEI, Manhattan, Brennan, IFRI, NSArchive) → rated by analogy: `medium_high` for archive-declassified material (NSArchive); `medium` for clearly evidence-based analysis (Brookings, IFRI); `medium_low` to `low` for advocacy (Heritage, CATO) used for finding/corroboration only.
- **reputable media** (NYT, WaPo, WSJ, BBC, CNN, Reuters, AP, Guardian, LAT, NBC, ABC, NBC; PBS NewsHour; ProPublica) → `medium_high` for chronology/attribution → `final_evidence` only when corroborated or when the claim is itself the report.
- **encyclopedia** (Wikipedia) → `medium_high` only for orientation; locator must point to the cited primary source, not the Wikipedia article.
- **Grokipedia and equivalent AI encyclopedias** → never used.
