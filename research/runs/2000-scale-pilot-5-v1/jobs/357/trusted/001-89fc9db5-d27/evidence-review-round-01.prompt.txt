## 2. Broad Ruler-Period Reconnaissance (cross-chapter)

Obasanjo came to office on **29 May 1999** after the Abacha-era transition, on the PDP platform. The 2000 calendar year was the **first full year** of Fourth-Republic civilian rule. The information environment was partly open (private press active, HRW/CPJ operating in country, NGOs publishing) but the security forces and judiciary were largely inherited from military rule.

Cross-chapter facts that recur across multiple lenses (recorded once and reused):
- **CTBT signature.** Obasanjo personally signed the Comprehensive Nuclear-Test-Ban Treaty in New York at the UN Millennium Summit in September 2000. (UN/FAS/Wilson Center — directly serves Chapter 1B.)
- **ICPC and Corrupt Practices Act.** The Corrupt Practices and Other Related Offences Act was signed June 2000; the Independent Corrupt Practices Commission was inaugurated 29 September 2000. (Chatham House, SOAS, Wikipedia, ICPC — directly serves Chapter 7B.)
- **Poverty Alleviation Programme (PAP).** Launched 2000 with a N10 billion budget. (ResearchGate critique paper.)
- **National Poverty Eradication Programme (NAPEP).** Established 2001 under Obasanjo — adjacent-period context only.
- **Paris Club / debt negotiations.** Nigeria secured a Paris Club invitation in 2000 and began intensive IMF negotiations on a reform program. (Brookings/Rieffel; Developments UK.)
- **Abacha-loot recovery.** March 2000: Obasanjo government announced foreign governments were positively considering its requests; frozen assets rose from $1.3 bn to $1.93 bn within a year. (Sage / Enweremadu 2013.)
- **Niger Delta Development Commission (NDDC).** Established 2000 by Obasanjo (Wikipedia "Conflict in the Niger Delta").
- **Odi massacre accountability.** No public investigation of Odi (Nov 1999) as of HRW World Report 2002; Obasanjo visited Odi in March 2001 but "no one was brought to justice for these abuses." (HRW World Report 2002 / 2005 / 2006 / 2008.)
- **Sharia riots in Kaduna.** Feb 2000 and May 2000 — over 2,000 killed cumulatively. (New Humanitarian, Wikipedia.)
- **Press attacks in 2000.** CPJ documented multiple cases of police/agent harassment of journalists at Punch, The News, and others. (CPJ 2001.)
- **ECOMOG/Sierra Leone peacekeeping.** Obasanjo inherited and continued Nigeria's leading ECOMOG role; UNAMSIL's deployment was tied to ECOMOG's posture. (UNAMSIL Wikipedia; Clinton White House Fact Sheet, August 2000.)
