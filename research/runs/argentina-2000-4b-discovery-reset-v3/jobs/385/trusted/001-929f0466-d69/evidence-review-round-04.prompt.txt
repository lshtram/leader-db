### New located source-claim units

- `ARG2000-4B035-CORRIENTESLAW` — Argentina Law 25.236, arts. 1–8, promulgated 16 Dec 1999. Congress intervened Corrientes, terminated the governor, vice-governor, legislature, and placed provincial judges in commission; the executive appointed the federal intervenor and elections were required. The law was signed/promulgated by de la Rúa. Period: continuing intervention in 2000. Attribution: direct legal implementation, but congressional authorization was decisive. Contrary: formal legality does not resolve partisan purpose. Links: 4B.2, 4B.4, 4B.5, 4B.7. [Official text](https://www.argentina.gob.ar/normativa/recurso/61589/texact/htm)

- `ARG2000-4B036-CORRIENTESPROROGATION` — Presidential Decree 480/2000, arts. 1–2, dated 12 June 2000. The executive extended the intervention for 180 days and reappointed Ramón Mestre. Its stated grounds included fiscal collapse, state paralysis, institutional crisis, political representativeness, public-service restoration, judicial reform, and police professionalization. Contrary: these are executive findings, not independent validation; extension itself remains an interventionist act. Links: 4B.2, 4B.4, 4B.5, 4B.7. [Official decree](https://www.argentina.gob.ar/normativa/nacional/decreto-480-2000-63350/texto)

- `ARG2000-4B037-CORRIENTESCAUSE` — Página/12, 16 Dec 1999, paragraphs beginning “La propuesta se basa…”. The government presented serious social disturbances and threats to institutional stability as the rationale for intervention. Attribution: stated executive rationale only. Contrary: contemporaneous press does not establish that the rationale was accurate or nonpartisan. Links: 4B.2, 4B.4, 4B.7.

- `ARG2000-4B038-PRESSFREEDOMIPI` — International Press Institute, *2000 World Press Freedom Review*, PDF pp. 19–21. IPI reports modest national improvement: the government referred Periodistas reports to Congress and courts convicted eight of ten people in the Cabezas murder. It simultaneously records threats, assaults, shootings, journalist abductions, and local political intimidation, stating that stronger federal protection was needed. Attribution: positive national measures are government-linked; adverse conduct is mainly local/provincial. Links: 4B.3, 4B.6, 4B.7. [PDF](https://www.infoamerica.org/documentos_pdf/ipi_ar_00.pdf)

- `ARG2000-4B039-PRESSLAWCPJ` — CPJ, *Attacks on the Press 2000: Argentina*, PDF pp. 4–6. De la Rúa asked Congress to study criminal-defamation reform; the proposal stalled after the Senate bribery scandal. CPJ records that investigative journalism exposed the scandal and that Álvarez resigned after de la Rúa refused to dismiss two implicated officials. No violent press attacks were recorded in 2000, though threats and restrictive defamation judgments remained. Links: 4B.3, 4B.5, 4B.6, 4B.8. [PDF](https://www.infoamerica.org/documentos_pdf/cpj_ar_01.pdf)

- `ARG2000-4B040-SIDE_RESTRUCTURE` — Página/12, 8 Feb 2000. SIDE chief Fernando de Santibañes reported eliminating more than 1,000 “inorganic” agents and roughly one-third of permanent staff, describing the restructuring as intended to guarantee public liberties and improve presidential information capacity. Attribution: direct institutional reform under a presidential appointee, but not proof of operational restraint. Contrary: the same agency later became central to the bribery controversy. Links: 4B.5, 4B.9. [Report](https://www.pagina12.com.ar/2000/00-02/00-02-08/pag03.htm)

- `ARG2000-4B041-SIDE_SECRET_DISCLOSURE` — De la Rúa’s Decree 833/2000, reported in the Argentine government’s later court filing. The decree authorized disclosure of SIDE expenditure records to Judge Liporaci during the Senate-bribery investigation. Attribution: direct presidential cooperation with judicial fact-finding. Contrary: disclosure occurred amid allegations involving the executive’s intelligence chief and does not establish full accountability. Links: 4B.4, 4B.5, 4B.9. [Government filing](https://www.argentina.gob.ar/sites/default/files/recurso_de_casacion_causa_857-07.pdf)

- `ARG2000-4B042-ALVAREZ_PRIMARY` — La Nación, 7 Oct 2000, paragraphs 102–127. Álvarez resigned over cabinet changes, especially the promotion of Alberto Flamarique and retention of SIDE chief Fernando de Santibañes; de la Rúa tried to persuade him to stay. Attribution: direct cabinet decisions and attempted coalition preservation. Contrary: the effort to retain the coalition complicates any claim of deliberate suppression of dissent. Links: 4B.3, 4B.5, 4B.8, 4B.9. [Report](https://www.lanacion.com.ar/politica/alvarez-renuncio-por-discrepar-con-las-decisiones-de-de-la-rua-nid35969/)

- `ARG2000-4B043-ALVAREZ_CONTRARY` — Washington Post, 11 Oct 2000, paragraphs 16–32. De la Rúa denied administration involvement, regretted Álvarez’s resignation, and said the coalition would continue, while rejecting calls to dismiss Santibañes. Attribution: direct presidential statements and retention decision. Contrary: denial and coalition-preservation claims are contrary evidence to intentional obstruction; retention remains an adverse accountability fact. Links: 4B.3, 4B.5, 4B.8, 4B.9. [Report](https://www.washingtonpost.com/archive/politics/2000/10/11/bribery-scandal-singes-argentine-anti-graft-crusader/6e32f5a2-9e4d-497d-8011-3b4a249cf6ca/)

- `ARG2000-4B044-MINORITY_PARTICIPATION` — Presidential Decree 1246/2000, arts. 1–13, dated 28 Dec 2000. De la Rúa’s government required the 30% women’s-list quota to apply to senators, deputies, and national constituent candidates, specified list placement rules, and required parties to prepare for the 2001 legislative renewal. Contrary: the decree formalized access but does not demonstrate actual representation or broader minority equality. Links: 4B.7, 4B.8. [Official decree](https://www.argentina.gob.ar/normativa/nacional/decreto-1246-2000-65634/texto)

- `ARG2000-4B045-CONSTITUTIONAL_TERM_RULE` — Argentine Congress constitutional text, §90. Presidential and vice-presidential terms are four years, with only one consecutive reelection or reciprocal succession permitted. This is a formal rule in force during 2000, not evidence that de la Rúa personally defended it. Links: 4B.8. [Congress text](https://www.congreso.gob.ar/constitucionSeccion2Cap1_ingles.php)
