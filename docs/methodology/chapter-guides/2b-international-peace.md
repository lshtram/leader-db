# Chapter 2B — International Peace, Restraint, And Lawful Security

Status: **draft; revised after 2020 v2 acceptance audit; requires v3 rejudgment**

## Chapter Identity

- Chapter: `2B`
- Rating: international peace versus aggression and war
- Evidence strategy: local-first structured evidence plus cited manual research
- Rubric version: `chapter_2b_v3`
- Output: one chapter score (`1`–`10`) and one confidence assessment per ruler-period

## Purpose And Non-Goals

This chapter evaluates whether a ruler used diplomacy and force responsibly, avoided
aggression, constrained partners and proxies, protected civilians, pursued peace, and
left international relations more peaceful and lawful. It does not equate military
spending or battlefield victory with aggression, demand pacifism in the face of attack,
or blame a ruler automatically for an inherited war. Domestic repression belongs
primarily in Chapter 3B; economic opportunity cost is contextual here. The client
matrix is never evidence.

## Ten Evidence Lenses

1. **2B.1:** Did the ruler choose diplomacy, compromise, and de-escalation when credible peaceful alternatives existed, rather than treating force as the preferred first option?
2. **2B.2:** Did the ruler initiate, expand, prolong, or justify wars of choice, cross-border coercion, annexation, covert destabilization, or proxy conflict beyond defensive necessity?
3. **2B.3:** Did the ruler distinguish genuine defensive security needs from prestige, revenge, nationalism, manufactured threats, diversionary politics, or regime-survival motives?
4. **2B.4:** Did the ruler respect civilian protection, humanitarian law, prisoner treatment, necessity, and proportionality in military operations?
5. **2B.5:** Did the ruler restrain security forces, militias, allies, proxies, clients, and arms recipients from atrocities or destabilization, and accept responsibility for foreseeable proxy conduct?
6. **2B.6:** Did the ruler truthfully explain security threats to the public, or manipulate intelligence, fear, historical grievance, and misinformation to build support for conflict?
7. **2B.7:** Did the ruler pursue credible ceasefires, peace talks, confidence-building measures, lawful settlements, or post-conflict reconciliation when possible?
8. **2B.8:** Did the ruler use military spending and mobilization to meet real security needs, or to enrich networks, reward security elites, intimidate neighbors, or project personal strength?
9. **2B.9:** Did the ruler accept accountability for military failures, civilian harm, illegal conduct, and later evidence that contradicted the stated justification for conflict?
10. **2B.10:** Did the ruler leave regional/international relations more peaceful, stable, and lawful than they inherited them, accounting for inherited conflicts and external constraints?

The lens statuses and wording are advisory. Missing lenses reduce confidence; they do
not invalidate the dossier, force zero, or require a rigid status vocabulary.

## Researcher Guidance

Begin with resolved identity and local D13 UCDP and D14 SIPRI facts. Research coherent
themes: inherited conflicts and threat environment; initiation and alternatives;
objectives/justifications; conduct and civilian harm; proxy/arms-recipient control;
public claims; negotiations and settlements; military patronage; accountability; and
end-state change. Reuse evidence across lenses when substantively justified.

Prefer UCDP/PA-X/UN records, treaties and court findings, ICRC and UN humanitarian
reporting, official operational/legal records, legislative or independent inquiries,
credible NGOs, peer-reviewed histories, and reputable reporting. Distinguish allegation,
documented event, legal finding, command policy, foreseeable consequence, and ruler
direction. Belligerent official claims require independent corroboration. Researchers
collect cited claims and counterevidence, not a final score.

Establish a conflict/exposure gate before judging: active or inherited interstate
war, cross-border operations, proxy support, coercive diplomacy, peace-process
authority, or a documented absence of such exposure. If conflict exposure is
plausible but initiation, alternatives, civilian conduct, proxy control, and ruler
authority are absent, continue research or return null rather than assigning a
midpoint. Purely domestic repression and ordinary policing belong in 3B. Nuclear
threats enter 2B only when they materially serve an international conflict or
coercive-security choice; nuclear-risk responsibility itself belongs in 1B.

## Sparse, Historical, And Closed-Regime Evidence

Use diplomatic archives, contemporaneous records, military histories, peace agreements,
and later scholarship for older rulers, labeling hindsight and contested causation.
Closed regimes and wartime censorship make silence especially weak evidence; triangulate
opposing claims, neutral monitoring, satellite/event data, refugee testimony, and later
investigations. A ruler without an international crisis is a limited-exposure case, not
automatically an exemplary peacemaker. Unknown conduct lowers confidence. Score a thin
record cautiously when the central peace/aggression choices are nevertheless clear.

## Attribution And Inherited Baseline

Identify formal and practical command, coalition constraints, parliamentary/cabinet
roles, patron-state influence, and whether the ruler initiated, adopted, escalated,
restrained, ended, or could realistically change inherited policy. Do not attribute every
military act personally; do attribute ordered, authorized, systematically tolerated,
foreseeably enabled, concealed, or unremedied conduct in proportion to authority. Judge
both absolute conduct and change from inherited war, alliances, threats, and territorial
disputes.

## Chapter-Level 1–10 Rubric

| Score | Chapter anchor |
|---:|---|
| 1 | Clear aggressive war, annexation, extreme proxy destabilization, or systematic unlawful conduct driven or sustained by the ruler, with grave consequences and rejection of peace/accountability. |
| 2–3 | Severe recurring aggression, escalation, civilian disregard, proxy abuse, manipulated justification, or obstruction of credible peace, with weak restraint. |
| 4–5 | Materially mixed record: legitimate security concerns or some diplomacy coexist with avoidable escalation, disproportionate conduct, proxy failures, or missed peace opportunities. |
| 6–7 | Generally defensive, restrained, and diplomacy-oriented conduct with meaningful flaws, limited harmful episodes, weak accountability, or constrained peace outcomes. |
| 8–9 | Strong sustained restraint, civilian protection, credible diplomacy, proxy control, truthful justification, accountability, and consequential improvement in peace. |
| 10 | Exceptional, durable, and well-evidenced leadership that prevented or ended grave conflict and strengthened lawful peace despite major pressure and real alternatives. |

A score of 6 or above requires demonstrated restraint, lawful security conduct, or
peace action—not peaceful rhetoric or the mere absence of known aggression. A score
below 4 requires direct chapter-relevant evidence of ruler-attributed aggression,
reckless escalation, grave civilian/proxy conduct, or obstruction of credible peace.

Lens weights are qualitative and unequal. Aggressive initiation, mass civilian harm, or
ending a major inherited war may dominate multiple lesser indicators. Do not compute a
ten-item average. Defensive necessity mitigates initiation but never erases duties of
proportionality, civilian protection, truth, proxy restraint, and accountability.
Scoring is opportunity-adjusted. Limited conflict exposure or authority is not itself a
high or low score. Consistent responsible restraint can justify a high score when the
ruler faced meaningful choices—such as credible escalation, coercion, arms-transfer, or
peace opportunities—and chose well within actual authority. If no discriminating
international-security opportunity exists, return null with an explicit exposure
assessment rather than treating quiet circumstances as proven excellence or assigning
a fabricated midpoint.

## Judge Workflow Across Rulers

### V3 international-conduct gate

A numeric score requires documented target-period international conduct attributable
to the ruler. Domestic protest handling, internal repression, inherited conflict
exposure, and an adverse outcome without a supported causal or restraint judgment do
not meet the floor. No documented external conduct means null, not a neutral score.

One Chapter 2B judge reviews the full comparison batch. Establish inherited conflicts,
threats, authority, and credible alternatives; identify aggressor/defender and mixed-role
cases; compare severity, scale, duration, intent, restraint, consequences, and peace
efforts; provisionally order cases; then assign one score and confidence per ruler. State
decisive positive and negative evidence, counterevidence, attribution, baseline change,
and rejected adjacent anchors. Recheck the complete ordering for great-power, victor,
and visibility bias. If sharding is unavoidable, use overlapping anchors and a final
cross-shard calibration.

## Minimum Evidence And Graceful Degradation

Require at least one traceable relevant citation, structured priors or an explained
`not_available`, and explicit authority, conflict exposure, temporal fit, and inherited
baseline. Serious score-changing allegations should not rest solely on one belligerent
or low-confidence source. Extreme/high-confidence ratings normally need independent
source types. Missing proxy, internal deliberation, or accountability evidence reduces
confidence; it does not invalidate the dossier. Withhold only where identity, central
attribution, or citation authenticity prevents a defensible judgment.

## Common Evidence-Item Semantics

Each material evidence item should carry a stable evidence ID; a concise factual claim;
source URL, title, publisher, and publication/access date where available; source type
and confidence with a short reason; a quotation or precise locator when available;
target-year/ruler-period fit; ruler attribution and inherited-baseline relevance; its
positive, negative, mixed, contextual, or contrary role; and the supported lens IDs.
Fields may be normalized later and natural-language equivalents are acceptable. The
substantive minimum is a traceable source, intelligible claim, temporal fit, attribution,
and evidence role. One item may support multiple lenses without duplication.

## Bias And Comparability Checks

Check winner/loser and hindsight bias; great-power and media visibility; defensive-threat
context; conflict duration and population scale; asymmetric and proxy warfare; official
and belligerent-source bias; legal-finding versus allegation; inherited-war attribution;
peace-opportunity counterfactuals; recency; and cross-ruler anchor consistency. Do not
count event volume as culpability without role, or treat failed negotiations as bad faith
without credible alternatives and conduct evidence.

## Draft Acceptance Checklist

- All ten exact lens texts appear.
- Research distinguishes role, necessity, conduct, proxy responsibility, and peace effort.
- One comparative judge produces one score and confidence per ruler.
- Lenses are unequal and non-arithmetic; missing lenses only reduce confidence.
- Inherited wars, actual authority, and credible alternatives are explicit.
- Contrary evidence and underlying citations survive handoff.
- Smoke testing and independent review occur before activation.
